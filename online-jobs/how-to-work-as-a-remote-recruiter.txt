<p>A recruiter, also called a talent acquisition specialist, finds, screens, and interviews people for job openings, then helps the employer hire the best one. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics describes the work in plain terms: recruitment specialists find, screen, and interview applicants, check references and backgrounds, and extend job offers. A remote recruiter does the same work from home, using video calls, email, and hiring software.</p>
<p>A typical day has five parts. First, sourcing, which means searching for candidates on job boards, LinkedIn, and CV databases. Second, screening, which means reading CVs and holding short calls to check whether a candidate fits the role. Third, scheduling, which means booking interviews between candidates and hiring managers across time zones. Fourth, interviewing and feedback, which means running deeper interviews and passing clear notes to the hiring manager. Fifth, closing and reporting, which means making the offer, checking references, and recording what happened. Some days are full of calls, and other days are full of writing and admin, but a hire only happens when the admin is done well.</p>

Commission, which is a payment based on the hires you make, usually starts at the full-cycle stage.</p>
<h2 id="agency-in-house-rpo-and-freelance-which-model-fits-whom">Agency, in-house, RPO, and freelance: which model fits whom</h2>
<p>There are four ways to work as a recruiter, and they feel very different. Pick the one that fits your goals.</p>
<p>An agency recruiter works for a staffing agency and fills roles for many client companies. The pace is fast, the targets are clear, and the pay often includes commission. This suits people who like variety and sales style work. An in-house recruiter works for one company and fills its own roles. The pace is steadier, the pay is usually a fixed salary, and you learn one business deeply. This suits people who want stability. An RPO recruiter, where RPO stands for recruitment process outsourcing, works for an outside firm that runs hiring for a client company. A freelance recruiter works for themselves and takes on hiring projects for clients. The pay can be high per placement, but the income is uneven, and you do your own admin, tax, and marketing. Beginners should start in an agency or in-house, where training and a steady wage exist, before they try freelance work.</p>
<h2 id="skills-employers-ask-for">Skills employers ask for</h2>
<p>The most important skill is written communication. You will write job adverts, messages to candidates, interview notes, and feedback, and each one must be clear, honest, and free of errors. The second skill is sourcing, which means finding people with search tools, including Boolean search, a way of combining words such as AND, OR, and NOT to narrow a search. The third skill is judgement: compare candidates fairly, check their claims, and give the hiring manager a clear recommendation. The fourth skill is relationship building, because good candidates come back to recruiters they trust. The fifth skill is data literacy: track simple numbers such as time to fill, the days from opening a role to making an offer, and source of hire, where the hired person came from.</p>
<p>You also need remote work skills: clear video call habits, fast replies, careful notes, and the discipline to follow up when nobody is watching. Employers test these in interviews, so give real examples from any job where you managed people or a busy schedule.</p>
<h2 id="tools-to-learn">Tools to learn</h2>
<p>Learn one applicant tracking system, called an ATS, which stores candidates, tracks interviews, and records decisions. Common systems are Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, and Workday. Learn one well and be ready to learn another on the job. Learn LinkedIn Recruiter, the paid search tool for finding and messaging candidates, and practise Boolean search inside it. Learn Slack and Zoom, because most remote teams live in them, and a simple spreadsheet tool for tracking your pipeline.</p>

<h2 id="qualifications-and-certifications">Qualifications and certifications</h2>
<p>Most recruiter roles do not need a degree, although many adverts list one. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says human resources specialists typically need a bachelor's degree in human resources, business, or a related field, but some positions accept relevant work experience instead. Customer service, sales, admin, and support work all count, because they teach the communication and organisation skills recruiting needs.</p>
<p>Two certifications can help once you have some experience. SHRM-CP, from the Society for Human Resource Management, covers general human resources knowledge. AIRS Certified Recruiter focuses on sourcing and internet research. Neither is required for a first role. A short course in sourcing or Boolean search is a better first spend.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-get-the-first-remote-recruiter-job-with-no-experience">How to get the first remote recruiter job with no experience</h2>
<p>The agency training route is the most reliable way in. Staffing agencies hire beginners and pay a base wage while they learn. Start by learning sourcing for two weeks: practise Boolean search, build a sample shortlist for a real role, and write three sample messages to candidates. Then apply to agencies for sourcing, screening, or coordinator roles, and say that you want to learn full-cycle recruiting.</p>
<p>If agencies do not reply, try a second route: apply for recruitment coordinator or HR assistant roles, which are easier to win, then move into sourcing once you are inside. A third route is to volunteer: help a small charity or a local business with a hire, and use that as your first example in interviews. Whatever route you take, keep a record of your work.</p>
<h2 id="where-to-find-remote-recruiter-jobs">Where to find remote recruiter jobs</h2>
<p>Start with the staffing agencies themselves, because they hire the most beginners. Look at the careers pages of large agencies and apply for remote sourcing and coordinator roles. Add LinkedIn, with a saved search for remote recruiter, remote talent acquisition, and remote sourcing specialist, and turn on alerts. Add general remote boards such as We Work Remotely and Remote.co. Then approach companies directly: pick 20 companies that are hiring fast, find their talent team on LinkedIn, and send a short message saying you want to learn recruiting and asking whether they have a junior opening.</p>
<h2 id="salary-and-pay-structures">Salary and pay structures</h2>
<p>Pay for remote recruiters varies widely, because the pay structure matters as much as the headline number. The four common structures are fixed salary, hourly pay, commission, and a hybrid of salary plus commission.</p>
<p>The official figures come from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, checked on its site in November 2026. The median annual wage for human resources specialists was 75,940 dollars in May 2025, the lowest 10 percent earned less than 47,180 dollars, and the highest 10 percent earned more than 128,720 dollars. Employment is projected to grow 6 percent from 2025 to 2035, with about 73,700 openings a year. The <a href="https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/human-resources-specialists.htm" rel="noopener" target="_blank">BLS human resources specialists page</a> gives these figures and notes that recruitment specialists are a type of human resources specialist.</p>
<p>DailyRemote, whose recruiter guide was updated on 15 November 2026, reports an average of 75,000 dollars a year, with entry level coordinators and sourcing specialists at 45,000 to 55,000 dollars, and senior recruiters and recruiting managers at 90,000 to 130,000 dollars or more. Those are the guide own figures with no named source, but they sit close to the BLS median. RemoGrid, another guide, gives no figures and warns that entry level sourcing and screening work may pay only a few dollars an hour or a modest monthly rate, while commission roles pay the most but with variable income.</p>
<p>Commission only roles deserve a warning. In a commission only role, you earn nothing unless a hire is made, and a hire can take weeks or months. Some beginners take these roles because they sound flexible, then earn nothing for months. Before you accept any role, ask for the pay structure in writing: the base pay, the commission rate, when commission is paid, and what happens if a hire leaves early.</p>
<h2 id="example-only-how-a-placement-fee-works">Example only: how a placement fee works</h2>
<p>This example shows how commission maths works. It is an example only, with made up rates.</p>
<p>Suppose an agency charges a client a fee of 15 percent of a new hire first year salary. If the salary is 70,000 dollars, the fee is 70,000 times 0.15, which is 10,500 dollars. If the recruiter keeps 40 percent of that fee, the recruiter earns 10,500 times 0.40, which is 4,200 dollars for that placement. Two placements in a month would be 2 times 4,200, which is 8,400 dollars for the month, before tax and before any base pay. But if the same recruiter makes no placements for two months, the commission is zero, which is why a base salary matters.</p>
<h2 id="cv-and-interview-preparation">CV and interview preparation</h2>
<p>Keep your CV to one or two pages. Put a short line at the top that says you want a remote recruiting role and what you bring, such as strong writing and fast learning. List any experience that shows communication, organisation, or sales, even from a shop, a call centre, or volunteer work. Use numbers where you can, such as the customers you served or the candidates you sourced in practice. Read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-write-cv-for-remote-jobs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to write a CV for remote jobs</a> for the full CV structure.</p>
<p>For interviews, prepare for common questions: how would you find candidates for a hard role, how would you handle a candidate who stops replying, and how would you give bad news to a hiring manager. Answer with the STAR method: situation, task, action, and result. Practise a short sourcing demo, such as how you would search for a nurse or a developer, because some employers ask you to show your search live. Ask the interviewer how the team measures success, what the commission structure is, and what training a beginner gets. Read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-pass-remote-job-interview" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to pass a remote job interview</a> for more preparation, and our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-negotiate-salary-for-a-remote-job" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to negotiate salary for a remote job</a> before you discuss pay.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Do not take a commission only role as your first job.</li>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Recruiting has its own scams, and beginners are the target. A real employer never asks you to pay for a job, a training course, or a list of candidates before you start. A real agency pays you, not the other way round. Watch for fake agencies that charge a fee to join, that ask for your bank details in the first message, or that promise high commission with no base pay and no written terms. Watch for fake job adverts that collect CVs to steal identities.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">US Federal Trade Commission guide to job scams</a> says that honest placement firms do not typically charge a fee to job candidates, and that the hiring company pays them instead. If a firm asks you for a fee, especially one you have to pay in advance, walk away. If a listing feels wrong, search the company name with the word scam, and read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-spot-online-job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to spot online job scams</a> before you reply. Also learn the basics of fair hiring: do not ask candidates about protected traits, keep their data safe, and follow the hiring laws of the country you recruit in.</p>

<h3 id="what-does-a-remote-recruiter-do">What does a remote recruiter do?</h3>
<p>A remote recruiter finds, screens, and interviews people for job openings, then helps the employer hire the best one, all from home. The work includes sourcing, first calls, booking interviews, feedback, offers, and records.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-become-a-remote-recruiter-with-no-experience">How do I become a remote recruiter with no experience?</h3>
<p>Learn sourcing for two weeks, practise Boolean search, and build a sample shortlist for a real role. Then apply to staffing agencies for sourcing, screening, or coordinator roles. You can also start as a recruitment coordinator or HR assistant and move into sourcing once you are inside.</p>

<p>Yes, if you start in the right place. Agency sourcing and coordinator roles train beginners and pay a base wage while they learn. The work suits people who like talking to others, writing clearly, and keeping organised. It does not suit people who dislike calls, targets, or rejection.</p>
