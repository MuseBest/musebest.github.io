How to Find Legitimate Remote Jobs

Real remote jobs exist — and so do thousands of fake listings. Learn where to look, how employers actually hire, and the red flags that mean walk away.

Where legitimate remote jobs are listed

Start with the places employers actually use: company careers pages, established job boards with remote filters, and specialist remote job boards. Recruitment agencies that specialise in remote work are also worth contacting. Be wary of listings that only appear on social media posts or messaging apps.

How real hiring works

A legitimate employer will:

  • Interview you, usually by video, before offering a job.
  • Give you a written contract or offer letter.
  • Pay you through a normal payroll or payment system.
  • Never ask you to pay for training, equipment, or a "processing fee" to start.

If any of those are missing, pause and investigate.

Red flags that mean a listing is probably a scam

  • You are offered a job without an interview.
  • The salary is far above the market rate for the role.
  • You are asked to pay money upfront, buy equipment from a specific seller, or cash cheques.
  • The employer's email is a free webmail address and there is no verifiable company website.
  • The job description is vague, full of spelling errors, or copied from elsewhere.
  • You are contacted on WhatsApp or Telegram by someone claiming to be a recruiter, with no LinkedIn profile or company email.

See our full guide to online earning scams for more detail on each of these.

Preparing a remote-ready application

Remote employers look for evidence you can work without supervision:

  • A CV that states your tools and time zone clearly.
  • Examples of work, even from personal projects.
  • A short cover note that answers the job's actual requirements, not a generic template.
  • Reliable internet and a quiet place to work — mention it if the job asks.

The application routine

Apply to a focused list of roles you genuinely fit, rather than spraying hundreds of generic applications. Tailor the first paragraph of each application to the role, follow up once after a week, and keep a simple spreadsheet of where you applied and when.

What to do if you are unsure

Search the company name plus "scam" or "review", check the domain's age and registration, and ask in a professional community. If something feels wrong, it usually is — a real employer will not mind you verifying them.
