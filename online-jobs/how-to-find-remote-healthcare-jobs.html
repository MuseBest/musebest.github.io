How to Find Remote Healthcare Jobs in 2026

To find remote healthcare jobs, you need to know which healthcare jobs can genuinely be done from home and which cannot. A nurse can triage patients by phone, a pharmacist can counsel patients online, and a medical records worker can update files from anywhere. This guide maps the remote roles, explains the licence and privacy rules, and shows you where to apply and how to interview. Medical coding is only mentioned briefly here, because our guide on how to work as a remote medical coder covers it in depth.

Remote healthcare roles at a glance

Healthcare work splits into two kinds: clinical work, which means dealing directly with patients, such as diagnosing, treating, and prescribing, and non-clinical work, which means healthcare related work that does not involve treating patients, such as admin, IT, and research. Clinical roles need a licence and usually limit you to the state or country where you are licensed. Non-clinical roles are more flexible on location.

The table below shows the main remote roles, whether they are clinical or non-clinical, what they need, and the median pay reported by Coursera from US Bureau of Labor Statistics data.

The BLS also projects about 1.9 million healthcare job openings a year, although only some of those roles are remote. Pay is in US dollars and reflects United States data.

Telehealth roles

Telehealth is the practice of providing healthcare remotely using phone, video, and messaging technology. It has grown fast, and it is now normal for doctors, nurses, and therapists to see patients by video for follow ups, mental health sessions, and minor illness checks.

Physicians can run virtual clinics, review test results, and prescribe where the rules allow. Pharmacists can counsel patients, check prescriptions, and manage medication therapy from home. Psychologists and therapists can hold video sessions, which many patients prefer from their own homes. Speech-language pathologists can run therapy sessions by video with screen sharing and digital tools. Each role needs the right degree and a licence, plus a quiet room, a good camera, and a reliable internet connection.

Nursing and care roles from home

Nurses have more remote options than most people realise. A telehealth nurse answers patient calls, gives advice, and follows up after treatment. A triage nurse, which is a nurse who judges how urgent a patient problem is, sorts incoming calls and decides who needs urgent care and who can wait. A case manager organises care for a patient, books appointments, and checks that the patient gets the right support. A utilisation review nurse checks whether a treatment is needed and covered, and reviews records for insurers and hospitals.

These roles need a nursing licence, and they suit nurses who like talking to patients and solving problems on the phone. The work is less physical than ward work, but it needs sharp judgement, because you cannot see the patient in person. If you are a nurse who wants to leave shift work, a case management or triage role is a good first step.

Non-clinical roles

Non-clinical roles suit people who want to work in healthcare without treating patients. Health informatics, which is the work of managing health data and computer systems, covers roles such as health information technologist, who advises on computerised healthcare systems and analyses clinical data. Medical writing covers patient leaflets, trial reports, and health articles, and it suits people with a science background and clear writing. Health administration covers scheduling, billing support, and running the office side of a clinic. Insurance and customer service roles cover patient questions about cover, claims, and appointments. Clinical research associates act as a link between research sponsors and clinics, checking that trials run smoothly and ethically, although some travel is usually needed.

These roles often need a bachelor's degree, but they do not need a clinical licence, which makes them more open to career changers. If you have admin, writing, or IT skills, look at non-clinical roles first.

Roles that need no degree

Several remote healthcare jobs need no degree, although a short certificate helps. Medical assistants who work from home handle scheduling, phone follow ups, and admin tasks. Medical records technicians maintain patient files and check that records follow the rules. Medical transcriptionists turn voice recordings from doctors into written reports, and the work is easy to do from home because it is mostly typing and proofreading. Health insurance and patient support call centre roles answer questions and book appointments.

These roles pay less than clinical roles, but they are a real way into healthcare. A certificate programme of one year or less is often enough, and some employers train you on the job. Check the certificate cost before you pay, and ask the employer whether they accept the certificate you plan to take.

Licences, qualifications, and the location problem

Licences are the biggest limit on remote healthcare work. A clinical licence is usually valid only in one state or country, which means a nurse licensed in one place cannot legally treat patients in another place without a second licence. Some countries have licence compacts, which are agreements that let a licence work in more than one place, but you must check the rules for each place where your patients live. Always check the licence first, then apply.

Privacy rules are the second limit. In the United States, HIPAA, which is the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, sets rules for how patient health information must be protected. The US Department of Health and Human Services HIPAA home page explains the rules and says the Office for Civil Rights enforces them. At home, that means a private room for calls, a locked computer, and no patient details on a shared screen. Other countries have their own rules, such as data protection laws in the UK and the EU, so check the rules for your country before you start. Employers will test you on this in interviews.

Where to find remote healthcare jobs

Use healthcare specific boards first, because they filter out the noise. Try FlexJobs, which lists screened remote healthcare roles, and Remote.co, which has a healthcare section. Add general boards such as LinkedIn and Indeed, with saved searches for telehealth nurse, remote pharmacist, remote case manager, and remote medical records. Then go direct: check the careers pages of telehealth companies, insurers, and hospital groups, because many roles appear there first. Join online communities for remote nurses and healthcare workers, where members share openings that never reach a board.

Companies hiring remotely

Several large employers hire remote healthcare workers on a regular basis. FlexJobs, whose guide was updated on 11 June 2025, names 15 companies with recent remote healthcare roles, including UnitedHealth Group, Centene, Elevance Health, Clover Health, HCA, Hims and Hers, Included Health, and Talkiatry, as well as hospital groups such as Brigham and Women's Hospital, MD Anderson, and Sutter Health, and insurers such as Blue Cross Blue Shield. The roles they hire for include case management, health administration, nursing, mental health, pharmacy, and medical research.

Treat that list as a starting point, not a promise. The job samples in the FlexJobs guide are from early 2025 and may no longer be open, so check each company careers page for current roles. Use the list to build your own target list of 20 companies, then check their pages weekly. Telehealth providers such as Doctor on Demand, PlushCare, and MDLive, and mental health platforms such as BetterHelp, also hire remote clinicians, although their pay models vary, so read the terms before you apply.

Applying and interviewing for remote healthcare roles

Your CV must show your licence, your qualifications, and your remote readiness in the first few lines. Put your licence or registration, where it is safe to share, and the states or countries where you are licensed. List your clinical or admin experience with numbers where you can, such as the patients you handled or the records you processed. Mention your home office setup: a private room, a reliable connection, and a headset. Read our guide on how to write a CV for remote jobs for the full CV structure.

Interviews for remote healthcare roles test three things: your clinical or admin skill, your privacy knowledge, and your remote setup. Expect questions such as how you would handle a patient call when the connection drops, how you protect patient data at home, and how you stay in touch with a team across time zones. Prepare a short story for each one, and be ready to show your workspace on camera. Ask the interviewer about the licence requirements, the on-call rota, and the training for the systems you will use.

Example only: a 26 week route into a no-degree role

This example shows how the time adds up. It is an example only, not a promise of a job.

A beginner with no healthcare background could aim for a medical records role in 26 weeks at 10 hours a week. That is 26 times 10, which is 260 hours in total. Split it like this: 120 hours on a medical records certificate course, 60 hours learning medical terms and typing practice, 40 hours on a CV, a LinkedIn profile, and applications, and 40 hours on interview practice and privacy basics. After 26 weeks, the same person would have a certificate, a working knowledge of medical terms, and about 20 applications sent. That is a realistic pace for someone with a day job. Clinical roles take far longer, because a nursing degree alone takes years.

Expect a slow start. Non-clinical roles can take three to six months from study to offer. Clinical roles take longer, because the degree and licence come first. Part-time remote healthcare work exists, especially in triage, transcription, and customer service, but most telehealth clinical roles are full time. Keep your current income while you prepare.

Common mistakes

  • Do not assume any healthcare job can be done remotely.
  • Do not ignore licence limits.
  • Do not forget privacy rules.
  • Do not pay for a certificate before checking whether employers accept it.
  • Do not apply for clinical roles without a licence.
  • Do not hide your location in applications.
  • Do not give up after a few weeks.

Risks and scams to watch for

Job scams target healthcare job seekers too, especially for work from home roles. A real employer never asks you to pay for a job, a training course, or equipment before you start. A real employer never asks for your bank details, your password, or a copy of your ID in the first message. Watch for adverts that promise a high salary for no qualifications, that use a free email address, or that ask you to buy a starter kit or a certificate from a link they send.

Watch for fake check scams, where a supposed employer sends you a check and asks you to send part of it back. The US Federal Trade Commission guide to job scams explains these tricks and says that honest employers never ask you to pay to get a job. If a listing feels wrong, search the company name with the word scam, and read our guide on how to spot online job scams before you reply.

Frequently asked questions

What healthcare jobs can I do from home?

Telehealth roles such as physician, pharmacist, therapist, and nurse can be done by video or phone. Non-clinical roles such as case manager, clinical research associate, medical writer, and health informatics can also be done from home. Hands on care, such as surgery, ward nursing, and lab work, cannot be done remotely.

Do I need a medical degree to work in healthcare from home?

No, not for every role. Clinical roles need a degree and a licence, but non-clinical roles such as medical records, transcription, admin, and customer service need only a certificate or a short course. A degree helps and pays more, but it is not the only way into remote healthcare work.

Which remote healthcare jobs pay the most?

The highest median pay in the Coursera and BLS figures is for physicians at 239,200 dollars a year, followed by pharmacists at 137,480 dollars and clinical research associates at 114,000 dollars in total pay reported by Glassdoor. Registered nurses earn a median of 93,600 dollars. These are United States medians, and pay varies by country, employer, and hours.

What qualifications do I need for a remote healthcare job?

It depends on the role. Clinical roles need a degree and a licence in the place where your patients live. Non-clinical roles usually need a bachelor's degree or a certificate. No-degree roles need a high school diploma and often a short certificate. Always check the licence first, then the certificate, then apply.

Can I work as a remote healthcare worker from any country?

Not always. Clinical licences are usually valid only in one state or country, which limits where you can treat patients. Non-clinical roles are more flexible, but tax, data, and employment rules still differ by country. Check the location line in each advert and the licence rules for your country before you apply.

Is medical coding a good remote healthcare job?

Yes, medical coding is a popular remote role, but it needs training and a coding certificate, and the work is detailed and rule based. Our guide on how to work as a remote medical coder covers the training, the pay, and how to find roles.

Conclusion

To find remote healthcare jobs, pick a role that fits your qualifications, check the licence rules for where you live, and apply through healthcare boards, telehealth companies, insurers, and hospital career pages. Put your licence and your home office setup on your CV, prepare for privacy and remote work questions in interviews, and compare pay figures from official sources such as the BLS before you negotiate.
