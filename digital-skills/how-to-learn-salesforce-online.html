How to Learn Salesforce Online in 2026

Learning how to learn Salesforce online is one of the most practical ways into a technology career without a degree, because the main learning platform is free and the first certification has a clear study path. The problem is that Salesforce is huge, and beginners often wander between random videos, half-finished trails and paid courses they do not need. This guide gives you a clear order instead: choose one role, learn through Trailhead, practise in a free practice org, build two small projects and prepare for one certification.

This guide is for complete beginners who have never used Salesforce. You will learn what Salesforce is, which role to aim for, how Trailhead works, how to get hands-on practice for free, and how long the first 90 days should take.

What Salesforce is and what a CRM does

Salesforce is a cloud-based platform that businesses use to manage their relationships with customers. CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management, and it means the practices and technology a business uses to track customers, sales and service. When a shop remembers what you bought, when a support team can see your last five messages, or when a sales team knows which deals are close to closing, a CRM is usually doing that work behind the scenes.

Salesforce is the most widely used CRM platform, and it is made of many products. Sales Cloud helps sales teams track deals. Service Cloud helps support teams handle customer cases. Marketing Cloud helps marketers send campaigns. There are many more, and nobody learns all of them. Beginners start with the core platform: records, fields, reports and automation. A record is one item in the system, such as one customer or one sale. A field is one piece of information on a record, such as a phone number or a deal value.

You do not need to be a programmer to learn Salesforce. Most admin work is point-and-click: you configure the system through menus and forms rather than writing code. Developers do write code, but that is a separate path you can choose later. If you are comparing this path with other technical skills, our guide to the best digital skills to learn puts Salesforce next to the alternatives.

Step 1: Choose one role to aim for

Salesforce has several distinct roles, and each one needs different skills. Choose one before you start learning, because it decides which trails you follow.

Role Main work Needs coding?
Salesforce Administrator Configure the system, manage users, build reports No
Salesforce Developer Build custom features and integrations with code Yes, Apex and web components
Business Analyst Gather requirements and turn them into solutions No
Consultant Advise companies on how to set up Salesforce No, but needs broad knowledge
Marketer Run campaigns in Marketing Cloud No

The Administrator role is the best first target for most beginners. It is the most common entry job, it needs no coding, and it teaches you the whole platform. A business analyst role is also a good target if you enjoy talking to people and understanding business problems. The developer path suits you only if you already like coding or are willing to learn it properly. Our guide on how to learn coding for beginners will help you judge whether that path fits you.

Write your chosen role down in one sentence, and check every course and trail against it. If a resource does not serve your chosen role, skip it for now.

Step 2: Create a Trailhead account and start the Admin Beginner trail

Trailhead is Salesforce's free online learning platform, and it is the single best place to start. You can create an account at Trailhead without being a Salesforce customer, and the core learning content is free. The platform also offers preparation for over 60 official Salesforce certifications, but the learning itself costs nothing.

Trailhead organises learning into small pieces. A module is a short lesson on one subject, made of smaller units that end in a quiz or a hands-on challenge. A trail is a guided path of related modules in a recommended order. A project is a practical task with step-by-step instructions. A superbadge is an advanced practical assessment with fewer instructions, where you solve a business scenario yourself. A trailmix is a custom path you build from modules, projects and trails. When you finish a module or project, you earn a badge and points, which appear on your Trailblazer profile. A badge shows you finished a learning activity. It is not a certification, and it should never be presented as one.

Start with the Admin Beginner trail. It covers data modelling, data management, customising the Lightning Experience, which is the modern Salesforce interface, user engagement, and reports and dashboards. The trail is listed as about 7 hours and 20 minutes long, though your own time will vary with your pace and experience. Work through it in order, and do every hands-on challenge instead of skipping to the quiz. If you chose the developer path instead, the Developer Beginner trail is listed as about 14 hours and 10 minutes and covers data modelling, Flow Builder, Apex and Lightning Web Components.

Step 3: Get a free practice org and do the hands-on challenges

Reading about Salesforce is not the same as using it. You need a practice environment, and Salesforce gives you one for free. A Developer Edition org is a free, full-featured Salesforce environment for learning, and a Trailhead Playground is a dedicated practice environment connected to your Trailhead account for completing hands-on challenges. Sign up for one from your Trailhead account and use it for every challenge.

In your practice org, do the work yourself instead of watching someone else do it. Create a custom object, which is a new type of record such as a simple booking or a support ticket. Add custom fields to it. Create a few records. Change a page layout. Set up a user with limited permissions and see what they can and cannot do.

Step 4: Learn the core admin skills in order

After the Admin Beginner trail, learn the core admin skills in this order. Each one builds on the last.

First, data modelling: how records, objects and fields fit together, and how records relate to each other. Second, data management: how to import, export and clean data, because bad data is the most common real-world problem. Third, user access and permissions: who can see and change what, and why that matters for security. Fourth, reports and dashboards: how to turn records into simple reports that a manager can read. Fifth, automation with Flow Builder: how to make the system do routine work, such as sending an email when a deal closes or updating a field when a case changes status.

Spend the most time on data modelling and permissions, because they cause the most mistakes in real work.

Step 5: Build two small practice projects

Projects are the proof that you can do the work. Build two small ones that solve a real problem for a made-up business, and finish each one before starting the next.

Project one: build a simple sales tracker for a small shop. Create a custom object for orders, add fields for customer, product, quantity and price, and build a report that shows total sales by product. Add a dashboard with one chart. This project proves you can model data and report on it.

Project two: build a simple support case tracker for the same shop. Create a custom object for cases, add fields for priority and status, and build a flow that sends an email when a high-priority case is created. This project proves you can handle service work and basic automation.

Write a short page for each project: what the business needed, what you built, the steps you followed and what went wrong. Two finished project pages beat ten half-finished trails. If you want to see how Salesforce skills connect with data work, read our guide on how to learn data analysis online.

Step 6: Prepare for the Administrator certification

A Salesforce certification is a formal credential you earn by passing an official exam. It is different from a Trailhead badge, which only shows you finished a learning activity. The first certification most people aim for is the Salesforce Certified Platform Administrator credential, and you can read its details on the Salesforce Certified Platform Administrator page.

Prepare with the official exam guide, the Admin certification trailmix on Trailhead, and practice questions. Study the topics the exam actually tests: configuration, data management, security, automation, reports and dashboards. Give yourself four to six weeks of focused study after you finish your projects, and book the exam only when practice tests go well. Check the current exam fee on the official page before you pay, because fees change and you should never pay through a third party seller. Salesforce releases new features three times a year, in Spring, Summer and Winter, so check the release notes for your exam version as well.

Your 90-day plan

Weeks Focus Output by the end
1 to 2 CRM basics and the Admin Beginner trail, first half Trailhead account, first modules done
3 to 4 Admin Beginner trail, second half, plus practice org Trail finished, practice org set up
5 to 6 Core admin skills: data, permissions, reports First report and dashboard built
7 to 8 Project one: sales tracker Project page written
9 to 10 Project two: case tracker with automation Project page written
11 to 12 Administrator exam preparation Practice tests going well

Two hours a day, five days a week, is enough to follow this plan. If you can only manage one hour a day, stretch it to six months. Consistency matters more than speed.

Worked example: a realistic first 90 days

Example only: these numbers are not typical results and they do not predict what you will earn or how fast you will find work.

Sara is a beginner with a customer service background and no coding experience. She studies two hours a day, five days a week, for 12 weeks. That is 120 hours in total. She spends 20 hours on CRM basics and the first half of the Admin Beginner trail, 25 hours finishing the trail and doing the hands-on challenges in her Trailhead Playground, 25 hours on core admin skills, 30 hours building her two practice projects, and 20 hours starting her Administrator exam preparation.

Her costs are zero for the first 90 days, because Trailhead and the practice org are free. She does not earn anything in this period, and she does not apply for Salesforce jobs yet. Her goal for the 90 days is a finished trail, two project pages and a clear exam plan. That is a realistic first milestone, and it is the foundation for the next three months of exam preparation and job applications.

Expect the basics to take a few weeks and the full admin foundation to take 3 to 6 months. Becoming ready for an entry role usually takes 6 to 12 months, including projects and exam preparation. A badge does not guarantee a job, and neither does a certification on its own. Employers also look at your projects, your practical skills and how you perform in interviews. Results vary with your background, your location, the local job market and how much time you can give each week. Common mistakes

  • Collecting badges without doing the hands-on challenges, so you can pass quizzes but cannot configure a real org.
  • Trying to learn admin, development and consulting at the same time, and finishing none of them.
  • Calling a Trailhead badge a certification on a CV or a profile, which employers notice and do not trust.
  • Skipping data modelling and permissions, then building things that break in real use.
  • Booking the Administrator exam too early, before practice tests go well.
  • Learning only from videos and never building anything in a practice org.
  • Comparing yourself with people who already work in Salesforce and deciding you are too far behind.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be careful with courses and bootcamps that promise a guaranteed Salesforce job or a fixed salary. No course can guarantee employment, and any seller that claims otherwise is not honest. Avoid sellers that pressure you to pay today for a discount that expires tonight. Check reviews from independent sources rather than the testimonials on the seller's own page.

Never pay for a "job placement fee" or a "starter kit" to start learning Salesforce. Legitimate learning platforms do not charge you to apply for a job. If a job advert asks for bank details, a payment or a copy of your ID before an interview, treat it as a scam. Our guide on how to find legitimate online jobs shows safer ways to search.

Also be careful with fake certification sellers. Only book Salesforce exams through the official Salesforce certification pages or authorised test centres. A cheap exam voucher from an unknown seller is usually a scam, and a fake certificate can end your career before it starts.

Frequently asked questions

Is Salesforce Trailhead free?

Yes. The core Trailhead learning platform is free to use, with no card required for the standard learning content. Certification exams and some instructor-led training cost extra, so check the official page before you pay.

Do I need coding to learn Salesforce?

No, not for the Administrator path. Most admin work is point-and-click, using menus and forms. Coding becomes relevant only if you move into Salesforce development, where you would learn Apex and Lightning Web Components.

How long does it take to learn Salesforce?

You can understand the basics within a few weeks using Trailhead and hands-on practice. Building practical admin skills takes 3 to 6 months, and preparing for the Administrator certification usually takes a few more months of focused study.

Which Salesforce certification should I get first?

The Salesforce Certified Platform Administrator credential is the most popular and recommended starting point. It covers the core concepts of Salesforce administration and gives you a solid foundation for other certifications.

Can I get a Salesforce job after completing Trailhead?

Trailhead can help you develop the skills, but it does not guarantee a job. Combine your Trailhead achievements with practical projects, a certification and interview preparation, and your application becomes much stronger.

What is the difference between a Trailhead badge and a Salesforce certification?

A badge recognises that you completed a learning activity, such as a module or a project. A certification is a formal professional credential earned by passing an official exam. They are different things, and employers treat them differently.

Conclusion

Learning Salesforce online comes down to a simple order: choose one role, learn through Trailhead, practise in a free org, build two small projects and prepare for one certification. Start with the Admin Beginner trail, keep your practice org tidy, and follow the 90-day plan above.
