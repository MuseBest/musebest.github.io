How to Build an Online Portfolio

A portfolio is not a gallery of everything you have done. It is a short argument: here is the problem, here is what I did, here is the result.

  • Keep it current

What a portfolio is for

A client or hiring manager spends seconds deciding whether to keep looking. Your portfolio has one job in those seconds: show the kind of work they need, done well. Everything else is decoration.

Choose three to six projects

Fewer, stronger projects beat a long list. For each project, include:

  • The brief: what the client or problem was, in one or two sentences.
  • Your role: what you actually did, not what the team did.
  • The result: what changed — more signups, a faster process, a clearer message. If you do not have numbers, describe the outcome honestly instead of inventing them.
  • The work itself: images, links, or documents a visitor can inspect.

If you have no client work yet, make self-initiated projects: redesign a local business's flyer, write a sample article series, build a small website for a fictional shop. Label them clearly as personal projects.

Structure that works

A simple structure is enough:

  1. A short introduction — who you are and what you do.
  2. Your best three projects, with a short case study each.
  3. A list of services and tools.
  4. Testimonials, only if they are real and attributed.
  5. A clear way to contact you.

Design and technical basics

  • Make it load fast and work on a phone; most visitors will be on mobile.
  • Use real text, not images of text, so search engines and screen readers can read it.
  • Keep navigation to one page or a handful of pages. A portfolio that is hard to navigate is a portfolio that is not read.
  • Add your name, location (if you are comfortable), and email on every page.

Keep it current

Review your portfolio every three months. Remove work that no longer represents you, add your best recent project, and update your services. An out-of-date portfolio quietly tells visitors you have stopped improving.

Common mistakes

  • Showing everything, including weak early work.
  • No context — images with no explanation of the brief or your role.
  • Broken links and slow-loading images.
  • No contact details, or a contact form that nobody answers.

A good portfolio does not need to be fancy. It needs to be honest, focused, and easy to use.
