22:Ta3e,<h2 id="what-a-portfolio-is-for">What a portfolio is for</h2>
<p>A client or hiring manager spends seconds deciding whether to keep looking. Your portfolio has one job in those seconds: show the kind of work they need, done well. Everything else is decoration.</p>
<h2 id="choose-three-to-six-projects">Choose three to six projects</h2>
<p>Fewer, stronger projects beat a long list. For each project, include:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The brief:</strong> what the client or problem was, in one or two sentences.</li>
<li><strong>Your role:</strong> what you actually did, not what the team did.</li>
<li><strong>The result:</strong> what changed — more signups, a faster process, a clearer message. If you do not have numbers, describe the outcome honestly instead of inventing them.</li>
<li><strong>The work itself:</strong> images, links, or documents a visitor can inspect.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you have no client work yet, make self-initiated projects: redesign a local business's flyer, write a sample article series, build a small website for a fictional shop. Label them clearly as personal projects.</p>
<h2 id="structure-that-works">Structure that works</h2>
<p>A simple structure is enough:</p>
<ol>
<li>A short introduction — who you are and what you do.</li>
<li>Your best three projects, with a short case study each.</li>
<li>A list of services and tools.</li>
<li>Testimonials, only if they are real and attributed.</li>
<li>A clear way to contact you.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="design-and-technical-basics">Design and technical basics</h2>
<ul>
<li>Make it load fast and work on a phone; most visitors will be on mobile.</li>
<li>Use real text, not images of text, so search engines and screen readers can read it.</li>
<li>Keep navigation to one page or a handful of pages. A portfolio that is hard to navigate is a portfolio that is not read.</li>
<li>Add your name, location (if you are comfortable), and email on every page.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="keep-it-current">Keep it current</h2>
<p>Review your portfolio every three months. Remove work that no longer represents you, add your best recent project, and update your services. An out-of-date portfolio quietly tells visitors you have stopped improving.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Showing everything, including weak early work.</li>
<li>No context — images with no explanation of the brief or your role.</li>
<li>Broken links and slow-loading images.</li>
<li>No contact details, or a contact form that nobody answers.</li>
</ul>
<p>A good portfolio does not need to be fancy. It needs to be honest, focused, and easy to use.</p>
