<h2 id="what-motion-graphics-is">What motion graphics is</h2>
<p>Motion graphics is animation applied to graphic design. It moves text, shapes, logos, and illustrations, usually to explain something or to brand a video. A title that slides in, a logo that draws itself, and a chart that grows bar by bar are all motion graphics.</p>
<p>It is different from video editing. Video editing cuts and joins filmed clips. Motion graphics creates movement from scratch, frame by frame, with keyframes and shapes. The two skills overlap, and many editors learn both, but they are not the same job.</p>
<p>It is also different from character animation, which moves people and animals with personality and story. Motion graphics moves design elements with timing and rhythm. If you like design and timing more than drawing characters, motion graphics is the better fit.</p>
<p>You will see motion graphics everywhere: YouTube intros, adverts, explainer videos, social media posts, TV titles, and app promos.</p>
<h2 id="the-core-principles-in-plain-words">The core principles in plain words</h2>
<p>Good motion graphics follows a few simple principles. Learn them before you open any software, because they decide whether your work looks smooth or clumsy.</p>
<p>Keyframes are the heart of animation. A keyframe is a marker that says what a thing looks like at one moment in time. You set one keyframe at the start, with a shape on the left, and one at the end, with the shape on the right. The software fills in the movement between them, which is called interpolation, and it is automatic.</p>
<p>Easing controls how the movement feels. Without easing, an object moves at a flat speed and stops dead, which looks robotic. Easy ease makes it start slowly, speed up, and slow down at the end, which looks natural. Learn the graph editor, which lets you shape easing curves, early in your study.</p>
<p>Timing is how long a movement takes. A fast move feels energetic, and a slow move feels calm. Most title animations last between half a second and two seconds. If a move takes too long, the viewer gets bored. If it takes too short, the viewer misses it.</p>
<p>Anticipation is a small move in the opposite direction before the main move. A character crouches before it jumps, and a shape pulls back slightly before it slides forward. That tiny pause makes the main move feel stronger. Squash and stretch is a related idea: a soft object squashes when it lands and stretches when it moves fast, which makes it feel alive.</p>
<p>Learn these five ideas by watching good work frame by frame. Pause a title animation, step through it slowly, and ask what moved, how fast, and what eased.</p>

The trial converts to a paid plan unless you cancel. Check the Adobe pricing page before you buy, because prices change.</p>

Blender is also free, and it can animate text and shapes in 3D. CapCut is free and simple, and it suits short social media animations.</p>

<h2 id="step-2-learn-your-tool-basics">Step 2: Learn your tool basics</h2>
<p>Open your chosen tool and learn the interface before you try to animate. Learn where the timeline is, where the layers live, and where the preview controls sit. A layer is one element in your animation, such as a line of text or a shape.</p>
<p>Learn to create a composition, which is the project file that holds your animation. Set the size to match where the video will play: 1920 by 1080 pixels for YouTube, and 1080 by 1920 pixels for vertical social video. Set the frame rate to 30 frames per second to start. A frame is one still image in the video.</p>
<p>Learn to import files, to trim layers on the timeline, and to preview your work with the space bar. Learn to save often, because motion graphics software can crash.</p>
<p>Spend your first week on basics only. Make ten tiny animations: a dot that moves across the screen, a square that grows, a word that fades in. Each one should take less than 30 minutes. These small exercises are the best way to learn motion graphics online.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-animate-text-and-shapes">Step 3: Animate text and shapes</h2>
<p>Text and shapes are the core of motion graphics. Learn them before you touch effects or 3D.</p>
<p>Start with a lower third, which is the name bar that appears at the bottom of a video. Build a coloured bar, add a name in white text, and animate the bar sliding in from the left while the text fades up.</p>
<p>Next, try kinetic type, which is text that moves in time with words or music. Animate each word to pop in as it is spoken. Keep the movement simple: fade, slide, or scale.</p>
<p>Then learn shape layers. A shape layer is a vector shape, such as a circle or a rectangle, that you can animate without importing a file. Learn to animate the path of a shape, so a line draws itself across the screen, and learn trim paths, which let you reveal a stroke bit by bit.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/learn-motion-graphics-online-keyframes.jpg" alt="A motion graphics timeline in After Effects showing keyframes on a text layer and a shape layer" /></p>
<h2 id="step-4-learn-compositing-and-tracking">Step 4: Learn compositing and tracking</h2>
<p>Compositing is the job of combining several layers into one finished shot. A title over a video clip, a logo over a photo, and a graphic over a green screen are all compositing. Learn to blend layers, to adjust opacity, which is how see through a layer is, and to use blending modes.</p>
<p>Motion tracking is the job of making a graphic follow something that moves in a video. A label that follows a walking person, or a title that sticks to a moving car, uses tracking. Learn the basic point tracker first, which follows one bright spot in the video and applies that movement to your graphic.</p>
<p>Green screen work, also called keying, removes a solid colour background so you can place a person over a new background. Learn Keylight, which is the main keying tool in After Effects, and learn to clean the edges with a light choke and a spill suppressor. Aim for edges that look clean on a phone screen.</p>

<h2 id="step-6-build-a-demo-reel">Step 6: Build a demo reel</h2>
<p>A demo reel is a short video that shows your best motion graphics work. It is the main thing clients and employers ask for. Keep it between 30 and 60 seconds long, and put your best clip first, because many viewers stop watching after ten seconds.</p>
<p>Include 4 to 6 short clips, each 5 to 10 seconds long. Show variety: a lower third, a logo animation, a kinetic type clip, and an explainer extract. Add your name and contact details at the end, and add music that fits the pace. Do not include unfinished work.</p>
<p>Host the reel on Vimeo or YouTube, and link it from your portfolio page. Update it every few months, and remove old clips when your new work is clearly better.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Do not animate everything at once, because too much movement hides the message.</li>
<li>Do not use easing on every keyframe by default, because some moves should be sharp and fast.</li>
<li>Do not copy a tutorial exactly and call it your work, because you learn nothing and the reel looks generic.</li>
<li>Do not make text too small or too fast, because viewers on phones will miss it.</li>
<li>Do not skip the storyboard, because animating without a plan wastes hours.</li>
<li>Do not buy an expensive certificate before you try the free route, because you may not enjoy the work.</li>
<li>Do not use music you do not have the right to use, because it can get your reel removed.</li>
<li>Do not make your demo reel longer than 60 seconds, because most viewers stop watching.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Paid motion graphics courses attract the same scams as other online courses. Check before you pay. A real course names its teacher, shows a full syllabus, and lets you see sample lessons. A fake course hides the teacher and pressures you to pay today.</p>
<p>Watch for these warning signs. The course promises a guaranteed job or a set income. The price is only shown after a sales call. The certificate is called accredited but no accrediting body is named. The seller asks for payment by bank transfer or gift card.</p>
<p>AI tools are now part of motion graphics, and they bring their own risks. AI can draft a background or a texture, but it can also produce images that copy another artist work. Check the licence for any stock, music, or AI asset before you use it in paid work.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="what-software-do-i-need-for-motion-graphics">What software do I need for motion graphics?</h3>
<p>After Effects is the standard tool, and most tutorials use it. If you cannot afford a subscription, start with DaVinci Resolve Fusion, which is free and can do real motion graphics work. Blender is also free and suits 3D motion graphics.</p>
<h3 id="is-after-effects-hard-to-learn">Is After Effects hard to learn?</h3>
<p>It is medium hard. The interface has many panels, and the first week feels confusing. After you learn the timeline, layers, and keyframes, the rest builds slowly. Most beginners can make a simple title animation in their first week.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-motion-graphics">How long does it take to learn motion graphics?</h3>
<p>Basic title animations take 2 to 4 weeks. A solid demo reel takes 2 to 3 months of steady practice at about 7 hours a week. Compositing and tracking take longer. These are guide estimates, not promises.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-learn-motion-graphics-for-free">Can I learn motion graphics for free?</h3>
<p>Yes. Free YouTube channels, free tools such as DaVinci Resolve and Blender, and free assets are enough to learn the whole skill.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-motion-graphics-and-video-editing">What is the difference between motion graphics and video editing?</h3>
<p>Video editing cuts and joins filmed clips. Motion graphics creates movement from scratch with keyframes, shapes, and text. The two skills overlap, but they are different jobs with different tools.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-to-know-how-to-draw-to-learn-motion-graphics">Do I need to know how to draw to learn motion graphics?</h3>
<p>No. Motion graphics uses shapes, text, and simple illustrations, not hand drawing. A sense of timing and a good eye for design matter more than drawing skill.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>To learn motion graphics online, start with the core principles, pick one tool, animate text and shapes first, then learn compositing and tracking, and finish with a short demo reel. The free route is genuinely strong, After Effects is the standard tool if you can afford it, and an expensive certificate programme is optional. Practise every week, finish each project in the ladder, and keep your reel short and strong.</p>
