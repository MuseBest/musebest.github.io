<h2 id="what-bookkeeping-is-and-what-it-is-not">What bookkeeping is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>Bookkeeping is the job of recording money in and money out, in an organised way, so a business always knows where it stands. A bookkeeper records sales, pays bills, matches bank statements, and prepares simple reports on profit, cash, and debts.</p>
<p>Bookkeeping is not the same as accounting. An accountant uses the bookkeeper records to give advice, plan tax, and sign off accounts. The table below shows the difference in plain words.</p>

<p>Start with honest basics. You need to add, subtract, multiply, and divide without fear. You do not need advanced maths, because spreadsheets do the heavy sums, but you must spot when a number looks wrong. You also need patience with detail, because a single wrong digit can throw a whole month out. Basic computer skills are enough.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-learn-the-core-concepts">Step 1: Learn the core concepts</h2>
<p>Every bookkeeping course starts with the same core ideas. Learn them in this order.</p>
<p>First, learn double entry bookkeeping. Double entry is the rule that every transaction has two sides. When a business sells a product for 100 dollars, the sales figure and the bank balance both go up by 100 dollars. When it pays 100 dollars in rent, the bank balance falls by 100 dollars and the rent cost rises by 100 dollars. This two sided record keeps the books balanced.</p>
<p>Second, learn the chart of accounts, which is simply the list of categories a business uses, such as sales, rent, stock, wages, and bank fees. Every transaction goes into one category.</p>
<p>Third, learn debits and credits. A debit is an entry on the left side of an account, and a credit is an entry on the right side. In plain words, a debit usually increases an asset or an expense, and a credit usually increases income or a liability. Learn it by recording ten real transactions and watching the two sides balance.</p>
<p>Fourth, learn the three main reports: profit and loss, which shows income minus costs; the balance sheet, which shows what the business owns and owes on one date; and cash flow, which shows money in and out over a period.</p>
<p>Fifth, learn bank reconciliation, which is the job of matching the bank statement to the books, line by line, until the two agree. It is the most common weekly task in bookkeeping, and it is where most errors are found.</p>
<p>Free courses cover all five ideas well. The <a href="https://www.khanacademy.org/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Khan Academy accounting and finance lessons</a> explain the basics in short videos with no cost. AccountingCoach also offers free lessons on the same ideas, with a paid option for practice questions.</p>

<h2 id="example-only-a-week-of-bookkeeping-with-the-maths-shown">Example only: a week of bookkeeping, with the maths shown</h2>
<p>This example uses made up numbers. It is an example only.</p>
<p>A small online shop starts Monday with 12,400 dollars in the bank. During the week it records 3,200 dollars in sales. It pays 900 dollars in rent, 1,100 dollars for stock, 180 dollars for delivery, and 45 dollars for software.</p>
<p>First, add the expenses: 900 plus 1,100 is 2,000, plus 180 is 2,180, plus 45 is 2,225 dollars.</p>
<p>Next, work out the closing bank balance. Start with 12,400 dollars. Add sales of 3,200 dollars, which gives 15,600 dollars. Subtract expenses of 2,225 dollars, which gives 13,375 dollars.</p>
<p>The closing bank balance should be 13,375 dollars. The bookkeeper then checks the bank statement. If it also shows 13,375 dollars, the books agree. If it shows a different number, the bookkeeper looks for the missing or wrong entry.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Do not skip the core concepts and jump straight to software, because the software will hide your mistakes.</li>
<li>Do not learn three software packages at once, because you will mix up their menus and shortcuts.</li>
<li>Do not pay for an expensive course before you try a free one, because you may not enjoy the work.</li>
<li>Do not ignore bank reconciliation, because it is the check that finds your errors.</li>
<li>Do not record transactions from memory at the end of the month, because you will forget half of them.</li>
<li>Do not mix personal spending with business spending in your practice books, because it makes the reports meaningless.</li>
<li>Do not trust a certificate to get you work on its own, because practice and a tidy portfolio matter more.</li>
<li>Do not study tax rules for the wrong country, because tax basics differ by country.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Paid bookkeeping courses attract scams, so check before you pay. A real course names its teacher, shows a full syllabus, and lets you see sample lessons. A fake course hides the teacher and pressures you to pay today.</p>
<p>Watch for these warning signs. The course promises a guaranteed job or a set income. The price is only shown after a sales call. The certificate is called accredited but no accrediting body is named. The reviews all use the same words and appear on the same day. The seller asks for payment by bank transfer or gift card.</p>
<p>Check a course before you pay. Search the course name with the word review, read the refund policy, and check whether the provider is a known platform such as Coursera, edX, or LinkedIn Learning. Never pay for a course that asks for your bank login details.</p>
<p>Tax rules differ by country. This guide does not give tax advice, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-i-learn-bookkeeping-online-with-no-experience">Can I learn bookkeeping online with no experience?</h3>
<p>Yes. Bookkeeping starts with simple ideas and builds slowly. You need basic maths and care with detail, not a degree. Start with a free course, practise in one software package, and follow the 6 week plan in this guide.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-bookkeeping">How long does it take to learn bookkeeping?</h3>
<p>The basics take 4 to 8 weeks of part time study, at about an hour a day. A certificate such as the Intuit Academy programme takes about 4 months part time.</p>
<h3 id="is-bookkeeping-certification-worth-it">Is bookkeeping certification worth it?</h3>
<p>It can help, but it is not required. A certificate gives your study a clear target and your CV a named credential. The NACPB CPB licence carries more weight in the US, but it needs exams, training, and supervised experience. Start with the free route, then decide if a certificate is worth the cost.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-a-degree-to-become-a-bookkeeper">Do I need a degree to become a bookkeeper?</h3>
<p>No. Most bookkeeping work needs a certificate or proven practice, not a degree. Employers and clients care more about accurate books and a tidy portfolio than about a university qualification.</p>
<h3 id="which-software-should-i-learn-first">Which software should I learn first?</h3>
<p>Learn QuickBooks Online first if you want the widest choice of work, because most small business adverts name it. Learn Xero first if you are in the UK, Australia, or New Zealand, where it is common. Use Wave or a spreadsheet for free practice.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-bookkeeping-and-accounting">What is the difference between bookkeeping and accounting?</h3>
<p>Bookkeeping records daily money in and money out. Accounting uses those records to give advice, plan tax, and prepare formal accounts. A bookkeeper keeps the records tidy and correct, and an accountant interprets them.</p>

<p>To learn bookkeeping online, start with the core concepts, pick one learning route, practise in one software package, and follow a short study plan. Free courses cover the basics well, a certificate adds structure, and the NACPB CPB licence is the deeper US path. Practise every week, reconcile a full practice month, and keep a small portfolio of tidy books.</p>
