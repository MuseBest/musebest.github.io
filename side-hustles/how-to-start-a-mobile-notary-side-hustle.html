How to Start a Mobile Notary Side Hustle in 2026

A mobile notary side hustle lets you earn extra money by travelling to clients to witness signatures and stamp documents, at times that fit around your main job. You must first become a commissioned notary public in your state, because only commissioned notaries may perform notarial acts. This guide explains the general steps, the costs, and the state rules you must check, so you can decide if this side hustle fits your area and your schedule.

What a mobile notary does

A notary public is a state commissioned official who witnesses signatures, checks identity, and stamps documents to help prevent fraud. A mobile notary does the same work but travels to the signer, for example to a home, hospital, office, or care facility. Clients pay for the convenience of a visit outside office hours or at short notice.

Common mobile notary work includes witnessing signatures on affidavits, powers of attorney, wills where state law allows, property documents, and business forms. The exact acts you may perform, and the fees you may charge, depend on your state law. This guide gives general steps only. Your state notary authority, usually the Secretary of State office, is the final source for your rules.

A mobile notary is not the same as a notary signing agent. A signing agent handles loan document packages for title and mortgage companies and needs extra training and certification. You can do general mobile notary work without loan signing work, and many part time notaries start that way.

Step 1: Check your state rules first

Before you spend any money, read the notary pages of your state government website. Confirm the age rule, residency rule, application steps, training or exam needs, background check needs, bond needs, and the maximum fees you may charge. These rules vary a lot by state, and one state process is not a guide for another state.

Write down four facts for your state. First, the total cost to get commissioned. Second, the maximum fee per notarial act. Third, whether you may charge a separate travel fee, and any limit on it. Fourth, how long the commission lasts and how renewal works. These four facts decide whether the side hustle can pay in your area.

The National Notary Association gives a clear general overview of the path, and you can read its guide on four steps to become a mobile notary. Use it for the general picture, then follow your state site for the exact steps.

Step 2: Apply for your notary commission

Most states follow a similar order, even though details differ. You complete an application, pay a filing fee, complete training or an exam if your state requires it, pass a background check if required, buy a surety bond if required, and file your paperwork within a set time limit. You then receive your commission certificate.

Do not advertise notary services until your commission is active and your seal is ready. Performing notarial acts without a valid commission can lead to fines and legal trouble. Keep copies of every form, receipt, and deadline in one folder, because a missed filing deadline can void your application in some states.

Allow time for processing. Approval can take weeks depending on your state and background check speed. Plan your launch date after approval, not before it.

Step 3: Buy your supplies and plan your fees

Once commissioned, buy the supplies your state requires. Most mobile notaries need an official seal or stamp, a bound journal to record each act, pens, and a secure bag or case for travel. Some states also require a thumbprint pad or specific journal details, so check your state supply list.

Plan your fees within your state limits. Many states cap the fee per notarial act at a low amount, and your profit often comes from the travel fee where state law allows it, and from appointments with several acts at once. Never charge above your state maximum. If your state does not allow a travel fee, your earnings per trip will be much lower, and short local trips matter more.

Step 4: Decide if loan signing work fits you

Loan signing agents witness and guide signers through mortgage and refinance document packages. Title companies and signing services hire agents for appointments that can pay more than single general notarizations, but the work demands accuracy, evening availability, printing, scanning, and strict deadlines.

Signing agent work needs extra training, a background check, and often certification through a recognised body. Some states restrict who may handle loan closings, and some require an attorney for parts of the process. Check your state rules and the requirements of any signing service before you invest in training.

For a side hustle alongside a day job, general mobile notary work in evenings and weekends is often the simpler start. You can add loan signing work later if you have the time, transport, and printing setup it needs. Do not promise loan signing services until you hold the training and checks those clients require.

Step 5: Set up your business basics

Decide how you will take bookings, payments, and records. A simple system works: a dedicated phone number or email, a calendar, a mileage log, and a folder for receipts. Track every appointment with date, client, acts performed, fee charged, travel fee if allowed, and miles driven.

Check local business rules. Some cities require a business licence, and your state may require sales tax registration for certain services. Tax rules for self employed work are strict, and our guide on freelance taxes for beginners explains record keeping in plain words. For United States readers, the IRS Gig Economy Tax Center states that side income must be reported even when it is part time. Rules differ by country and state, so confirm your duties with your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.

Buy errors and omissions insurance if you can afford it. The National Notary Association notes that no state requires this insurance, but it advises self employed notaries to carry it as liability protection. One claim can cost far more than a year of premiums.

Step 6: Find your first clients

Most mobile notary clients find you through search. Create a free Google Business Profile with your service area, hours, phone number, and services. Build a simple one page website or profile page that states your area, your hours, your fees within state limits, and how to book.

Visit local organisations that often need a notary. Law offices, real estate offices, care homes, hospitals, banks, and car dealers all meet clients who need documents witnessed. Leave a card, explain your hours and service area, and follow up politely. Repeat referrals from a few trusted partners often beat paid ads for a part time notary.

Worked example

Example only: these numbers are not typical results.

Imagine your state allows a fee of 10 US dollars per notarial act and a travel fee of 40 US dollars per visit, and you complete 6 visits in a month. Each visit has 2 acts.

  • Fee per visit for acts: 2 times 10, which is 20 US dollars
  • Travel fee per visit: 40 US dollars
  • Total per visit: 20 plus 40, which is 60 US dollars
  • Monthly gross pay: 6 times 60, which is 360 US dollars

Now subtract monthly costs. Say fuel and parking cost 60 US dollars, and you set aside 25 percent of gross pay for tax as a rough planning figure.

  • Monthly gross pay: 360 US dollars
  • Monthly travel costs: 60 US dollars
  • Amount left before tax: 360 minus 60, which is 300 US dollars
  • Tax set aside at 25 percent of 360: 90 US dollars
  • Amount left after costs and tax set aside: 300 minus 90, which is 210 US dollars

Your state may allow higher or lower fees, or no travel fee at all, which changes this example a lot. Recalculate with your own state maximum fees, your own travel costs, and your own tax rules before you rely on any figure.

What it costs and what to expect

Startup costs are modest compared with many businesses, but they are not zero. Expect state fees, possible training and background check costs, a bond premium where required, a seal and journal, insurance, and travel costs. The total varies by state, so use your state official figures, not a national average.

Expect slow and uneven demand at first. General notary work is often one off, so you must keep marketing to keep bookings coming. Evenings and weekends can be busy because clients need help outside office hours, which suits a side hustle but can clash with family time. Set a service area you can reach without losing money on fuel and time.

Common mistakes

  • New notaries often charge fees without checking the state maximum. Always confirm your state fee limits and travel fee rules before you quote a price.
  • Some notaries accept every call, then drive long distances for one low fee act. Set a service area and a minimum visit charge within your state rules.
  • Careless journal entries create legal risk. Record every act fully and neatly, exactly as your state requires.
  • New notaries sometimes give legal advice to signers. You witness signatures and check identity. You do not explain legal documents or advise clients, unless you are separately qualified and allowed to do so.
  • Many part timers forget renewal dates. Diarise your commission expiry and bond renewal well in advance so you never work with a lapsed commission.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch for training sellers who promise high earnings for a large upfront fee. Training can help, but no course can guarantee clients or income. Compare the official state training list, where one exists, before you pay a private provider.

Be careful with fake clients who ask you to notarise documents without proper identification, or who pressure you to backdate a stamp. Refuse. A single improper act can end your commission and create legal liability.

Protect client documents. Keep papers secure during travel, never photograph sensitive documents on a personal phone without a clear need and permission, and store your journal safely. Your stamp must stay under your control at all times.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a lawyer to become a mobile notary?

No, you do not need to be a lawyer in most states to become a notary public. You must meet your state age, residency, and background rules and complete the state application process. Check your state notary authority for the exact requirements.

How much does it cost to start a mobile notary side hustle?

Costs vary by state and include application fees, possible training or exam fees, a bond premium where required, a seal, a journal, and insurance. Get your state official figures first, then add supplies and travel costs. Avoid any provider that will not show prices clearly.

Can I charge a travel fee as a mobile notary?

It depends on your state. Some states allow a separate travel fee, some limit it, and some do not allow it at all. Check your state fee schedule before you quote any price, and never charge above the state maximum.

What is the difference between a mobile notary and a loan signing agent?

A mobile notary performs general notarial acts at client locations. A loan signing agent also handles mortgage document packages for title and mortgage companies and needs extra training, certification, and background checks. You can start with general work and add loan signing later if state rules and your schedule allow it.

Can I do mobile notary work in the evenings and weekends only?

Yes, many part time notaries work evenings and weekends because clients often need help outside office hours. State your hours clearly in your profile so clients know when you are available. Keep a firm service area so travel time stays manageable.

How long does it take to get commissioned?

It varies by state and can take several weeks from application to approval, especially where training, exams, or background checks apply. Do not advertise services until your commission is active. Check your state processing times on the official site.

Conclusion

Check your state rules first, complete the commission process in order, buy the required supplies, and price only within your state limits. Set up simple booking, payment, and mileage records, carry insurance, and market to local partners who meet signers every week. Start with general evening and weekend work, and add loan signing only if you complete the extra training and your schedule allows it.
