How to Start a House Sitting Side Hustle in 2026

Free rent in a nice home sounds like the perfect side hustle. The catch is that not every house sit pays cash. Many sits are an exchange: you look after the home and the pets, and you stay there for free. Some sits pay a nightly rate on top. This guide shows you how to start, which platforms to use, what the fees are, and how to stay safe.

What house sitting is, and what it is not

House sitting means staying in someone's home while they are away, and looking after the home, and often the pets, the plants, and the garden. The homeowner may be on holiday or travelling for work. In return you get a free place to stay, and sometimes cash pay as well.

House sitting is not free travel with no work. You have real duties, and the homeowner expects them done well. You cannot leave the home empty for days or bring extra guests without asking. It is also not a full time income. Sits are irregular, homeowners can cancel, and the best sits go to sitters with reviews.

Paid sits versus exchange sits

The first choice you must make is which kind of sit you want. The table below compares them.

Point Paid sits Exchange sits
What you get Cash per night or per day Free accommodation, no cash
Where to find them Local listings, agencies, referrals Platforms such as TrustedHousesitters
Duties Same duties, often more pets Same duties, often longer stays
Who it suits Sitters who need cash income Travellers and flexible workers
Downsides Fewer listings, more competition You still pay for food and travel

Most sits on the big platforms are exchange sits. Paid sits are more common through local listings and small agencies. Decide which one you need before you pay for a membership.

What house sitters actually do

The duties change with every home, but the core list is the same. You keep the home secure by locking doors and windows, turning lights on and off, and collecting the mail. You keep it clean, water the plants, and take the bins out. If there are pets, you feed them, walk them, clean up after them, and watch their health. Report problems at once, such as a leak or a sick pet.

Types of house sits

Sits come in several shapes. A live in sit means you stay in the home day and night. A live out sit means you visit daily but sleep at your own home. A pet sit is a live in sit where animal care is the main job. Local sits are near your home and suit beginners, because you can meet the homeowner first and you have no travel cost. Travel sits take you to another city or country, and they suit sitters who work online or who have free time. Start with local sits. They are easier to win, and they build the reviews you need for travel sits.

Step 1: Get experience with no experience

Every sitter starts with no reviews. The way past that is to get experience close to home. Offer to sit for friends, family, and neighbours, even for a weekend, and ask them to write you a short reference. Offer to look after a home while the owners are away, and treat it like a paid sit. Volunteer for a local animal shelter or a pet sitting job first, because pet care experience is what most homeowners ask about. Our guide on how to start a pet sitting side hustle explains how to build that experience, and it transfers directly to house sitting.

Two or three good references from people you know are enough to start applying on a platform.

Step 2: Build a profile that wins trust

Your profile is your shop window. Homeowners choose a stranger to trust with their home and their pets, so your profile must answer their worries before they ask. Use a clear, friendly photo of yourself. Write a short bio in plain words: who you are, why you house sit, what experience you have, and what kind of sits you want. Mention practical skills: pet care, gardening, basic repairs, and a clean driving licence. Add your references, and complete the identity and background checks the platform offers.

Step 3: Choose a platform

Most sitters find work through a platform, and each one charges a yearly membership fee. The table below shows the main platforms and their fees as reported in 2026 and 2024. Read the dates carefully.

Platform Membership fee What to know
TrustedHousesitters 129 to 259 dollars a year Largest platform; Basic and Standard add a 12 dollar fee per confirmed sit
Nomador 99 to 199 dollars a year Popular in Europe; fee reported in 2024, recheck before paying
MindMyHouse 29 dollars a year Low cost; fee reported in 2024, recheck before paying
HouseCarers 50 dollars a year Low cost; fee reported in 2024, recheck before paying

The TrustedHousesitters figures are the most current. According to TrustedHousesitters pricing reported in September 2026, sitter membership costs 129 dollars a year for Basic, 169 dollars for Standard, and 259 dollars for Premium, and Basic and Standard add a 12 dollar booking fee on every confirmed sit, while Premium has no booking fee. A 30 percent discount for new members is often available. The other figures come from a guide updated in February 2024, so recheck them on the official platform pages.

Free alternatives such as local listings on Indeed, LinkedIn, Craigslist, and Facebook groups cost nothing but carry more scam risk. Never pay a homeowner to sit, and never send money to secure a sit.

Step 4: Apply fast and interview well

Good sits attract many applications, so speed matters. Check new listings two or three times a day, and apply within hours of a listing going up. Read the whole listing, and write a short message that answers it directly. Say why you fit this sit, mention the pets by name, and note your dates and your experience. Do not send the same message to every listing, because homeowners can tell.

If the homeowner replies, expect a video call. Ask about the trip dates, the pets, the daily routine, the home rules, and what happens if plans change. Ask to see the home on video, and ask for the vet details and the emergency contacts. If a homeowner will not answer questions or pressures you to confirm at once, walk away.

Step 5: Agree everything in writing

Before you confirm a sit, agree the details in writing, in a message or a short document. This protects you and the homeowner. Agree the exact dates and arrival and departure times, the duties, the pet care routine, the pay if any, who pays for pet food and cleaning supplies, what happens if the homeowner returns early, and what happens if you must cancel. Agree the emergency plan: the vet, the plumber, a trusted neighbour, and how to reach the homeowner. Agree the house rules on guests, smoking, and use of the car. Keep a copy of the agreement and the listing.

How much you can make

Paid sits usually pay by the night. According to Rover data reported in 2024, house sitters earned about 25 to 55 dollars a night, up to 100 dollars a night in big cities such as New York and Los Angeles, and about 50 dollars a night in smaller cities. Those figures are from 2024, so treat them as a guide and check current local listings before you set your rate. Most sits are paid by the day, not by the hour, so a nightly rate is the better way to judge a sit.

Exchange sits pay in saved rent, not in cash. That saving is real money, but it is not income you can spend. Here is a worked example with the maths shown. Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and your city and costs will change them.

Ten night exchange sit Amount
Hotel cost saved, 10 nights at 60 dollars 600 dollars
TrustedHousesitters Standard membership 169 dollars
Booking fee for one confirmed sit 12 dollars
Travel to the sit 80 dollars
Value kept after costs 339 dollars

The maths is 169 dollars plus 12 dollars plus 80 dollars, which equals 261 dollars. Then 600 dollars minus 261 dollars equals 339 dollars. That is 339 dollars of value, not cash. Judge an exchange sit by the value it saves you, and a paid sit by the cash it pays you.

Is house sitting worth it

House sitting is worth it if you are flexible, you like animals and quiet homes, and you can work online or take time off. It is a poor choice if you need steady cash every week, if you cannot handle a cancellation, or if you dislike being alone in a strange home. Before you accept a sit, add up what you receive, in cash or in saved rent, and compare it with what is asked. If the work is heavy and the reward is small, say no. Staying safe in someone else's home

Staying in a stranger's home carries real risks, so take them seriously. Always meet the homeowner on a video call before you confirm, and check the listing for photos, reviews, and clear details. Tell someone you trust where you are going, the address, and the dates, and check in with them when you arrive and when you leave. On arrival, check that the home matches the listing and that you have the emergency numbers. If you feel unsafe, leave and contact the platform. Never share your bank details or passwords with a homeowner, and never pay money to secure a sit. If a listing asks for either, report it to the platform and check the US Federal Trade Commission guide to job scams.

Risks and scams to watch for

The main scam in house sitting is a fake listing that asks you for money. A real homeowner never asks a sitter to pay to secure a sit, to send a deposit, or to share bank details. A real platform never asks you to pay a listing fee for the homeowner. Check every listing for photos, reviews, and clear details, and meet the homeowner on a video call before you confirm. If a listing has no photos, no reviews, and a host who will not talk to you, walk away and report it to the platform.

When things go wrong

Things go wrong on sits, and a good sitter is ready for them. If a pet falls ill, call the vet the homeowner named, and tell the homeowner at once. If a pipe bursts or an appliance breaks, turn off the water or the power if it is safe to do so, take photos, and call the homeowner and the emergency contact. If the alarm goes off, follow the instructions the homeowner gave you, and call the alarm company if you cannot stop it. If the homeowner cannot be reached, use the emergency contact they named. If something breaks, tell the homeowner at once, take photos, and agree how to fix it.

What it costs and what to expect

The table below shows the usual costs in US dollars. Prices vary by country and by platform.

Cost Typical range Notes
Platform membership 29 to 259 dollars a year Depends on the platform and the tier
Booking fees 0 to 12 dollars per sit TrustedHousesitters Basic and Standard only
Travel to sits 0 to 200 dollars per sit Local sits cost little; travel sits cost more
Food and supplies 30 to 100 dollars per week You pay for your own food on most sits
Total for a first year 200 to 600 dollars Before any cash income

Insurance is a gap in house sitting. Your travel insurance may not cover damage to someone else's home, and the homeowner's insurance may not cover a sitter. Some platforms include liability cover in higher tiers, so read the terms before you pay. Paid sitting income is taxable in most countries, even in cash. Exchange sits, where you receive no cash, are usually not taxed as income, but rules differ by country. Keep a record of every sit, payment, and cost. The US standard mileage rate for business driving in 2026 is 72.5 cents per mile for January to June and 76 cents per mile for July to December, according to the IRS standard mileage rates page.

Common mistakes

  • Do not pay for a platform membership before you have references and a clear plan.
  • Do not accept a sit without a video call with the homeowner.
  • Do not agree a sit without the duties, the pay, and the cancellation terms in writing.
  • Do not take a sit with heavy pet care if you have no pet experience.
  • Do not hide damage or a sick pet. Tell the homeowner at once.

Frequently asked questions

What does a house sitter do?

A house sitter stays in someone's home while they are away, and looks after the home, the pets, the plants, and the garden. Duties include locking up, collecting mail, keeping the home clean, feeding and walking pets, watering plants, and reporting problems at once.

Do house sitters get paid?

Some do and some do not. Paid sits usually pay a nightly rate, often about 25 to 55 dollars a night according to Rover data reported in 2024, with higher rates in big cities. Most sits on the big platforms are exchange sits, where you stay for free and receive no cash.

How much can you make house sitting?

It depends on whether you take paid sits or exchange sits, and on your city. Paid sits of 25 to 55 dollars a night, as reported in 2024, can add up over a long sit, but sits are irregular and homeowners can cancel. Exchange sits save you rent, which is real value but not cash.

Do I need experience to house sit?

No, but you do need references. Start with sits for friends, family, and neighbours, even unpaid ones, and ask them to write you a short reference. Pet sitting experience helps a lot, because most sits involve pets. Two or three good references are enough to start applying on a platform.

Is house sitting safe?

It can be, if you take care. Always meet the homeowner on a video call first, check the listing for photos and reviews, and tell someone you trust where you are going and when. Check that the home matches the listing when you arrive, and leave if you feel unsafe. Never pay money to secure a sit, and never share your bank details with a homeowner.

What platforms are best for house sitters?

TrustedHousesitters is the largest platform, with sitter membership at 129 to 259 dollars a year as reported in September 2026, plus a 12 dollar booking fee per confirmed sit on Basic and Standard. Nomador, MindMyHouse, and HouseCarers are smaller and cheaper, but their fees were last reported in 2024.

Conclusion

Get a few local references first, build a trustworthy profile, choose a platform and check its current fees, apply fast, and agree everything in writing before you confirm.
