23:Tb1d,<h2 id="the-golden-rule">The golden rule</h2>
<p>A legitimate way to earn money online never asks you to pay money to start earning, never guarantees income, and never pressures you to decide in minutes. If an opportunity breaks any of those rules, walk away.</p>
<h2 id="scam-patterns-to-recognise">Scam patterns to recognise</h2>
<h3 id="pay-to-start">Pay to start</h3>
<p>"You just need to pay a $50 registration fee / buy the starter pack / pay for training." Real employers pay you; you do not pay them. Training costs exist in some legitimate fields, but they are never a condition of a job offer that arrived out of nowhere.</p>
<h3 id="guaranteed-income">Guaranteed income</h3>
<p>"Make $500 a day from your phone, guaranteed." No honest business can guarantee your income. Guarantees are a marketing claim, not a fact — and in most cases they are simply false.</p>
<h3 id="the-overpayment-cheque-scam">The overpayment / cheque scam</h3>
<p>A "client" sends you a cheque for too much money and asks you to send the difference back. The cheque later bounces, and you have lost the money you sent. Never accept overpayment, and never send money to a client.</p>
<h3 id="fake-tasks-and-crypto-doubling">Fake tasks and crypto doubling</h3>
<p>Tasks that pay you to like posts, or schemes that promise to double your cryptocurrency, are almost always fraud or money laundering. Your account, and in some cases your legal liability, is the price.</p>
<h3 id="stolen-job-listings">Stolen job listings</h3>
<p>Scammers copy real job adverts, change the contact details, and "hire" you — then ask for identity documents or bank details. Always apply through the employer's official careers page, and verify the recruiter's identity independently.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-verify-any-opportunity">How to verify any opportunity</h2>
<ol>
<li>Search the company or scheme name with the word "scam" or "review".</li>
<li>Check the website's age, contact details, and whether the company is registered where it claims to be.</li>
<li>Never send identity documents or bank details to someone who contacted you first.</li>
<li>Ask a trusted person, or a professional community, before paying anything.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="if-you-have-been-scammed">If you have been scammed</h2>
<p>Stop sending money immediately, keep every message and receipt, report it to your bank and to your country's fraud reporting service, and change any passwords you shared. Reporting helps others — scammers rely on silence.</p>
<h2 id="the-boring-truth">The boring truth</h2>
<p>Legitimate earning online is slow, skill-based, and uncertain at first. That is not a flaw in the honest opportunities; it is the reason the scams look so attractive. The safest earning route is the one you can explain to a sceptical friend in one sentence — and that friend would believe.</p>
