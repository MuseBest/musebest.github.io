<h2 id="what-a-tutoring-side-hustle-is-and-who-it-suits">What a tutoring side hustle is and who it suits</h2>
<p>A tutoring side hustle means you teach a small number of students part time for pay. You might teach school maths, English, science, a language, music, or a work skill such as coding or exam preparation. Most side hustle tutors teach one to one, for one hour at a time, either online by video call or in person near home.</p>
<p>This side hustle suits you if you can explain a topic clearly and you have a few free hours each week at set times. It suits you less if you dislike working with children or parents, or if your free time changes every week. Parents and adult students pay for reliability as much as knowledge, so a steady schedule matters.</p>
<p>This guide is about part time tutoring alongside a job. If you want to build a full tutoring company with staff and a website, read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-an-online-tutoring-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online tutoring business</a> instead. If you want employed tutoring work rather than your own students, read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-find-online-tutoring-jobs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to find online tutoring jobs</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-pick-one-subject-and-one-type-of-student">Step 1: Pick one subject and one type of student</h2>
<p>Start with one subject that you know well and can explain in simple words. Pick the level too, for example primary school maths, secondary school science, or adult English conversation. A narrow offer is easier for parents to understand and easier for you to prepare.</p>
<p>Ask yourself three questions. First, can you solve the problems at that level without help. Second, can you explain the same idea in two different ways when a student is stuck. Third, do students near you, or online, actually need this subject. Test preparation, maths, science, languages, and music tend to have steady demand, but demand varies by place and season, so check local listings and parent groups before you commit.</p>
<p>Do not offer every subject you passed at school. Parents trust a tutor who specialises. You can add a second subject later once your first students are settled.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-choose-how-you-will-teach">Step 2: Choose how you will teach</h2>
<p>You have three main options. You can teach online by video call, you can teach in person at your home or the student home, or you can join a tutoring platform that sends you students. Each option has a clear trade.</p>

<h2 id="step-3-set-your-rate-and-your-rules">Step 3: Set your rate and your rules</h2>
<p>Set one clear hourly rate, or a fixed price for a block of lessons. Look at what tutors near you charge for the same subject and level, then place yourself honestly based on your experience. New tutors usually start at the lower end and raise rates as reviews and results build.</p>
<p>Write your rules down before your first lesson. State the lesson length, how payment works, how much notice you need for a cancellation, and what happens if a student is late. Clear rules protect your evenings and prevent awkward money talks later.</p>
<h3 id="worked-example">Worked example</h3>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results.</p>
<p>Imagine you teach secondary school maths online for 6 hours a week, in the evenings after work. You charge 25 US dollars per hour, and you teach for 4 weeks in a month.</p>
<ul>
<li>Lessons per week: 6 hours</li>
<li>Rate: 25 US dollars per hour</li>
<li>Weekly gross pay: 6 times 25, which is 150 US dollars</li>
<li>Monthly gross pay: 150 times 4, which is 600 US dollars</li>
</ul>
<p>Now subtract simple costs. Say you pay 20 US dollars a month for a video call tool and printed worksheets, and you set aside 25 percent of gross pay for tax as a rough planning figure.</p>
<ul>
<li>Monthly gross pay: 600 US dollars</li>
<li>Monthly costs: 20 US dollars</li>
<li>Amount left before tax: 600 minus 20, which is 580 US dollars</li>
<li>Tax set aside at 25 percent of 600: 150 US dollars</li>
<li>Amount left after costs and tax set aside: 580 minus 150, which is 430 US dollars</li>
</ul>
<p>This example ignores unpaid preparation time, cancellations, and platform fees. If you teach through a platform that keeps part of the fee, your take home pay will be lower. Recalculate with your own rate, your own hours, and your own country tax rules before you rely on any figure.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-find-your-first-students">Step 4: Find your first students</h2>
<p>Your first students usually come from people who already know you. Tell friends, family, and work contacts what you teach, which level, and when you are free. Post a short, clear message in local parent groups and community groups, with your subject, level, times, and price.</p>
<p>Offer a short first meeting so the parent or student can check fit. A trial lesson at your normal rate, or a brief free chat, helps both sides decide. Do not offer endless free lessons, because free work rarely turns into paid work and it fills your limited hours.</p>
<p>If you join a platform, complete your profile in full. Use a clear photo, state your subject and level, list your qualifications honestly, and set only the hours you can truly keep. If you prefer employed part time work in general, our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-find-part-time-online-jobs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to find part time online jobs</a> may suit you better than finding your own students.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-run-good-lessons-and-keep-students">Step 5: Run good lessons and keep students</h2>
<p>Prepare each lesson before it starts. Know the goal for the hour, have two or three practice questions ready, and end with a short summary of what the student learned. Parents renew lessons when they can see progress, so send a brief note after each lesson with what you covered and what to practise.</p>
<p>Keep a simple record for each student. Note the topics covered, the homework set, and any weak spots to revisit. This record saves you time each week and shows parents that you take the work seriously.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/sh30-tutoring-planning.jpg" alt="A desk with a weekly planner, laptop and study books arranged for tutoring session planning" /></p>
<p>Ask happy parents for a short review and for referrals. One satisfied parent often knows other parents with the same need. Reviews and referrals are the main way a small tutoring side hustle grows without advertising spend.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-fit-tutoring-around-your-main-job">Step 6: Fit tutoring around your main job</h2>
<p>Protect your main job first. Check your employment contract for rules about outside work, and do not use work equipment or work hours for tutoring. If your contract bans side work, respect it or ask for written permission.</p>
<p>Set fixed tutoring blocks, for example two weekday evenings and one weekend morning. A fixed pattern helps students book ahead and helps you plan rest. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/side-hustles/manage-side-hustle-full-time-job" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to manage a side hustle with a full time job</a> gives a simple weekly planning method you can copy.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Startup costs are low. You need a laptop or tablet, a stable internet connection, a quiet space, and basic teaching materials. If you already own these, you can start for very little money. Optional costs include a whiteboard, a second screen, and subject books.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. It can take weeks to find your first two or three students, and a full schedule can take months. School holidays and exam seasons also change demand through the year. Tutoring income stops when you stop teaching, so treat it as paid hours, not passive income.</p>
<p>Side hustle income is taxable in most countries. Keep records of every payment and cost from day one. In the United States, the Internal Revenue Service explains that gig and side income must be reported even when it is part time, and you can read the official guidance on the <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gig-economy-tax-center" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IRS Gig Economy Tax Center</a>. Rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>New tutors often accept every student and every time slot, then burn out within a month. Set a firm weekly limit and keep it.</li>
<li>Many tutors charge too little at the start and then feel resentful about preparation time. Price for the full hour of work, including preparation and messages.</li>
<li>Some tutors skip written rules, then lose money on late cancellations. Put payment and cancellation terms in writing before the first lesson.</li>
<li>Tutors who prepare nothing waste lesson time and lose renewals. Spend ten minutes planning each lesson so the hour runs smoothly.</li>
<li>Parents lose trust when lessons start late or move often. Start on time, every time, and give early notice if you must reschedule.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Watch for fake clients who offer to overpay by cheque or transfer and ask you to send money back. This is a common fake payment scam. Never accept overpayment, and never send money to a new client for materials or fees.</p>
<p>Be careful with platforms or agencies that ask for large upfront fees to send you students. Honest platforms explain their fees clearly and take them from payments you receive. The United States Federal Trade Commission warns that honest employers never ask you to pay to get a job, and you can read its guidance on <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">job scams on the FTC consumer advice site</a>.</p>
<p>Protect student privacy. Do not share a child full name, school, or photos in public posts or reviews. Keep parent contact details private and store them safely.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-need-a-teaching-degree-to-tutor-as-a-side-hustle">Do I need a teaching degree to tutor as a side hustle?</h3>
<p>No, you do not always need a teaching degree to tutor privately, but you do need strong subject knowledge and clear explanations. Some platforms and schools require qualifications or background checks, so check the rules for the route you choose. Parents care most about whether their child improves and enjoys lessons.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-hours-a-week-should-i-tutor-alongside-a-job">How many hours a week should I tutor alongside a job?</h3>
<p>Start with four to eight hours a week and see how it feels alongside your job. Many side hustle tutors teach two or three evenings a week and keep one full rest day. Add hours only when your schedule and energy allow it.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-teach-online-or-in-person">Should I teach online or in person?</h3>
<p>Teach online if you want to save travel time and reach more students. Teach in person if your students are young or focus better with a tutor in the room. You can offer both, but keep your weekly blocks fixed so families can plan.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-set-my-tutoring-rate">How do I set my tutoring rate?</h3>
<p>Check what tutors in your area charge for the same subject and level, then set a rate that matches your experience. Start at the lower end while you build reviews, and raise your rate as your schedule fills. State your rate and cancellation terms in writing before the first lesson.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-before-i-find-my-first-student">How long before I find my first student?</h3>
<p>It can take a few weeks to find your first student, and longer to fill a weekly schedule. Tell your network clearly what you teach and when you are free, and post in local parent groups. Slow starts are normal in tutoring.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-tutor-if-my-job-contract-bans-side-work">Can I tutor if my job contract bans side work?</h3>
<p>No, you should not tutor for pay if your contract bans outside work. Read your contract first, and ask your employer for written permission if the rules are unclear. Protecting your main job comes first.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Pick one subject and one level, choose online or in person teaching, set a clear rate and written rules, and tell your network exactly when you are free. Prepare every lesson, track progress, and keep your weekly hours small enough to protect your main job and your rest. Start with two students, do the work well, and let referrals grow your schedule.</p>
