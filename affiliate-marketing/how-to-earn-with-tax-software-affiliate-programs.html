How to Earn with Tax Software Affiliate Programs in 2026

Tax software affiliate programs pay you when someone buys tax preparation software through your link. Most pay a percentage of the sale, not a flat bounty, and demand peaks sharply in tax season, which runs from January to April in the United States.

This guide compares the main tax software affiliate programs, explains how the commissions work, and shows how to time your content for tax season. It is written for finance bloggers, freelancers and bookkeepers who reach self-employed people and small business owners.

How tax software affiliate programs work

A tax software affiliate program pays you a share of the price when a reader buys a tax preparation product through your link. Most programs pay a percentage of the sale, usually reported between 15 and 40 percent, rather than a flat dollar amount. A few pay a flat fee per sale.

Four terms decide what you earn. The commission is the percentage or flat fee paid per sale. The cookie is the tracking window, which is the number of days after the click during which a purchase still counts as yours. The network is the platform that tracks sales and pays you. The payout threshold is the minimum balance you must reach before the network sends your money.

Tax software is different from most affiliate niches in two ways. First, it is seasonal. In the United States, most people file between January and April, so most sales happen in that window. Outside tax season, sales fall sharply. Second, the rules change every year. Tax software prices, features, and commission rates are updated for each tax year.

Country scope matters too. Almost all the programs in this guide are for the United States, because the software prepares US federal and state returns. Readers in other countries file under different systems and cannot use most of these products. Say that clearly in your content.

TurboTax shows the widest conflict. One guide reports 10 percent, another reports 15 percent, and a third reports up to 25 dollars per sale. The big five consumer programs

TurboTax, H and R Block, TaxAct, TaxSlayer and FreeTaxUSA are the five programs most readers know. TurboTax has the widest brand recognition, so it converts well even though its reported commission is unclear. H and R Block suits readers who want the choice of online filing or an in-person office, and it is reported at up to 15 dollars per new customer. TaxAct suits value seekers, and it is the most consistently reported rate in the set at 15 percent. TaxSlayer suits budget filers and military families, but its reported rate conflicts across guides. FreeTaxUSA suits simple returns with free federal filing, but its reported rate is the most confused in the set. If you are new to this niche, start with TaxAct or TaxSlayer, because their audiences are clear and their products are cheap enough to convert.

Budget and free filing programs

FreeTaxUSA, E-file.com and OnLine Taxes suit readers who want to file cheaply. FreeTaxUSA offers free federal filing, which makes it easy to promote, but its reported commission is confused, as the table above shows. E-file.com is reported at 40 percent with a 120-day cookie, which is the longest cookie in this niche. OnLine Taxes is reported at 25 percent.

Budget programs convert well, because the reader pays little or nothing. The commission per sale is small, because a percentage of a small price is a small amount, so these programs only work at volume.

Self-employed and freelancer tools

Self-employed readers have harder tax returns, and they need more than basic filing software. Keeper is an expense tracking app for self-employed people, and it is reported to pay up to 20 dollars per referral. TaxSlayer Self-Employed is built for freelancers, and QuickBooks is accounting software that many self-employed people use alongside tax software.

These tools suit a finance or freelancing audience. Our guide on how to work as a remote bookkeeper covers the bookkeeping side of this audience.

Specialist programs

Three specialist programs suit narrow audiences. CoinLedger is a crypto tax tool, reported at 25 percent lifetime recurring, which means you earn a share each year the customer renews. MyExpatTaxes is for US citizens living abroad, reported at 20 percent. Quaderno is a sales tax tool for online sellers, reported at 30 percent.

Specialist programs pay well per sale, but their audiences are small. They work best as a second program inside a related article, not as the main topic of your site.

Pro software for tax professionals

Drake Software and TaxSlayer Pro are built for tax professionals, not for people filing their own return. They cost far more than consumer software, but their audience is tiny and hard to reach. Drake does not publish its affiliate terms, so no rate is stated here.

Only promote pro software if your audience includes accountants or tax preparers. Promoting it to ordinary filers wastes traffic.

How to choose the right program for your audience

Audience fit comes first, and the rate comes second. A reader filing a simple return needs TaxAct or FreeTaxUSA. A reader with a small business needs Keeper or QuickBooks. A reader with crypto needs CoinLedger. Match the product to the reader before you look at the commission.

Then compare the commission and the cookie together. A 40 percent commission with a 30-day cookie can be worth less than a 15 percent commission with a 90-day cookie, because the longer cookie catches readers who take weeks to decide.

Also check the network. Tax software runs on CJ Affiliate, Impact, Awin and ShareASale. Our guide to software and SaaS affiliate programs covers the wider software niche.

How to apply, step by step

Step 1: Build at least five useful pages before you apply. A tax software comparison, a guide to filing as a freelancer, and a review of one product are a good start.

Step 2: Join the network that runs the program you want. TurboTax, TaxAct and TaxSlayer run through CJ Affiliate. FreeTaxUSA and CoinLedger run direct or through Impact. The CJ Affiliate site and the Impact site both explain how publisher accounts work.

Step 3: Read the program terms before you apply. Look for the commission, the cookie, the payout threshold, and the banned claims.

Step 4: Apply with honest information about your audience, your traffic, and your content. Do not inflate your numbers, because networks check.

Step 5: If you are accepted, add your disclosure near the top of each page that carries affiliate links, and add a second line that says the content is not tax advice.

Step 6: If you are rejected, improve the site and reapply in one to three months.

Timing your content for tax season

Tax season in the United States runs from January to April, and that is when most sales happen. Plan your content in three stages. In November and December, publish your comparisons and reviews, so they have time to rank. In January and February, publish filing guides and updates for the new tax year. In March and April, publish last-minute filing guides, because late filers search hardest then.

Update every rate and price in January, before the season starts. Tax software prices and commission rates change each tax year, and a guide with last year's prices will mislead readers and may break program rules. Outside tax season, tax content earns little, so use the quiet months to write your comparisons for next year and to build links to your tax pages.

Example only: what the maths looks like

This example uses made up numbers. It shows the maths, not what you will earn. Example only.

Suppose a tax comparison article gets 5,000 visits during tax season. Suppose 4 percent of visitors click an affiliate link. That is 5,000 times 0.04, which is 200 clicks. Suppose 5 percent of those clickers buy a product. That is 200 times 0.05, which is 10 sales. Suppose the product costs 50 dollars and the program pays 15 percent, which is 7 dollars and 50 cents per sale. That is 10 times 7.50, which is 75 dollars for the season from that one article.

Now suppose the same article gets only 500 visits outside tax season, with the same rates. That is 500 times 0.04, which is 20 clicks, and 20 times 0.05, which is 1 sale, and 1 times 7.50, which is 7 dollars and 50 cents. The same article earns ten times more in season than out of season.

Expect seasonal income, not steady income. Most of your sales will come between January and April. A realistic timeline is one full tax season of content before the first meaningful payout.

You do not need to be an accountant to start, but you do need to read the terms of every product you promote. Prices, features, and commission rates change each tax year.

Common mistakes

  • Do not quote a stale rate from last year, because tax software rates change every tax year.
  • Do not give tax advice, because affiliates are not tax advisers and programs ban that claim.
  • Do not promote US tax software to readers in other countries, because they cannot use it.
  • Do not hide your disclosure at the bottom of the page, because readers must see it before they click.
  • Do not promise a refund amount, because no affiliate can promise what a reader's refund will be.

Risks and scams to watch for

Tax affiliate marketing has real rules, and breaking them can end your account. The US Federal Trade Commission requires a clear disclosure when you earn a commission from a recommendation, and the disclosure must be easy to notice. The FTC Endorsement Guides explain what counts as a clear disclosure. Put it near the top of the page, in plain words, before the first affiliate link.

Never present yourself as a tax adviser unless you are qualified and licensed to give tax advice in your readers' country. Say clearly that your content is general information, not tax advice. Most programs ban claims such as "guaranteed refund" and "maximum refund".

Watch for scams that target new affiliates. No real program charges you a fee to join, and no real program asks you to pay for a starter kit or a list of high paying programs before you can apply. Be careful with any site that promises a fixed monthly income from tax links, because tax income is seasonal and no one can promise it. If a program is not listed on a known network such as CJ Affiliate, Impact, Awin or ShareASale, and you cannot find its official terms, do not promote it.

Affiliate income is income, and you must report it under the tax rules of your country. Keep records of every payout, and check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant if you are unsure.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best tax software affiliate program?

For most creators, TaxAct is the best starting point, because its reported rate of 15 percent is the most consistent in the guides reviewed. TurboTax converts well because of its brand, but its reported rate conflicts across guides.

How much does the TurboTax affiliate program pay?

Reported rates conflict. One guide reports 10 percent, another reports 15 percent, and a third reports up to 25 dollars per sale. Its cookie is reported at 7, 15, or 30 days depending on the guide. Check the live terms on the network listing before you quote a rate.

How do I join a tax affiliate program?

Build a site with several useful tax pages, add a clear disclosure and a line that says the content is not tax advice, then apply through the network that runs the program. Improve the site and reapply in one to three months if you are refused.

Do tax affiliate programs pay recurring commissions?

Most pay a one-time percentage per sale. A few, such as FreeTaxUSA and CoinLedger, are reported to pay a recurring share when a customer renews each year.

When is the best time to promote tax software?

Between January and April in the United States, which is tax season. Publish your comparisons in November and December so they have time to rank, and publish filing guides in January and February.

Can beginners join tax software affiliate programs?

Yes, but not every program. Comparison platforms and budget programs are the easiest entry point, because they accept smaller sites. The big brand programs are stricter and often want traffic history.

Conclusion

Tax software affiliate programs pay a percentage per sale, and they earn most during tax season from January to April in the United States. The guides disagree sharply on several rates, so check the live terms on the network before you quote a rate or apply. Start with a program that matches your readers, and publish your comparisons in November and December so they rank before the season.
