<p>Beauty affiliate programs pay you a commission when a reader buys skincare, makeup, or hair care through your tracked link. <h2 id="what-beauty-affiliate-programmes-are-and-who-they-suit">What beauty affiliate programmes are and who they suit</h2>
<p>A beauty affiliate programme pays you when a reader buys through your link inside the cookie window. The cookie is the tracking file that remembers your referral for a set number of days. Beauty cookies are often short, so the reader usually needs to buy soon after the click.</p>
<p>Beauty programmes suit you if you already publish routines, tutorials, or product reviews and you test products on your own skin or hair. They suit you less if you want to promote products you have never used. Readers ask detailed questions about skin type, shade, scent, and ingredients, and vague answers lose sales.</p>
<p>Beauty programmes come in three forms. Large retailers sell many brands in one basket. Single brands sell their own range and often pay a higher percentage. Networks let you apply to many smaller brands from one dashboard. Each form has different rules on discount codes and brand bidding, which means bidding on a brand name in paid search ads.</p>

<h2 id="step-1-choose-one-beauty-audience-and-one-routine">Step 1: Choose one beauty audience and one routine</h2>
<p>Pick one audience, such as beginners with sensitive skin, people with curly hair, or readers who want a simple makeup routine for work. Then pick one routine you can show step by step. A clear routine gives every product a job, so your links feel helpful rather than random.</p>
<p>Test products for long enough to describe texture, scent, finish, and wear. Note your skin type or hair type in every review. A foundation that suits dry skin may fail on oily skin, and your reader needs that detail before buying.</p>
<p>Keep a product diary. Write the date you started each product, how often you used it, and what changed. Do not claim a product cured a skin condition. Describe what you observed and tell readers to patch test new products and to seek medical advice for skin conditions.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-compare-retailer-programmes-and-brand-programmes">Step 2: Compare retailer programmes and brand programmes</h2>
<p>Compare programmes on commission, cookie length, payout threshold, and promotion rules. The payout threshold is the minimum balance you must reach before payment. In beauty, the cookie length often matters more than a small difference in percentage, because readers compare shades and prices before they buy.</p>
<p>As of November 2026, Sephora's official affiliate page says its United States programme runs through Rakuten Advertising and pays commission on all products without brand restrictions. The page does not publish a single commission rate or cookie length, so you must read the terms inside Rakuten after approval. The same page describes My Sephora Storefront, a separate creator route where you curate products on Sephora.com and earn when shoppers buy through your storefront. You can read the current routes on <a href="https://www.sephora.com/beauty/affiliates" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Sephora's official beauty affiliate page</a>.</p>
<p>Brand programmes often report higher percentages than retailers, but they cover a narrower range. List articles report beauty commissions from low single figures to around 20 percent, with cookies from 24 hours to 30 days or more. Treat those figures as reported ranges only. Confirm the rate, cookie, and code rules on the programme page before you publish.</p>

<h3 id="worked-example">Worked example</h3>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results.</p>
<p>Imagine your morning routine guide gets 2,000 visits in a month. Of those visitors, 200 click a retailer link, which is a 10 percent click rate. Of those 200 clicks, 10 readers buy inside the cookie window, which is a 5 percent conversion rate. Each basket is worth 60 US dollars, so total sales are 10 times 60, which is 600 US dollars. At a 5 percent commission, you earn 600 times 0.05, which is 30 US dollars before returns.</p>
<p>Now imagine the same guide links to a single brand that pays 10 percent, but only 4 readers buy because the range is narrower. Each basket is worth 45 US dollars, so total sales are 4 times 45, which is 180 US dollars. At 10 percent, you earn 180 times 0.10, which is 18 US dollars. The higher rate earned less in this example because fewer readers bought. Recalculate with your own click, conversion, and basket figures before you choose.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-apply-and-check-the-rules-on-claims-and-codes">Step 3: Apply and check the rules on claims and codes</h2>
<p>Apply with the blog, channel, or social account where you will place the links. Beauty brands review your content for fit, image quality, and honest wording. A small account with clear routines often passes more easily than a large account with copied product photos.</p>
<p>Read the promotion rules before you post. Many programmes forbid bidding on the brand name in paid ads. Some pay only when a buyer uses your personal code. Others exclude gift cards, sale items, or specific brands. Write these rules in your programme notes so you do not break them by accident.</p>
<p>Disclose every affiliate link and every free product clearly. The Federal Trade Commission says a material connection, which includes affiliate commission and free products, must be disclosed in a way that is hard to miss. Read the plain language rules in the <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FTC guide on disclosures for social media influencers</a> and place your disclosure before the first link or at the start of the video.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-publish-routine-guides-and-honest-reviews">Step 4: Publish routine guides and honest reviews</h2>
<p>Build each post around one routine or one decision. Show the order of products, the amount you use, and the result you observed. State the shade, skin type, or hair type for every product so readers can judge fit.</p>
<p>Write reviews that include downsides. Note strong scents, heavy textures, poor shade ranges, or slow delivery. Readers trust reviews that mention limits, and they return to creators who warn them before a bad purchase.</p>
<p>Learn the review format that earns trust. Structure each review with evidence, limits, and a clear verdict so readers can decide quickly. If you want a blog base for your reviews, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-start-a-beauty-blog-and-make-money" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start a beauty blog and make money</a>. If you prefer video, film the same routine step by step and link to the exact products you used.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/am30-beauty-review.jpg" alt="A person recording a makeup tutorial at a desk with a ring light and cosmetics" /></p>
<p>Update prices and availability often. Beauty products sell out, change formulas, and change shade names. Add the date you last checked each product and remove links to discontinued items.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-track-sales-returns-and-shade-and-size-issues">Step 5: Track sales, returns, and shade and size issues</h2>
<p>Check your dashboard every week. Record clicks, orders, commission, and reversals by post. A reversal happens when a buyer returns an order and the programme takes the commission back.</p>
<p>Watch for shade and size returns. If many readers return a foundation, your shade guidance may be unclear. Add swatches in natural light, compare shades to known products, and link to the retailer's shade finder where one exists.</p>
<p>Keep a simple programme record. Note the rate, cookie, code rules, and the date you checked the terms. A short weekly check of clicks, sales, and reversals keeps your programme record accurate.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Joining beauty programmes is usually free. Your costs are products you buy to test, basic lighting, and your time. Beauty testing takes weeks, because you need repeated use before you can describe results honestly. Many beginners spend more on products in the first months than they earn.</p>
<p>Expect slow growth. Search and social traffic build over months, and beauty readers compare several creators before they buy. Your first sales often come from followers who already trust your routine. Results vary with your audience, product prices, cookie length, and return rates.</p>
<p>Payment timing varies by network. Some networks validate sales for 30 to 60 days to allow for returns. Read the validation period before you rely on pending commission.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<p>Many beginners promote products they have never opened. Test every product yourself and describe only what you observed.</p>
<p>Some creators use filtered photos that hide texture and shade. Show products in natural light and state when a photo is edited.</p>
<p>Other creators copy brand claims about curing acne or removing wrinkles. Describe cosmetic results only and tell readers to seek medical advice for skin conditions.</p>
<p>A common error is joining a retailer programme and a brand programme for the same product without checking which link pays better after returns. Compare net earnings per 100 clicks, not headline rates.</p>
<p>Many posts hide the disclosure at the end of a caption. Place the disclosure at the start so readers see it before they click.</p>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Avoid programmes that charge a joining fee or require you to buy a starter kit to earn. Legitimate programmes are free to join and pay after a qualifying sale.</p>
<p>Check that each brand lists full ingredients, prices, delivery terms, and a returns policy. Avoid brands that promise miracle results or that hide ingredient lists. Promoting unsafe products can harm readers and end your programme accounts.</p>
<p>Never buy fake followers or fake reviews to pass a brand review. Brands check engagement quality, and platforms can close accounts that use fake activity.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-much-do-beauty-affiliate-programmes-pay">How much do beauty affiliate programmes pay?</h3>
<p>Beauty programmes pay in different ways, so there is no single rate. Retailers often pay a lower percentage on a wide range, while single brands often pay more on a narrow range. Check the current rate, cookie, and code rules on the programme page as of November 2026 before you rely on any figure.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-many-followers-to-join">Do I need many followers to join?</h3>
<p>You do not always need a large following, but brands review your content for fit and quality. A small account with clear, honest routines can pass. Some creator storefronts set their own follower or content requirements, so check each route before you apply.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-promote-beauty-products-without-a-website">Can I promote beauty products without a website?</h3>
<p>Yes, many programmes accept social creators and video creators. You must disclose every affiliate link and free product clearly on each post. Check the rules for your platform and for discount codes before you post.</p>
<h3 id="why-did-my-beauty-commission-get-reversed">Why did my beauty commission get reversed?</h3>
<p>Commission usually reverses when a buyer returns an order, cancels, or buys outside the cookie window. Shade mismatches are a common cause in beauty. Improve shade guidance and check the validation period in your network account.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-start-with-a-retailer-or-a-single-brand">Should I start with a retailer or a single brand?</h3>
<p>Start with the option that matches your routine content. A retailer suits mixed routines with many brands. A single brand suits deep reviews of one range. Many creators use one retailer plus one or two brands for the same audience.</p>
