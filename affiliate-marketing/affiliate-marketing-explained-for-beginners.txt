23:Tb5d,<h2 id="what-affiliate-marketing-is">What affiliate marketing is</h2>
<p>When you join an affiliate programme, you get a unique link for a product or service. If someone clicks your link and buys, you earn a commission — usually a percentage of the sale. The buyer pays the same price.</p>
<p>It is a real business model, but it is not passive income on day one. You need an audience that trusts you, and content that helps them decide.</p>
<h2 id="how-people-actually-earn">How people actually earn</h2>
<p>Most affiliate income comes from one of three places:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reviews and comparisons:</strong> "Best X for Y" articles that compare real options.</li>
<li><strong>Tutorials:</strong> guides that show how to use a product to solve a problem.</li>
<li><strong>Recommendations inside useful content:</strong> a tool mentioned because it is genuinely the right tool for the job.</li>
</ul>
<p>What does not work well is a page of links with no useful content. Search engines and readers both ignore it.</p>
<h2 id="getting-started">Getting started</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Pick a topic you know.</strong> Finance tools, software, courses, and physical products all have affiliate programmes — but you need to understand the topic to recommend well.</li>
<li><strong>Build the content first.</strong> A blog, YouTube channel, or newsletter with genuinely useful material comes before the links.</li>
<li><strong>Apply to programmes.</strong> Start with the programmes of products you already use. Large networks exist, but direct programmes often pay better and give better support.</li>
<li><strong>Disclose clearly.</strong> Every page with affiliate links must say so, in plain language, near the top. This is a legal requirement in many countries, and it is also how you keep trust.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="choosing-what-to-promote">Choosing what to promote</h2>
<p>Ask three questions before promoting anything:</p>
<ul>
<li>Would I recommend this if there were no commission?</li>
<li>Have I used it, or researched it thoroughly?</li>
<li>Is the commission structure clear, and does the company pay reliably?</li>
</ul>
<p>If the answer to any of these is no, do not promote it.</p>
<h2 id="mistakes-beginners-make">Mistakes beginners make</h2>
<ul>
<li>Promoting too many products, so nothing is credible.</li>
<li>Writing reviews of products they have never tried.</li>
<li>Hiding the disclosure in small print at the bottom.</li>
<li>Expecting income in the first month. Affiliate income usually follows audience growth, which takes months.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="a-realistic-timeline">A realistic timeline</h2>
<p>Months one to three are for content and learning. Months three to six may bring the first small commissions. Sustainable income, if it comes, usually follows a year or more of consistent, honest publishing. Anyone promising faster results is selling a course, not a fact.</p>
