How to Earn with Education Affiliate Programs in 2026

Education affiliate programs pay you when a reader buys a course, a certificate, a tutoring lesson, or a learning subscription through your tracked link. The niche pays well on paper, but long research times and strict claim rules catch many beginners. This guide shows you how to choose one learner audience, compare education services fairly, and publish honest guides that help readers pick the right course.

This guide covers education services and schools, including course platforms, tutoring, test preparation, and language learning. If you want to promote tools that help creators sell their own courses, read our separate guide on how to earn with online course affiliate programs instead.

What education affiliate programmes are and who they suit

An education affiliate programme pays you a share of each qualifying purchase your readers make inside the cookie window. The cookie is the tracking file that remembers your referral for a set number of days. Education buyers often research for days or weeks, so cookie length matters.

Education programmes suit you if you teach, tutor, coach careers, or publish study and skills content. They suit you less if you want fast sales with no expertise. Readers ask detailed questions about syllabus, time needed, and whether a certificate helps their job search.

Education programmes fall into four groups. Course platforms sell individual courses and certificates. Subscription libraries sell monthly or annual access to many classes. Tutoring marketplaces sell live lessons with a teacher. Test preparation services sell practice tests and study plans for exams.

Step 1: Choose one learner audience and one outcome

Pick one audience, such as career changers who want a data job, parents who need maths tutoring, or adults who want to learn a language. Then pick one outcome, such as passing an exam, finishing a certificate, or speaking at a basic level.

Study the syllabus yourself before you recommend a course. Note the hours needed, the projects included, and the skills the course does not teach. Readers trust guides that state limits clearly.

Check who the course suits and who should skip it. A beginner course wastes money for an advanced learner. An advanced course frustrates a true beginner. State the starting level in every guide.

Step 2: Compare course platforms, tutoring, and test prep

Compare programmes on commission, cookie length, eligible products, and refund rules. The payout threshold is the minimum balance you must reach before payment. In education, also check whether subscriptions pay on the first month only or on every month.

As of November 2026, Coursera's official affiliate page states baseline commissions between 15 percent and 45 percent on eligible purchases made within 30 days of a qualified click, with bonuses for strong performance. The same page says monthly subscriptions commission the first month only and that degree purchases are excluded. You can read the current terms on Coursera's official affiliate programme page.

Other education programmes report different models. Course marketplaces often report lower percentages with short cookies. Tutoring services often report a share of the first lesson. Language programmes often report tiered rates that rise with volume. Treat every figure as a reported range until you confirm it in your own network account.

Worked example

Example only: these numbers are not typical results.

Imagine your career guide gets 4,000 visits in a month. Of those visitors, 400 click a course platform link, which is a 10 percent click rate. Of those 400 clicks, 20 readers buy inside the cookie window, which is a 5 percent conversion rate. Each purchase is worth 50 US dollars, so total sales are 20 times 50, which is 1,000 US dollars. At a 20 percent commission, you earn 1,000 times 0.20, which is 200 US dollars before refunds.

Now imagine the same guide promotes a monthly subscription at 50 US dollars that pays 20 percent on the first month only. If 20 readers subscribe, you still earn 20 times 10, which is 200 US dollars in month one, but you earn nothing from their later months. If a different programme paid 10 percent every month and all 20 readers stayed for six months, you would earn 20 times 5 times 6, which is 600 US dollars over six months. Recalculate with your own click, conversion, and retention figures before you choose.

Step 3: Apply and check claim rules and exclusions

Apply with the website, channel, or newsletter where you will place the links. Education brands review your content for accuracy and fit. A small study blog with clear guides often passes more easily than a large general site.

Read the claim rules before you post. Many programmes forbid promising a job, a salary, or an exam pass. Some forbid bidding on the brand name in paid ads. Others exclude degrees, boot camps, or team plans. Write these exclusions in your notes so your guides never send readers to excluded products.

Disclose every affiliate link clearly. The Federal Trade Commission says you must disclose a material connection, which includes affiliate commission, in a way that is hard to miss. Read the plain language rules in the FTC guide on disclosures for social media influencers and place your disclosure before the first link.

Step 4: Publish comparison guides and study plans

Build each post around one decision. Compare two or three courses on syllabus, time needed, projects, and total cost. State who each option suits and who should choose a different route.

Add a simple study plan. Show how many hours per week the course needs and what the reader should finish each month. Honest timelines help readers choose well and reduce refunds.

Link your guides together. Plan topics around learner searches so each guide supports the next one. If you prefer video, film the same comparison and study plan so learners can follow along. If you want to teach rather than promote, read how to find online tutoring jobs.

Update prices and syllabus details often. Courses change prices, add modules, and retire certificates. Add the date you last checked each course and remove links to retired products.

Step 5: Track enrolments, refunds, and subscription rules

Check your dashboard every week. Record clicks, enrolments, commission, and reversals by post. A reversal happens when a learner refunds and the programme takes the commission back.

Watch the gap between clicks and enrolments. Many clicks with few enrolments often mean the price is too high, the level is wrong, or the cookie is too short for slow decisions. Few clicks with steady enrolments often mean the guide deserves more internal links.

Keep a simple programme record. Note the rate, cookie, exclusions, and the date you checked the terms. A short weekly check of clicks, enrolments, and reversals keeps your programme record accurate.

What it costs and what to expect

Joining education programmes is usually free. Your costs are courses you buy to review, tutoring lessons you book to test, and your time. Reviewing a course properly takes weeks, because you need to complete modules before you can judge quality. Many beginners spend more on courses in the first months than they earn.

Expect a slow start. Education readers research for weeks, compare syllabuses, and wait for sales. Your first enrolments often come from readers who already follow your study content. Results vary with your audience, course prices, cookie length, and refund rates.

Payment timing varies by network. Some networks validate enrolments for several weeks to allow for refunds. Read the validation period before you spend pending commission.

Common mistakes

Many beginners promise that a certificate will guarantee a job or a salary. Never promise employment outcomes. Describe skills and projects only and let readers judge fit.

Some creators recommend courses they have not opened. Complete at least the first modules and state how much you finished.

Other creators send readers to degree pages that pay no commission. Check the exclusion list before you build a guide around a product.

A common error is ignoring total cost. State the full price, the renewal price, and the hours needed so readers can compare fairly.

Many posts hide the disclosure at the end. Place the disclosure before the first affiliate link.

Risks and scams to watch for

Avoid programmes that charge a joining fee or promise guaranteed income. Legitimate programmes are free to join and pay after a qualifying purchase.

Check that each school lists its syllabus, prices, refund policy, and teacher credentials. Avoid services that promise exam answers, fake certificates, or guaranteed passes. Promoting dishonest services harms learners and can end your accounts.

Protect your accounts with unique passwords and two factor sign in. Never share payment details outside the network dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

How much do education affiliate programmes pay?

Education programmes pay in different ways, so there is no single rate. Course platforms often pay a percentage per course, while tutoring services often pay a share of the first lesson. Check the current rate, cookie, and exclusions in your account as of November 2026 before you rely on any figure.

Do education programmes pay recurring commission?

Some programmes pay recurring commission and many pay on the first payment only. Monthly subscriptions often commission the first month only. Read the subscription rule in your programme terms before you forecast income.

Can I promote education programmes without a website?

Yes, many programmes accept video creators, podcasters, and newsletters. You must disclose every affiliate link clearly on each post. Check the rules for your platform and for paid ads before you post.

Why did my education commission get reversed?

Commission usually reverses when a learner refunds, cancels a subscription early, or buys an excluded product such as a degree. Check the validation period and exclusion list in your dashboard. Improve level and price guidance to reduce refunds.

Should I start with courses or tutoring?

Start with the service your audience already wants. Courses suit self paced learners who want a certificate. Tutoring suits learners who need live help and feedback. Many creators test one of each for the same audience and keep the better converter.

Conclusion

Earn with education affiliate programmes by choosing one learner audience and one outcome, comparing services on eligible products and subscription rules, studying every course yourself, and never promising jobs or exam passes.
