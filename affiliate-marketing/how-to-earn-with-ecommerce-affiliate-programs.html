How to Earn with E-commerce Affiliate Programs in 2026

Learning how to earn with e-commerce affiliate programs is one of the most accessible ways to start affiliate marketing, because the products are things people already buy every day. From kitchen tools and phone accessories to clothes, books and home goods, e-commerce affiliate programs let you recommend products you know and earn a commission when someone buys through your link.

This guide shows you how e-commerce affiliate programs work, what the main programs pay, how to choose between them, and how to build content that actually converts. It is for bloggers, reviewers, YouTubers, TikTok creators and Pinterest publishers who already reach people who shop online.

What e-commerce affiliate programs are and how they pay

An e-commerce affiliate program lets you promote products sold by an online store or marketplace, and earn a commission when someone buys through your tracked link. You join the program, get a unique link for each product, and share it in your content. When a visitor clicks and buys within the cookie window, the sale is credited to you.

The commission is usually a percentage of the sale price, and it varies by product category. Some programs pay a fixed fee per sale instead, such as a set amount for each referral who signs up for a service. The percentage matters less than the product price: 4 percent on a 200 US dollar product earns 8 US dollars, which is more than 10 percent on a 20 US dollar product.

The cookie window decides how long after the click you still get paid. Most e-commerce programs use a short window, often 24 hours to 30 days. If a shopper clicks today and buys three weeks later, you only earn if the window is still open. That is why the cookie length matters as much as the rate.

E-commerce commissions are lower per sale than finance or software programs, because the products are cheaper and the margins are thinner. Two facts shape every decision in e-commerce. First, the product mix matters more than the headline rate. A creator who promotes kitchen tools at 4 percent on a 100 US dollar product earns 4 US dollars per sale. A creator who promotes luxury goods at 10 percent on a 500 US dollar product earns 50 US dollars per sale. Second, volume matters. E-commerce is a numbers game: many small commissions add up, but only if your traffic is large and your conversion rate is strong.

The five main e-commerce programs

Amazon Associates is the largest and best-known e-commerce affiliate program. It is easy to join, it covers almost every product category, and shoppers trust Amazon, so conversion rates are high. The trade-off is the rate: 1 to 10 percent depending on the category, with most everyday products at the low end. The cookie lasts 24 hours, but if a shopper adds an item to the cart within that window, you can still earn for up to 89 days, as long as they buy it before the cart expires. There is also a rule that matters for beginners: you must make 3 qualifying sales within your first 180 days, or your account may be closed. Amazon pays commissions about 60 days after the end of the month in which they were earned. The program's own operating agreement requires a specific disclosure sentence, which is "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." You can read the full terms in the Amazon Associates Operating Agreement.

The eBay Partner Network pays about 1 to 4 percent depending on the category, with a 24-hour cookie. It suits creators who review second-hand goods, collectibles, car parts or refurbished electronics, because eBay is the natural place to buy them. The audience is smaller than Amazon's, but the intent is strong: eBay shoppers often know exactly what they want.

Shopify Affiliate pays up to 150 US dollars per referral who signs up for a full-priced Shopify plan. The cookie lasts 30 days, and trial tracking runs for 400 days, so you earn even if the referral starts a free trial first. The minimum payout is 10 US dollars, and commissions are paid around the 22nd of each month. Shopify is free to join, and its affiliate program page explains the terms in plain language. It suits creators who teach people how to start an online store, which is why it pairs well with our guide to starting an e-commerce store with Shopify.

TikTok Shop Affiliate pays 10 to 30 percent, with the rate set by each seller. Creators promote products inside short videos and live streams, and viewers buy without leaving the app. The rate is generous, but the model depends on video reach, and the seller sets the terms, so the same product can pay different rates from different sellers.

ClickBank is a network that sells mostly digital products, such as courses, software and ebooks. Commissions typically run 50 to 75 percent, with a 60-day cookie, which is far higher than physical goods. The trade-off is quality: some ClickBank products are poor, so check every product before you promote it.

The affiliate networks that host e-commerce offers

Many e-commerce brands do not run their own affiliate program. They list their offers on a network, which handles tracking, payment and reporting. The main networks are Impact, Awin, ShareASale and CJ Affiliate. Each one hosts hundreds of brands, from large retailers to small shops.

Using a network has two advantages. First, you apply once and get access to many programs, instead of applying to each brand separately. Second, you get one dashboard and one payment, instead of chasing small payouts from many brands.

Step 1: Choose a niche inside e-commerce

E-commerce is too broad to cover well. A creator who tries to promote everything earns less than a creator who owns one corner of it. Pick a niche where you can genuinely help readers decide.

Good niches are ones where buyers research before they buy: kitchen tools, home office gear, fitness equipment, baby products, pet supplies, beauty tools and tech accessories. These niches have clear products, clear prices and clear questions, which makes comparison content easy to write.

Check three things before you commit to a niche: that the products cost enough to pay a meaningful commission, that buyers search for reviews and comparisons, and that you can test or use the products yourself. A niche where you cannot test the product is a niche where your content will be thin.

Step 2: Pick the programs that fit your niche

Do not join every program. Join the two or three that match your niche and your audience.

If your niche is everyday products, Amazon Associates is the obvious starting point, because shoppers already trust it and the range is huge. If your niche is second-hand or collectible goods, add eBay Partner Network. If your niche is helping people start a store, add Shopify Affiliate. If your niche is short video, add TikTok Shop. If your niche is digital products, add ClickBank.

Check the rate by category, not the headline rate. Amazon pays 10 percent on luxury beauty and 3 percent on many everyday categories. A program that pays 10 percent on a category you never cover is worthless to you.

Check the cookie window too. A 30-day cookie gives you far more chances to earn than a 24-hour cookie, especially for products people think about before they buy. A shopper who clicks on a 200 US dollar kitchen mixer today may not buy until payday, and a 24-hour cookie will have expired by then.

Step 3: Apply and set up tracking

Most e-commerce programs are free to join and approve quickly, often within a day or two. Amazon Associates is the exception: you must make 3 qualifying sales within 180 days of joining, or your account may be closed. That rule means you should not join Amazon until you have traffic ready to send.

Apply through the program's own affiliate page. You will usually need a website, channel or social profile, a short description of your audience, and your traffic sources. Some programs ask for your tax details at sign-up, because they need them to pay you.

Once approved, set up tracking properly. Use separate tracking IDs or sub-IDs for each content source, so you can see which pages and channels drive sales. Track three metrics: the click-through rate, which is clicks divided by views; the conversion rate, which is sales divided by clicks; and the earnings per click, which is total commission divided by total clicks. If your conversion rate is below 1 percent, your content or your product choice needs work.

Step 4: Create content that converts

Product reviews convert best when they are honest and specific. A review that lists the features, shows the product in use, names the flaws and says who it does not suit will earn more than a review that only praises it. Readers trust a reviewer who admits a weakness.

Comparison posts convert well too: "best kitchen mixers under 100 US dollars" or "Brand A versus Brand B for home office chairs" attract people who are close to buying. Put the products side by side in a table, list the price, the key features and the trade-offs, and link each product to the store.

Listicles work for broad searches: "10 useful kitchen tools for small kitchens" or "7 gifts for coffee lovers". They rank well and they give you many chances to earn from one page. Keep each item short and link only to products you would genuinely recommend.

Tutorials work when the product solves a problem: "how to set up a home office for under 200 US dollars" or "how to make better coffee at home". The tutorial gives the reader a reason to buy, and the product links feel natural instead of forced.

Video converts well in e-commerce, because viewers can see the product in use. A short product review video, a unboxing or a comparison filmed on a phone can earn well, especially on TikTok and YouTube. Film the product, show it working, and put the link in the description and the pinned comment.

Step 5: Choose your traffic channels

Search is the most stable channel for e-commerce affiliates. People search for reviews and comparisons before they buy, and a well-ranked article can earn for years. It is slow to build, but it is the primary channel for affiliates who focus on long-term income.

Pinterest works well for visual niches such as home goods, fashion, beauty and gifts. A pin that links to a review or a listicle can drive traffic for months. It suits creators who make good images and write clear titles.

Email works well for repeat buyers. A list of readers who trust your recommendations can earn from a single email, especially when you share a genuine deal or a new product you tested. Do not send daily sales emails, or your list will unsubscribe.

A worked example of the maths

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can model your own plan.

Take an article that reviews a kitchen mixer priced at 100 US dollars, with a 4 percent commission from Amazon Associates. Each sale earns 4 US dollars. If the article gets 10,000 views a month, 2 percent of viewers click the link, and 5 percent of clickers buy, that is 200 clicks and 10 sales, or 40 US dollars a month from one article.

Now take a Shopify Affiliate referral. Each referral who signs up for a full-priced plan earns up to 150 US dollars. If a tutorial gets 5,000 views a month, 1 percent click, and 2 percent of clickers sign up, that is 50 clicks and 1 signup, or 150 US dollars a month from one article.

The lesson is simple: a few high-value referrals can beat many small product sales. Mix low-priced products for volume with high-value programs for income.

What it costs and what to expect

Joining e-commerce affiliate programs is free. The real costs are a domain and hosting if you blog, your time, and any paid ads you choose to run. Most beginners start with search content and social media, which cost time rather than money.

Approval is usually fast, often within a day or two, except for Amazon, which requires 3 qualifying sales within 180 days. Payouts are monthly, and Amazon pays about 60 days after the end of the month in which the commission was earned, so your first payment can take two to three months to arrive.

Common mistakes

Promoting products you have never used is the most common mistake. Readers can tell when a review is written from the product page, and they will not trust it. Test the product, or say clearly that you have not tested it and explain why you still recommend it.

Chasing the highest commission is the second. A 10 percent rate on a product your audience will never buy earns less than a 4 percent rate on a product they buy every week. Check the fit first.

Ignoring the cookie window is the third. A 24-hour cookie means you only earn if the shopper buys the same day. For expensive products, that is a real problem. Prefer programs with longer windows where you can.

Forgetting the Amazon disclosure is the fourth. Amazon requires the exact sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." on any page with Amazon links. Missing it can get your account closed.

Risks, rules and scams to watch for

E-commerce affiliate marketing is low risk compared with finance or crypto, but it still has rules. In the United States, the FTC requires clear and visible disclosure of affiliate relationships. In the UK, the ASA has similar rules. A disclosure line near the top of the post, such as "This post contains affiliate links. I may earn a commission if you buy through them," is the standard. You can read the rules in the FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking.

Watch for fake products and fake reviews. Some sellers pay for fake reviews, and some products are counterfeit. Check the seller, read the one-star reviews, and test the product yourself before you recommend it.

Watch for click fraud and cookie stuffing. Never use tools that force clicks or set cookies without a real click. That is against every program's rules and can get your account closed and your commissions voided.

Frequently asked questions

Which e-commerce affiliate program pays the most?

It depends on what you promote. ClickBank pays 50 to 75 percent on digital products, which is the highest rate, but the products are digital, not physical. TikTok Shop pays 10 to 30 percent, set by each seller. Shopify pays up to 150 US dollars per referral. Amazon pays 1 to 10 percent by category. The best program is the one that matches your niche and your audience, not the one with the highest headline rate.

Do I need a website to earn with e-commerce affiliate programs?

No. You can promote products on YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest or Instagram, and earn from links in the description or bio. A website helps, because search traffic is stable and you can build comparison pages, but it is not required. Some programs, such as Amazon Associates, ask for a website or a social profile when you apply.

How much can I make with e-commerce affiliate programs?

Earnings vary widely, because commissions are small and volume matters. A beginner with a focused site might earn a few tens of US dollars a month in the first year. A creator with a large audience and strong reviews can earn thousands a month. A few hundred US dollars a month is a good achievement for a beginner. Anyone who promises fast, large income is selling something.

Is Amazon Associates free to join?

Yes, it is free to join, but you must make 3 qualifying sales within your first 180 days, or your account may be closed. You should not join until you have traffic ready to send. The minimum payout is 10 US dollars, and commissions are paid about 60 days after the end of the month in which they were earned.

Can I promote the same product on multiple programs?

Yes. You can link the same product on Amazon, eBay and a brand's own program, and let the reader choose where to buy. Use separate tracking links for each program, and check the terms, because some programs ban promoting the same product on a competing platform in the same article.

How do I get paid from e-commerce affiliate programs?

Most programs pay monthly by bank transfer, PayPal or gift card, depending on the program. Amazon pays by direct deposit, cheque or gift card. Shopify pays around the 22nd of each month. ClickBank pays weekly or fortnightly. Check the payout method and minimum before you promote a program.

Conclusion

Learning how to earn with e-commerce affiliate programs comes down to a simple sequence: choose a niche where buyers research before they buy, pick the two or three programs that fit that niche, apply with traffic ready to send, publish honest reviews and comparisons that help readers decide, and follow the disclosure rules every time. The commissions are small, so you need volume, but the products are things people already buy, which makes the niche accessible and honest. Start with one niche, test the products yourself, and build from there.
