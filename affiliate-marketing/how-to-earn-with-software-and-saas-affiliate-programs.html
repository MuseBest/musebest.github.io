How to Earn with Software and SaaS Affiliate Programs in 2026

Learning how to earn with software affiliate programs is worth your time if you already help people choose tools. Software as a service, usually shortened to SaaS, means software you pay for by subscription, such as email tools, design tools, or a customer database. When you refer a paying customer to a SaaS company, many programs pay you a share of every payment that customer makes, sometimes for years. This guide explains how those programs work, how to compare them, and how to start without hype.

This guide is for beginners who blog, make videos, or run a newsletter about business tools, marketing, design, or freelancing. You will learn the four commission models, how to shortlist programs, how to get accepted, what content converts, and what a realistic first year looks like.

How software and SaaS affiliate programs work

A software affiliate program is a partnership. You join the program, you get a unique tracking link, and you share it in content that helps people choose software. When a reader clicks your link and becomes a paying customer, you earn a commission. A commission is the payment you earn for a sale you referred.

Software is different from most affiliate niches because the product is a subscription. A pair of shoes is bought once. A software subscription is paid every month or every year, and many programs share each of those payments with you. That is why SaaS affiliates talk about recurring commission, which is a commission that repeats on each payment while the customer stays subscribed.

Tracking works through a small file called a cookie, which is stored in the visitor's browser when they click your link. A cookie window is the number of days after the click during which a purchase still counts as yours. Software buyers rarely decide on the spot. They sign up for a free plan, test the product, compare alternatives, and upgrade weeks later. A short 30 day cookie can miss those sales, so look for 60 days or more. HubSpot publishes a 180 day cookie, and SE Ranking publishes 120 days, according to guides that checked those pages in October 2026.

Most programs also use a hold period, usually around 30 days, before a commission is approved. A hold exists because customers can ask for a refund, and a refunded payment means the commission is reversed. You are only ever paid on revenue the company actually keeps. Payouts usually arrive monthly, once your approved balance passes a minimum threshold, commonly 25 to 100 US dollars. Common payment methods are PayPal, bank transfer, Wise, or a partner platform such as PartnerStack or Impact. Impact is the partnership platform used by many software companies, including HubSpot, Coursera and Udemy. Read more at Impact.

The four commission models compared

The model matters more than the headline percentage. A 50 percent rate that stops after 12 months can earn less than a 40 percent rate that pays for life. Compare the four models before you join anything.

Here is why duration matters. Picture a customer paying 20 US dollars a month who stays for three years. A 50 percent commission for 12 months earns you 120 US dollars in total. A 40 percent lifetime commission earns you 288 US dollars. The lower rate wins, and it keeps winning every extra month the customer stays. Always do the maths with how long a typical customer stays, not just the headline rate.

Step 1: Pick one software niche

Do not promote ten unrelated tools at once. Pick one niche your existing content or audience already touches, such as email marketing, social media management, design, accounting, or project management. Then promote the two or three tools that solve real problems in that niche.

Ask three questions. Does your audience already need this software? Can you show how it works, with screenshots or a short walkthrough? And would you recommend it if there were no commission? If the answer to any of these is no, pick a different tool.

A narrow niche also helps you rank in Google. A post such as "Best email tool for a small newsletter" is easier to rank than "Best software", and its readers are closer to buying. For the wider method, read how to choose a profitable affiliate niche.

Step 2: Shortlist programs on five numbers

Five numbers decide whether a program is worth your time, and only one of them is usually in the marketing.

First, the rate, and whether it is the entry rate or the ceiling. Some programs advertise up to 40 percent while new affiliates start at 20 percent. Assume the advertised number is the ceiling until the terms say otherwise.

Second, the commission window: lifetime, capped at a number of months, or one-time. This changes two year earnings by a factor of two or more, and it is usually one line in the terms.

Third, the attribution window, which is how long after a click you still get credit. A 30 day cookie is tight for software, because buyers test free plans first. Ninety days or more is comfortable.

Fourth, the payout threshold, which is how much you must accumulate before you see money. HubSpot pays out at 10 US dollars. Some programs hold commission until 250 US dollars. On a small traffic base, that difference is months of waiting.

Fifth, the promotional restrictions: whether you can run paid search, bid on brand terms, promote with coupons, or email your list. Most programs ban bidding on their brand name. Some reject coupon sites outright. Most hide these rules inside the agreement, which you only see after approval, so ask the affiliate manager if anything is unclear.

Also check that the program is still open. Programs close without telling anyone. One directory found that 14 of its 94 listed programs were paused, invite-only, closed, or had no public program at all, yet several still appeared near the top of other people's best program lists with commission rates that no longer existed. Check the program page before you write, not after.

Step 3: Get accepted

Most good SaaS programs approve applications manually, and the criteria are more consistent than they appear. One program publishes its rejection reasons outright: an unfinished website, not owning your domain, a bare list of tools with no detail, no affiliate disclosure, being a coupon site, or applying with social profiles alone. Every one of those applies to other programs too.

Before you apply anywhere, have these in place: a finished site on a domain you own with real published content, a visible affiliate disclosure, at least one substantial article about the software category, and a working contact page and about page. Approval times vary from two business days to three or four weeks. Add your payout details on day one, because missing details are the most common reason a payout is delayed.

Step 4: Publish content that converts

Software buyers want proof a tool solves their exact problem before they commit to a monthly payment. The content that converts best helps someone finish an evaluation they have already started.

Honest comparisons between two named products work well, because the reader is choosing. Implementation walkthroughs work well, because the reader sees the tool in use. Migration guides work well, because the reader is already unhappy with their current tool. Pricing breakdowns work well when they include the costs the vendor hides, such as add-ons and higher tiers.

Publish five to ten pieces in your niche before you judge the results. A small batch might be: two comparisons, three tutorials, two problem solving posts, and one honest review. Link them to each other so readers can move from a problem post to a comparison to a tutorial. For the writing craft, read how to write affiliate product reviews.

Video helps too. A short screen recording that shows the tool solving one problem answers objections that text alone cannot. You do not need a large following. A smaller, niche focused audience with high intent often converts better than a broad audience with no shared need. For video basics, read how to start a YouTube channel.

Step 5: Track and compound

Use the affiliate dashboard to track clicks, conversion rate, and earnings per click. Earnings per click is your total commission divided by total clicks, and it tells you which programs are actually worth your effort. A program with a high rate but a low conversion rate can earn less per click than a modest program that converts well.

Recurring commissions compound. Each month you add new referrals while last month's referrals keep paying, so your income grows even in months when you publish nothing new. That only works if your referrals stay subscribed, which is why product quality matters more than the rate. A tool people cancel within weeks earns you almost nothing, however high the percentage.

Review your numbers monthly. Drop programs that never convert, update posts with outdated prices or features, and write more of whatever already converts. For tracking methods, read how to track affiliate earnings.

Worked example: what recurring commission really adds up to

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, including churn, which most guides leave out.

Imagine you refer 10 customers a month to a program paying 30 percent recurring commission on a 50 US dollar monthly plan. Each customer earns you 15 US dollars a month. In month one, 10 customers earn you 150 US dollars. If every customer stayed, month six would bring 900 US dollars from 60 accumulated customers.

Now add churn, which is the share of customers who cancel each month. At 5 percent monthly churn, about 52 of those 60 customers are still subscribed in month six, earning you about 780 US dollars that month. At 10 percent monthly churn, about 43 are still subscribed, earning about 645 US dollars. The rate matters, but retention matters just as much. A sticky product with a lower rate often beats a flashy rate on a product people cancel quickly.

Recheck: 30 percent of 50 is 15. 10 customers times 15 is 150. 60 customers times 15 is 900. With 5 percent monthly churn over six months, roughly 52 customers remain, and 52 times 15 is 780. The maths checks out as an illustration, not a promise.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting costs are low. A domain costs about 10 to 15 US dollars a year, and hosting costs about 35 to 60 US dollars in the first year. Most SaaS programs are free to join, and many tools offer a free plan you can use to learn the product before you promote it. Your main cost is time: expect 5 to 10 hours per useful article or video.

Expect a slow start. Software buyers take weeks to decide, and your first commission often arrives 30 days after the sale because of the hold period, then waits behind a payout threshold. Most beginners see their first payout after three to six months of steady publishing, and meaningful recurring income takes a year or more of accumulated referrals. Results vary with traffic, content quality, program terms, and how well the product fits your audience. Nothing here is a promise of income.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing the headline rate without checking how long the commission lasts.
  • Bidding on a brand name in paid search, which most programs ban.
  • Buying through your own link, which breaks almost every program's terms and gets the commission cancelled.
  • Promoting a product you have never used, which readers can tell from a generic description.
  • Spreading across ten unrelated programs instead of focusing on two or three.
  • Skipping the payout setup, then wondering why the first payment never arrives.
  • Trusting a commission rate without a date, when programs change their terms often.
  • Building on a single program, which leaves you with nothing if it closes or changes its terms.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch out for referral schemes dressed up as affiliate programs. Some tools pay in account credit, not money, and that credit cannot be withdrawn. A real affiliate program pays money, through a dashboard you can check, with clear payout terms. If the reward is credit only, say so plainly and do not present it as income.

Be careful with lists of best SaaS affiliate programs that copy each other's figures. Rates go out of date fast: one well known tool replaced a 40 percent recurring model with a flat bounty, and the old number still circulates years later. Check the program's own page and note the date you checked it.

Never promise readers that a tool will make them money, and never fake a review of software you have not tried. If you have not used the tool, say so, and base your content on the official documentation, the pricing page, and the free plan. For disclosure rules, the FTC endorsement guides explain when and how to disclose a paid recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

How much do SaaS affiliate programs pay?

Most pay 20 to 50 percent of the subscription revenue. Some pay for the first 12 months only, while others pay for the customer's lifetime. A few pay a flat bounty per sale instead. What you actually earn depends on the plan price, how long customers stay, and how many referrals you make.

What is the difference between recurring and lifetime commissions?

A recurring commission pays on repeat payments but may stop after a set period, such as 12 months. A lifetime commission is recurring for as long as the customer stays subscribed. Lifetime pays less per month in some cases but keeps paying for years.

Do I need a website to join a SaaS affiliate program?

For most of the good ones, yes. Programs check for a finished site with real content, a disclosure page, and a contact page. Some accept YouTube channels, newsletters or communities, but applying with social profiles alone is a common reason for rejection.

How long do SaaS affiliate cookies last?

Usually 30 to 120 days. A cookie of 60 days or more is a good sign, because software buyers often take weeks to decide. HubSpot publishes 180 days, which is one of the longest.

Do SaaS affiliate programs pay on free signups?

Generally not. You earn when a referred customer pays. That is why programs with a free plan work well: the free plan gets people in, and you earn when they upgrade.

Can I make a full-time income from SaaS affiliate marketing?

It is possible with consistent effort over months or years, not weeks. Affiliates who build recurring referrals across a few well matched programs and keep publishing focused content see monthly income grow as accumulated subscriptions compound. It is never guaranteed.

Conclusion

To earn with software and SaaS affiliate programs, pick one niche, shortlist programs on rate, commission window, cookie, payout threshold and rules, get your site ready before you apply, then publish comparisons, tutorials and honest reviews that help buyers decide. Favour lifetime commissions over short ones, check the terms and the date on every figure, and track earnings per click so you know what actually converts.
