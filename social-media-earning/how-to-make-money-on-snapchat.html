How to Make Money on Snapchat in 2026

Learning how to make money on Snapchat starts with a surprise. Snapchat looks like a messaging app, not an income app. Yet it has a real monetisation programme, a short video feed called Spotlight, and a young audience that brands want to reach. The rules are strict, the programme is invite only, and much of the advice online is out of date.

This guide uses Snapchat's own help page, read in November 2026, for the current rules. You will learn how the Monetisation Programme works, what it actually requires, the seven ways creators earn on Snapchat, what you can realistically expect, and the scams to avoid.

Can you really make money on Snapchat?

Yes, but not by views alone. Snapchat does not pay a fixed rate per view, and a video with a million views does not come with a set payment. The platform shares ad revenue with creators who qualify for its Monetisation Programme, and it pays through that programme only. Outside the programme, creators earn through brand deals, affiliate links, and their own products, which often pay more reliably than the programme itself.

Small creators can earn on Snapchat without qualifying for the programme. A small audience that trusts you can buy from a link, book a service, or join a subscription.

How Snapchat monetisation works

There are two paths to earning on Snapchat: inside the programme, and outside it. The table below shows the difference, so you can see which path fits where you are now.

Path How it pays Who it suits Main limit
Inside the programme A share of ad revenue from Public Stories and eligible Spotlight videos Large creators who post public content daily Invite only, with high thresholds
Outside the programme Brand deals, affiliate links, product sales, subscriptions Small and mid-size creators with a trusted audience You must find the brands and the buyers yourself

Both paths need the same foundation: public content that holds attention, a clear audience, and a reason for people to come back. Private Snaps to friends do not earn money.

The Monetisation Programme, step by step

The Monetisation Programme is Snapchat's built-in way to share ad revenue with creators. It is invitation only. Snapchat reviews creators and emails the address on the account when it decides to invite someone. You cannot apply for an invitation, and no third party can get you one. Snapchat charges no fee for an invitation, and no third party controls eligibility. Anyone who offers to sell you an invitation is running a scam.

The current invitation criteria, as listed on Snapchat's own help page in November 2026, are in the table below. Older guides quote different figures, such as 10 million Snap views or 25 million monthly views. Those figures are outdated. Use the figures below.

Requirement Current rule, as of November 2026
Followers At least 50,000 followers
View time At least 15,000 hours of view time over the last 28 days, with at least 3,000 of those hours from Spotlight
Age 18 or older
Status Snap Star status
Content Original, advertiser-friendly content
Country Residence in an eligible country
Rules Compliance with the Monetisation Terms and Creator Monetisation Policy

Two more rules matter. From 7 May 2026, creators must maintain at least 100 hours of Total Spotlight View Time over 28 days to qualify for maximum Creator Rewards. And a Spotlight video must be at least 30 seconds long to be eligible to earn revenue. Short clips under 30 seconds do not qualify, however many views they get. Once you are fully onboarded for payouts, you can cash out a minimum of 100 US dollars daily. Snapchat explains all of this on its official page, About Snapchat's Monetisation Programme.

What Snapchat does not pay for

Snapchat does not pay a fixed amount per view or per 1,000 views. There is no published rate, so any guide that quotes a rate per 1,000 views is guessing. Two creators with similar views can earn different amounts, because their audiences, their content, and the ads shown are different. Snapchat does not pay for Spotlight views alone. Views only count when they are part of the programme, on eligible videos, and after an invitation. The old Spotlight Rewards Programme, which paid monthly cash prizes to top performers, ended on 31 January 2025. Guides that still describe it as current are out of date.

Way 1: Spotlight and Public Stories done well

Spotlight is Snapchat's short video feed, where anyone can watch public videos without following you. Public Stories are the longer, day-long collections of Snaps that your followers see. Both can earn through the programme, but only if the content holds attention.

Start with a strong first two seconds. Show the result, the problem, or the surprise first, then explain it. A video that starts with a slow introduction loses viewers before the useful part arrives. Use captions, because many people watch without sound. Keep the idea simple: one tip, one demo, or one story per video. Post regularly, and check which videos hold viewers the longest. Watch time matters more than views, because the programme thresholds are measured in hours, not in views alone.

Way 2: Brand deals and sponsored content

Brand deals are often the highest paying route for Snapchat creators, and they are open to smaller creators than the programme is. A brand pays you to show its product in a Story or a Spotlight video, or to create a sponsored Lens.

Turn on the Brand Partnerships toggle for paid posts, so the post is marked as a partnership. Label the post in plain words too, such as "Paid partnership with [brand name]." The Federal Trade Commission explains this rule in its Endorsement Guides FAQ: paid posts must be labelled clearly, and any material connection must be disclosed. The Guides were revised in 2023.

To find brands, start with products you already use and like. Send a short message that says who your audience is, what you can make, and what it would cost. A media kit with your audience size, your best Stories, and your rates helps, but a clear message is enough to start. For the full method, read how to get brand deals as a small creator and how to make money as a UGC creator.

Way 3: Affiliate marketing

Affiliate marketing means you share a link to a product, and you earn a commission when someone buys through it. An affiliate commission is a small percentage of the sale, paid by the seller to you. On Snapchat, links work through Stories and your bio, where followers can tap through to a product page.

This route suits creators who review products, share tutorials, or show what they use every day. A short demo in a Story, with a link to the product, is more honest and more effective than a bare link with no context. Only promote products you have used yourself, or can check properly. A product you have never tried can damage the trust that makes your audience buy. Disclose the link in plain words, such as "This is an affiliate link, so I may earn a small commission if you buy." Keep links rare, so your Stories stay useful. For the full method, read how to start affiliate marketing.

Way 4: Selling your own products or services

Selling your own product gives you the most control, because you set the price and keep the sale. The product can be physical, such as a print on demand item, or digital, such as a template, a guide, or a preset pack.

Snapchat suits products that are visual and quick to explain. A short Story can show the product, show it in use, and link to the shop in a few seconds.

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine a creator with 3,000 followers who shares a link to her own 25 dollar template pack. In one month, 150 people tap the template link, and 2 percent of them buy. That is 150 times 0.02, which is 3 sales, and 3 times 25 dollars, which is 75 dollars for the month. The example is small on purpose. Before you build a product, read how to create your first digital product.

Way 5: AR lenses

An AR lens is an augmented reality filter that changes what the camera sees, such as adding an effect, a mask, or a 3D object. Snapchat pays some lens creators through Lens+ Payouts, and brands also pay creators to build lenses for them. This route suits creators who enjoy design and 3D work, or who can learn it. A simple lens that fits a trend, a season, or a local event can reach many people quickly. A brand lens is a longer job: you build a lens that shows the brand's product in a fun way, and the brand pays a fee for the work.

Way 6: Creator Subscriptions and premium content

Creator Subscriptions let followers pay a monthly fee for extra content from a creator. Snapchat introduced this feature in 2026, and it is still rolling out gradually, so it is not available to every creator yet. If it is available to you, it can give you steady monthly income from your most loyal followers. Premium content means extra Stories, behind the scenes posts, or early access that only paying followers see. Keep the free content useful, so new followers still have a reason to follow you. If everything good is behind a paywall, growth will slow.

Way 7: Using Snapchat to grow a business

Snapchat can also bring customers to a business you already run. A local cafe can post its daily menu in a Story. A tutor can answer one question a day and link to a booking page. A shop can show new stock and send followers to its website. This route works best when the business is visual and local, because Snapchat's audience is young and its Map and Discover features suit local discovery. Track the result in the same way you track any marketing: count the bookings, the orders, or the messages that came from Snapchat, not only the views.

How much can you realistically earn

There is no reliable average for Snapchat earnings, because the platform publishes no rate per view, and most creators do not share their income. Two creators with similar views can earn different amounts, because their audiences, their content, and the ads shown are different. What you can say is this. Programme income depends on watch time, not views alone, and it varies by month. Brand deals usually pay more than programme income for the same audience, because a brand pays for access to your specific followers. Affiliate links and products pay the least at first, but they grow with trust, and they are open to creators of any size.

Scams to avoid

The most common Snapchat scam is a paid invitation to the Monetisation Programme. Snapchat charges no fee for an invitation, and no third party controls eligibility. Anyone who asks you to pay for an invitation, or who promises a guaranteed payout, is trying to take your money. Other scams include fake brand deals that ask you to pay for shipping or for a sample first, and fake agents who offer a large payment if you share your login details. A real brand deal comes with a clear brief, a named brand, and a contract. Never share your password, and never pay to start a brand deal.

What it costs and what to expect

Snapchat itself is free to use, and joining the programme costs nothing. The costs come from the tools around your route: a phone with a good camera, a ring light, editing apps, and, for products, a shop page that may take a small fee per sale. Most creators can start with the phone they already own. Expect a slow start. Reaching 50,000 followers and 15,000 hours of view time takes most creators many months of daily posting, and an invitation is not guaranteed even then. The outside routes can pay sooner, but they still need weeks of useful posts.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting old thresholds from outdated guides, and planning around figures Snapchat no longer uses.
  • Posting videos under 30 seconds and expecting them to earn through the programme.
  • Chasing views instead of watch time, and making videos people skip after two seconds.
  • Putting an affiliate link in every Story, so followers stop watching.
  • Promoting products you have never used, and losing the trust that makes people buy.
  • Paying for an invitation, a guaranteed payout, or a shortcut to eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

Can you make money on Snapchat?

Yes. Creators earn through the Monetisation Programme, which shares ad revenue with invited creators, and through brand deals, affiliate links, products, and subscriptions outside the programme. Snapchat pays no fixed rate per view.

How many followers do you need to get paid on Snapchat?

The Monetisation Programme requires at least 50,000 followers, plus 15,000 hours of view time over 28 days, with at least 3,000 of those hours from Spotlight, as of November 2026. You also need to be 18 or older, have Snap Star status, and live in an eligible country. Small creators can still earn through brand deals and affiliate links.

How much does Snapchat pay per 1,000 views?

Snapchat publishes no rate per 1,000 views, so there is no reliable figure to quote. Earnings depend on watch time, the audience, and the ads shown. Any guide that gives a fixed rate per 1,000 views is guessing.

How do I get monetized on Snapchat?

You cannot apply for the Monetisation Programme. Snapchat reviews creators and invites those who meet the criteria, including 50,000 followers and 15,000 hours of view time in 28 days. While you wait, build public Stories and Spotlight videos, and earn through brand deals and affiliate links.

Does Snapchat pay for Spotlight views?

Not on their own. Spotlight views only earn when the video is at least 30 seconds long, the creator is in the programme, and the video meets the programme rules. The old Spotlight Rewards Programme ended on 31 January 2025.

Can small creators make money on Snapchat?

Yes. Small creators earn through brand deals with local or niche brands, affiliate links to products they use, and sales of their own products or services. These routes need trust, not a large following.

Conclusion: start outside the programme, aim for it later

Snapchat's Monetisation Programme is real, but it is invite only, and its thresholds are high: 50,000 followers and 15,000 hours of view time in 28 days, with at least 3,000 hours from Spotlight. Do not wait for an invitation to start earning.
