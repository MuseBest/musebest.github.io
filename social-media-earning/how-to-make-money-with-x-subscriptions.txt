2d:T4e61,<h1>How to Make Money with X Subscriptions in 2026</h1>
<p>Learning how to make money with X Subscriptions starts with a hard truth. Most accounts will not qualify for a long time, if ever. X sets a high bar: 2,000 verified followers and 5 million organic impressions in three months, plus an active X Premium subscription. That bar is the reason most guides about X monetisation are misleading. They describe Subscriptions as if any account can turn them on, then show example maths for an audience you do not have yet.</p>

<h2 id="what-x-subscriptions-are">What X Subscriptions are</h2>
<p>X Subscriptions, once called Super Follows, let your followers pay a monthly fee for extra content. Subscribers get subscriber-only posts, subscriber-only replies, direct messages with you, a badge beside their name, and access to subscriber-only Spaces, which are live audio rooms. You set the monthly price from the price points X offers, and subscribers are billed each month until they cancel.</p>
<p>Think of it as a membership, not a tip jar. A tip jar asks for support with no promise attached. A subscription promises something concrete every week, and subscribers will cancel if you do not deliver it. That promise is the product.</p>

It pays from how often Premium users see your original posts. Subscriptions pay from how many followers choose to pay you each month. You can be in both at once, but Subscriptions has the higher bar, so most accounts reach Rewards first.</p>

<p>Payouts go through Stripe, with a $50 minimum balance before a payout is sent. Payments arrive about 60 days after the end of the month the money came in, which is slower than most creators expect. Plan your cash flow around that delay.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-build-the-audience-that-can-pay">Step 1: Build the audience that can pay</h2>
<p>Subscriptions are not a growth tool. They are a way to earn from an audience you already have. The work that qualifies you is posting useful, original content in one niche, every day, for months.</p>
<p>Pick one lane and stay in it. A finance creator who posts investing tips, budgeting help and market analysis builds an audience that might pay for deeper analysis. A generalist who posts about politics one day, sport the next and crypto the day after builds an audience that will not pay for any of it. Focus creates loyalty, and loyalty is what Subscriptions sell.</p>
<p>Post in formats that invite replies, because replies drive impressions. Reaction-driven posts, short insight threads and honest analysis hold attention longer than a bare link or a reposted image. The same content that builds your impression count also builds your case for Subscriptions. Join conversations in your niche, reply thoughtfully to larger accounts, and answer your followers' questions in public.</p>
<p>A realistic conversion rate is 1 to 3% of your engaged followers becoming subscribers. That means 10,000 followers may bring 100 to 300 subscribers. An account with 50,000 inactive followers will convert worse than an account with 5,000 loyal ones. Count engaged followers, not total followers.</p>

<h2 id="step-2-get-x-premium-and-check-creator-studio">Step 2: Get X Premium and check Creator Studio</h2>
<p>You cannot monetise on X without a qualifying X Premium plan. Premium Basic does not qualify. Premium is the entry plan, and Premium+ and Premium Business also qualify. The cost is a monthly fee, and annual billing is cheaper. Treat it as a business expense for at least three to six months while you build the audience that will pay it back.</p>
<p>Once you have Premium, check your own status instead of guessing. Open X, go to Creator Studio, and select Subscriptions. X shows your eligibility there, including your verified follower count and your impression total. If you are far from 2,000 verified followers, the work is growth, not setup.</p>
<p>Set up two-factor authentication and a verified email at the same time, because X requires them for payouts. Add your bank details through Stripe when X asks for them.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-set-the-price-and-write-the-promise">Step 3: Set the price and write the promise</h2>
<p>Price from the points X shows you in Creator Studio, and start at the low end. The commonly quoted range is $2.99 to $9.99 a month, and most new creators start at $2.99 or $4.99. A low price is easy to try, and a subscriber who stays for a year at $3 is worth more than one who cancels after one month at $10.</p>
<p>Write the promise before you set the price, because the promise decides what the price can be. A promise must name something concrete that subscribers get and the public does not. Examples include "One detailed market breakdown every week" or "A full essay every week that never appears on the public feed". A vague promise such as "more content" will not convert anyone.</p>
<p>Keep the promise to things you can deliver every week for a year. A weekly post is a promise. A daily post is a promise that will break in your first busy month. Choose a schedule you can keep when you are tired, travelling or ill, because consistency is what subscribers pay for.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-plan-subscriber-only-content">Step 4: Plan subscriber-only content</h2>
<p>Subscribers pay for content they cannot get anywhere else, so the paid content must be clearly different from your public posts. If your public posts already give everything away, there is no reason to pay.</p>
<p>Depth means longer, more detailed work: a full analysis instead of a summary, or a complete template instead of a tip. Access means direct contact with you: a subscriber-only Space each month, or replies to subscriber posts that the public does not get. Timing means early access: subscribers see your best work days before the public feed does.</p>
<p>A simple weekly plan looks like this. On Monday, post one exclusive piece, such as a breakdown, a template or an essay. On Wednesday, post a short update that only subscribers see. On Friday, run a poll that shapes next week's content, or answer subscriber questions. Once a month, hold a subscriber-only Space. That rhythm is easy to keep.</p>
<p>Do not post the same type of content behind the paywall that you post for free. Do not promise three posts a week and deliver one. Missing promised perks is the fastest way to lose subscribers. If you need a break, tell subscribers in advance. Honesty keeps more subscribers than silence.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-launch-and-keep-subscribers">Step 5: Launch and keep subscribers</h2>
<p>Announce the launch to your public followers, but do not launch to an empty promise. Publish your first two or three subscriber-only posts before you announce, so the first subscribers find something waiting for them. Write a short launch post that names the promise, the price and who it is for.</p>
<p>Give early subscribers a reason to stay past the first month. A founding member badge, a welcome post, or a small bonus such as a template pack costs you little and makes the first month feel special. Thank new subscribers publicly when they join, with their permission.</p>
<p>Watch churn, which is the share of subscribers who cancel each month. If churn is high in the first month, the promise is wrong or the content is late. Ask cancelling subscribers why they left, and fix the most common answer first. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-turn-small-social-media-following-into-income" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to turn a small social media following into income</a> covers the same retention thinking for small accounts.</p>

<h2 id="what-to-do-while-you-are-under-the-bar">What to do while you are under the bar</h2>
<p>Most readers are under the bar, and that is normal. Earn from the audience you have now, using routes with no threshold. <strong>Tips.</strong> Turn on the Tips button, so fans can send one-off payments through Cash App, Venmo, Strike or a Bitcoin wallet. X takes no cut. <strong>Affiliate links.</strong> Post a link for a product you genuinely use, and earn a commission on each sale. Disclose it clearly. <strong>Digital products.</strong> Sell a template, a guide or a course to your followers. A sale needs only a few buyers. <strong>Email list.</strong> Offer a free template or checklist in exchange for an email address, and build a list you own. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-make-money-with-facebook-groups" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to make money with Facebook Groups</a> covers community income on another platform.</p>
<h2 id="worked-example-what-a-qualifying-account-could-earn">Worked example: what a qualifying account could earn</h2>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.</p>
<p>Imagine a finance creator with 20,000 followers, of whom 3,000 are verified, and 6 million organic impressions in the last three months. The account qualifies for Subscriptions. The creator charges $4.99 a month. About 2% of engaged followers subscribe, which is 200 subscribers. The gross is 200 multiplied by 4.99, which is $998 a month. X's share is up to 97% to the creator after platform fees, so the creator keeps about $968 a month before app store fees and tax.</p>
<p>The creator also earns about $150 a month from Original Content Rewards, because the same posts that build impressions also earn ad revenue. The total is about $1,118 a month. The creator spends about 10 hours a week on public posts and subscriber content, plus $8 a month for X Premium. That is a realistic shape for a qualifying account. An account with 2,000 verified followers and no engaged core will earn far less, and most accounts will take a year or more to reach the bar at all.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>X Premium is the first cost, at a monthly fee that varies by region and plan, with annual billing cheaper. Budget for it for at least three to six months before any income arrives. Your time is the bigger cost: plan for 8 to 12 hours a week on public posts, subscriber content and replies once the subscription is live.</p>
<p>A realistic timeline is 12 to 24 months of daily posting to reach 2,000 verified followers and 5 million impressions, if you reach them at all. Verified followers are the harder half of the bar, because they are followers who pay for Premium themselves. Treat Subscriptions as a long-term goal, and earn from Tips, affiliate links, products and your email list while you build toward it.</p>
<p>Tax rules apply to X income too, and they differ by country. Keep records of your Premium cost and your Stripe payouts from the first month.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Treating Subscriptions as a quick payout, when the bar takes a year or more to reach.</li>
<li>Posting the same content behind the paywall as on the public feed, so nobody has a reason to pay.</li>
<li>Promising daily subscriber content and missing it within a month, which drives cancellations.</li>
<li>Trusting one guide's price points or revenue share, when sources disagree and X shows the real figures in Creator Studio.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Paid verification sellers are the most common trap. An account offers to sell you a checkmark, verified followers or a "monetisation unlock" for a fee. X verification comes only through X's own subscription. Paying one risks your money and your account.</p>
<p>Engagement pods are another trap. A group agrees to like and repost each other's posts to inflate impressions. X's rules treat coordinated inauthentic activity as a breach, and those impressions do not convert to subscribers.</p>
<p>Fake brand deals also target X creators. A brand account offers a generous sponsored post, then asks you to pay for the product first, or to buy a starter pack. A real sponsor pays you or sends the product free. Never pay to join a sponsorship, and never share your X login with anyone who claims they can grow your account.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-do-x-subscriptions-work">How do X Subscriptions work?</h3>
<p>X Subscriptions let followers pay a monthly fee for extra content, such as subscriber-only posts, replies, direct messages, a badge and subscriber-only Spaces. You set the price from the points X offers, and subscribers are billed monthly until they cancel. You keep up to 97% of the revenue after platform fees until $100,000 in lifetime earnings across X monetisation methods, then up to 90%.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-verified-followers-do-you-need-for-x-subscriptions">How many verified followers do you need for X Subscriptions?</h3>
<p>At least 2,000 verified followers, plus at least 5 million organic impressions in the previous three months, an active X Premium subscription, and activity in the past 30 days. You must also be 18 or older and live in a supported country. Meeting the numbers does not guarantee approval.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-x-take-from-subscriptions">How much does X take from subscriptions?</h3>
<p>X's published share is up to 97% to the creator after platform fees, until $100,000 in lifetime earnings across X monetisation methods, then up to 90%. Some secondary sources quote the threshold as $50,000, so check the current terms in Creator Studio. App store fees apply on top for subscribers who pay inside an app.</p>
<h3 id="can-small-accounts-earn-with-x-subscriptions">Can small accounts earn with X Subscriptions?</h3>
<p>Not through Subscriptions themselves, because the bar is 2,000 verified followers and 5 million impressions. Small accounts earn sooner from Tips, affiliate links, digital products, brand deals and an email list, which have no threshold. Build the audience with those routes, and treat Subscriptions as a later goal.</p>

Build one niche, post original content every day, check your real numbers in Creator Studio, and write a clear promise before you set a price.
