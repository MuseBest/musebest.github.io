How to Earn Money with Facebook Reels in 2026

Yes, Facebook pays for Reels in 2026, and the answer to how to earn money with Facebook Reels starts with one programme. Facebook Content Monetization pays creators for the performance of their eligible public Reels, photos, Stories, and text posts. It is invite-only, it publishes no follower minimum, and it pays on performance, not on a fixed rate per view. This guide explains the six income routes from Reels, the real requirements, how to switch monetisation on, and what to do if you are not eligible yet.

What changed: the old programmes are gone

Many guides still describe the old system, so get this clear first. The Reels Play bonus, which paid creators for Reels views, ended in 2023. Meta then merged in-stream ads, Ads on Reels, and the performance bonus into one programme, Facebook Content Monetization, in a beta that started on 2 October 2024. Those three old programmes ended on 31 August 2025. Any guide that tells you to apply for in-stream ads, Ads on Reels, or the performance bonus as separate programmes is out of date. Do not follow its thresholds either. The old in-stream ads bar of 5,000 followers and 60,000 view minutes in 60 days belonged to a programme that no longer exists, and it is not a Content Monetization threshold. One guide prints a minutes figure far larger than the other two, which looks like a typo. Do not use a minutes figure from a guide; check Meta's current pages instead.

Meta also changed what your dashboard shows. Since 18 March 2026, the dashboard shows a Qualified View, an Earnings Rate per 1,000 qualified views, and a breakdown of views that did not qualify, so you can see why some views earn and others do not. In March 2026 Meta said it paid creators nearly 3 billion US dollars in 2025, up 35 percent on the year before, with 60 percent of that going to Reels. That is a platform total, not a promise for any one creator.

The six income routes from Reels

Route What it pays for Who can use it
Content Monetization Performance of eligible public Reels and other content Invited creators only
Stars Viewers sending Stars on your Reels and Lives Creators who meet the Stars bar
Subscriptions Monthly paying supporters Pages that meet the subscriptions bar
Brand partnerships A brand paying you to feature its product Any creator, with no follower minimum
Affiliate links and own products Commission or sales from links and products in your Reels Anyone, from day one
Creator Fast Track Guaranteed monthly pay for three months Established creators from other platforms, by invitation

You do not need all six. Most creators start with affiliate links and products, add Stars when they qualify, and wait for a Content Monetization invitation while they build an audience.

Requirements checklist

  • You must be 18 or older.
  • You must use a Facebook Page or a profile in professional mode. A normal personal profile cannot earn.
  • You must live in an eligible country, and the bank or payout details you connect must be in an eligible country too.
  • Your account must have an established presence of at least 30 days.
  • You must follow the Partner Monetization Policies and the Content Monetization Policies.
  • Your content must be original, in a supported language, and follow the Community Standards.
  • Eligible Reels must be at least 10 seconds long. Eligible Stories must be at least 5 seconds long. Content with under 5 seconds of watch time does not qualify.

Check your own dashboard for your status. Meta publishes no follower minimum and no view threshold for Content Monetization, because it is invite-only. If a guide gives you a follower number for it, that number belongs to an old programme.

Step by step: check eligibility and switch monetisation on

  1. Switch your profile to professional mode, or create a Facebook Page for your content. Professional mode makes your profile public by default, so check your old posts before you switch.
  2. Post original Reels for at least 30 days, so your account has an established presence.
  3. Open the Professional dashboard on your Page or profile, then open the Monetization tab. This is where Meta lists the programmes you can use.
  4. Look for Facebook Content Monetization. If it shows as available, follow the steps to enrol. If it shows an interest form, fill it in. Meta says the form is not an application and does not guarantee an invitation.
  5. Switch on Stars from the same Monetization tab when it shows as available. The Stars bar is lower, so it often appears first.
  6. Set up your payout account. Add your bank details or PayPal, and complete the tax form: a W-9 if you are a US creator, or a W-8BEN if you are outside the US. You cannot be paid until this is done, so do it early, not when the first payout is due. That is the full setup for learning how to earn money with Facebook Reels.

How Content Monetization pays, and what a qualified view is

Content Monetization pays on the performance of your eligible public content. Meta publishes no rate card and no fixed amount per view. Your dashboard shows your own Earnings Rate, which is your approximate earnings per 1,000 qualified views. That rate moves with your content type, your audience country, and engagement, so two creators with the same views can earn different amounts.

A qualified view is a view on your content that may be eligible to earn money. Meta counts only views that meet its rules: the viewer must watch long enough, the view must come from an eligible country, the content must be original, and the content must not break a policy. Views that do not qualify are shown separately in your dashboard, so you can see the gap between your total views and your earning views.

What does not count: views from ineligible countries, views on re-uploaded or unoriginal content, views on content that breaks a policy, and views shorter than 5 seconds. Cross-posted Reels from Instagram are a grey area. Facebook pays for native posts, and minutes from cross-posted content were excluded from the old eligibility counts. Post natively on Facebook when you want the views to count.

Do not trust any guide that quotes a fixed pay per 1,000 views as a Meta rate. The most quoted figure in 2026, about 0.10 US dollars per 1,000 qualifying views, comes from a third-party report, not from Meta. Treat it as one report, not as a rate you can plan on.

Stars: the lowest bar

Stars are the easiest monetisation to switch on. Viewers buy Stars and send them on your Reels and Lives. Each Star is worth 0.01 US dollars to you. To qualify, you need 500 followers held for at least 30 consecutive days, you must be 18 or older, you must be in a Stars-supported country, and you must follow the Partner Monetization Policies.

Ask for Stars in a natural way. Thank people who send them by name in your next video. Put a short note in your caption, such as "Stars help me keep making these videos". Do not beg, and do not promise something you cannot deliver in return for Stars. A few Stars a day from loyal viewers is a good start. It grows with your audience, not with how hard you ask.

Subscriptions and brand partnerships

Fan subscriptions let your most loyal followers pay a monthly fee for extra content, badges, and access. The bar is higher than Stars: about 10,000 followers, or 250 or more returning viewers, plus 50,000 engagements or 180,000 watch minutes in 60 days, and the feature is for Pages only. Do not open subscriptions until you can deliver extra content every week. Subscribers leave fast when the extras stop.

Brand partnerships have no follower minimum. A small creator with a loyal niche audience can get a brand deal. Agree the fee, the number of Reels, the wording, and the deadline in writing before you film. Use the paid partnership label on every sponsored Reel, and follow the disclosure rules where you live. For the full process, read how to get brand deals as a small creator.

Affiliate links and your own products: the route that needs no eligibility

This is the route to start with, because it needs no invitation and no follower minimum. Put an affiliate link in your caption or in a pinned comment, and earn a commission when a viewer buys. Facebook also runs Affiliate Partnerships with shops such as Amazon in the US and Shopee in Southeast Asia, which give you ready-made links. Commissions commonly run from 5 to 25 percent, depending on the product.

You can also sell your own products from a Reel: a digital product, a course, or a physical product through Facebook Shop. Link the product in the caption and show it in the video. This route works from day one, and it is the fallback for every creator whose country is not eligible for Content Monetization yet. Learn the product route in how to make money on Facebook.

Creator Fast Track

Creator Fast Track launched in March 2026. It is for established creators on Instagram, YouTube, or TikTok who have not posted a Facebook Reel in the past six months. It pays a guaranteed amount each month for three months, and it gives immediate access to Content Monetization.

The tiers, as reported from Meta's programme page in August 2026, are: 20,000 to 99,999 followers on the other platform pays 100 to 450 US dollars a month; 100,000 to 999,999 followers pays 1,000 US dollars a month; and 1,000,000 or more followers pays 3,000 US dollars a month. You must also have 30,000 video views on the other platform in 60 days, and you must post 15 eligible Reels over at least 10 days each month. The programme runs in the US, Canada, the UK, and Australia, and availability can change, so check the current list on Meta for Creators. It is the only Facebook Reels route with a published guaranteed amount.

What kind of Reels earn most

The niches that earn best on Facebook tend to be news commentary, do-it-yourself, cooking, personal finance, motivation, history explainers, and animals. Facebook's audience is older than TikTok's, and finance, home, health, and business topics tend to pay better, because advertisers in those areas spend more. Pick a niche you can sustain, not the one that sounds richest.

Hook the viewer in the first two seconds. Start with the result, the question, or the surprise, not with an introduction. Say "Here is the mistake that costs you 50 dollars a month" instead of "Hi, welcome to my page". Use one clear format and repeat it: a weekly tip, a before and after, a myth and the truth, or a 30-second how-to. Repeat formats are easier to film and easier for viewers to recognise.

Faceless Reels can be monetised, as long as the content is original and follows the rules. Use your own voice, your own footage, or your own animation. Do not re-upload someone else's clips, and do not use a TikTok watermark, because Meta treats re-uploads and watermarked clips as unoriginal content. If you use AI to make a Reel, label it as AI-made where Meta asks you to.

A realistic first month: the 30-day plan

This 30-day plan is a steady way to learn how to earn money with Facebook Reels without chasing quick wins.

Week What to post What good looks like
1 Four Reels in your niche, each 15 to 30 seconds, one format repeated You learn which hook holds viewers past two seconds
2 Four Reels, plus one LIVE of 15 minutes answering questions Your first Stars, or your first affiliate clicks
3 Four Reels, plus one Reel that links to a product or affiliate offer You can see which topic brings clicks, not just views
4 Four Reels, review the dashboard, and repeat the best two formats A clear picture of your qualified views and your Earnings Rate

Post three or four Reels a week, not twenty. Volume helps, but only when each Reel is worth watching. A new Page should expect small numbers in month one: a few hundred views per Reel, a few Stars if you are eligible, and maybe a first affiliate sale. That is a normal start, not a failure. Earnings grow when viewers return, and returning viewers take weeks to build.

What it costs and what to expect

Posting Reels costs nothing but your time and a phone. A tripod and a ring light help, and together they cost less than a cheap dinner out. You do not need a camera, a studio, or editing software you pay for.

Payouts are monthly, between the 17th and the 22nd of the month, and the minimum depends on the feature: 25 US dollars or 100 US dollars. Check your own dashboard, because the threshold is shown there. You need a payout account and a tax form on file before Meta will pay you.

Expect slow and uneven income. Content Monetization pays on performance, and performance varies by week. Some creators earn a few dollars a month from Reels for a long time. That is honest, and it is why the affiliate and product routes matter: they pay from day one, while you wait for an invitation.

Mistakes that lose monetisation

  • Reposting other people's videos, or your own TikTok videos with the watermark still on them.
  • Posting low-effort slideshows or looped clips with no original value.
  • Using commercial music you have no licence for, which can bring a copyright claim.
  • Using engagement bait, such as "comment YES to win", which Meta penalises.
  • Posting misinformation, which can limit your whole Page.
  • Forgetting to label AI-made content where Meta asks for it.
  • Buying views or followers, which Meta detects and which can end your monetisation.

If you are not eligible yet

First, check the reason in the Professional dashboard. Meta shows whether the block is a policy issue, a country issue, or an account issue. Fix what it names. Common fixes are removing a video that broke a rule, waiting out a 30-day account age requirement, or switching a personal profile to professional mode.

If the reason is that Content Monetization is invite-only and you have no invitation, do not wait doing nothing. Switch on Stars if you qualify, sell through affiliate links and your own products, and keep posting original Reels three or four times a week. Invitations go to creators who post consistently and follow the rules, so your job is to be visible and clean when the next round of invitations goes out. If your country is not eligible at all, the affiliate and product routes are your main income from Reels until that changes. Frequently asked questions

Does Facebook pay for Reels in 2026?

Yes. Facebook pays for Reels mainly through Facebook Content Monetization, which is invite-only and pays on the performance of eligible public content. Stars, subscriptions, brand deals, and affiliate links are other ways to earn from the same Reels.

How many followers do you need to monetize Facebook Reels?

Meta publishes no follower minimum for Content Monetization, because it is invite-only. Stars needs 500 followers held for 30 days. Affiliate links and your own products need no followers at all.

How much does Facebook pay per 1,000 Reels views?

Meta publishes no fixed rate. Your dashboard shows your own Earnings Rate per 1,000 qualified views, and it moves with your content, your audience, and engagement. Do not plan on any fixed figure from a guide.

Can you monetize Facebook Reels with a personal profile?

No. You need a Facebook Page or a profile in professional mode. A normal personal profile cannot use monetisation features.

Is the Facebook Reels bonus still available?

No. The Reels Play bonus ended in 2023, and the performance bonus ended on 31 August 2025 when the old programmes merged into Content Monetization. Any guide that still offers a Reels bonus is out of date.

Can faceless Reels be monetized?

Yes, if the content is original and follows the rules. Use your own voice, footage, or animation, and label AI-made content where Meta asks you to. Re-uploads and watermarked clips do not qualify.

Conclusion

That is how to earn money with Facebook Reels in 2026: post original vertical Reels three or four times a week, switch on Stars when you qualify, set up your payout account and tax form early, and earn from affiliate links and your own products while you wait for a Content Monetization invitation. Hook viewers in the first two seconds, keep your content original, and check your dashboard for your real Earnings Rate instead of trusting a fixed figure from a guide.
