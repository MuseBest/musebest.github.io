<p>Learning how to use Instagram Reels to earn money starts with clearing up a common belief. Instagram does not pay you a flat rate for Reels views. A Reel with 100,000 views does not come with a set payment, and there is no published rate per view. Many guides quote a rate per view anyway, and those figures are wrong.</p>
<p>What Instagram does offer is a set of tools that pay in different ways: gifts from viewers, monthly subscriptions, an invite-only bonus, and brand deals through the Creator Marketplace. Reels work best as the front door to those tools, and to something you own, such as an email list or a shop.</p>

<h3 id="does-instagram-pay-for-reels-views">Does Instagram pay for Reels views?</h3>
<p>No, not as a rule. Instagram does not pay a flat rate per view, and it publishes no rate per 1,000 views. The idea that a creator earns a set amount per view comes from guides that invent the figure. One widely quoted guide claims creators earn 0.01 to 0.05 US dollars per view through a Reels bonus, which would mean 10 to 50 dollars per 1,000 views. That figure is unsourced, and it conflicts with Instagram's own pages, which publish no per-view rate. Do not plan your income around it.</p>
<p>What Instagram does pay for is specific actions: a viewer sending a gift, a follower paying for a subscription, a brand paying for a sponsored Reel, or a buyer clicking through to a product. Views still matter, because more views mean more chances for those actions, but views alone do not pay.</p>

<h3 id="tool-1-gifts-on-reels">Tool 1: Gifts on Reels</h3>
<p>Gifts are small payments that viewers send on your Reels, using Stars. A Star is Instagram's virtual gift token. The reported rate is 0.01 US dollars per Star, which means a viewer must send 100 Stars for you to earn 1 US dollar.</p>
<p>The reported threshold is 500 followers and a professional account. Gifts are reported to be available in 49 countries. If your country is not on the list, the tool will not appear in your Professional dashboard, and you cannot use it. Check your own dashboard, because the list can change. Ask for gifts in a natural way, such as thanking viewers who send them, and do not beg for them in every Reel.</p>

<h3 id="tool-2-subscriptions">Tool 2: Subscriptions</h3>
<p>Subscriptions let followers pay a monthly fee for extra content, such as subscriber-only Reels, Stories, and live videos. You set the monthly price, and you decide what subscribers get.</p>
<p>Subscriptions suit creators with a loyal audience that wants more. A teacher who posts short lessons can offer longer tutorials to subscribers. A fitness creator can offer a weekly workout plan. The extra content must be worth the fee, or subscribers will cancel after the first month. Keep the free Reels useful, so new followers still have a reason to follow you. For the growth side of that work, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-grow-instagram-followers" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to grow Instagram followers</a>.</p>
<h3 id="tool-3-the-bonus-for-reels">Tool 3: The bonus for Reels</h3>
<p>The bonus for Reels is an invite-only programme. Instagram invites some creators to earn a bonus based on the performance of their Reels. It is not a general programme you can apply to join, and it is not available everywhere.</p>
<p>The reported bar is 5 million views in the previous 3 consecutive calendar months, and the programme is reported to be limited to the United States and South Korea. Instagram's own pages also disagree with each other on the bonus regions, so do not treat any single rule as final. Your Professional dashboard is the final word: if the bonus is available to you, it will appear there. Do not plan your income around the bonus. Most creators will never be invited.</p>
<h3 id="tool-4-brand-deals">Tool 4: Brand deals</h3>
<p>Brand deals are usually the highest paying route for Reels creators. A brand pays you to make a Reel about its product, and you label it with the paid partnership label.</p>
<p>The Creator Marketplace is Instagram's own tool for matching brands with creators. The reported threshold is 1,000 followers and a professional account. Brands can find you there, or you can pitch them directly. A media kit helps: a short page with your audience size, your best Reels, and your rates.</p>
<p>Partnership ads are a related tool. A brand can pay to boost your Reel as an ad, so it reaches people beyond your followers. The brand pays for the ad spend, and you are paid for the partnership.</p>
<p>How much should you charge for a sponsored Reel? There is no fixed rate, because rates differ by niche, audience size, and the brand. A simple starting point is to charge for the work and the audience together: a fee for making the Reel, plus a fee for the reach it gives the brand. Quote a price, and be ready to explain what the brand gets: one Reel, the caption, the length of time it stays up, and whether the brand can reuse it.</p>
<p>Label every paid Reel clearly, with the paid partnership label and plain words in the caption, such as "Paid partnership with [brand name]." The Federal Trade Commission explains this rule in its <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Endorsement Guides FAQ</a>: paid posts must be labelled clearly, and any material connection must be disclosed. The Guides were revised in 2023.</p>

<h3 id="tool-5-affiliate-links-and-shopping">Tool 5: Affiliate links and shopping</h3>
<p>Affiliate marketing means you share a link to a product, and you earn a commission when someone buys through it. An affiliate commission is a small percentage of the sale, paid by the seller to you. Reels cannot carry clickable links inside the video, so the link goes in your bio, and the Reel sends viewers there.</p>
<p>Say in the Reel what the link is and where to find it, such as "The link is in my bio." Disclose the link in plain words, in the caption or in the Reel itself, such as "This is an affiliate link, so I may earn a small commission if you buy." Only promote products you have used yourself. Note that Instagram's own earn money page lists no affiliate programme of its own. For the full method, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/how-to-start-affiliate-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start affiliate marketing</a>.</p>
<h3 id="tool-6-your-own-products-and-services">Tool 6: Your own products and services</h3>
<p>Your own products give you the most control, because you set the price and keep the sale. A Reel can show the product in use, answer a question about it, or show a result, and then send viewers to your shop or booking page. Digital products, such as templates and guides, suit Reels well, because they are quick to explain in a short video.</p>
<p>You do not need a large following for this route. UGC deals, where a brand pays you to make a video it uses on its own channels, need no following at all, because the brand provides the audience. Services work the same way: a coach, a designer, or a tutor can use Reels to show their work and send viewers to a booking link.</p>
<h3 id="reels-as-a-funnel">Reels as a funnel</h3>
<p>A funnel is a simple path from a Reel to a sale: one Reel, one offer, one next step. The Reel gives one useful idea, and it ends with one clear action, such as visiting the link in your bio, joining your email list, or booking a call. One Reel should not try to sell three things at once, because viewers will do none of them.</p>
<p>An email list is the most durable end of the funnel, because it is yours even if your account is lost. A shop is the next best, because a buyer can act at once. A booking page suits services. Pick one end point for each Reel, and say it clearly. For the list itself, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-build-email-list-for-a-blog" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to build an email list for a blog</a>.</p>
<h3 id="a-worked-example-one-reel-a-week-aimed-at-one-offer">A worked example: one Reel a week aimed at one offer</h3>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine a creator who teaches simple meal planning. She posts one Reel a week, each one showing a single meal plan tip, and each one ends with a link in her bio to a 15 pound template pack.</p>
<p>In one month, her four Reels bring 400 people to the link in her bio. Of those, 2 percent buy the pack. That is 400 times 0.02, which is 8 sales, and 8 times 15 pounds, which is 120 pounds for the month. The example is small on purpose. The lesson is not the amount. It is that one Reel a week, aimed at one offer, is a plan you can repeat.</p>
<h3 id="the-originality-rules">The originality rules</h3>
<p>Instagram rewards original content and limits the reach of content that is copied or reused. From 30 April 2026, Instagram extended these rules to photos and carousels as well as Reels.</p>
<p>What counts as original is simple: a Reel you filmed yourself, with your own voice, your own editing, and your own idea. What does not count is a Reel you downloaded from another creator and reposted, a video with another person's watermark still on it, or a clip you copied with only a caption changed.</p>
<p>If your account is limited, check Account Status in the app. It will tell you whether your account has a problem and what to do about it. To become eligible again, remove the content that breaks the rules, post original work for a few weeks, and check the status again. Do not try to get around a limit with a second account, because that can break the platform rules and lead to a longer restriction. Google gives similar advice in its guide on <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content" rel="noopener" target="_blank">creating helpful, reliable, people-first content</a>: show first-hand expertise and real depth, because trust matters most. The same test applies to your Reels.</p>
<h3 id="setting-up-payouts">Setting up payouts</h3>
<p>Before you can earn through Instagram's tools, you need a payout account. This is the account Instagram uses to pay you, and you set it up in the Professional dashboard, under Monetization status.</p>
<p>The payout rules include a warning worth knowing. Without a payout account, you can earn up to 500 US dollars per tool, and 1,500 US dollars across three tools. If you add no payout account within 6 months, those earnings are forfeited, which means you lose them. Set up the payout account as soon as you can earn, not months later. <h3 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h3>
<p>Instagram itself is free to use, and a professional account costs nothing. The costs come from the tools around your route: a phone with a good camera, a tripod or ring light, editing apps, and, for products, a shop page that may take a small fee per sale. Expect a slow start. Gifts and subscriptions need a loyal audience, and that takes months to build. A realistic timeline is 3 to 6 months of steady Reels before you can judge whether they can bring regular income.</p>
<h3 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Chasing views instead of clicks, sign-ups, and sales, and making Reels that entertain but sell nothing.</li>
<li>Reposting other people's work, and losing reach under the originality rules.</li>
<li>Having no clear next step, so a viewer who likes the Reel has nowhere to go.</li>
<li>Using fake engagement, such as bought likes or comments, which Instagram can detect.</li>
<li>Falling for fake bonus invitations that ask for your username or password.</li>
<li>Quoting a per-view rate as if Instagram publishes one, and planning income around it.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h3>
<p>The most common Reels scam is a fake bonus invitation. A message claims you have been invited to the bonus programme, and asks for your username, your password, or a payment to join. A genuine invitation appears in your Professional dashboard, inside the app, and it never asks for your password. Never share your password, and never pay to start a brand deal.</p>
<h3 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>How do you earn money from Instagram Reels?</h4>
<p>You earn through gifts from viewers, monthly subscriptions, an invite-only bonus, brand deals, affiliate links, and your own products and services. Instagram pays no flat rate per view. Reels work best as the front door to one of these routes, with one clear next step in each Reel.</p>
<h4>How many followers do you need to get paid on Instagram?</h4>
<p>It depends on the tool. The reported thresholds, as of November 2026, are 500 followers for gifts, 1,000 for the Creator Marketplace, and 10,000 for subscriptions, all with a professional account. Brand deals and your own products have no fixed follower threshold.</p>
<h4>Does Instagram pay for Reels views?</h4>
<p>No, not as a rule. Instagram publishes no rate per view, and there is no reliable figure for what 1,000 views pays. Views matter because they bring clicks, sign-ups, and sales, but views alone do not earn money.</p>
<h4>Can I earn on Instagram with fewer than 500 followers?</h4>
<p>Yes, through routes that need no following. UGC deals pay you to make videos a brand uses on its own channels, and your own products or services can sell to a small audience that trusts you. Gifts and subscriptions need more followers.</p>
<h4>Is the Instagram bonus available in my country?</h4>
<p>The bonus for Reels is reported to be limited to the United States and South Korea, and it is invite only. Instagram's own pages disagree with each other on the bonus regions, so check your Professional dashboard, under Monetization status, to see whether it is available to you.</p>
<h4>How do I monetize Instagram Reels?</h4>
<p>Switch to a professional account, open the Professional dashboard, and check Monetization status to see which tools your account can use. Set up a payout account, pick one route to start with, and post one Reel a week aimed at one offer, with a clear next step.</p>
<h3 id="conclusion-make-reels-the-front-door-not-the-pay-cheque">Conclusion: make Reels the front door, not the pay cheque</h3>
<p>Instagram pays no rate per Reels view, so do not build your income on views. Use Reels as the front door: one Reel, one offer, one next step. Pick one tool to start with, set up your payout account early, and keep your content original.</p>
