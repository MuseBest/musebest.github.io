How to Make Money with Facebook Affiliate Marketing in 2026

To make money with Facebook affiliate marketing in 2026, forget the old method of dropping a raw link into a post and hoping for sales. Facebook's reach for that kind of post is low, and accounts that spam links get restricted. The method that works now is slower and more honest: build a small trusted audience in a niche, recommend products you have checked, and use Facebook's own tools, Groups, and a simple blog funnel to turn attention into commissions.

What is Facebook affiliate marketing?

Affiliate marketing means you recommend a product with a special tracking link, and you earn a commission when someone buys through it. An affiliate commission is a share of the sale paid to you by the seller. You do not handle stock, shipping, or customer service.

On Facebook, this happens in four places: a Page, a Group, a personal profile in Professional Mode, and paid ads. A Page is good for your brand and for ads, but organic Page reach is low. A Group is far better for organic reach, because members chose to join. Paid ads can scale a proven offer, but they cost money and break the rules fast if you use raw affiliate links as the ad destination.

Facebook itself does not run a general affiliate programme for creators. The money comes from outside programmes, such as Amazon Associates and affiliate networks, plus a newer native feature called Facebook Affiliate Partnerships, which is covered below.

How to make money with Facebook affiliate marketing

Three routes carry most Facebook affiliate income today.

Native Affiliate Partnerships. In March 2026, Meta launched Facebook Affiliate Partnerships, starting with Amazon in the United States and Shopee in Singapore, Vietnam, and Brazil. You connect your affiliate account and add a clickable product banner to a post or Reel. The banner has strict rules: one banner per post or Reel, the product must appear in the content and be available to buy, Reels must be longer than 10 seconds, viewers must be in the same country as you, and Groups and Live are not supported. This is the cleanest way to link a product, because the link is part of Facebook's own tool rather than a link pasted into a post.

Organic posts and Groups. You can put an Amazon link in an organic post, but Facebook reduces the distribution of posts with external links. That is why experienced affiliates put the link in the first comment and write the post as a useful story or review.

Paid ads with a landing page. Meta's advertising policies do not allow raw affiliate links as ad destinations. You must send ad traffic to a landing page or a bridge page that you control, and that page can then link to the affiliate offer. Amazon links can appear in organic posts, but boosting a post that contains an Amazon link risks a restriction. Some networks are stricter still: direct promotion of ClickBank links is not allowed in Facebook posts or ads, so check each programme's rules before you promote it.

Choose your niche and affiliate programs

Your niche decides everything: what you post, which programmes accept you, and how much each sale pays. Pick a niche where people already spend money and where you can write 50 useful posts without repeating yourself.| Niche | Example products | Why it works on Facebook | |---|---|---| | Home and garden | Tools, storage, garden gear | Visual, practical, strong Groups | | Health and fitness | Equipment, supplements, programmes | Large audience, clear problems | | Parenting and family | Baby gear, learning toys, meal kits | Active Groups, trusted recommendations | | Technology | Gadgets, software, accessories | High buyer intent, easy demos | | Home office and remote work | Desks, chairs, monitors, apps | Facebook audience is working age |

Once you pick a niche, join two or three programmes rather than twenty. Amazon Associates is the easiest starting point, because it covers millions of products and pays up to 10 percent in commission on qualifying purchases, with payment roughly 60 days after the end of the month in which it was earned. Add one specialist network, such as Awin or ShareASale, and one brand programme in your niche. Before you join any programme, check the commission rate, the cookie duration, and whether Facebook promotion is allowed.

Step 1: Set up your Facebook presence

Set up three things before you post your first affiliate link: a profile, a Page, and a Group.

  1. Switch your profile to Professional Mode. Open your profile settings and turn on Professional Mode. This gives you a public following, insights on your posts, and access to monetisation eligibility checks.
  2. Check your monetisation eligibility. Open the monetisation section in your settings and confirm your account meets the Partner Monetisation Policies and Community Standards.
  3. Create a Page for your niche. Give it a clear name that says what it covers, such as "Budget Home Office Ideas". Add a profile picture, a cover image, and an About section that says who the Page helps.
  4. Create a Group for your niche. A Group is where your best work happens. Name it for the problem it solves, write clear Group rules, and plan 50 to 100 useful posts before you invite anyone, so new members find value on day one. Our guide to how to make money with Facebook Groups explains the Group playbook in depth.
  5. Set up a simple blog or landing page. You do not need a big website, but you do need somewhere to send people that you control. Our guide to how to monetise a blog with affiliate marketing explains the same funnel in more detail.

Step 2: Add affiliate links the right way

How you place the link matters as much as which product you pick. Use the method that fits the format.

Native banner. If Affiliate Partnerships is available in your country, connect your Amazon or Shopee account in the post composer, add the product, and publish. The banner appears on the post or Reel. Remember the rules: one banner per post, the product must appear in the content, and Reels must run longer than 10 seconds.

Link in the first comment. For an ordinary post, write the post without any link, publish it, and then add your affiliate link as the first comment. Tell readers in the post itself where the link is. This avoids the distribution penalty that posts with external links suffer.

Landing page. For ads, and for products that need more explanation, send people to a page you control. The page should explain the product, show who it suits, and then link to the seller. A landing page also lets you collect email addresses.

Disclosure, every time. Say clearly that you may earn a commission when someone buys through your link. Put the disclosure near the link, in plain words, such as "This post contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you." The United States Federal Trade Commission explains this rule in its Endorsement Guides.

Step 3: Post content that converts

People buy from posts that help them decide, not from posts that shout about a deal. Use the formats below.

  • Reviews with photos. Show the product in your own home, say what you liked and what you did not, and say who should not buy it.
  • Tutorials. Show the product solving a real problem. A tutorial earns trust first and a sale second.
  • Comparisons. Compare two or three products side by side, with a clear winner for each kind of buyer.
  • Top picks lists. A short list of the best products for one need, with one line on each.
  • Before and after posts. Show the result of using a product, such as a tidy room or a finished project.
  • Short video demos. A Reel of 30 to 60 seconds that shows the product working, with a hook in the first two seconds and captions for viewers watching without sound. Our guide to earning money with Facebook Reels covers short video on Facebook in more depth.

Follow the 80/20 rule on a Page, which means about 80 percent useful content and 20 percent promotion. In a Group, use the stricter 90/10 rule. Post consistently, two or three times a week on a Page and daily in a Group, and answer every comment in the first hour.

Step 4: Build the blog to email funnel

A funnel turns one-time attention into repeat income. Facebook owns your reach, but your email list is yours.

  1. Post a useful piece of content on Facebook. A tutorial, a review, or a comparison that solves one problem.
  2. Send interested readers to your blog post. The blog post goes deeper and contains your affiliate links.
  3. Offer something free on the blog in exchange for an email address. A checklist, a template, or a short guide.
  4. Email new subscribers once or twice a week. Share useful tips, and include one product recommendation when it helps.
  5. Retarget blog visitors with Facebook ads later. People who visited your blog but did not buy are far more likely to convert than a cold audience.

This funnel also fixes the link problem. Your Facebook post has no link, so it reaches more people. Your blog holds the links, and your email list keeps the relationship alive.

Step 5: Use Facebook Ads when the maths works

Ads can scale an affiliate funnel, but they can also burn money fast. Only run ads after an offer has already converted from organic traffic.

Start with a small daily budget and a landing page, never a raw affiliate link. Test one audience and one creative at a time, and stop any ad that spends without a sale. The breakeven maths is simple: divide your commission by your conversion rate to find the most you can pay per click. For example, if your commission is 20 dollars and one in 50 visitors buys, you can afford up to 40 cents per click. If your cost per click is higher, the ad loses money on every sale, so stop it and fix the offer or the audience first.

Retargeting is the cheapest use of ads for affiliates. Show an ad to people who visited your blog or watched your video, because they already know you. Never run ads for a product you have not checked yourself, and never use a misleading claim in an ad, because Meta will restrict the account.

Track your results

Track four numbers every week: clicks on each link, the click-through rate of each post, sales and commissions per product, and earnings per 1,000 people reached. Use UTM parameters on your links, which are small tags added to a web address that tell your analytics tool where each visitor came from. If you run ads, install the Meta Pixel on your landing page, which reports visits and purchases back to Facebook, and use it to find which audiences buy.

Worked example: what a small affiliate funnel could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. This example shows the maths, not a promise. Real results depend on your niche, your prices, and your conversion rates.

Imagine you run a Group about home office setups with 5,000 members, and you recommend a lamp that pays a 20 dollar commission.

  • Group members who see your review post: 1,500, which is 30 percent.
  • Members who click your link: 3 percent of 1,500, which is 45 people.
  • Clickers who buy: 4 percent of 45, which is about 2 people.
  • Commission earned: 2 times 20 dollars, which is 40 dollars from one post.

Now add an email list of 500 readers. If 200 open your weekly email, 10 click a product link, and 1 buys, that adds 20 dollars a week, or about 80 dollars a month.

One post earns little. A system of posts, a blog, and an email list earns steadily.

What it costs and what to expect

You can start Facebook affiliate marketing for free, because a profile, a Page, and a Group cost nothing.

Item Typical cost Notes
Facebook profile, Page, Group Free Required
Blog or landing page Free to a few dollars a month for hosting Needed for the funnel and for ads
Facebook Ads Only when an offer already converts Start small, test, and stop losers fast
Your time 5 to 10 hours a week Posting, replying, and tracking

Expect the first month to bring clicks and almost no sales. Expect the first sale in your second or third month if you post consistently and pick products your audience wants. Expect steady income only after six to twelve months of building a Group and a list.

Common mistakes

  • Posting raw affiliate links in every post, which Facebook shows to fewer people and which readers soon ignore.
  • Promoting products you have never checked, which leads to refunds, complaints, and a loss of trust.
  • Running ads to a raw affiliate link, which breaks Meta's advertising policies and can restrict your account.
  • Forgetting the disclosure, which breaks trust with readers and the rules in several countries.
  • Quitting after a month of no sales, when most affiliates need three to six months to find what converts.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch for "affiliate managers" who message you with a programme that asks for an upfront fee to join. Real affiliate programmes are free to join. Watch for products that promise guaranteed income, because those programmes are usually selling the dream rather than a product.

Protect your own account too. Never share your login details, turn on two-factor authentication, and keep your Group rules strict about spam. Keep records of your affiliate income from the start, because tax rules differ by country.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do affiliate marketing on Facebook?

Yes. Facebook allows affiliate links in organic posts and in Groups, and its Affiliate Partnerships feature adds a clickable product banner to posts and Reels. Disclose the relationship, do not spam links, and do not use raw affiliate links as ad destinations.

Does Facebook allow affiliate links?

Yes, in organic posts, comments, and Groups, and through the native Affiliate Partnerships banner. Amazon links are allowed in organic posts, but boosting a post that contains one risks a restriction. Some programmes, such as ClickBank, do not allow direct promotion on Facebook at all.

How do I add affiliate links to Facebook Reels?

If Affiliate Partnerships is available in your country, connect your affiliate account and add the product banner when you create the Reel. The Reel must be longer than 10 seconds and the product must appear in the video. If the feature is not available, put the link in the first comment.

What are the best affiliate programs for Facebook?

The best programmes depend on your niche, but Amazon Associates is the easiest starting point. Add one affiliate network, such as Awin or ShareASale, and one brand programme in your niche.

How much can you earn with Facebook affiliate marketing?

Most beginners earn little or nothing at first, while building an audience. A small Group with a few thousand active members can earn tens to a few hundred dollars a month once a few products convert. Larger Groups and email lists earn more, but only after months of consistent posting.

Is Facebook still worth it for affiliate marketing in 2026?

Yes, for the right niche. Facebook still has over 3 billion monthly users, and its Groups give affiliates organic reach that is hard to find elsewhere. It works best for niches with active Groups, such as home, parenting, fitness, and hobbies.

Conclusion

To make money with Facebook affiliate marketing in 2026, pick one niche, set up a Page and a Group, join two or three programmes, and post useful content with an honest recommendation sometimes. Use the native Affiliate Partnerships banner where it is available, put other links in the first comment, and build a blog and email list so your income does not depend on Facebook's algorithm alone. Track what converts and give the system six months before you judge it.
