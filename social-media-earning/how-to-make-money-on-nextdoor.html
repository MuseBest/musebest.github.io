How to Make Money on Nextdoor in 2026

Learning how to make money on Nextdoor in 2026 starts with a plain fact near the start, as it should. Nextdoor pays no creator revenue. Nextdoor does not pay you for posts, views or followers. You earn when neighbours hire you, buy from you or recommend your business. This guide shows you how to set up a business presence, win local trust, offer clear services and get paid safely.

Nextdoor is a neighbourhood network where verified neighbours share local news, ask for recommendations and find local services. That local trust is the value. A recommendation from a nearby neighbour often carries more weight than a general advert. If you sell general social content, read other guides in this category. This guide stays on local clients and business presence.

What Nextdoor is and how earning works there

Nextdoor connects people who live near each other. Members verify their address, join their neighbourhood and see posts from nearby areas. Businesses can claim a business page where neighbours can find contact details, leave recommendations and send messages. Nextdoor earns money by selling advertising to businesses. It does not share that money with posters as creator pay.

Your income comes from real work. Examples include cleaning, handyman tasks, pet care, tutoring, tech help for older neighbours, garden work, moving help and local shop sales. You can also sell used items to neighbours through sale posts where the platform allows them. Check current Nextdoor rules in your account before you post, because features and posting limits can change by area.

Step 1: Choose a local service neighbours need

Pick one service you can do well and safely. Look for repeated neighbour requests, such as furniture assembly, deep cleaning, dog walking, lawn care or phone setup help. Choose work that fits your skills, tools, transport and schedule. Do not offer electrical, gas or structural work unless you are qualified and allowed by local rules.

Define your service area in streets or neighbourhoods you can reach on time. State what is included, what is not included and your minimum booking time. Clear limits prevent unpaid extra tasks. For service ideas, read how to start a handyman side hustle and how to make money with a cleaning side hustle.

Prepare simple proof. Take clear photos of past work with permission, or practise setups at home. Write a short service menu with base prices or price ranges. Neighbours book faster when they understand the offer without many messages.

Step 2: Claim and verify your business page

As of November 2026, the official Nextdoor Help article on claiming or creating a business page states that Nextdoor creates business pages automatically for local businesses listed in each neighbourhood. Search for your business first. If a page exists, claim it. If no page exists, create one through the Add business page option while logged in, or through the logged out route described in the help article.

Complete your page with the same business name, address area, phone number, website and hours you use elsewhere. Add a short description that names your services and service area. Add clear photos of your work, team or shop. Then verify the page as Nextdoor requires, using a current business document that shows your business name. Follow the steps in your account, because document rules can vary by country.

Decide which account runs the page. If staff will share the login, keep the business account separate from your personal neighbour account. Use strong passwords and limit access to people who need it. A complete verified page helps neighbours trust you and helps you reply to recommendations.

Step 3: Build trust with helpful neighbour posts

Post as a helpful neighbour first and a seller second. Answer questions, share local tips and take part in normal conversations. When you post about your service, keep it specific and useful. Name the service, your area, your availability and how to contact you. Avoid posting the same sales message again and again.

Respect Nextdoor rules for business posts. Self promotional posts should come from your business page where required. Check posting limits and ad options in your account before you rely on free posts. If you test paid ads, set a small budget, target only areas you serve and track enquiries. Paid reach without tracking wastes money.

Ask happy customers for a recommendation on your business page. Ask politely after the work is done and explain where to leave it. Never pay for recommendations or write fake ones. Fake reviews can break rules and destroy local trust. Review care for local firms is covered in how to offer AI review management services to local businesses.

Step 4: Turn recommendations into bookings

Reply to enquiries quickly and politely. Neighbours often need help soon, so a fast clear reply matters. Ask about the task, address area, timing and any access needs. Give a price range with clear boundaries, such as a minimum time and what could change the price.

Confirm bookings in writing through platform messages or email. State the date, time window, service, price and payment method. Send a reminder on the day. Arrive on time, explain what you will do, and note any extra work before you start it. Extra work needs agreement before it begins.

After the job, thank the customer and ask if anything needs fixing. Then ask for a recommendation if they are happy. Follow up once the next day for larger jobs. Good follow up turns one job into repeat work and referrals. For early customer steps, read how to get your first customers for a new online business and adapt the ideas to local work.

Step 5: Price local work and get paid safely

Price from time, travel, tools, materials and risk. Set a minimum charge so small jobs still cover travel. Offer flat prices for predictable tasks where you can, such as a standard clean or a set assembly job. State what the flat price includes. Track every job in a simple log with date, customer area, hours, price and payment status.

Agree payment before work starts. Use methods you can record, such as bank transfer or a payment app with a receipt. Send a simple invoice with the service, date and total. Never share bank login details. For larger jobs, use staged payments tied to clear stages.

Compare marketplace routes with care. Some workers also list on task platforms. Read how to earn money on TaskRabbit if you want a structured booking system as well. Do not move platform bookings off platform to avoid fees where terms forbid it. Keep each platform work within its rules.

Step 6: Sell items and grow repeat work

Item sales can add small income while you build services. List used items with honest photos, clear condition notes and a fair price. State pickup area and whether delivery is possible. Mark items sold when they sell. Meet buyers safely, ideally in daylight and in a public place for small items where practical.

Grow repeat work with simple systems. Keep a customer list with permission, note service dates and send polite reminders for seasonal work. Offer bundles that make sense, such as a move in clean plus furniture assembly, with one clear total price. Referral offers must be honest and easy to understand.

Keep your business page current. Update hours, services and photos when they change. Reply to recommendations and messages. A page that looks active and accurate earns more trust than a page with old details. Facebook local routes are covered in how to make money on Facebook if you also sell there.

Worked example: what local Nextdoor work could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results and they are not a promise. They show how to test your own pricing.

Suppose you complete 8 local jobs in one month at an average price of 75 US dollars each. Job income is 8 multiplied by 75 dollars, which is 600 dollars.

Suppose you also sell 5 used items at 20 dollars each. Item sales are 5 multiplied by 20 dollars, which is 100 dollars. Suppose travel and materials cost 90 dollars in that month. Income before tax after those costs is 600 dollars plus 100 dollars, which is 700 dollars, minus 90 dollars, which is 610 dollars. Check the maths again: 600 plus 100 is 700, and 700 minus 90 is 610. Your real figures depend on demand, pricing, travel and repeat work.

What it costs and what to expect

Costs include tools, transport, materials, phone data and any paid ads you choose to test. A business page itself is a presence tool, but paid promotion costs money if you buy it. Check current prices in your account before you spend. Start with free presence and helpful posts before paid ads.

Expect trust to build slowly. Early enquiries may be few. Recommendations compound over time when work is reliable. Demand varies by neighbourhood, season and service type. Plan for quiet weeks and keep records so you can see which services actually book.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting Nextdoor to pay for posts or views.
  • Posting repeated sales messages without taking part as a neighbour.
  • Offering services you are not qualified or insured to do.
  • Quoting without a minimum charge, travel limits or clear scope.
  • Asking for or posting fake recommendations.
  • Meeting buyers or clients without basic safety steps.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be careful with buyers who offer overpayment by cheque, ask you to ship items to distant places, or rush you to share bank details. Keep communication in platform messages where you can, confirm payment has cleared through safe methods, and trust your judgement. Report suspicious posts through Nextdoor tools.

For in home work, agree the scope in writing, share your location with a trusted person where appropriate, and leave if a situation feels unsafe. Check local licence, insurance and tax rules for your service. If you work in the United States, the IRS Gig Economy Tax Center explains that gig income is taxable and must be reported. Keep business money separate and keep invoices. This guide gives general information only and is not legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nextdoor pay creators for posts?

No. Nextdoor pays no creator revenue for posts, views or followers. You earn when neighbours hire you or buy from you. Any guide that promises Nextdoor view pay should be treated with care.

How do I claim my Nextdoor business page?

Search Nextdoor for your business while logged in. If a page exists, claim it. If not, use Add business page and enter your details. Then complete the page and verify it with a business document as Nextdoor requires.

What services sell well on Nextdoor?

Useful local services with clear need sell best, such as cleaning, handyman tasks, pet care, garden work, tutoring and tech help. Choose work you can do safely and price clearly.

How do recommendations help me earn?

Recommendations on your business page show neighbours that real customers trust you. They help new customers choose you. Earn them through good work and ask politely. Never buy or fake them.

Can I sell used items on Nextdoor?

Yes, where sale posts are available in your area and within Nextdoor rules. Use honest photos and condition notes, state pickup terms and meet buyers safely. Mark items sold promptly.

How long does it take to get clients on Nextdoor?

It varies by area and service. Some workers get enquiries in the first weeks, while others need months of helpful presence. A complete verified page and fast replies improve your chances.

Conclusion

Remember that Nextdoor pays no creator revenue, claim and verify your business page, help neighbours first, and turn recommendations into booked local work with clear prices. Keep records and meet people safely.
