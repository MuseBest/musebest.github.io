40:T4a7a,<h2 id="how-to-make-money-on-facebook-in-2026">How to Make Money on Facebook in 2026</h2>
<p>You may post on Facebook for months and see no income at all. That is normal, and it does not mean you are doing it wrong. Facebook pays in two very different ways, and most beginners mix them up. Some income comes from Meta itself, through its own creator programs. Other income comes from buyers, brands, and affiliate programs, and Facebook is only the place where those people find you. If you learn how to make money on Facebook, start with that split, because it decides your first steps.</p>
<p>This guide explains both paths. It covers the main Meta programs, Marketplace selling, affiliate links, brand deals, and selling your own products. It also shows the costs, the rules, and a realistic plan for your first 30 days. It does not promise a fixed income. Facebook publishes no fixed pay rate for views, so any guide that quotes one is guessing.</p>

<h2 id="the-two-paths-to-facebook-income">The two paths to Facebook income</h2>
<p>Meta payouts are the first path. You earn through tools such as Content Monetisation, Stars, and subscriptions, and Meta pays you through its payout system. These tools have eligibility rules, and some are invite only. You cannot switch them on by wishing for them, and you cannot control when an invitation arrives.</p>
<p>The second path is income from other people. A buyer pays you on Marketplace. A brand pays you for a sponsored post. An affiliate program pays you a commission when someone buys through your link. A customer buys your own product or service. These routes do not need a Meta invitation, so they are the sensible starting point for most beginners.</p>

<h2 id="meta-programs-in-order-of-accessibility">Meta programs, in order of accessibility</h2>
<h3 id="stars">Stars</h3>
<p>Stars are small virtual gifts that viewers send during live videos, Reels, and other eligible content. Each Star is worth a small amount to the creator, and the research for this guide found the value described as one cent per Star in an opened competitor guide. That figure should be checked in your own payout settings, because Meta can change it. Stars are the most accessible Meta tool for many creators, because the follower bar is lower than for subscriptions. The research found a threshold of 500 followers for 30 days described in at least two opened guides, along with other account standing rules. Check your Professional Dashboard for the current rule in your country, because thresholds change and they differ by region.</p>
<h3 id="content-monetisation">Content Monetisation</h3>
<p>Content Monetisation is the flagship Meta program for creators. It covers Reels, photos, text posts, and Stories under one program. Meta merged older tools into it in October 2024, including in-stream ads, Ads on Reels, and the Performance bonus. That merger matters, because old guides still quote old in-stream ads figures. Do not copy those old figures. They no longer describe the current program.</p>
<p>Content Monetisation is invite only. You can fill in an interest form in your Professional Dashboard, but that form does not guarantee an invitation. Meta does not publish a fixed rate per view or per 1,000 views for this program. Earnings depend on factors such as originality, qualified views, engagement, and watch time, and the exact mix is not published in full. That means you should never plan your budget around a guessed rate.</p>
<h3 id="subscriptions">Subscriptions</h3>
<p>Subscriptions let your most loyal followers pay a monthly fee for extra content, badges, and other perks. This is a trust product, not a reach product. A small, loyal audience can support subscriptions better than a large, passive audience. The research found a threshold described as 10,000 followers, or 250 return viewers plus either 50,000 engagements or 180,000 watch minutes in 60 days, in at least two opened guides. Treat that as a lead to check, not as a settled rule, because Meta updates eligibility pages often.</p>
<h3 id="creator-fast-track">Creator Fast Track</h3>
<p>Creator Fast Track is a different kind of program. It is aimed at established creators who are new to Facebook, and it offers guaranteed monthly pay for a set period if they meet posting and performance terms. The research found it described as an application program, and it stressed that creators should apply before posting their first Reel on Facebook, because the terms relate to new activity on the platform. Do not assume you can join it later on the same terms.</p>
<p>Two conflicts in the research must be flagged honestly. First, the country list is unresolved. One opened guide listed only the United States and Canada, while another listed the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Australia. Second, the tier amounts differ in how they are presented, with a low tier described as a range in one guide and a full tier table in another. Do not quote a country list or a tier amount as fact until you check Meta's current Fast Track page. If you are an established creator elsewhere, check eligibility early, because timing matters for this program.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/social-facebook-page-dashboard.jpg" alt="Person checking a Facebook Page dashboard on a laptop" /></p>
<h2 id="selling-outside-meta-programs">Selling outside Meta programs</h2>
<h3 id="sell-on-facebook-marketplace">Sell on Facebook Marketplace</h3>
<p>Marketplace is the fastest route to cash for many beginners, because it needs no audience and no invitation. You list an item, a local buyer contacts you, and you agree a price and a collection or delivery method. For shipped orders, the research found a fee described as 10 percent of the sale price, with a minimum of 0.80 dollars, in an opened guide. Check the current fee in your Marketplace settings before you price an item, because fees and payout rules change.</p>
<p>Worked example, labelled clearly. Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they show the maths only. You sell a used desk lamp for 50 dollars through a shipped Marketplace order. If the fee is 10 percent, the fee is 5 dollars. You keep 45 dollars before postage, packaging, and your time. If postage costs 8 dollars and you pay it, you keep 37 dollars. That simple check stops many sellers from pricing too low.</p>
<p>Marketplace also has risks. Meet buyers in safe, public places for local sales. Never share bank login details. Keep messages inside Facebook where possible, so you have a record. Photograph items honestly, including faults, because a misleading listing leads to disputes and refunds.</p>
<h3 id="affiliate-links">Affiliate links</h3>
<p>Affiliate marketing means you recommend a product and earn a commission when someone buys through your link. On Facebook, you can share affiliate links in posts, in groups you run, and in video descriptions, as long as you follow both Facebook's rules and the affiliate program's terms. Some programs restrict direct linking, and some require a website in between. Read the terms before you post.</p>
<p>Disclosure is not optional. Say clearly that a link is an affiliate link and that you may earn a commission. Readers trust you more when you are open, and disclosure is also a legal requirement in many countries. For a fuller guide to choosing programs and making a first sale, see <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/how-to-start-affiliate-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start affiliate marketing</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/how-to-make-first-affiliate-sale" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to make your first affiliate sale</a>.</p>
<h3 id="brand-deals">Brand deals</h3>
<p>A brand deal is a paid post, video, or series made for a brand. The brand pays you, not Meta. Small creators can win brand deals when their audience is narrow and engaged, for example local parents, new gardeners, or beginner cooks. Brands care about trust and fit more than raw follower count, but you still need proof. Save screenshots of your reach, comments, and past results, and keep a simple rate card.</p>
<p>Always use the paid partnership label when a post is sponsored. The label tells viewers that a brand paid for the content, and it keeps you inside both Facebook's rules and advertising rules in your country. Agree the fee, the number of posts, the usage rights, and the payment date in writing before you film anything. For more on small creator deals, see <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-creator" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to get brand deals as a small creator</a>.</p>
<h3 id="your-own-products-and-a-facebook-shop">Your own products and a Facebook Shop</h3>
<p>Selling your own products gives you the most control. You can sell digital products such as templates or guides, physical products, or services such as coaching or design work. A Facebook Shop lets people browse products on your Page, and you can connect it to a shop platform if you use one. Check the Shop rules for your country, because availability and checkout options differ by region.</p>
<p>If you sell services, a clear offer page matters more than a large audience. State what you do, who it is for, what it costs, and how a client books you. For pricing help, see <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-set-freelance-rates" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to set freelance rates</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-create-your-first-digital-product" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to create your first digital product</a>.</p>
<h2 id="the-setup-checklist">The setup checklist</h2>
<p>Use this checklist before you chase any payout tool.</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose a Page or professional mode for a profile, and keep personal and business content separate where it helps.</li>
<li>Open your Professional Dashboard and check which monetisation tools are available to you.</li>
<li>Set up your payout account with correct tax details, and check the payout minimum in your own settings. The research found conflicting minimums described as 25 dollars or 100 dollars depending on the tool, so do not rely on a single blog figure.</li>
<li>Post only original content, or content you have the right to use, and avoid watermarks from other apps.</li>
<li>Turn on two factor authentication, and keep your login details private.</li>
<li>Write a simple content plan for one audience and one topic, so viewers know what to expect from you.</li>
</ol>
<p><img src="/covers/social-facebook-marketplace-sale.jpg" alt="Seller photographing a chair to list on Facebook Marketplace" /></p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Facebook itself is free to use, but earning on Facebook is not free in time or in tools. You may need a phone with a decent camera, basic lighting, editing software, and time to plan, film, and reply to comments. If you sell physical products, add stock, packaging, postage, and Marketplace or Shop fees. If you run ads to grow a Page, treat that as a cost, not as income. Ads can bring viewers, but they do not pay you.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Most creators earn nothing from Meta programs in the first weeks, because they have no invitation yet or no regular audience. Early income, if it comes at all, is more likely from Marketplace, a service, or a small affiliate sale. Earnings vary with your niche, your country, your posting consistency, demand, competition, and platform fees. Results also vary with how much time you can give each week. A realistic timeline is months of steady posting before Meta payouts become regular, and even then the amounts can rise and fall.</p>
<p>Tax rules differ by country. Keep records of every payment, fee, and expense from the start, and check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant when income becomes regular. For a beginner guide, see <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/freelance-taxes-for-beginners" rel="noopener" target="_blank">freelance taxes for beginners</a>.</p>
<h2 id="a-realistic-first-30-days">A realistic first 30 days</h2>
<p>Week 1: Set up your Page or professional profile, complete the checklist above, and choose one topic. List one or two Marketplace items if you have suitable items to sell. Write your first five post ideas.</p>
<p>Week 2: Post three to five times, mixing Reels, photos, and text posts. Reply to every genuine comment. Share one honest affiliate recommendation only if it fits your topic and you disclose it.</p>
<p>Week 3: Check your Professional Dashboard for eligibility and insights. Keep the formats that earn comments and shares, and drop the formats that only collect silent views. If you sell a service, publish a clear offer post.</p>
<p>Week 4: Review what worked. Note which posts brought profile visits, messages, or sales, not just likes. Plan the next month around those winners, and fill in the Content Monetisation interest form if it is available to you.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes-that-cost-earnings">Common mistakes that cost earnings</h2>
<ul>
<li>Reposting other people's clips and expecting Meta to pay for them.</li>
<li>Chasing follower count instead of building trust with one clear audience.</li>
<li>Forgetting Marketplace fees and postage when you set a price.</li>
<li>Hiding affiliate links or sponsored posts instead of disclosing them.</li>
<li>Relying on one income stream, so a policy change wipes out all income.</li>
<li>Posting irregularly for two weeks, then disappearing for a month.</li>
<li>Applying for Fast Track after you have already started posting on Facebook, when the terms may require a new start.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Never pay for a monetisation invitation, a follower package, or a course that promises Facebook payouts. Meta does not sell invitations, and bought followers harm your reach because they do not watch, comment, or buy. Be careful with buyers who ask you to move off Facebook, pay by unusual methods, or share bank details. Be careful with brands that ask you to post first and promise payment later with no written agreement. If an offer sounds too good, pause and check the brand and the terms before you agree.</p>
<p>Account safety also matters. If a Page loses good standing, or a monetisation tool is removed, earnings can stop at once. Keep backups of your content, keep your contact list outside Facebook where possible, and never build your whole income on one platform. For a wider view, see <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-turn-small-social-media-following-into-income" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to turn a small social media following into income</a> and the <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Social Media Earning category</a>.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-many-followers-do-i-need-to-make-money-on-facebook">How many followers do I need to make money on Facebook?</h3>
<p>It depends on the method. Marketplace needs no followers. Affiliate links and services need trust more than a follower number. Stars and subscriptions have eligibility bars that you should check in your Professional Dashboard, because they change and they differ by country. Content Monetisation is invite only, so follower count alone does not unlock it.</p>
<h3 id="does-facebook-pay-you-for-views">Does Facebook pay you for views?</h3>
<p>Facebook does not publish a fixed pay rate per view. Content Monetisation earnings depend on qualified views, engagement, watch time, and originality, among other factors. That is why two creators with similar views can earn very different amounts, and why you should not budget around a guessed rate.</p>
<h3 id="when-do-meta-payouts-arrive">When do Meta payouts arrive?</h3>
<p>The research found payout timing described in general terms as monthly, often between the 17th and 22nd of the month, in an opened guide. Check your own payout settings for the current timing and minimum, because both vary by tool and by country. Keep records so you can spot a missing payout quickly.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-make-money-on-facebook-without-showing-my-face">Can I make money on Facebook without showing my face?</h3>
<p>Yes, for some routes. Marketplace, affiliate links, product sales, and text or photo content do not need face videos. Reels and live videos often build trust faster, but you can start with clear voiceovers, demonstrations, and well written posts, and add face videos later if you wish.</p>
<h3 id="is-creator-fast-track-open-in-my-country">Is Creator Fast Track open in my country?</h3>
<p>That is unresolved in the research for this guide. One opened guide listed only the United States and Canada, while another listed four countries. Do not rely on either list. Check Meta's current Fast Track page and your Professional Dashboard before you plan around it.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Start with what you can control. Sell something real, offer a clear service, or recommend products you genuinely use, with clear disclosure. Build original content for one audience, check your Professional Dashboard often, and treat Meta payouts as a later bonus, not as your first plan. That order keeps your income honest and less fragile.</p>

Earning results vary, and nothing on this site is financial advice.</p>
