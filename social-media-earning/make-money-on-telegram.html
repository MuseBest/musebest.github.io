How to Make Money on Telegram in 2026

Learning how to make money on Telegram starts with what Telegram does and does not pay for. Telegram pays channel owners in a few clear ways, but only when they have a real, engaged audience. It does not pay per view, per subscriber, or per post. This guide explains each method, what it needs, and how the money reaches you, so you can choose one route and build it properly.

What Telegram actually pays for, and what it does not

Telegram has one official way of paying channel owners directly: the ad revenue share. Owners of public channels with at least 1,000 subscribers receive 50 percent of the revenue from ads Telegram displays in their channels. The share is based on valid ad impressions, not on how many subscribers you have. Telegram says so on its own blog and in its developer documentation, and the rule was still in place when checked in August 2026.

Everything else you earn on Telegram comes from your audience, not from Telegram. Readers pay you with Stars, join your paid channel, buy your products, click your affiliate links, or pay a sponsor to reach your readers. Telegram provides the tools for these, and takes its cut where the rules say so, but the money comes from people, not from the platform.

That distinction matters. A channel with 200 readers who trust you can earn more than a channel with 20,000 silent subscribers. Build trust first, and the money routes follow. That is the honest starting point for learning how to make money on Telegram.

Before you monetise: the five basics

1. Pick one niche

A channel about one topic grows faster than a channel about everything. Pick a topic you can write about three times a week for a year. Finance, tech tools, language learning, fitness, and crafts all work, as long as you know the topic well enough to help a reader.

2. Make the channel public

Only public channels can earn the ad revenue share. A public channel also has a short link you can share anywhere, which is how new readers find you.

3. Choose a readable username

Pick a short name people can spell after hearing it once. Avoid numbers and odd spellings. Your channel name and username are your shop sign.

4. Post on a fixed schedule

Three or four useful posts a week beats ten posts in one day followed by silence. Readers stay when they know when to expect you. Each post should help, entertain, or teach something in under a minute of reading.

5. Switch on two-step verification

Go to Settings, then Privacy and Security, and switch on two-step verification before you monetise anything. You will need it to withdraw earnings, and it protects your channel from being stolen. A stolen channel is hard to recover, so do this on day one.

The seven methods, and how each one pays

1. Official ad revenue share

Telegram places sponsored messages in large public channels and shares the money. You need a public channel with at least 1,000 subscribers. Telegram pays 50 percent of the revenue from valid impressions of those ads. Premium subscribers do not see ads, so they do not generate ad revenue. You see your balance under Channel Settings, then Statistics, then Monetization. Payouts are made in Gram, the coin of the TON blockchain, through Fragment. One important naming fact: the coin was called Toncoin until 15 June 2026, when it was renamed Gram after a community vote. The blockchain is still called TON, balances did not change, and no swap or migration was needed. Older pages that say Toncoin mean the same coin.

2. Sponsored posts and suggested posts

A business can pay you to post about its product, or to suggest its channel to your readers. Price by your average views per post, not by your subscriber count, because views are what the sponsor buys. Agree the price, the wording, and the disclosure in writing before you post, and label the post as sponsored. Suggested posts are a Telegram feature where a channel can pay Stars or Gram to appear as a suggestion in other channels. Payment for a suggested post is made 24 hours after it is published, and Stars bought through Apple can be refunded within 21 days, which can claw the payment back.

3. Telegram Stars

Stars are Telegram's in-app currency, and they are the main way readers pay you inside the app. You can earn them through paid posts that readers unlock with Stars, Star reactions on your posts, paid monthly subscriptions, and gifts. You can also earn Stars from paid messages if you have Telegram Premium: you set a price for messages from people outside your contacts, and you keep 85 percent of the Stars paid.

Telegram's creator terms value each earned Star at about 0.013 US dollars when you withdraw it as a reward, or 0.02 US dollars when you spend it on Telegram ads. You can only withdraw Stars you earned as a creator, not Stars you bought for yourself. To withdraw, your balance must reach at least 1,000 Stars, and each Star only becomes available 21 days after you received it. You withdraw through Fragment to a TON wallet, and the payout arrives in Gram. Stars you earned expire after three years if you do not convert them. These figures come from Telegram's creator and developer terms as reported in October and November 2026, so check the current terms in the app before you build a price around them.

4. Paid private channel

Create a second, private channel and charge a monthly subscription for access. Members pay with Stars through an invite link, or through an external payment method. One guide reports paid channels priced from 5 to 50 US dollars a month, with smaller niche channels at the low end. Whatever you charge, deliver on a fixed schedule: a weekly lesson, a monthly live session, or early access to your best posts. Members leave fast when a paid channel goes quiet, so count the support time before you set the price.

5. Affiliate marketing

Share links to products your readers want and earn a commission on each sale. This works well for tool, book, and course recommendations in a niche channel. Disclose the link every time, and only recommend products you have used or checked. Telegram also runs a mini app affiliate programme, where you earn for bringing users to a mini app. Learn the basics in how to promote affiliate products.

6. Selling your own products and services

A Telegram channel can sell digital products, templates, presets, and courses directly to readers. You can also sell a service, such as design, writing, or coaching, and use the channel to show your work. Note one rule: digital goods sold inside a bot or mini app must be paid for in Stars. If you sell outside the bot, you can use your own payment link. Read how to make money as a social media manager for the service route.

7. Bots and mini apps

A bot can answer questions, sell a digital product, or run a small tool for your readers. A mini app is a small web app that runs inside Telegram, such as a quiz, a calculator, or a shop front. Both need some technical skill or a developer to build, and both can earn Stars from users. Bot hosting costs money each month, so build a bot only when you have a clear job for it, not as a first step.

How to set up the official ad revenue share, step by step

  1. Grow your public channel to 1,000 subscribers. The share does not switch on before that.
  2. Open your channel, tap its name, then go to Statistics, then Monetization. This screen only appears once you qualify.
  3. Check that your channel follows Telegram's terms and that your country is covered. Telegram launched the share in nearly 100 countries in 2024, but availability varies, so your Monetization screen is the true answer for you.
  4. Watch your balance build from valid ad impressions. There is nothing else to switch on. Telegram places the ads itself.
  5. When you want the money, use the Collect option on the Monetization screen, which takes you to Fragment. Connect a TON wallet, such as Tonkeeper or MyTonWallet, and follow the steps there.

Note that the payout is in Gram, not in your local currency. You will need to convert it on an exchange, pay the exchange fee, and keep records for tax. Never pay a third party to collect it for you.

How to set up Telegram Stars, step by step

  1. Open your channel and check that paid posts are available to you. Availability varies by country.
  2. To make a paid post, write the post, then set a Star price before you publish it. Readers who pay the price can read the full post. Keep the price low at first, so readers learn the habit.
  3. To switch on Star reactions, open the channel settings and allow paid reactions. Readers can then send Stars to a post they liked.
  4. To sell a monthly subscription, create an invite link with a Star price for your private channel. Share that link in your public channel and pin it.
  5. To set a price for private messages, go to Settings, then Privacy and Security, then Messages, and set your price. This needs Telegram Premium.
  6. Check your Stars balance in Settings, then My Stars. Only Stars earned by the channel or bot can be withdrawn. When the balance reaches 1,000 Stars and the Stars are more than 21 days old, use the Withdraw option, which opens Fragment.

Set your prices with the net value in mind. A Star pays you about 0.013 US dollars, so 100 Stars is about 1.30 US dollars before conversion and exchange fees. Price a paid post at what the content is worth to the reader, not at what you hope to earn.

Growing from zero to your first 100 and then 1,000 subscribers

Growth on Telegram is slow at first because there is no feed that recommends small channels. You have to bring the first readers yourself.

Start with your existing contacts. Share the channel link with people who already care about the topic, and ask them to forward one post they liked. Post your best work on other platforms you already use, with a link to the channel. Join public groups in your niche, answer questions well, and link your channel only when it directly helps the person asking. List the channel in Telegram directories for your topic.

Aim for the first 100 readers in the first month. That is a realistic target for a new channel with no audience. From there, post three or four times a week, keep the quality steady, and cross-promote with one similar channel a month. The 1,000 subscriber mark for the ad revenue share often takes three to six months of steady posting. Do not buy subscribers. Bought subscribers never read, never pay, and can get your channel flagged. Steady posting is how to make money on Telegram over time, because every method here needs readers who return.

Getting paid: Fragment, wallets, holds, and minimums

Fragment is Telegram's own marketplace on the TON blockchain, and it is where creator rewards are collected. It is not a wallet and not an exchange. You bring a wallet of your own, such as Tonkeeper or MyTonWallet, and Fragment only handles the payment step.

To collect ad revenue, open Channel Settings, then Statistics, then Monetization, and use the Collect option. Ad revenue can be withdrawn two days after it is credited to your balance. To collect Stars, use the Withdraw option in your Stars balance. That needs 1,000 Stars and a 21-day wait on each Star.

Once the Gram is in your wallet, you can keep it, spend it on Telegram ads, or sell it on an exchange for your local currency. The exchange will charge a fee, and the Gram price moves, so the amount you receive in your local currency is not fixed. Keep a record of every payout, every conversion, and every fee. Income from Telegram is taxable in most countries, and your records are what your tax return is built on. Check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant about how to declare it.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting a Telegram channel costs nothing. The real costs are your time, and later, bot hosting if you build one, and ad spend if you pay to grow. A small bot can cost a few dollars a month to host. Growing with Telegram ads can cost much more, so set a small test budget first and measure what each new subscriber costs you.

Expect no income for the first weeks. The first money usually comes from affiliate links or a small paid post, not from the ad revenue share, because that needs 1,000 subscribers first. A channel with 1,000 engaged readers might earn a small amount each month from ads, and the figure depends on the niche, the readers' countries, and how many of them use Premium. No guide can give you a reliable monthly figure for your channel, and this guide will not invent one. Results vary with your niche, your consistency, and your audience.

Common mistakes

  • Buying subscribers to reach 1,000 faster, which leaves you with an audience that never reads.
  • Posting ads in every post, which drives readers away.
  • Mixing niches in one channel, so no reader knows what to expect.
  • Depending on one income method, usually the ad share, and ignoring the rest.
  • Setting Star prices so high that nobody pays, then giving up on Stars.
  • Promising a paid channel weekly content and then going quiet.
  • Running a crypto or trading signals channel without checking your local financial rules.

Risks and scams to watch for

  • Anyone who asks you to pay a deposit to unlock Telegram earnings is running a scam. Telegram never asks for a deposit.
  • Guaranteed daily returns from a Telegram bot or group are not real. No honest channel can promise them.
  • Task jobs that ask for a fee before you can start are scams. A real job pays you, not the other way round.
  • Referral pyramids that pay you for recruiting others are not a business. They collapse, and the last people in lose their money.
  • Never share your seed phrase, the recovery words for a crypto wallet, with anyone. No real support agent will ever ask for them. Anyone who has them can empty your wallet.
  • Beware of fake Fragment or wallet sites. Only use the link Telegram itself gives you in the app, and check the address before you connect a wallet.
  • If someone offers to buy your channel, use an escrow service and verify the buyer. Channel sales are a target for fraud.

Frequently asked questions

Does Telegram pay you?

Telegram pays channel owners a 50 percent share of ad revenue in public channels with at least 1,000 subscribers. It does not pay per view or per subscriber. All other income comes from readers, through Stars, subscriptions, sponsorships, or sales.

How many subscribers do you need to earn on Telegram?

You need 1,000 subscribers in a public channel for the ad revenue share. Stars, affiliate links, and selling your own products have no subscriber minimum, so you can earn with a small channel if the readers trust you.

How do I withdraw money from Telegram?

Ad revenue is collected through Fragment from your channel Monetization screen, two days after it is credited. Stars are withdrawn through Fragment once your balance reaches 1,000 Stars and each Star is more than 21 days old. Both pay out in Gram to a TON wallet.

What is Telegram Stars?

Stars are Telegram's in-app currency. Readers spend them on paid posts, reactions, subscriptions, and gifts. Creators withdraw earned Stars through Fragment at about 0.013 US dollars per Star. Stars you buy for yourself cannot be withdrawn.

Can I make money on Telegram without a channel?

Yes. You can manage channels for others, write content, sell sponsorships, build bots, or offer growth services, all without owning a channel. You can also earn as a freelancer on Telegram by finding clients in groups for your skill.

Is it legal to earn on Telegram?

Yes, in most countries, as long as you follow local tax and financial rules. Crypto and trading signal channels face extra rules in some countries, so check your local regulator before you start one.

Conclusion

That is how to make money on Telegram in 2026: pick one niche, grow a public channel with steady useful posts, switch on two-step verification, and add income methods in order: Stars and affiliate links first, then the ad revenue share at 1,000 subscribers, then sponsorships and a paid channel when your readers trust you. Collect payouts through Fragment, keep records, and never pay anyone to unlock earnings.
