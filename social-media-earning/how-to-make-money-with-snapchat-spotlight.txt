32:T4e13,<h1>How to Make Money with Snapchat Spotlight in 2026</h1>
<p>Learning how to make money with Snapchat Spotlight is harder than most guides admit, because the rules changed and many guides did not. Snapchat used to run a Spotlight Rewards Program that paid top videos from a prize pool. That program ended on 31 January 2025. In its place is one <a href="https://newsroom.snap.com/snapchat-new-creator-monetization" rel="noopener" target="_blank">unified Monetization Program</a> that shares ad revenue with invited creators, and the bar is high: 50,000 followers and thousands of hours of view time. If you start today, you will probably earn nothing from Spotlight for months. What you can do is build the audience and the watch time that qualify you, while earning from brand deals and your own products along the way.</p>

<h2 id="what-spotlight-is-and-what-changed-in-2025">What Spotlight is, and what changed in 2025</h2>
<p>Spotlight is Snapchat's feed of short, vertical videos, similar to TikTok's For You feed or Instagram Reels. Anyone can submit a video, and Snapchat shows the best ones to people who do not follow the creator. That discovery is the whole point, and it is why creators use it to grow.</p>
<p>The old system worked like a contest. Snapchat set aside money, and the most engaging videos each day won a share of it. Some creators won large amounts, and many guides still quote the old "$1 million prize" figure as if it were current. It is not. The Spotlight Rewards Program ended on 31 January 2025, and Snapchat replaced it with a unified Monetization Program that covers both Public Stories and Spotlight. Under the new program, Snapchat places ads around your content, and invited creators receive a share of the revenue. You cannot apply. Snapchat reviews creators and invites them by email.</p>
<p>That change matters. Views alone do not pay, and the new program rewards watch time, not just viral spikes. A video that holds viewers for 30 seconds is worth more than one that gets a million quick swipes.</p>
<h2 id="how-spotlight-pays-now">How Spotlight pays now</h2>
<p>Snapchat's Monetization Program pays a share of the advertising revenue shown around your content. Snapchat does not publish a fixed rate per 1,000 views, so any guide that promises an exact dollar figure per view is guessing. Your earnings depend on how much advertising runs, how long people watch, and where your viewers live.</p>
<p>Keep three outcomes separate. Views help you grow, but they do not establish that you are eligible for payment. Monetisation revenue is the ad share you earn once you are invited. Business income is what you earn outside the program, such as brand deals or your own products. Most creators who earn from Spotlight in their first year earn through the third route.</p>
<p>Spotlight videos must be at least 30 seconds long to be eligible to earn revenue. Shorter videos can still be shown and can still grow your audience, but they do not earn under the program. That rule shapes your editing: plan videos of 30 to 60 seconds, with the main point in the first few seconds and the payoff before the end.</p>

<h2 id="step-1-set-up-a-public-profile">Step 1: Set up a Public Profile</h2>
<p>A Public Profile is the public home for your Spotlight videos, Public Stories and creator information. Without one, people who discover your videos have nowhere to follow you, and your work does not build an audience.</p>
<p>Open Snapchat, go to your profile, and look for the Public Profile option. The exact screens differ by region, age and app version. Fill in a short bio that says what you post and who it is for, and add a clear profile photo. A Public Profile alone does not qualify you for monetisation, but it is the first step.</p>
<p>Turn on two-factor authentication for your account at the same time. You will need it for payouts later, and it protects the audience you are about to build.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-pick-a-niche-you-can-film-every-week">Step 2: Pick a niche you can film every week</h2>
<p>A niche is the topic your videos cover. Posting random videos is fun, but a recognisable topic helps people understand what to expect and helps Snapchat show your videos to the right viewers. Pick a subject you can film three to five times a week for months without running out of ideas.</p>
<p>Good Spotlight niches are visual and quick to understand: comedy sketches, simple recipes, travel and local discoveries, beauty and personal style, technology tips, fitness routines, and short explainers. A technology creator might film one useful phone trick per video. A food creator might film one 30-second recipe per video. The format is the same each time, so viewers learn what to expect and come back.</p>
<p>Test your niche with 20 videos before you judge it. If one style holds viewers longer, make more of it. If filming feels like a chore after 20 videos, pick a different niche.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-make-videos-that-hold-attention">Step 3: Make videos that hold attention</h2>
<p>Viewers can swipe away in a second, so the opening matters most. Show the result, the problem or the most interesting moment first. A recipe video should show the finished dish in the first two seconds, and a comedy sketch should start with the situation that makes it funny.</p>
<p>Film vertical video with sound, at 9:16, with a minimum horizontal resolution of 640 pixels. Use Snapchat's own library music, not a chart song you do not have the right to use. Keep the video original. Downloading popular clips and uploading them again will not earn, and Snapchat's monetisation policy excludes unoriginal content. Add captions where they help, because many people watch with the sound off.</p>

<h2 id="step-4-post-3-to-5-times-a-week">Step 4: Post 3 to 5 times a week</h2>
<p>The program measures view time over the last 28 days, so it rewards a regular posting habit over a single hit. A video that goes viral once will not keep you above the threshold. Three to five original videos a week is a realistic pace for one person, and it gives you enough chances to learn what works.</p>
<p>Keep a simple record of each video: the topic, the date, the view time, and whether viewers stayed to the end. After a month, look for patterns. If short tutorials hold attention, make more of them. If one video outperforms the rest, make a follow-up within a few days.</p>
<p>Add a topic tag when you submit to Spotlight, so Snapchat can show your video to people interested in that topic. Use topics that match the video, not popular tags that do not fit. A misleading tag may bring a few extra views, but those viewers will leave fast, and that hurts your retention.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-track-view-time-and-work-toward-snap-star">Step 5: Track view time and work toward Snap Star</h2>
<p>Snap Star status is one of the published criteria for the Monetization Program. Snap Stars are recognised creators and public figures, and meeting a follower threshold alone does not guarantee the status. Build a public presence around original content, a clear identity and a real audience, and apply through Snapchat's own verification process when your account is ready.</p>
<p>Watch your Insights each week. The numbers that matter are total view time over the last 28 days, Spotlight view time within that total, and whether viewers stay to the end. The Daily Rewards Eligibility section shows your Total Spotlight View Time, which decides maximum Creator Rewards once you are in the program.</p>
<p>Keep your account email up to date. Snapchat says it contacts invited creators by email, and it does not charge any fee for an invitation. Anyone who offers you a paid shortcut into the program is not working with Snap.</p>
<h2 id="other-ways-to-earn-on-snapchat">Other ways to earn on Snapchat</h2>
<p>Spotlight can help people discover you, but it is not the only income route, and for most creators it is not the first one that pays.</p>
<p><strong>Brand partnerships.</strong> Brands may work with creators who reach a relevant audience. A cooking creator might partner with a kitchenware brand, while a technology creator might partner with an app or accessory company. Use Snapchat's paid partnership tools and follow its commercial content rules when you publish sponsored material. Always disclose when you are paid to promote something.</p>
<p><strong>Creator Marketplace.</strong> Snapchat's Creator Marketplace connects creators with brands looking for sponsored content. Build a strong profile there, show your best Spotlight videos, and pitch brands that fit your niche instead of waiting to be found.</p>
<p><strong>Creator Subscriptions.</strong> In February 2026, Snap announced Creator Subscriptions as an alpha test with a select group of US-based Snap Stars, with expansion planned to Canada, the UK and France. It is not yet a route most creators can use, so do not plan income around it until it reaches your country.</p>
<p><strong>Your own products and affiliate links.</strong> You can use your audience to support other income, such as selling a digital product or recommending a tool through an affiliate link, where the rules allow it. No income yet. The creator keeps a simple record, notices that quick budget dinners hold viewers longest, and makes more of those.</p>

The creator earns roughly $100 to $200 a month from occasional brand deals and a digital recipe pack sold through a link in the profile. The Monetization Program is still a goal, not an income source. Plan for a year of building before platform payments.</p>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>The most common scam is the paid invitation. Someone messages you offering to get you into the Monetization Program for a fee, or selling a "verification" service. Snapchat says it charges no fee for an invitation and no third party controls eligibility. Never pay anyone for access.</p>
<p>Fake payout services are another trap. A site or a direct message promises to "unlock" your Spotlight earnings if you log in or pay a small fee. That login steals your account. Only manage payouts inside the Snapchat app, through the official screens.</p>
<p>Copyright is a quieter risk. Only use music from Snapchat's own library, and only upload content you created or meaningfully transformed. Re-uploaded clips from television, film or other creators will not earn and can lead to account action. Tax rules apply to Snapchat income too, and they differ by country. <h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-you-make-money-on-snapchat-spotlight">Can you make money on Snapchat Spotlight?</h3>
<p>Yes, but only through Snapchat's invitation-only Monetization Program, or through brand deals and your own products. The old Spotlight Rewards Program ended in January 2025. Going viral does not automatically mean you will get paid, because you need to meet the invitation criteria and be invited first.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-followers-do-you-need-for-snapchat-monetization">How many followers do you need for Snapchat monetization?</h3>
<p>Snapchat's published criteria list at least 50,000 followers, plus 15,000 hours of view time over the last 28 days, with at least 3,000 of those hours from Spotlight. You also need Snap Star status, to be 18 or older, and to live in an eligible country.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-must-a-spotlight-video-be-to-earn">How long must a Spotlight video be to earn?</h3>
<p>At least 30 seconds. Shorter videos can still be shown on Spotlight and can still grow your audience, but they do not earn revenue under the Monetization Program. Since 7 May 2026, creators in the program must also keep at least 100 hours of Total Spotlight View Time over the last 28 days for maximum Creator Rewards.</p>
<h3 id="does-snapchat-pay-per-view">Does Snapchat pay per view?</h3>
<p>No fixed rate is published. Snapchat shares advertising revenue with invited creators, and the amount depends on ad demand, watch time, viewer location and the program terms. Be sceptical of any site that quotes an exact dollar figure per 1,000 views without showing its source.</p>
<h3 id="can-you-earn-from-spotlight-without-many-followers">Can you earn from Spotlight without many followers?</h3>
<p>You can earn from brand deals and your own products with a small but engaged audience, because brands pay for relevance, not just size. But you cannot earn ad revenue from Spotlight itself until you meet the invitation bar. Focus on growing a niche audience first.</p>

Spotlight can grow your audience, and the Monetization Program pays invited creators a share of ad revenue, but the bar is 50,000 followers, thousands of hours of view time and an invitation. Build a Public Profile, pick a niche you can film three to five times a week, make videos of at least 30 seconds, and track your view time. While you work toward the bar, earn from brand deals and your own products, and never pay anyone for an invitation.</p>
