How to Make Money with Pinterest Idea Pins in 2026

Learning how to make money with Pinterest Idea Pins starts with a fact most ranking guides get wrong. There is no separate Idea Pin button in 2026. Pinterest merged Idea Pins into standard Pins in May 2023, and the multi-page, video-first format now lives inside the regular Pin you create. The name survives only as a paid advertising format. That change is good news, because it fixed the format's biggest flaw. Old Idea Pins could not carry a clickable link. Current video Pins can.

This guide explains what happened to Idea Pins, how the format earns money now, how to build a multi-page video Pin that sells. It covers the multi-page video format only. For the whole platform, read our guide to how to make money on Pinterest.

What happened to Idea Pins

Pinterest launched Idea Pins in 2021 as a multi-page, story-style format. Each Idea Pin could hold several pages of images, video clips, text, music and stickers, and it stayed on your profile instead of disappearing after 24 hours. The format was built for discovery. It showed your idea step by step, and it was good at earning saves and follows.

It had one serious flaw. Idea Pins could not carry a clickable destination link. A viewer who liked your tutorial could save it or follow you, but they could not tap through to your blog, your shop or your email list.

On 11 May 2023, Pinterest announced that Pins and Idea Pins would share one creation flow, simply called Pins, as explained in Kadenzo's guide to what happened to Idea Pins. The features that made Idea Pins distinctive, such as multi-page layouts, video, music, stickers and text overlays, moved into the standard Pin. The features that belonged to standard Pins, such as destination links, scheduling and editing after publishing, opened up to everyone. Old Idea Pins were not deleted. They still exist as Pins. The name itself moved to advertising, where Idea ads let brands run multi-page campaigns.

Here is the change in one table.

Feature Old Idea Pin Current video Pin
Pages Multiple pages Multiple pages, up to 20, with 3 to 5 working best
Video Yes Yes, 4 seconds to 5 minutes
Music, stickers, text Yes Yes
Destination link No Yes
Editing after publishing No Yes
Scheduling No Yes
Separate creation flow Yes No, one Create Pin flow

If a guide tells you to "create an Idea Pin" as a separate step, it is out of date. What you do now is create a video Pin with several pages, using the regular Create Pin flow. The strategy that made Idea Pins work still applies, and now it can send traffic somewhere.

How the format earns money now

Idea Pins never paid per view. There is no CPM payout, no built-in ad revenue, and no automatic affiliate linking. Any guide that quotes a pay rate per 1,000 Idea Pin views is inventing a number.

The income is indirect, and it works in two layers. The first layer is the Pin itself, which builds reach, engagement and trust. A good multi-page Pin gets saved, shared and followed. The second layer is where the money is made: your profile link, your standard Pins, your email list, your shop and your affiliate links. The Pin warms the audience. The other assets close the sale.

Judge a Pin by what happens after it, not by the Pin alone. A Pin that brings 100 people to your email list is worth more than a Pin that gets 10,000 views and sends nobody anywhere. Build every Pin with a next step in mind.

The 5 ways to earn with multi-page video Pins

Model How it pays What you need Best for
Affiliate priming Commission when viewers buy later Affiliate-linked standard Pins and a profile link Reviewers and niche experts
Digital products One-off sales of templates, guides or printables A product and a shop or sales page Designers, planners, teachers
Email list growth Later sales to subscribers A free resource and an email tool Bloggers and course sellers
Blog traffic and ads Ad revenue from visitors A blog with display ads Bloggers with traffic
Brand deals A flat fee for a sponsored Pin An engaged audience and a media kit Creators with a clear niche

Affiliate priming is the most common model. The video Pin teaches or demonstrates, and the viewer later finds your standard Pin for the same product, which carries the affiliate link. The Pin does not sell directly. It builds the trust that makes the later sale happen. Always disclose affiliate links clearly.

Digital products suit Pinterest well, because the audience is already looking for solutions. A Pin that shows a planner in use, page by page, is more persuasive than a static image of the same planner. Sell through Etsy, Shopify or your own site, and link the Pin to the sales page.

Email list growth is the slowest route and the most valuable. Offer a free resource, such as a checklist or a mini-guide, in exchange for an email address. The Pin teaches, the profile link offers the freebie, and the email list sells later.

Blog traffic and ads work because Pinterest traffic lasts. A Pin can send clicks for months or years after you publish it. If your blog runs display ads, that long traffic life turns into steady income.

Brand deals pay a flat fee for a sponsored Pin. Brands care about engagement, not just clicks, so a multi-page Pin that shows your process and holds attention is good proof of performance for your media kit. Tag paid partnerships clearly, as Pinterest's rules require.

Step 1: Pick a niche and one product

A niche is the topic your Pins cover. Pinterest rewards niche accounts, because the platform is a search engine dressed as a social network. People search for solutions, and a focused account is easier to find and trust. Pick a topic you enjoy, that people search for, and that has a proven path to income. Home organisation, meal planning, parenting, budgeting, fitness and crafts all work.

Then pick one product to sell first. It can be your own digital product, an affiliate product you genuinely use, or a free resource that grows your email list. One product keeps your Pins focused. If every Pin points to a different offer, viewers never learn what you sell.

Check what already works in your niche before you create anything. Search Pinterest for your topic, open the top Pins, and note what they cover and what they link to. Your Pin should be at least as useful as the best one you find.

Step 2: Build a multi-page video Pin

Open Pinterest and tap Create, then choose Pin. There is no separate Idea Pin option, and that is correct. Build the Pin from several images, video clips, or a mix of both. Keep it to 3 to 5 pages, because that length holds attention and completion better than a long sequence, even though Pinterest allows up to 20 pages.

Each page should do one job. Page one is the hook, with a bold promise in large text, such as "Declutter your pantry in one afternoon". Page two shows the problem. Page three shows the steps, one per page, with short text and a clear image. The last page is the call to action, such as "Save this for later". Use the same colours and fonts on every page.

Film or photograph in vertical format. A 2:3 ratio, such as 1000 by 1500 pixels, is the standard for Pinterest. Use good light, a clean background and high-quality images. Add text overlays in a large, easy-to-read font, and keep the words short. Avoid script fonts for long phrases. Add music from Pinterest's own library where it fits, and keep the video clips short.

Step 3: Add the link and the keywords

This is the step the old format could not do, and it is the step that turns a Pin into income. In the Pin details, add a destination link to the page that matches the Pin: the product page, the blog post, or the free resource. The link must match the promise. A Pin about a pantry checklist that links to a generic homepage will lose the click and the trust.

Write a title and a description that use the words people search for. Find those words by typing your topic into Pinterest's search bar and noting the suggested phrases. Use them naturally in the title, the description, and on the Pin image. Add 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, not 100 random ones.

Name your board well too. A board called "Pantry organisation ideas" with a keyword-rich description will rank better than a board called "Stuff I like". Pin your video Pin to the most relevant board first, then to one or two related boards over the next few days, not all at once.

Step 4: Turn viewers into email subscribers

A Pin that only earns a save is a missed opportunity. Give viewers a reason to leave Pinterest and join your list, where you can reach them again. The simplest way is a free resource that solves the next problem after the Pin. A Pin that shows a pantry declutter can offer a free printable pantry label set.

Put the freebie link in your profile, and mention it on the last page of the Pin with words such as "Get the free labels from my profile". When someone joins your list, send a short welcome sequence: one email with the freebie, one with your best tips, and one that introduces your paid product. Do not pitch on the first email. Step 5: Repeat what works

Pinterest Analytics shows you which Pins earn saves, clicks and outbound traffic. Check it every week and look for patterns. If step-by-step Pins outperform single-image Pins, make more step-by-step Pins. If one topic brings clicks and another brings only saves, put your links on the topic that brings clicks. Saves are nice, but clicks pay.

Repurpose your best Pin with a new design or a new angle, instead of starting from zero each time. A recipe Pin that worked can become a Pin about the same recipe for a different season. Batch-create Pins around your best products, and schedule them with a tool such as Tailwind so your account stays active.

Worked example: what a small Pinterest funnel could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.

Imagine a creator who sells a $15 printable home organisation bundle. She publishes 3 multi-page video Pins a week for six months. After six months, her Pins bring about 5,000 clicks a month to her site. About 2% of visitors buy the bundle, which is 100 sales, or $1,500 a month.

Her Pins also bring about 300 new email subscribers a month. Her welcome sequence sells the same bundle to about 5% of them, which is 15 more sales, or $225. Her total is about $1,725 a month. She spends about $10 a month on a scheduling tool and 8 hours a week making Pins. That is a realistic shape for a working funnel: the Pin builds traffic, the link captures the sale, and the email list adds a second layer.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting on Pinterest costs nothing. A business account is free, and it unlocks analytics. The real costs are time and tools. Plan for 5 to 8 hours a week to design Pins, write descriptions and check analytics. A scheduling tool costs about $10 to $15 a month, and a design tool such as Canva is free at the start. Product costs depend on what you sell, but digital products cost nothing to ship or store.

A realistic timeline is 3 to 6 months before Pins bring steady traffic, because Pinterest rewards fresh content over time and Pins keep working for months after you publish them. Income before that point is small and uneven. Plan for months with no sales at all. Our guide to how to sell digital products with Pinterest covers the product side in detail.

Common mistakes

  • Following old advice about Idea Pins, and building Pins with no link, when current Pins can carry one.
  • Treating a video Pin as a direct sales post, when viewers come to Pinterest to solve a problem, not to be sold to.
  • Using one generic board for everything, instead of keyword-rich boards for each topic.
  • Chasing virality instead of search intent, so Pins get views but no clicks.

Risks and scams to watch for

"Pinterest money" courses are the most common trap. A seller promises a fixed daily income, usually $50 to $100 a day, if you buy their course or "proven formula". No course can guarantee Pinterest income. Learn the platform from Pinterest's own help pages first.

Fake follower and engagement services are another trap. They sell followers, saves and repins that come from bots, and Pinterest can remove them or restrict your account.

Paid schedulers that break Pinterest's rules are a quieter risk. Use tools that Pinterest approves. Tax rules apply to Pinterest income too, and they differ by country, so check your local tax authority when income starts.

Frequently asked questions

Can you make money with Pinterest Idea Pins?

Yes, but not directly from the Pins themselves. Idea Pins never paid per view, and there is no separate Idea Pin format in 2026. You earn indirectly, by using multi-page video Pins to build reach and trust, then sending viewers to your profile link, your shop, your affiliate links or your email list, where the sale happens.

Do Idea Pins still exist in 2026?

Not as a separate organic format. Pinterest merged Idea Pins into standard Pins in May 2023. The multi-page, video, music and sticker features now live inside the regular Pin you create. Old Idea Pins still exist as Pins, and the name survives only as the paid Idea ads format.

Can you add a link to an Idea Pin?

You could not add a link to the old Idea Pin format, but you can add a destination link to a current video Pin. That is the biggest change from the merge.

How do you make money with Pinterest Idea Pins without a blog?

Sell a digital product through a shop such as Etsy or Shopify, and link the Pin to the product page. Or grow an email list with a free resource and sell to subscribers later. Affiliate links can also work, through standard Pins that carry the link, with the video Pin acting as the primer that builds trust first.

How many pages should a multi-page video Pin have?

Pinterest allows up to 20 pages, but 3 to 5 pages tends to hold attention and completion better than a longer sequence. Keep each page to one idea, put the hook on page one, and put the call to action on the last page.

Conclusion

Learning how to make money with Pinterest Idea Pins in 2026 means learning what the format became, not what it was. Pinterest retired the separate Idea Pin in 2023 and folded its best features into standard Pins, which can now carry a destination link. The format never paid per view, so income comes from what the Pin enables: profile visits, affiliate sales, digital products, email signups and brand deals. Build multi-page video Pins that teach something useful step by step, link each one to a matching page, and turn viewers into subscribers. Publish steadily, check your analytics, and repeat what brings clicks, not just saves.
