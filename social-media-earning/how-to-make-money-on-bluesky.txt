<h2 id="does-bluesky-pay-creators">Does Bluesky pay creators?</h2>
<p>No. As of November 2026, Bluesky has no ad revenue sharing, no creator fund, no in-app tipping, and no creator subscriptions. You cannot earn money from views, likes, or reposts.</p>
<p>Bluesky has said it plans to change this one day. In its Series A announcement in October 2024, the company said it would build payment services so people could support their favourite creators, plus a subscription model for premium features such as higher quality video uploads. You can read the plan in <a href="https://bsky.social/about/blog/10-24-2024-series-a" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Bluesky's own Series A announcement</a>.</p>
<p>Since then, Bluesky has grown fast but has still not launched a creator payout product. The Series B announcement in March 2026 reported growth from 13 million users to over 43 million, with 100 million dollars raised, yet no payout tool followed. A Live Now badge is being tested in beta, but it sends viewers to Twitch or Streamplace rather than paying the creator through Bluesky.</p>
<p>The one thing Bluesky does sell is custom domain handles. You can pay to use your own web address as your handle instead of yourname.bsky.social. That is money flowing to Bluesky, not from it.</p>
<p>The honest answer is simple: build the audience on Bluesky and get paid somewhere else.</p>
<h2 id="how-bluesky-monetisation-actually-works">How Bluesky monetisation actually works</h2>
<p>Bluesky monetisation is indirect. You post useful content, people follow you, and a small share of them click the link in your profile and pay you on another platform. Two facts help: Bluesky does not push down posts that contain external links, and the audience is small but unusually engaged, with many users in tech, media, writing, design, and research, so a niche expert can stand out fast.</p>

<h2 id="7-ways-to-make-money-on-bluesky">7 ways to make money on Bluesky</h2>
<p>Each method below works on its own, and most creators combine two or three. Pick the one that matches what you already know how to do.</p>
<h3 id="1-paid-newsletter">1. Paid newsletter</h3>
<p>A paid newsletter is the most natural fit for Bluesky, because the platform rewards writers. You post short, useful takes every day, and your most interested followers subscribe to a longer weekly email.</p>
<p>Set the newsletter up on a platform such as <a href="https://substack.com" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Substack</a>, which is free to start and takes a share of paid subscriptions. Price it low at first, often around 5 dollars a month. Post one free insight a day on Bluesky and end the week with a post that points to the full newsletter. A newsletter earns when the list is large and loyal, not when it is new.</p>
<h3 id="2-digital-products-and-courses">2. Digital products and courses</h3>
<p>If you know how to do something people want to learn, package it. A short guide, a set of templates, or a recorded workshop can sell from a Bluesky link. Start with one small product that solves one clear problem.</p>
<p>Price it between 9 and 49 dollars, depending on how much time it saves the buyer. Post the problem it solves on Bluesky several times a week, in different words, and link to the product page. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-create-and-sell-digital-workbooks-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to create and sell digital workbooks online</a> walks through the same idea in detail.</p>
<h3 id="3-freelance-services-and-consulting">3. Freelance services and consulting</h3>
<p>This is the fastest way to earn, because you sell your time and skill rather than waiting for an audience to grow. If you can write, design, edit video, manage social accounts, or advise on a topic, Bluesky can bring you clients. Post short examples of your work, share small wins for clients, and say plainly that you take on new clients.</p>
<p>Keep a simple services page linked in your bio, with three packages and a starting price for each. Reply to posts from people in your niche who ask for help, and answer their questions in public so others see your skill. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-social-media-manager" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a freelance social media manager</a> covers the same habits in depth.</p>
<h3 id="4-affiliate-marketing">4. Affiliate marketing</h3>
<p>Affiliate marketing means you recommend a product with a special link, and you earn a commission when someone buys through it. An affiliate commission is a small share of the sale paid to you by the seller. On Bluesky, this works best for tools and services your audience already needs, such as writing apps, design tools, books, or courses.</p>
<p>Recommend only products you have used or checked carefully, and say clearly that your link earns you a commission. One honest recommendation a week beats ten sales posts a day, because Bluesky users are quick to mute accounts that only sell. Our guides to <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-earn-money-with-instagram-affiliate-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Instagram affiliate marketing</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/social-media-earning/how-to-make-money-with-tiktok-affiliate-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">TikTok affiliate marketing</a> explain disclosure and product choice in the same way.</p>
<h3 id="5-sponsorships-and-brand-deals">5. Sponsorships and brand deals</h3>
<p>Sponsorships on Bluesky are rare and small compared with Instagram or YouTube, because the audience is smaller. They do exist in niches such as software, books, and design tools, where a few thousand engaged followers can be worth more to a brand than a large passive audience elsewhere.</p>
<p>Wait until you have a few thousand followers and steady engagement before you pitch brands. Put together a one-page media kit with your follower count, your average replies and reposts per post, and your audience niche. Pitch brands whose products you already use. Turn down products you would not recommend to a friend, because one bad recommendation costs more trust than the fee is worth.</p>
<h3 id="6-tips-and-paid-requests">6. Tips and paid requests</h3>
<p>Tips are the simplest method of all. You add a link to a tip page in your bio, and followers send you a few dollars when a post helps them. Third-party tools let followers send one-off tips or fund a small goal, such as a short film or a research project, and they take a small fee from each payment. A tip page also works for paid requests, such as a short video greeting or a custom illustration.</p>
<p>Tips will not pay your rent on their own, but they are useful early income and a clear signal of what your audience values. If people tip you for a certain kind of post, make more of that kind.</p>
<h3 id="7-private-community-and-coaching">7. Private community and coaching</h3>
<p>Once you have an audience that trusts you, you can offer a private group or one-to-one coaching. A private community might be a small chat group with a monthly fee, a weekly call, and shared resources. Coaching might be a set of four calls that help one person solve a specific problem, such as launching a newsletter or finding freelance clients.</p>
<p>Charge for the outcome, not for your time alone. A coaching package that helps someone land their first client is worth far more than an hour of general chat. Keep the group small at first and deliver real value every week. A community that goes quiet will lose members fast, so only start one when you have the time to run it properly.</p>
<h2 id="grow-your-bluesky-audience-first">Grow your Bluesky audience first</h2>
<p>Starter Packs are curated lists of accounts in a niche that new users can follow in one tap. Join the Starter Packs in your niche, and ask to be added to the ones run by people you know. A single inclusion can bring you hundreds of followers in a day, because a new user who follows a Pack follows everyone on it.</p>
<p>Custom Feeds are topic-based feeds that anyone can build or follow. Post in the style each feed rewards, and your posts will reach people who already care about your topic. You can also build your own Custom Feed for your niche.</p>
<p>Beyond those two tools, the basics still matter. Post two to three times a day, reply to bigger accounts in your niche with useful additions rather than praise, and repost the best work of others with a short comment. Pin your best post to your profile and update it every few weeks. Growth on Bluesky is slower than on TikTok, but the followers you gain tend to read, reply, and click.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/bluesky-earning-posts.jpg" alt="A person typing a post on a laptop keyboard in a home office, a phone and a notebook on the desk" /></p>
<h2 id="your-first-30-days-on-bluesky">Your first 30 days on Bluesky</h2>
<h3 id="week-1-set-up-and-listen">Week 1: Set up and listen</h3>
<p>Create your account, choose a clear handle, and write a bio that says who you help and how. Add a profile photo and a link to your newsletter signup or services page. Follow 50 accounts in your niche, join two or three Starter Packs, and post once a day. Spend more time reading and replying than posting.</p>
<h3 id="week-2-post-daily-and-start-your-list">Week 2: Post daily and start your list</h3>
<p>Post twice a day, morning and evening. Reply to at least five posts a day in your niche. Set up your off-platform home, such as a free newsletter on Substack or a simple services page, and link it in your bio. Note which posts get replies, and make more of those.</p>
<h3 id="week-3-launch-one-offer">Week 3: Launch one offer</h3>
<p>Pick your first offer, such as a small service, a tip page, or a short digital product. Pin a post about it to your profile. Post about the problem it solves three times during the week, in different words, and answer every reply. Keep posting useful free content alongside the offer.</p>
<h3 id="week-4-review-and-repeat">Week 4: Review and repeat</h3>
<p>Look at your numbers. Which posts brought followers, replies, and clicks? Do more of those and less of the rest. If you have any subscribers or clients, ask them what they want next. Most Bluesky accounts earn nothing in the first month, and that is normal.</p>
<h2 id="worked-example-what-a-small-bluesky-audience-could-earn">Worked example: what a small Bluesky audience could earn</h2>
<p><strong>Example only: these numbers are not typical results.</strong> This example shows the maths, not a promise. Real results depend on your niche, your consistency, and how many followers actually click.</p>
<p>Imagine you post daily for six months and grow to 5,000 followers. You run a free newsletter and offer a paid tier at 5 dollars a month.</p>
<ul>
<li>Profile visits from your posts each month: 500, which is 10 percent of your followers.</li>
<li>Visitors who click through to the newsletter page: 10 percent of 500, which is 50 people.</li>
<li>Visitors who sign up free: 40 percent of 50, which is 20 new free subscribers a month.</li>
<li>Free subscribers after six months: 20 times 6, which is 120 people.</li>
<li>Free subscribers who upgrade to paid: 5 percent of 120, which is 6 people.</li>
<li>Monthly income: 6 times 5 dollars, which is 30 dollars a month.</li>
<li>After the newsletter platform takes its share, you keep around 27 dollars a month.</li>
</ul>
<p>That is a small amount after six months of daily posting, but the shape of the maths is the point. Income grows when followers grow, when more of them click, and when more of them pay.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Posting only about your product or service, which makes people mute you instead of following you.</li>
<li>Choosing a niche that is too broad, such as "business" or "life", instead of one clear topic.</li>
<li>Copying viral posts from other platforms instead of writing in your own voice for the Bluesky audience.</li>
<li>Ignoring replies, which wastes the best growth tool on the platform, because replies put you in front of new readers.</li>
<li>Quitting after three weeks because the first month earned nothing.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Watch for direct messages from accounts that claim to be brands and offer you a paid deal, then ask you to pay a fee, buy a product first, or share bank details. Real brands pay you, not the other way round.</p>
<p>Do not buy followers or pay for a "growth service" that promises thousands of followers in a week. Bought followers do not read, click, or buy, and they damage your engagement rate. If someone offers you a deal that sounds too good, treat it as a scam until proven otherwise.</p>
<p>Keep records of any income from the start, because tax rules differ by country and you may need to declare earnings even when they are small. Never share your login details or a verification code with anyone, and turn on two-factor authentication on every account that holds your money.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="does-bluesky-pay-creators-for-views-or-engagement">Does Bluesky pay creators for views or engagement?</h3>
<p>No. Bluesky has no payment for views, likes, or reposts as of November 2026. Creators earn by sending their audience to a newsletter, a product, a service, or a tip page outside Bluesky, and no launch date for payment services has been announced.</p>
<h3 id="can-you-make-money-on-bluesky-without-many-followers">Can you make money on Bluesky without many followers?</h3>
<p>Yes, if you sell a service. A freelance writer, designer, or consultant can earn from a few hundred followers when those followers are the right people. Newsletters and products need a bigger audience, because only a small share of followers ever pays.</p>
<h3 id="will-bluesky-pay-creators-in-the-future">Will Bluesky pay creators in the future?</h3>
<p>Probably, in some form, but nobody outside Bluesky knows when or how much. Until a product actually launches, treat any income plan that depends on Bluesky paying you as a guess, and build your income off the platform instead.</p>
<h3 id="do-you-need-a-website-to-earn-from-bluesky">Do you need a website to earn from Bluesky?</h3>
<p>Not strictly, but you need somewhere for money to change hands. A newsletter page, a product page, or a simple services page all work. A link in your bio that leads nowhere earns nothing, so set up the destination before you start growing.</p>
<h3 id="is-bluesky-good-for-creators">Is Bluesky good for creators?</h3>
<p>It can be, for the right creator. Writers, researchers, designers, and niche experts do well, because the audience is engaged and the feed does not punish external links. If your income depends on viral short video or a huge audience, Bluesky is the wrong platform today.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>To make money on Bluesky in 2026, build an audience on the platform and get paid off it. Start with the fastest method for your skills, usually freelance services or affiliate marketing, then add a newsletter or a digital product as your audience grows. Post daily, join Starter Packs, use Custom Feeds, and launch one clear offer in your first month. Give the plan three to six months before you judge it.</p>
