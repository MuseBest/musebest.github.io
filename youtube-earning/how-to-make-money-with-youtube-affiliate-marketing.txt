35:T5fd0,<h2 id="how-to-make-money-with-youtube-affiliate-marketing-in-2026">How to Make Money with YouTube Affiliate Marketing in 2026</h2>
<p>YouTube affiliate marketing means you recommend a product in a video and earn a commission when a viewer buys through your link. It is one of the best income streams for small channels, because it has no subscriber minimum. You can earn commission from your first video, while ad revenue needs 1,000 subscribers first. This guide shows you how to make money with YouTube affiliate marketing in 2026, pick products that fit your videos, place links where viewers can find them, disclose honestly and track what actually earns.</p>
<p>This guide is for creators who already have a channel, or who are starting one with a clear niche. You do not need a big audience to start. You need videos that answer buying questions, products you have actually used and a simple system for links and disclosure. For the basic channel setup steps, read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/how-to-start-youtube-channel" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start a YouTube channel</a>. This guide covers affiliate marketing only.</p>

<h2 id="how-youtube-affiliate-marketing-works">How YouTube affiliate marketing works</h2>
<p>There are two ways to earn commission on YouTube. The first is your own affiliate links. You join a programme, get a tracking link for a product and put it in your video description and pinned comment. This works at any channel size, and you can promote anything sold online. The second is the YouTube Shopping affiliate programme. Eligible channels tag products directly in videos, Shorts and live streams, and viewers buy on the retailer's site. YouTube pays the commission through AdSense.</p>
<p>The two routes can run side by side. Tags catch viewers on a phone who never open a description. Links cover every product outside the Shopping programme's retailer list. Most channels start with their own links, because the Shopping programme has eligibility rules.</p>
<p>Affiliate income is different from ad revenue. Ads pay for views. Affiliate commission pays for purchases. A video with 5,000 views that recommends a product viewers want can out-earn a video with 200,000 views about something nobody buys. That is why the video matters more than the link. The video does the selling before anyone clicks.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-pick-a-niche-with-buying-intent">Step 1: Pick a niche with buying intent</h2>
<p>Affiliate marketing works best when viewers are already close to a buying decision. Product reviews, tutorials, tech, software, finance, education tools and creator tools usually perform well, because viewers search for help choosing, using or comparing products.</p>
<p>A simple test helps. If people search for "best", "review", "versus", "how to use" or "top tools" in your niche, it likely has affiliate potential. If people only search for entertainment, affiliate income will be slow. Pick a niche where the product is part of the answer, not an afterthought.</p>
<p>Stay in one niche. A channel about cameras converts on camera gear and earns nothing on kitchen appliances, whatever the commission rate looks like. Trust builds when every recommendation fits the channel. Read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/how-to-choose-affiliate-niche" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose an affiliate niche</a> before you commit to one.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-choose-products-that-match-the-viewer">Step 2: Choose products that match the viewer</h2>
<p>Do not promote random products. The best affiliate content is built around the viewer's problem. If your video teaches YouTube SEO, the natural affiliate angle may be a keyword tool, a thumbnail tool or an editing app. If your video is about home workouts, the natural angle may be resistance bands, a fitness app or a yoga mat.</p>
<p>Use the product yourself before you recommend it. Film it in use, show its downsides honestly and say who should not buy it. A review that only praises reads like an ad, and viewers can tell. Honesty is what makes the recommendation believable, and believable recommendations are what earn clicks.</p>
<p>Check the commission and the cookie window before you build a video around a product. The cookie window is the number of days after a click during which a purchase still earns you commission. Software programmes often pay recurring commission each month a referred customer stays subscribed. Physical goods usually pay a smaller one-time commission. Compare programmes in our guide to the <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/best-affiliate-programs-for-beginners" rel="noopener" target="_blank">best affiliate programs for beginners</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-join-programmes-that-allow-youtube-traffic">Step 3: Join programmes that allow YouTube traffic</h2>
<p>Not every programme treats YouTube the same way. Before you build a video around a product, check three things in the programme terms.</p>
<p>First, is YouTube an approved promotional method? Many networks ask you to list your websites and social channels during signup. Add your YouTube channel as a promotional property on every network you use, such as Impact, CJ, Awin or PartnerStack. If a brand approves you based on a blog only, it may not expect video traffic.</p>
<p>Second, does the programme know about your channel? Amazon treats your social media content as part of your "site". Add your channel in your Associates account settings so Amazon can see where clicks come from. Amazon's rules also ban hiding the source of your links and ban redirect links that send viewers to Amazon through another site. For Amazon, use Amazon's own links in your description.</p>
<p>Third, are there limits on claims, coupons or brand terms? Some brands restrict coupon codes, price claims or comparison language. Software and finance programmes are often strict about claims. Read the terms before you film, and email the affiliate manager if anything is unclear.</p>

<p>Some videos earn commissions. Others only earn views. The ones that earn usually match a viewer who is close to a buying decision. Plan your first 10 affiliate videos as a mix of reviews, comparisons, tutorials and roundups, all in one niche.</p>

Give one clear reason to click, such as "I linked the tool I use for thumbnails in the description if you want to try it." Do not say "links below" with no context, because viewers ignore vague calls to action.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-place-links-where-viewers-can-find-them">Step 5: Place links where viewers can find them</h2>
<p>Placement decides how many people ever see your link. The description is where most links live, and the first two lines carry the weight. Anything below that stays hidden on mobile until a viewer taps to expand, so put the link people need at the top rather than in a list of twelve.</p>
<p>Use a simple description structure. Line 1 and 2 hold a one-sentence summary, your main affiliate link and a short disclosure. Then add chapters with timestamps, a product list with one link per product in the order you cover them, and a full disclosure line at the end. Keep the language natural and helpful.</p>
<p>Pin a comment with your top recommendation and link. Many viewers read comments before the description, so the pinned comment is a second chance to catch them. Keep the disclosure in the pinned comment too, because it may be the only thing a viewer reads.</p>
<p>Say the link out loud on camera as well. Telling viewers the link is below costs three seconds and lifts clicks more than any formatting trick. Make sure your links are clickable. YouTube's help page on sharing links says URLs in long-form video descriptions and comments are clickable, but you must enable your channel's access to advanced features to add clickable external links. URLs in Shorts comments and descriptions are not clickable, so use Shorts to send viewers to a long video where your links live. Set your upload defaults in YouTube Studio with your disclosure line and permanent links, so they appear on every new upload without retyping.</p>

<h2 id="step-6-use-youtube-shopping-tags-when-you-qualify">Step 6: Use YouTube Shopping tags when you qualify</h2>
<p>The YouTube Shopping affiliate programme lets eligible creators tag products from participating brands right in their content. Viewers can browse a product shelf below the video and buy on the retailer's site, while the commission is paid through AdSense.</p>
<p>To be invited, your channel must be in the YouTube Partner Programme, meet the subscriber threshold YouTube sets, be based in one of the supported markets and not be a music channel or an Official Artist Channel. Your audience must not be set as made for kids, and you must not have many videos set as made for kids. As of November 2026, the supported markets include the United States, the United Kingdom, India, Japan, Brazil and several others. Check the <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398" rel="noopener" target="_blank">YouTube Shopping affiliate programme eligibility page</a> for the current list, because it changes over time.</p>
<p>Tagging happens in YouTube Studio. You search for a product, attach it to a video, a Short or a livestream, and the shopping row appears for viewers. Tag products in your best-performing reviews first. Commissions are paid through AdSense within 60 to 120 days after purchase, and returns can reverse a commission, so track your sales with that delay in mind.</p>
<p>The Shopping programme is a useful extra, not a replacement for your own links. It only covers products from participating sellers. Your own links cover everything else, and they work from day one without any subscriber threshold. For the ad revenue side of the programme, check the <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851" rel="noopener" target="_blank">official YouTube Partner Programme eligibility page</a> and read <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/how-to-monetize-youtube-channel" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to monetize a YouTube channel</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-disclose-clearly-on-camera-and-in-writing">Step 7: Disclose clearly, on camera and in writing</h2>
<p>Disclosure has two layers, and you need both. The first layer is YouTube's paid promotion setting. When a brand paid you, sent you the product for free or you have a deal beyond a standard affiliate link, tick the paid promotion box in Studio. YouTube then adds a disclosure label at the start of the video. The box alone does not cover you for the law, so you need the second layer too.</p>
<p>The second layer is telling viewers directly. Say it out loud near the start of the video: "Some links below are affiliate links, so I may earn a commission if you buy." Show the same message on screen for a few seconds. Put a short disclosure in the first two lines of the description and in the pinned comment. Repeat the disclosure in live streams, because viewers who join late miss the start.</p>
<p>Never hide an affiliate link. Never call a paid promotion an honest review without disclosure. A channel that discloses clearly keeps trust, and trust is what makes viewers click. Read our affiliate disclosure page for how EarnQuill handles this, and link to it from your own site if you run one.</p>
<h2 id="step-8-track-clicks-and-sales-then-repeat-what-works">Step 8: Track clicks and sales, then repeat what works</h2>
<p>Affiliate marketing only improves when you know which videos earn. Create a separate tracking link for each product rather than reusing one link across a series, because a description with eight identical links is unreadable in your reports. Most networks show clicks, conversions and earnings per link, so check them weekly.</p>
<p>Track two numbers early. Click through rate on your links tells you whether viewers are finding them. Conversion rate tells you whether the product matched the audience. Low clicks is a placement problem, so move the link higher or say it out loud. Clicks without sales is a product problem, so try a different product or a different video angle.</p>
<p>Build clusters around the same product category or problem. For example, a set of videos on the best tools for YouTube growth, a tutorial for one tool, a comparison of two tools and a roundup of free tools can all point to the same offers. Clusters improve trust, topical authority and click consistency.</p>

<h2 id="worked-example">Worked example</h2>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works for a small affiliate channel.</p>
<p>Imagine a review video that gets 5,000 views in a month. Suppose 2 percent of viewers click the affiliate link. Multiply 5,000 by 0.02 to get 100 clicks.</p>
<p>Now suppose 5 percent of those clickers buy the product. Multiply 100 by 0.05 to get 5 sales. If each sale earns a $20 commission, multiply 5 by $20 to get $100.</p>
<p>So one video earns $100 in that month from affiliate commission. This example uses made-up rates to show the method. Real click rates, conversion rates and commissions vary widely by product and audience, so your own numbers will differ.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Promoting products unrelated to the video or the channel.</li>
<li>Using too many links in one description, so viewers click none of them.</li>
<li>Hiding the affiliate disclosure at the bottom of the description.</li>
<li>Making videos with weak trust signals, such as no demo and no downsides.</li>
<li>Choosing products with poor audience fit because the commission looks high.</li>
<li>Ignoring analytics and keeping low-converting offers in every video.</li>
<li>Routing Amazon links through your own redirect, which Amazon's rules ban.</li>
<li>Building a link farm channel with no real content, which breaks YouTube's spam rules.</li>
<li>Promoting a product you have never used, or hiding that a link pays you.</li>
<li>Judging affiliate income after three videos instead of after thirty.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Affiliate marketing has real risks. Some programmes delay or refuse payment, so read the payment terms before you promote a product. Check the minimum payout, the payment method and the payout timing. Some programmes pay only after a returns window of 60 to 120 days, so plan your cash flow with that delay in mind.</p>
<p>Watch for fake affiliate programmes that ask you to pay a joining fee or buy a "starter kit" before you can earn. Real programmes are free to join. They earn money when you earn money. If a programme asks for payment upfront, walk away.</p>
<p>Be careful with your reputation. Promoting a poor product for a high commission costs you trust, and trust is what makes viewers click your next link. If a product disappoints you, say so in the video and remove the link. Your audience will respect the honesty more than they would have liked the product.</p>
<p>Check the tax rules in your country. Affiliate income is taxable income in most countries, and some networks ask for tax forms before they pay you. Keep records of every payment, and check with a qualified accountant if you are unsure how to report it.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-need-subscribers-to-start-youtube-affiliate-marketing">Do I need subscribers to start YouTube affiliate marketing?</h3>
<p>No. Affiliate links in your description or pinned comment need no approval from YouTube and no minimum audience. You can add them from your first video, as long as your channel has access to advanced features for clickable links. The YouTube Shopping affiliate programme is different: it needs Partner Programme membership and a subscriber threshold, so treat it as a later step.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-can-i-earn-from-youtube-affiliate-marketing">How much can I earn from YouTube affiliate marketing?</h3>
<p>It depends on what you promote rather than how many views you get. A small audience recommending expensive products can out-earn a large one recommending cheap ones. Software programmes often pay recurring commission, while physical goods usually pay a smaller one-time commission. Most creators earn very little in their first few months, and income grows as the video library grows.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-the-youtube-partner-programme-for-affiliate-links">Do I need the YouTube Partner Programme for affiliate links?</h3>
<p>No. The Partner Programme covers ad revenue and Shopping tags. Affiliate links from a network work on any channel, whatever its size. You do need the Partner Programme for the Shopping affiliate programme and for ad revenue, so check the official eligibility page when you are ready for those.</p>
<h3 id="where-should-i-put-affiliate-links-on-youtube">Where should I put affiliate links on YouTube?</h3>
<p>Put your main link in the first two lines of the video description, because the rest stays hidden on mobile until a viewer taps to expand. Pin a comment with the same link, and say it out loud on camera. If you qualify for the Shopping programme, tag the product in the video too. Keep the list short: one link per product, in the order you cover them.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-disclose-affiliate-links-on-youtube">How do I disclose affiliate links on YouTube?</h3>
<p>Say it out loud near the start of the video, show it on screen for a few seconds, put a short disclosure in the first two lines of the description and in the pinned comment, and tick the paid promotion box when a brand paid you or sent you the product for free. The spoken disclosure matters most, because most viewers never read a description.</p>
<h3 id="which-affiliate-programmes-work-best-on-youtube">Which affiliate programmes work best on YouTube?</h3>
<p>Programmes that match your niche work best. Software and tools often pay recurring commission of 20 to 40 percent. Physical goods through Amazon Associates usually pay a smaller percentage with a short cookie window. Finance and business products can pay a fixed fee per lead. Compare programmes by commission, cookie window, payment terms and audience fit, not by commission alone.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Making money with YouTube affiliate marketing takes videos that answer buying questions, products you have actually used and honest disclosure. Pick one niche with buying intent, make reviews and comparisons that show the product in use, place one clear link where viewers can find it and track what earns. Then repeat what works and build clusters around the same offers. Do not chase quick money with hidden links or products you would not use yourself, because trust is the asset that pays in this niche.</p>
