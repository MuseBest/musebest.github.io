<p>YouTube SEO is how you help the right viewers find your videos, and it is the skill that separates a good video nobody watches from a good video that grows. This guide explains how to rank your videos on YouTube search, how Suggested videos work, and how to get a video into Google search too. It is written for beginners with a small channel.</p>

<h3 id="how-youtube-search-ranking-actually-works">How YouTube search ranking actually works</h3>
<p>YouTube SEO means optimising a video so that YouTube understands what it is about and shows it to people who search for that topic. YouTube ranks videos on three things: relevance, engagement, and quality. Relevance means the video matches the search, which YouTube judges from the title, description, captions, and the words spoken in the video. Engagement means viewers click, watch, and stay, which YouTube reads as a sign the video is useful. Quality means the video answers the query well, which YouTube judges over time from how viewers behave.</p>
<p>Views alone do not rank a video. A video with 100,000 views on a broad topic will not outrank a focused video with 2,000 views when a viewer searches for a narrow question, if the focused video answers that question better. YouTube wants to show the video most likely to satisfy the viewer, not the video with the biggest number. That is good news for a small channel.</p>
<p>No one can promise a ranking. YouTube does not publish its exact ranking formula, and rankings shift as new videos appear. What you can do is give YouTube clear signals about your video, make the video good enough to keep viewers watching, and repeat that on one narrow topic.</p>
<h3 id="the-two-surfaces-youtube-search-and-suggested">The two surfaces: YouTube search and Suggested</h3>
<p>Your video can be found in two places inside YouTube, and each one needs different work. YouTube search is where a viewer types a question and picks a result. Suggested videos are the list beside or below the video a viewer is already watching. Search rewards clear keywords and a direct answer. Suggested rewards watch time and a strong match with the video the viewer just watched.</p>
<p>Search is the better target for a new channel, because a viewer who searches has a clear need, and a good answer can win even from a small channel. Suggested is harder at the start, because YouTube shows Suggested videos from channels it already trusts. As your channel grows on one topic, Suggested starts to work. Most of your videos should target search, because that is where a small channel can grow.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-pick-a-keyword-people-search">Step 1: Pick a keyword people search</h3>
<p>Start with the keyword before you record anything. A keyword is the phrase a viewer types into the search box. Good keywords for a small channel are specific, such as how to change a bicycle tyre, not broad, such as cycling. Broad keywords are fought over by big channels with years of history. Specific keywords have fewer rivals, and the viewers who search them know exactly what they want.</p>
<p>Test a keyword in three checks before you commit to it. First, type it into the YouTube search box and read the autocomplete suggestions. If your phrase appears, people search it. Second, open the top three results and ask whether you can beat them. If they are strong, detailed, and recent, pick a narrower version of the keyword. Third, search the same phrase on Google and look for a video block in the results. If Google shows videos, the topic also has a chance to rank outside YouTube. Write the keyword down and keep it in front of you while you plan, record, and upload.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/youtube-keyword-research-notes.jpg" alt="A notebook with a handwritten list of short phrases and a pen, beside a laptop showing a search box, on a bright desk" /></p>
<h3 id="step-2-plan-the-video-around-the-keyword-before-you-record">Step 2: Plan the video around the keyword before you record</h3>
<p>Plan the video around the keyword before you press record, because YouTube reads the words you say as well as the words you type. Say the keyword out loud in the first 30 seconds, in a natural sentence. Answer the question directly in the first 30 seconds too, before you explain the detail. A viewer who searches for an answer and gets it at once is more likely to stay, and staying is what YouTube measures.</p>
<p>Outline the video as three to five points that answer the keyword, in the order a viewer needs them. For a how-to keyword, the order is the steps of the job. For a question keyword, the order is the short answer first, then the detail, then the exceptions. Check that every point serves the keyword, and cut any point that does not.</p>
<h3 id="step-3-write-the-title">Step 3: Write the title</h3>
<p>Put the keyword at the front of the title, in plain words, and add one clear promise after it. A good title for the keyword how to change a bicycle tyre is "How to Change a Bicycle Tyre in 10 Minutes (No Special Tools)". It starts with the keyword, it promises a time, and it removes a worry. A weak title is "My Bike Day!", which tells YouTube and the viewer nothing.</p>
<p>Keep the title under 70 characters where you can, because long titles get cut off on phones. Use one promise, not three, and do not stuff the title with extra keywords. If the video is aimed at beginners, say so, because beginners search with the word beginner. Do not change a title that is already working. Change it only when the video is not ranking after a few weeks, and change only the title, not the thumbnail at the same time, so you can tell which change helped. For more detail, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/how-to-write-youtube-titles" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to write YouTube titles</a>.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-write-the-description">Step 4: Write the description</h3>
<p>The description is the text below the video, and its first two lines matter most, because they show in search results and on the watch page. Put the keyword in the first two lines, in a natural sentence that says what the video covers. Then write a short summary of the video in two to four sentences, covering the main points in order.</p>
<p>Add chapters to the description, with a time and a short name for each section, such as 00:00 Introduction, 01:20 Tools you need, 03:40 Removing the tyre. Chapters help viewers jump to the part they need, and they give YouTube more text to understand the video. Add links after the chapters, such as a link to your related video or playlist. Do not fill the description with keywords or links. It can be up to 5,000 characters, but most viewers will read only the first few lines. Write those lines as if they were the only ones that matter, because for search, they nearly are.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-design-the-thumbnail">Step 5: Design the thumbnail</h3>
<p>The thumbnail is the still image viewers see before they click, and it works with the title to earn the click. Use one clear idea, readable at the size of a phone screen. Show the result, the tool, or the key moment of the video, with strong contrast and two or three words at most. Do not repeat the full title in the thumbnail. The title and the thumbnail should add to each other, not say the same thing twice.</p>
<p>YouTube recommends a custom thumbnail, and custom thumbnails are the norm on the best-performing videos. Upload a 1280 by 720 pixel image, and check it at a small size before you publish. If you cannot read it when it is the size of a postage stamp, simplify it. Keep the style steady across your videos, so viewers learn to spot your videos in a list.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/youtube-video-thumbnail-ideas.jpg" alt="A desk with printed thumbnail sketch cards showing simple bold shapes and one plain design, with a laptop and markers nearby" /></p>
<p>For more detail, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/how-to-design-youtube-thumbnails" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to design YouTube thumbnails</a>.</p>
<h3 id="step-6-add-chapters-and-corrected-captions">Step 6: Add chapters and corrected captions</h3>
<p>Chapters divide the video into named sections, and each chapter name is a chance to use a searchable phrase. Name chapters with the words a viewer would search, such as "Tools you need" or "Removing the old tyre", not with vague labels such as "Part 1". Add the chapters to the description with timestamps, and YouTube will show them on the progress bar. Viewers who can jump to the part they need stay longer, and a video that keeps viewers is a video YouTube will show more.</p>
<p>Captions are the text version of the spoken words, and they help YouTube understand the video. YouTube can generate captions for you, but you should read them and fix the errors, especially names, numbers, and technical terms. Say the keyword out loud in the video, and check that the captions spell it right. Corrected captions are a small job with a real payoff, because they give YouTube accurate text for the whole video, not only the title and description.</p>
<h3 id="step-7-tags-and-file-name">Step 7: Tags and file name</h3>
<p>Tags are short labels you add in YouTube Studio, such as bicycle tyre change or beginner bike repair. They play a minimal role in ranking, according to YouTube's own documentation, so do not spend long on them. Add five to ten tags that describe the video, including the keyword and one or two close variations, then move on. Tags will not save a video with a weak title and a poor first 30 seconds, and they will not hurt a good one.</p>
<p>The file name of the video matters a little more than tags, because YouTube reads it when you upload. Name the file with the keyword, in hyphens, such as how-to-change-a-bicycle-tyre.mp4, instead of a camera default such as VID_0042.mp4. It takes ten seconds, and it is one more clear signal about the topic.</p>
<h3 id="step-8-publish-and-promote-in-the-first-48-hours">Step 8: Publish and promote in the first 48 hours</h3>
<p>The first 48 hours after you publish are when YouTube tests the video on a small audience and watches what happens. Help it by sending real viewers to the video. Share it with your email list, post it in your community tab, embed it on your own website where it fits the page, and answer the first comments quickly. Early comments and early watch time tell YouTube that the video is worth showing to more people.</p>
<p>Do not buy views, subscribers, or watch time. Buying engagement breaks YouTube rules, and the official <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392" rel="noopener" target="_blank">YouTube channel monetisation policies</a> page says that artificially inflating views, subscribers, likes, watch time, or ad impressions can lead to removal from the Partner Program or termination of the channel. A small number of real viewers who watch to the end is worth far more than bought views that leave in ten seconds. Publish at a steady time, so your viewers learn when to expect a new video.</p>

<h3 id="step-9-read-the-numbers-that-matter">Step 9: Read the numbers that matter</h3>
<p>After two weeks, open YouTube Studio and read four numbers. Impressions click-through rate is the share of people who clicked after seeing the thumbnail. Average view duration is how long viewers watched, on average. The retention curve shows where viewers left, second by second. The search terms report shows the exact words viewers typed before they found your video.</p>
<p>Read them in order. If the click-through rate is low, the title or thumbnail is the problem, not the video. If viewers leave in the first 30 seconds, the hook is the problem. If they leave halfway through one section, that section is slow or unclear. If the search terms report shows words you did not expect, those words are ideas for your next video. Check at 14 days and at 30 days, write down the four numbers, and compare them with the last video. For a full guide to the numbers, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/youtube-analytics-explained" rel="noopener" target="_blank">YouTube analytics explained</a>.</p>
<h3 id="step-10-iterate-one-change-at-a-time">Step 10: Iterate, one change at a time</h3>
<p>If a video is not ranking after a month, change one thing, then wait two weeks and check again. Change the title, or change the thumbnail, but never both at once, because then you cannot tell which change helped. A good first change is a clearer title that puts the keyword first. A good second change is a simpler thumbnail with one idea. A good third change is a better hook in the first 30 seconds, which means re-editing the start of the video, not re-uploading it as a new video.</p>
<p>When a video ranks and then slips, the same rule applies. Do not panic and rewrite everything. Check whether a newer video now answers the search better, and if so, update your video to close the gap. Small, patient changes beat big, rushed rewrites.</p>
<h3 id="ranking-in-google">Ranking in Google</h3>
<p>Ranking in Google search is a separate job from ranking in YouTube search, and it has one key rule. Google shows a video thumbnail in its results mainly when the video is the main content of the page it sits on. Embedding a video on a page about the same topic can help, but embedding it on a page that cannot rank will not make it rank. The page must be useful on its own, with the video as the centre of it.</p>
<p>To help a video rank on Google, embed it on a page that answers the same question in text, with the video near the top, and with the page title matching the search. Add VideoObject structured data to the page if your site supports it, with the video name, thumbnail, and upload date. That data helps Google understand the video, but it does not guarantee a ranking. Most small channel videos will rank on YouTube search first, and only the strongest will also appear on Google.</p>
<h3 id="channel-level-seo">Channel-level SEO</h3>
<p>Channel-level SEO is the work you do once, on the channel itself, that helps every video. Start with the name and handle. The channel name should say what the channel is about, or be a name viewers can remember and spell. The handle, the short name after the @ sign, should match the name closely.</p>
<p>Write the About section with the channel promise in the first line, then two or three sentences on who the channel is for and what they get. Use the main topic words naturally, but do not stuff the About section with keywords. Organise videos into playlists by topic, so each video lifts the others. A playlist on one narrow topic keeps viewers watching in order, and that watch time helps the whole playlist rank.</p>
<h3 id="shorts-seo-in-brief">Shorts SEO in brief</h3>
<p>Shorts SEO follows the same rule: say the phrase in the caption, on screen, and out loud. Put the keyword in the Short title or caption, show it as text on screen in the first seconds, and say it out loud early. Keep the caption short, add two or three hashtags that match the topic, and link the Short to the related long video where YouTube allows it. For the full method, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/youtube-earning/how-to-repurpose-long-videos-into-youtube-shorts" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to repurpose long videos into YouTube Shorts</a>.</p>
<h3 id="what-a-new-channel-should-expect">What a new channel should expect</h3>
<p>A new channel should expect months, not days, before videos rank. YouTube needs time to learn what your channel is about, and that learning comes from volume on one narrow topic. A realistic plan is eight to twelve videos on one topic, published weekly, before you judge whether the channel can rank. One video tells YouTube almost nothing. Ten videos on the same topic tell it a lot.</p>
<p>Expect most early videos to get few views. That is normal, and it does not mean the videos are bad. It means the channel has no history yet. Each video that answers a searched question adds a small signal, and those signals build over months. If you need to earn from YouTube, know the thresholds first. The official <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851" rel="noopener" target="_blank">YouTube Partner Program overview and eligibility</a> page, checked on November 7, 2026, lists 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.</p>
<h3 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Stuffing the title and description with keywords, so the video reads like spam and viewers skip it.</li>
<li>Buying views, subscribers, or watch time, which breaks YouTube rules and can end the channel.</li>
<li>Changing the title and the thumbnail at the same time, so you cannot tell which change helped.</li>
<li>Chasing a broad keyword that big channels already own, instead of a narrow keyword you can win.</li>
<li>Skipping chapters and captions, which are the cheapest ranking signals you have.</li>
<li>Spending an hour on tags, which play a minimal role, while the first 30 seconds of the video stay weak.</li>
<li>Checking rankings every day, which creates worry and leads to rushed changes.</li>
<li>Re-uploading a video as new when it slips, instead of updating the title, thumbnail, or opening.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>How long does it take for a YouTube video to rank?</h4>
<p>A video can take weeks to rank, and a new channel should expect months before its videos rank steadily. YouTube needs time to learn what the channel is about, and that learning comes from several videos on one narrow topic. Check the numbers at 14 and 30 days, and judge the channel after eight to twelve videos, not after one.</p>
<h4>Do YouTube tags help videos rank?</h4>
<p>Tags play a minimal role in ranking, according to YouTube's own documentation. Add five to ten tags that describe the video, including the keyword and one or two close variations, then move on. The title, the first 30 seconds, the chapters, and the captions matter far more than tags.</p>
<h4>What is the best length for a YouTube title?</h4>
<p>Keep the title under 70 characters where you can, because long titles get cut off on phones. Put the keyword at the front, add one clear promise, and use one idea only.</p>
<h4>How do I get my YouTube video to show up in Google search?</h4>
<p>Embed the video on a page where the video is the main content, with the page answering the same question in text and the video near the top. Add VideoObject structured data with the video name, thumbnail, and upload date if your site supports it. Google ranks pages that satisfy the searcher, so the page must be useful on its own, and a ranking is never guaranteed.</p>
<h4>Should I buy views to help a new video rank?</h4>
<p>No. Buying views breaks YouTube rules, and the official monetisation policies say that artificially inflating views, subscribers, likes, watch time, or ad impressions can lead to removal from the Partner Program or termination of the channel.</p>
<h3 id="conclusion-one-keyword-one-video-ten-times-over">Conclusion: one keyword, one video, ten times over</h3>
<p>YouTube SEO is a simple method repeated with care. Pick one keyword people search, plan the video around it before you record, put the keyword first in the title and in the first lines of the description, add chapters and corrected captions, design one clear thumbnail, and read the numbers at 14 and 30 days. Then do it again on the same topic, eight to twelve times, and let the channel earn trust.</p>
