How to Make Money with YouTube Channel Memberships in 2026

Learning how to make money with YouTube channel memberships is worth your time if you already have loyal viewers who watch most of your videos. A membership is a monthly payment a viewer makes to your channel. In return, you give that viewer extra perks, such as badges, emoji, early access to videos, and members only content. It is a small but steady income stream, and it rewards creators who look after a community.

This guide is for beginners with a small channel who want recurring income from their most loyal viewers. You will learn who can turn on memberships, how to set up levels and prices, which perks are worth your time, how to promote the Join button, and how the money reaches you. For the general channel setup steps, read how to start a YouTube channel.

What YouTube channel memberships are

A YouTube channel membership is a monthly subscription to one channel. A viewer who joins becomes a member, and YouTube charges that viewer every month until the viewer cancels. A member is different from a subscriber. A subscriber follows your channel for free. A member pays each month and gets extra perks in return. Most subscribers never become members, and that is normal.

Members usually get loyalty badges that change the longer they stay, custom emoji for comments and live chat, and access to members only posts, videos, and live streams. Some creators also offer early access, which means members see a video a few days before it goes public. The strongest perks are the ones only members can use, because a badge alone rarely convinces a viewer to pay every month.

Who can turn on channel memberships

Memberships are part of the YouTube Partner Program, which is the programme that lets creators earn money on YouTube. The YouTube Partner Program overview page, checked on 2026-11-11, lists the full tier for ad revenue: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Watch hours means the total time viewers spend watching your videos, and only public long-form video watch hours count. You also need to follow the channel monetisation policies, live in a country or region where the programme is available, have no active Community Guidelines strikes, have 2-Step Verification turned on, have advanced features access, and have one active AdSense for YouTube account linked. Meeting the threshold does not guarantee acceptance, and the review typically takes about 1 month.

A lower fan funding tier is reported by several guides that cite the YouTube Help expanded programme page. Under that tier, a channel needs 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. That tier unlocks channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks, YouTube Shopping, and Creator Partnerships, but it pays no ad revenue. The overview page as opened on 2026-11-11 does not state this tier, so treat it as reported and confirm it in YouTube Studio under Earn. For the full monetisation picture, read how to monetise a YouTube channel.

Memberships have extra rules, stated on the channel memberships help page. Your channel must be in a location where memberships are available, and it must not be set as made for kids. Made for kids means a channel or video aimed mainly at children. Your channel must not have a significant number of made for kids videos or ineligible videos, such as videos with music claims. Some music channels under a certain type of contract are not eligible. You and any network you work with must also accept the membership terms, including the Commerce Product Module, which covers paid products on YouTube.

From February 1, 2027, YouTube announced on 10 August 2026 that new applicants for the ad revenue tier will need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. The fan funding tier is reported to stay unchanged. Members must accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027. Confirm the current terms in YouTube Studio, because the overview page as opened on 2026-11-11 did not yet state the change.

Step 1: Turn on memberships in YouTube Studio

YouTube Studio is the dashboard where you manage your channel. Open it on a computer, click Earn in the left menu, find Memberships, and click Get Started. Read the terms, including the Commerce Product Module, and accept them. Activation can take up to 2 days after you are accepted into the YouTube Partner Program. Create your levels and perks, covered in the next two steps, before you switch the Join button on. Then check your channel page on a computer and on a phone. The Join button should appear next to the Subscribe button.

Step 2: Choose your levels and prices

A level is one price tier in your membership programme, and each level has its own set of perks. YouTube Help allows up to 6 levels, each with 1 to 5 perks, as cited by guides that re-checked YouTube Help in October 2026. Start with 2 to 3 levels, because more levels confuse viewers and add work for you. Prices come from a fixed list set by YouTube. In the United States, the list runs from 0.99 to 499.99 US dollars a month. Start low, prove you can deliver perks every month, and raise prices only when members ask for more. You can change a level price only once every 12 months, and a change applies to new members only.

Level Example price per month Perks to include
Supporter 2.99 US dollars Badges, emoji, members only posts
Member 4.99 US dollars Supporter perks, plus early access and a monthly live Q and A
Super member 9.99 US dollars Member perks, plus one members only video a month

The prices in this table are examples only, chosen from inside the United States price range. Viewers in other countries see prices in their local currency. Keep the gap between levels small, so a member who pays 9.99 US dollars a month gets something clearly extra.

Step 3: Pick perks you can deliver every month

A perk is an extra benefit a member gets for paying. The mistake most creators make is promising perks that take too many hours to deliver, and then running out of time. Plan your perks by the hours they cost you, and drop the expensive ones first when you are busy. Badges and emoji alone do not sell memberships, so every level needs at least one perk that a non-member cannot get.

Perk Creator hours per month Why it works
Badges and emoji 2 hours to set up, then under 1 hour Members feel recognised in chat
Members only posts and polls About 4 hours Members help choose video topics
Early access to videos About 1 hour Members see videos early, no extra filming
Monthly members only live stream About 2 to 3 hours Members talk to you directly
One members only video a month About 4 to 6 hours A clear reason to stay a member
Shout-outs by name About 1 hour Members feel seen, but keep it short

Use a simple monthly calendar: week 1, your members only or early access video; week 2, a members only poll; week 3, your live stream; week 4, a behind the scenes update. If a month is busy, drop the members only video first and keep the live stream and the poll. Never promise a perk you cannot repeat next month.

Step 4: Plan your path from 500 subscribers to your first 100 members

No honest guide can tell you how fast members will join, because YouTube publishes no average member count or average income, and every channel is different. What you can do is plan the work in stages, and measure each stage in YouTube Studio. The table below is a plan to work toward, not a promise.

Milestone What to do Time to plan for
500 subscribers Check the fan funding tier under Earn, turn on memberships, set up 2 levels Month 1 after 500 subscribers
First 10 members Announce memberships in one video and one Community post, thank new members by name in your live stream Months 1 to 2 after launch
First 50 members Add a monthly members only video and a monthly poll, mention memberships briefly in one video a week Months 3 to 6 after launch
First 100 members Add a third level only if members ask for it, run a monthly live stream, review which perks members use Months 6 to 12 after launch

To reach 500 subscribers, publish useful videos on a regular schedule and answer comments. Read how to grow your YouTube channel for the growth method, and check YouTube Analytics explained to see which videos bring back returning viewers. Watch the Earn section in YouTube Studio each month, and note how many members join, how many cancel, and which perk gets used most.

How to promote the Join button without sounding pushy

Members join because they already like your channel, not because you beg them. Mention memberships briefly, once per video at most. A short line works well: "If you want early access and members only videos, the Join button is next to Subscribe." Say what members get, in one sentence, and then move on. Do not spend two minutes of a ten minute video asking for members, because regular viewers will leave.

Use your channel page too. Your channel trailer can mention memberships in one line at the end, and your About section can list the levels and perks in plain words. A Community post once a month can remind subscribers what members got that month, so non-members can see the value before they pay. In live streams, thank new members by name. How to make money with YouTube channel memberships

YouTube Help gives the split as 70 percent to the creator and 30 percent to YouTube, after applicable taxes and fees, with card fees currently covered by YouTube, as cited by guides that re-checked YouTube Help in October 2026. That 70 percent is a ceiling, not a take home figure, because taxes, app store fees on some devices, refunds, and cancellations all reduce what you actually receive. Joining inside the YouTube app can also add an app store fee, so tell viewers to join on the YouTube website on a computer when they can.

Do not trust income claims in other guides. Competitor guides quote conversion rates, churn rates, and monthly incomes from partner data that a reader cannot check, and they disagree with each other. Churn means the share of members who cancel in a given month. No figure for churn or for typical membership income is published by YouTube, so this guide gives no typical figure. The worked example below uses invented numbers only.

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can test your own figures.

Imagine a channel with 100 members. Suppose 80 members pay 2.99 US dollars a month, and 20 members pay 4.99 US dollars a month. The 80 members pay 80 times 2.99, which is 239.20 US dollars. The 20 members pay 20 times 4.99, which is 99.80 US dollars. Add them together and the channel takes in 339.00 US dollars a month before YouTube takes its share. The creator share is 70 percent of 339.00, which is 237.30 US dollars a month, before tax, fees, refunds, and cancellations.

Recheck: 80 times 2.99 is 239.20. 20 times 4.99 is 99.80. 239.20 plus 99.80 is 339.00. 339.00 times 70 percent is 237.30. The maths checks out as an illustration, not a promise.

Now look at churn with the same example numbers. Suppose 10 of those 100 members cancel in one month. That is an example only, not a typical churn rate. To stay at 100 members, the channel must win 10 new members that month. If only 6 new members join, the channel falls to 96 members, because 100 minus 10 plus 6 is 96. That is why keeping members matters as much as finding new ones.

How and when you get paid

Membership money is paid through AdSense for YouTube, in the same way as ad revenue. Your membership earnings are added to your other YouTube earnings in AdSense, and you are paid only when your balance reaches the payout threshold. The payout threshold is 100 US dollars, which means you receive no payment until your AdSense balance reaches 100 US dollars. A small channel may take several months to reach that threshold, and that is normal.

Payment timing follows the usual AdSense cycle. Earnings are finalised between the 7th and the 12th of the following month, and payment is usually made by the 21st or the 26th of that month. Finalised means YouTube has checked the earnings and confirmed the amount. Keep records of every payment, because membership income is taxable in most countries. Check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant about the rules where you live.

Channel memberships versus Patreon

Patreon is a separate website where creators offer paid memberships, and it is the option most often compared with YouTube channel memberships. The main difference is where the member stays. With YouTube memberships, the member stays on YouTube, sees the Join button under your videos, and pays without leaving the site they already use. With Patreon, the member must visit a second site and sign up there, which adds a step and loses some people.

Patreon gives you more control. You can set your own prices, offer more levels, and contact members by email, which YouTube does not let you do in the same way. Start with YouTube memberships if your audience lives on YouTube, and add Patreon later only if you need email contact or more pricing freedom. Running both at once doubles your perk workload. Banned perks, paused mode, and refunds

YouTube bans some perks outright, and breaking these rules can cost you the membership programme. The banned perks, stated on the channel memberships help page, are downloads of content available on YouTube, including music, in person one to one meetings, anything where one or some but not all members are chosen to get something at random, including contests, lotteries, and sweepstakes, perks marketed to or targeted at children, and perks that encourage children to ask a parent to join. Do not offer a prize draw, a meeting in person, or a download of a video or song that is already on YouTube. If you are unsure whether a perk is allowed, do not offer it until you have read the help page again.

Paused mode pauses your whole membership programme. When you turn it on, payments pause, the Join button disappears, and members lose access to their perks. Use it only for a short break, because members will cancel if the pause lasts too long. If you do not exit paused mode within 120 days, the programme ends, all members are lost, and members are refunded their last month from your share.

Refunds work against you, not for you. YouTube has sole discretion on refund claims, which means YouTube decides whether a member gets a refund. When a refund is granted, the amount is deducted from your share in AdSense for YouTube. If the programme is terminated by you, by removal from the YouTube Partner Program, by abuse or fraud, or by breach of the terms, all active paying members get a refund of their last payment, deducted from your share.

Join button troubleshooting checklist

If your viewers cannot see a Join button, work through this checklist in order, and check after each step.

  • Confirm you are in the YouTube Partner Program and have met the fan funding requirements, and check the Earn section in YouTube Studio for an eligibility message.
  • Confirm your channel is not set as made for kids, and that it has no significant number of made for kids videos.
  • Confirm memberships are turned on and paused mode is off, because paused mode removes the Join button.
  • Confirm your levels and perks are saved and complete, because an unfinished level can stop the button from showing.
  • Check your channel page on a computer while signed out, because the Join button may not show to you as the channel owner.
  • Ask a viewer to check on the YouTube website on a computer, because the button may not appear in some versions of the mobile app.
  • Ask the viewer to check their age and location, because a viewer must be 18 to join, or 19 in Korea and Algeria, and memberships are not available in every country.
  • Check that the viewer is not using a supervised account, such as a child account managed by a parent.
  • If the button still does not appear, wait 2 days after activation, then contact YouTube Creator Support from YouTube Studio, and describe each check you already tried.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting memberships costs nothing in money. YouTube takes its 30 percent share from what members pay, so there is no fee to pay upfront. The real cost is your time. Expect 2 to 4 hours a month for a simple programme with badges, polls, and early access, and 6 to 10 hours a month if you add a members only video and a monthly live stream.

Expect a slow start. A channel with 500 subscribers may win its first members in its first month, or it may take three months, because most subscribers will never pay. Your first payment may take months to arrive, because you must reach the 100 US dollar AdSense threshold first. Results vary with your niche, your viewers' country, content quality, and how loyal your audience is. Nothing here is a promise of income. Memberships work best as one income stream next to ads, affiliates, and sponsorships. Read how to get YouTube sponsorships before you take your first brand deal.

Common mistakes

  • Turning on memberships before your perks are ready, so the first members get nothing in their first week.
  • Offering 6 levels at launch, which confuses viewers and doubles your workload.
  • Promising a members only video every week, then missing weeks and losing trust.
  • Offering only badges and emoji, which rarely convince a viewer to pay every month.
  • Pricing the entry level too high for a small channel, then finding you cannot change it for new members for 12 months.
  • Mentioning memberships in every video for two minutes, which drives regular viewers away.
  • Offering a banned perk, such as a prize draw or an in person meeting, and risking the programme.
  • Turning on paused mode and forgetting it, then losing all members after 120 days.
  • Counting the 70 percent share as take home pay, and forgetting tax, fees, refunds, and cancellations.
  • Comparing your month 3 to a large channel's year 5, and quitting before members have time to join.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch out for anyone who offers to sell you members, subscribers, or watch hours so you can reach the 500 subscriber tier faster. Bought subscribers are not real viewers, they will never become paying members, and YouTube can remove them and take action against your channel. Grow to 500 subscribers with real videos, not with a paid shortcut.

Watch out for fake membership offers in comments and emails. A scammer may pretend to be a fan, a sponsor, or YouTube staff, and ask you to click a link, pay a fee to activate memberships, or share your password. YouTube never asks you to pay a fee to turn on memberships, and you should never share your password or your 2-Step Verification codes with anyone. Turn on memberships only inside YouTube Studio.

Watch out for tax and money risks. Membership income is taxable in most countries, so keep records of every payment and set aside money for tax. Do not spend membership income before it reaches your bank account, because refunds and cancellations can reduce it. If a member asks for a refund, do not promise one yourself, because YouTube decides refund claims.

Frequently asked questions

How many subscribers do I need for YouTube channel memberships?

Memberships are a fan funding feature, and the reported expanded tier needs 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. That tier is reported by guides citing the YouTube Help expanded programme page, so confirm it in YouTube Studio under Earn.

How much does YouTube take from channel memberships?

YouTube keeps 30 percent and the creator gets 70 percent, after applicable taxes and fees, with card fees currently covered by YouTube, according to YouTube Help as re-checked by guides in October 2026. That 70 percent is a ceiling, not a take home figure, because taxes, app store fees on some devices, refunds, and cancellations reduce what you actually receive.

How much can a small channel earn from memberships?

It depends on how many members join, what price they pay, and how many cancel each month, and YouTube publishes no typical figure. A small channel may earn nothing for months, and the worked example in this guide uses invented numbers only. Treat memberships as one small income stream next to ads, affiliates, and sponsorships.

Can I change my membership price later?

You can change a level price only once every 12 months, and the change applies to new members only. Existing members keep paying their old price. That is why you should start low, prove you can deliver perks every month, and raise a price only when members ask for more.

What happens if I pause my memberships?

Paused mode pauses payments, removes the Join button, and removes perk access for members. If you do not exit paused mode within 120 days, the programme ends, all members are lost, and members are refunded their last month from your share. Use paused mode only for a short break, and tell members before you turn it on.

Why does my channel not show a Join button?

The most common reasons are that the channel has not met the fan funding requirements, the channel is set as made for kids, memberships are not turned on, paused mode is on, or a level is unfinished. The button may also not show in some versions of the mobile app, or to a viewer who is under 18 or in a country where memberships are not available. Work through the checklist in this guide, wait 2 days after activation, and then contact YouTube Creator Support from YouTube Studio.

Conclusion

To learn how to make money with YouTube channel memberships, join the YouTube Partner Program at the fan funding tier, set up 2 to 3 levels with a low entry price, offer perks you can deliver every month, promote the Join button briefly and honestly, watch cancellations as closely as new joins, and treat memberships as one steady income stream next to ads, affiliates, and sponsorships. Do not promise perks you cannot repeat, do not offer banned perks, and do not count the 70 percent share as take home pay.
