How to Start a Tech YouTube Channel and Earn Money in 2026

Learning how to start a tech YouTube channel is a good choice if you like testing gadgets, explaining software, or helping people choose the right laptop, phone, or tool. The trap for small tech channels is racing the big channels on launch day. A new phone launches on Tuesday, the big channels review it on Wednesday, and a small channel lands on page nine. This guide shows you a different way to win: pick one buyer, review late on purpose, test honestly, and earn from affiliates and sponsorships long before ad revenue matters.

This guide is for beginners who want to make tech videos about phones, laptops, software, or AI tools. You will learn how to pick a niche inside tech, which video formats work, what starter kit you need and what it costs, how to get review units when you are small, how to test a product and keep the review honest, what the disclosure rules are, and what a realistic first year looks like. For the general channel setup steps, read how to start a YouTube channel. This guide covers only what is specific to tech.

Pick a niche inside tech, not tech itself

Tech is too wide for a small channel. Do not make videos about phones, laptops, software, gaming, and AI tools all at once. Pick one buyer instead of one gadget. A buyer is a person with a specific problem and a budget: students who need a laptop for coding under 600 US dollars, small business owners who need AI tools they would actually pay for, or people in a shared flat who need a quiet keyboard. When you pick a buyer, your videos answer a real buying question, and your titles write themselves.

Test the niche with the 50 video test. Write down 50 video ideas for that buyer. If you can only think of 15, the niche is too narrow for you, or you do not know it well enough yet. Check each idea on YouTube and read the top results. If a large channel already covers the exact question well, find a smaller angle, such as the same laptop after three months of use, or the same phone for one specific buyer.

Step 1: Choose your tech content formats

Different tech videos serve different viewers. Use this map to choose the right format for each idea, and start with one format before you add a second.

Format When to use it What it needs
Review A viewer is deciding whether to buy one product Days of real use, a test method, honest pros and cons
Comparison A viewer is choosing between two products The same test on both, a clear winner for one buyer
Tutorial A viewer wants to do something with a product Clear steps on screen, no skipped steps
Explainer A viewer wants to understand a new tool or idea Simple language, one idea per video
Unboxing A viewer wants to see what is in the box Honesty that an unboxing is not a review
Setup tour A viewer wants ideas for their own desk or setup Real costs, real trade-offs
Shorts A teaser that points to a long video One idea, a link to the full video

Reviews and comparisons earn the most affiliate income, because the viewer is close to buying. Tutorials earn the most search traffic, because people search for how to do things every day. Unboxings are the weakest format on their own, because they say nothing about whether the product is good. If you make an unboxing, follow it with a review after you have used the product. Use Shorts as trailers for your long videos, with the related video feature linking to the full review.

Step 2: Buy the honest starter kit

You do not need expensive gear to start a tech channel. A modern phone films well enough, and audio matters more than camera quality. Viewers forgive a soft picture, but they leave fast when the sound is bad. Buy a microphone first, use window light, and edit with free software. Do not buy gear on credit before you have income.

The real cost of a review channel is not the filming gear. It is the products you review. Before any brand sends you a free unit, you must buy or borrow the products yourself, and reviewing flagship phones means buying expensive hardware. Start with products you already own, with software and AI tools that cost nothing to test, and with budget products that are cheap to buy.

Step 3: Make your first 10 videos

Your first 10 videos are tuition, not a portfolio. They will be rough, and that is fine. Treat them as practice, do not delete them, and keep publishing. Pick one specific question per video, such as "Is a refurbished laptop worth it for coding in 2026?" rather than "Best laptops". Write the opening line first, because the first 15 seconds decide whether a viewer stays. Record in short takes, in good light, with the microphone on. Edit ruthlessly: cut every sentence that does not help the viewer. Make the title and thumbnail before you upload, with one clear idea per thumbnail, and keep titles under about 60 characters.

Publish one video a week for a year. One video a week for a year beats three videos a week for a month, because a library of searchable videos compounds. Read your numbers monthly, not daily. Early views measure almost nothing, and a video that gets 50 views in its first week can earn search traffic for years. Meaningful traction often takes a year of consistent publishing.

Step 4: Get review units when you are small

This is the gap every competitor guide skips, and it is the hardest part of starting a tech channel. Launch day review units go to large channels first. Brands send a new phone to channels with hundreds of thousands of subscribers, not to a channel with 200 subscribers. That is fine, because you can win a different way: review late on purpose.

A review titled "After three months" or "Is it still worth it in 2026" answers the question buyers actually ask after launch day, when the first reviews are old and the price has dropped. Late reviews need no launch access, they rank in search for longer, and they are more honest, because you have used the product properly. Start with products you already own, products you can borrow from a friend, and software you can test for free.

When you are ready to pitch a brand, keep the email short. Say who your audience is, in one sentence. Say which video you want to make, and why it suits that audience. Show your numbers honestly: subscribers, monthly views, and your best two videos, with links. Ask in writing whether the unit is a loan you must return, a unit you keep, or a sponsored unit, and who pays return shipping. Expect most brands to say no, or to never reply. If a brand offers a unit only if you promise a positive review, walk away. That deal breaks the rules and your trust.

Step 5: Test a product and keep the review honest

A good tech review is a test, not an opinion. Use the product for days, not minutes, before you film. Test the things your buyer cares about: battery life in real use, performance on the tasks they actually do, camera quality in the light they will use, build quality, and software over a week. Write the price down on the day you record, and say the date on screen, because tech prices change fast and an old price misleads buyers.

If you do not like a product, say so, and show why. A "Why I Returned It" video builds more trust than ten positive reviews. If a product breaks or disappoints after a positive review, make a follow-up video and say so. If you got a spec or a price wrong, pin a comment with the correction and add a note in the description. Keep sponsored segments separate from unsponsored reviews in the same video, and say clearly which part is sponsored.

Stay current without chasing every launch. Follow the launch calendar for your niche, and note embargo dates, which are the dates before which you may not publish a review. Read the spec sheet and the press release yourself, and check the price on the day you record. When a new model replaces one you reviewed, add a pinned comment or a short update video, and say the old video is out of date. For software and AI tools, which change monthly, date every tutorial, and check the tool again before you recommend it.

Affiliate income for tech channels

Affiliate income is usually the first money a small tech channel earns, and it often arrives before meaningful ad revenue. When a viewer clicks your affiliate link and buys, you earn a commission. Amazon Associates and brand programmes are the usual starting points for tech creators. Read how to use YouTube for affiliate marketing for the method, and how to write YouTube titles to make your review titles searchable.

Put affiliate links in the video description, in a pinned comment, and in a YouTube Shopping product tag where that is available. YouTube Shopping affiliate features opened to YouTube Partner Program creators with at least 500 subscribers on 25 March 2026, according to the YouTube Official Blog as reported by Cowral. That is a Shopping threshold, not the full YPP ad revenue threshold, so keep the two separate. Do not state any commission rate in your videos unless you have checked it on the programme's own page on the day. For thumbnails that sell the click, read how to design YouTube thumbnails.

Disclosure rules, in plain language

Disclosure is not optional, and it is not a small print job. If you received a free product, if a link earns you a commission, or if a video is sponsored, you must say so clearly, in the video itself, not only in the description. Say it near the start of the video, and put it on screen as well. Say "The brand sent me this phone for free, and I can keep it" or "The links below are affiliate links, and I earn a commission if you buy through them, at no extra cost to you." The word "affiliate link" alone may not be enough, because many viewers do not know what it means. The words "paid link" next to the link are clearer, according to the FTC endorsement guides FAQ. Tick the YouTube "includes paid promotion" box as well, but that box alone is not a guarantee that you have disclosed properly. The FTC endorsement guides explain the standard, and the same principle applies in most countries.

Two more rules matter. First, a brand cannot pay for a specific opinion. The FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, in effect since 21 October 2024, bars businesses from offering compensation conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment. If a brand asks you to promise a positive review, refuse the deal. Second, rules differ by country. If your audience is in Pakistan or elsewhere outside the US, local rules may also apply, so check them.

YouTube Partner Program thresholds for 2026

Ad revenue comes later for most tech channels. To join the YouTube Partner Program at the full tier, a channel needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, as stated on the YouTube Partner Program overview page checked on 2026-11-11. You also need to follow the channel monetisation policies, live in a country or region where the programme is available, have no active Community Guidelines strikes, have 2-Step Verification turned on, have advanced features access, and have one active AdSense for YouTube account linked. Meeting the threshold does not guarantee acceptance, and the review typically takes about 1 month. Shorts Feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000 hours, and only public long-form videos count. If a channel has not uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for 6 months or more, YouTube may turn off monetisation.

From February 1, 2027, YouTube announced on 10 August 2026 that new applicants for the ad revenue tier will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, while the fan funding tier stays unchanged. Members must accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027. Confirm the current terms in YouTube Studio, because the overview page as opened on 2026-11-11 did not yet state the change. For the full monetisation picture, read how to monetise a YouTube channel.

Sponsorships: when they are realistic

Sponsorships usually become realistic after a few thousand subscribers, when a brand can see that your audience matches its product. Say yes when the product suits your buyer, when you have tested it yourself, and when the brand lets you give an honest opinion. Say no when the product is off topic for your niche, when you have not tested it, or when the brand tells you the conclusion in advance. Ask for the deal in writing: what you will deliver, how many videos, what the brand can use afterwards, when you will be paid, and how you will be paid. Do not state a rate figure from a guide as a fact, because sponsorship rates vary widely by niche, audience size, and country. Read how to get YouTube sponsorships before you negotiate your first deal, and read YouTube Analytics explained so you can show a brand your real audience numbers.

Worked example: what a small tech channel could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can test your own figures.

Imagine a small tech channel with 3,000 subscribers and 40 videos. It gets 30,000 views a month. Suppose 10 percent of those viewers, or 3,000 people, click an affiliate link in a description. Suppose 2 percent of those clicks becomes a purchase, which is 60 purchases. At an average commission of 3 US dollars per purchase, that is 180 US dollars a month from affiliates.

Now add ads. Suppose the channel has joined the Partner Program, and 20,000 of its 30,000 monthly views are on videos that carry ads. At an example rate of 5 US dollars per 1,000 ad views after YouTube's share, that is 20,000 divided by 1,000, which is 20, times 5, which is 100 US dollars a month from ads. Add the two together and the channel earns about 280 US dollars in that month, before tax. If the channel grows to 100,000 views a month with the same rates, the same maths gives about 933 US dollars a month.

Recheck: 30,000 times 10 percent is 3,000. 3,000 times 2 percent is 60. 60 times 3 is 180. 20,000 divided by 1,000 is 20. 20 times 5 is 100. 180 plus 100 is 280. At 100,000 views: 100,000 times 10 percent is 10,000. 10,000 times 2 percent is 200. 200 times 3 is 600. Ad views 66,667 divided by 1,000 is 66.667, times 5 is 333. 600 plus 333 is 933. The maths checks out as an illustration, not a promise. Your real result depends on traffic, conversion rates, commission rates, and your viewers' country.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting costs are low if you start with a phone. A cheap lavalier microphone costs about 10 to 20 US dollars, a small ring light costs about 20 to 40 US dollars, and free editing software costs nothing, so you can start for under 60 US dollars. The bigger cost is the products you review, and the time: expect 5 to 10 hours per video when you include testing, scripting, filming, and editing. Testing a product properly takes days, and that time is the real price of an honest review.

Expect a slow start. Ad revenue is modest on a small tech channel, and it varies by audience and location, with creators outside the US and UK often earning lower ad rates than the US figures quoted in guides. Most channels take months, often more than a year, to reach the Partner Program, and affiliate income usually starts first, from a review that ranks in search. Sponsorships usually come later, after a few thousand subscribers. Results vary with your niche, your viewers' country, content quality, and how well the products you review match your audience. Nothing here is a promise of income.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with tech in general instead of one buyer with a specific problem and budget.
  • Racing big channels on launch day instead of reviewing late on purpose.
  • Calling an unboxing a review, when you have not tested the product.
  • Repeating a spec from a press release without checking it, then publishing a wrong figure.
  • Hiding affiliate links, or disclosing a free product only in the description and never in the video.
  • Accepting a deal that tells you the conclusion in advance.
  • Buying expensive gear on credit before you have income.
  • Making Shorts only, and never building a library of long videos that earn search traffic.
  • Checking analytics daily instead of monthly, and quitting before the library has time to compound.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch out for brands that offer a free product or payment in exchange for a positive review. That deal breaks the FTC rules and your trust, and a viewer who buys a bad product on your advice will not trust your next review. Walk away from any deal that tells you what to conclude. Watch out for fake review sites and fake follower services. Do not buy views or subscribers, because YouTube can remove them, and a brand that checks your analytics will see the gap between subscribers and real views. Do not use manufacturer press images or copyrighted music without permission, because a claim can block a video or a channel. Watch out for tax and money risks. Affiliate and sponsorship income is taxable in most countries, so keep records of every payment and check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant. Do not spend money on gear you cannot afford, and do not promise a brand a result you cannot control, such as a number of sales. Finally, protect your time. Chasing every launch burns creators out, and a tired creator makes mistakes. Pick a pace you can keep for a year, and keep it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a tech YouTube channel?

You can start for about 10 to 20 US dollars, which is the price of a cheap lavalier microphone, if you already own a phone and use free editing software and window light. A budget setup with a better microphone and a small ring light costs about 50 to 90 US dollars. The bigger cost is the products you review, because you must buy or borrow them before any brand sends you a free unit.

Do I need a good camera, or is a phone enough?

A phone is enough to start. Modern phone cameras are good, and audio matters more than camera quality, because viewers leave fast when the sound is bad. Spend your first money on a microphone, not a camera. Upgrade the camera later, when your channel earns enough to pay for it.

How do I get free products to review when I am small?

Start with products you already own, products you can borrow, and software you can test for free, and review late on purpose, with titles such as "After three months". When you pitch a brand, keep the email short, show your real numbers, and ask in writing whether the unit is a loan, a unit you keep, or a sponsored unit. Expect most brands to say no at first.

Do I have to disclose free products and affiliate links?

Yes. Disclose in the video itself, near the start, and on screen, as well as in the description. Say clearly that the product was free, or that a link earns you a commission. The words "paid link" next to the link are clearer than "affiliate link" alone. Tick the YouTube paid promotion box as well, but that box alone is not enough.

How many subscribers do I need to earn money from a tech channel?

Affiliate income can start with no minimum subscriber count, as soon as you have a useful review that ranks in search. Ad revenue needs the YouTube Partner Program, which is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, until February 1, 2027, when new applicants need 8,000 hours or 20 million Shorts views instead. YouTube Shopping affiliate features are open to Partner Program creators with at least 500 subscribers.

How long does it take to earn money from a tech YouTube channel?

Most channels take months, often more than a year, to reach the Partner Program, and meaningful traction often takes a year of consistent publishing. Affiliate income can start sooner, from a single review that ranks well, but it is usually small at first. Sponsorships usually come later, after a few thousand subscribers. Do not expect income in your first month.

Conclusion

To start a tech YouTube channel and earn money, pick one buyer, not one gadget, choose one video format and do it well, start with a phone and a cheap microphone, make your first 10 videos as practice, review late on purpose, test every product properly, disclose every free product and affiliate link in the video itself, and earn from affiliates first, then ads, then sponsorships. Do not race the big channels on launch day, do not accept a deal that tells you the conclusion, and do not buy gear on credit. Your first year is slow, but a library of honest reviews compounds.
