How to Get YouTube Sponsorships in 2026

A sponsorship is a brand paying you to feature its product or service in your video. For many creators, it is the first income that does not depend on ad views alone. Learning how to get YouTube sponsorships means learning when you are ready, which brands to approach, what to put in a media kit and pitch, how to price the work fairly, and how to deliver and report it well. This guide covers the full YouTube specific process, from readiness to renewal, without inventing rate tables or promising deals.

Step 1: Know when you are ready

There is no single subscriber number that makes you ready for a direct sponsorship. Brands care more about average views, audience fit, and trust than about a headline subscriber count. A small, engaged niche channel can beat a large, passive channel for a brand that needs that niche audience. That point matters, because many guides quote a subscriber minimum as if it were a rule. Treat any such number as a rough guide from that source, not as a gate you must pass.

Use this readiness check instead. First, do your last 10 to 15 long videos average a steady view count after 30 days, not just one spike. Second, do viewers comment with real questions and replies, not only short praise. Third, does your audience location and age range fit a product you could honestly recommend. Fourth, have you published consistently for a few months, so a brand can trust you will deliver on time. Fifth, can you show one clear format, such as a 60 second integration, that a brand can buy without confusion. If you can answer yes to most of these, you can start prospecting, even if your subscriber count is modest.

Step 2: Learn the sponsorship formats

Brands buy different levels of attention, and each format has a different price logic. An integrated sponsorship is a segment inside a normal video, often about 60 to 90 seconds, placed near the middle where viewers are still watching. A dedicated video is a whole video built around one product or topic, and it costs more because it uses the full video. A short mention is a brief pre roll or end card mention of 15 to 30 seconds, and it costs less than a full integration. A sponsored Short is a short vertical video built around a product, priced below a long integration because the viewing time is shorter. Product seeding is not a paid deal at all, unless a fee is agreed, so do not treat a free product as payment for a full review unless you agree that trade clearly.

Step 3: Understand what brands check

Brands check average views per video first, because that number predicts reach. Use the average over the last 10 to 15 long videos after 30 days, not your best video, because the best video overstates typical reach. Brands also check audience location and age, niche fit, publishing consistency, comment quality, and past sponsors. A channel with steady views, a clear niche, and thoughtful comments looks safer than a channel with one viral video and silent viewers.

Step 4: Find brands already paying creators in your niche

Start with brands that already pay creators like you. That list is warmer than a cold list of big companies with no creator history. Search YouTube for your niche plus the word sponsor, filtered to the last 6 months, and note which brands appear. Look for the "Includes paid promotion" label on videos in your niche, and check video descriptions for tracking links and discount codes. Those signals show a brand has a live creator budget. Search for a brand name plus "creator programme" or "creator program" to find formal programmes, and note the spelling used in your target country.

Step 5: Build a one page media kit

A media kit is a one page document that lets a brand judge you in a minute. Keep it to one page, and update it every quarter, or whenever your averages change a lot. Include these fields, and nothing extra that buries the key numbers.

Media kit field What to include
One sentence summary Who you help and how, in plain words
Audience size and averages Subscribers, plus 90 day average views, Shorts separate
Audience detail Top countries and age range, from Studio
Engagement Average likes and comments per video
Formats and starting prices Integration, dedicated video, Short, or rates on request
Past sponsors Names and one honest result each, if any
Contact Business email and response time

Step 6: Find the decision maker and write a pitch

Keep the pitch short, about 150 to 250 words. Use this structure. Hook: one sentence that names the audience fit, not your subscriber count. Proof: one typical result, such as average views or a past sponsor result, with the period stated. Pitch: the format, the placement, and the length, such as a 60 second integration at the two minute mark, plus a description link and a pinned comment. Ask: one clear next step, such as a short call in a named month, or a request for the brand brief. Link the media kit, do not attach it. Send on a weekday morning in the brand timezone when you can, and keep the subject line specific, such as the brand name plus your channel niche.

Step 7: Use YouTube Creator Partnerships and Open Calls

YouTube Creator Partnerships, formerly called YouTube BrandConnect, helps eligible creators be found by brands. Eligible creators can have a Creator Partnerships tab under Earn in Studio, where brands can send enquiries, set starting earning amounts, and ask for review uploads. Eligibility requires being at least 18 years old, a Partner Programme member, based in a supported country or region, and having no active Community Guidelines strikes, with no separate application for the tab itself. Check the current eligibility and country list on the official YouTube Creator Partnerships Help page at the time you apply, because availability changes by region.

Open Calls are a related feature where brands post sponsored opportunities and eligible creators can submit a video for review. Open Call availability is narrower, and in the research it was limited by invitation, country, subscriber range, and recent Shorts activity, so check the current Open Call rules before you count on it. Open Call rules are on the official YouTube Help page on Open Call. Treat Creator Partnerships as one channel, not the only channel. Direct outreach, marketplaces, and inbound enquiries through a business email still matter, especially for creators outside the supported regions.

Step 8: Price with logic, not with a copied table

Pricing causes the most confusion, because published rate tables conflict. Two guides in the research give materially different tier tables for the same subscriber bands, and both label their figures as estimates or benchmarks, not audited deal data. For example, one guide gives a higher range for small channels than the other, and their niche and format multipliers differ too. Do not adopt either table as market fact. Use the pricing logic both guides share, then set your own floor and ask from your own numbers.

Step 9: Get the contract terms in writing

Never start filming on a verbal promise alone. Get the terms in writing, even for a small deal, in an email that both sides confirm, or in a signed agreement for larger deals. Cover deliverables and placement, such as integration length and position in the video. Cover approval rights and revision rounds, so you know how many changes are included. Cover the publish date and how long the video must stay public. Cover usage rights, meaning whether the brand can reuse your clip in ads or on its own channels, and for how long. Cover payment timing, such as net 30 days after publishing, and whether a deposit is paid for a first time brand. Cover reporting, meaning which metrics you will send and when.

Exclusivity is the clause that catches creators out most. Exclusivity means you agree not to work with competing brands in a category for a set time. That time can run for months, and it can block other income. Exclusivity should always be paid for, with a clear category definition, a clear start and end date, and a fee that reflects the income you give up. If a brand demands wide exclusivity without paying for it, narrow the category, shorten the time, or decline that clause. Never agree to exclusivity in a casual email without reading the scope, because a short phrase can lock you out of a whole niche.

Step 11: Spot sponsorship scams

Fake sponsorships target small creators, because small creators are eager for a first deal. Use this checklist before you reply to any inbound offer. Check the sender domain against the real brand domain, letter by letter, because lookalike domains are a common trick. Never open an executable file, a password protected archive, or a "demo software" file from an unknown sponsor, because such files can steal login sessions. Never share passwords, login codes, or full Analytics access. Be wary of offers that promise a large fee for a tiny channel with no questions about audience fit. Be wary of pressure to sign fast, or to keep the deal secret from viewers. Be wary of a brand that asks you to skip disclosure, or to buy a starter kit, or to pay a fee to receive payment.

Common mistakes

  • Pitching on subscriber count instead of average views.
  • Using your best video as typical reach.
  • Sending a long pitch with no clear ask.
  • Attaching a large media kit file instead of linking it.
  • Copying a rate table as if it were market fact.
  • Accepting wide exclusivity without payment.
  • Skipping disclosure or hiding it in the description only.
  • Claiming a product benefit you have not verified.
  • Opening files from an unknown sponsor without checks.
  • Sending a report only when the numbers look good.

Frequently asked questions

How many subscribers do I need for a YouTube sponsorship

There is no single subscriber rule for direct deals. Brands focus on average views, audience fit, and trust, and a small engaged niche channel can win deals over a larger passive channel. Use average views over the last 10 to 15 long videos after 30 days as your main proof, and start prospecting when those views stay steady and your audience fits a product honestly.

How much should I charge for a sponsorship

Charge from your own numbers, not from a copied rate table, because published tables conflict and label their figures as estimates. Start with average views times a niche rate per 1,000 views, divided by 1,000, then adjust for format and add for usage rights, exclusivity, revisions, and production work. That total is your floor, and your ask should sit above it to allow negotiation and to reflect the full value you deliver.

You can include starting prices in your media kit, or state rates on request, and both approaches are used in practice. If you name a rate early, base it on the pricing logic in this guide and be ready to explain it. If you prefer the brand to name a budget first, ask for the budget range, then compare it with your floor before you accept or narrow the scope.

Partner Programme membership is required for YouTube Creator Partnerships features, which is a separate route, so check the current eligibility page if you want that tab in Studio.

How do I disclose a sponsorship on YouTube

Use the paid promotion label in YouTube Studio, give a clear spoken disclosure at the start of the sponsored segment, and mention the sponsorship in the description. Do not hide the disclosure, and do not skip it if a brand asks you to. Regulators in the United States, the United Kingdom, and the European Union can act against creators directly for undisclosed advertising.

Conclusion

Get sponsorships by proving steady, honest value to the right brands. Build a short media kit from typical numbers, prospect brands already paying creators in your niche, pitch with one clear format and ask, price from your own views and add ons, get terms in writing, disclose clearly, and report at 7 days and 30 days with a renewal idea. Start with well fit brands, decline poor fit deals, and let trust compound into repeat work.
