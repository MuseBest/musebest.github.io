32:T56bb,<h1>How to Offer AI Data Labelling Services in 2026</h1>
<p>Learning how to offer AI data labelling services is a practical way to earn from the AI boom without building an AI product yourself. Every AI system needs labelled data to learn from, and someone has to create those labels. A self-driving car learns to spot a pedestrian because a human drew a box around thousands of pedestrians in training photos. A chatbot learns to answer questions because humans tagged thousands of example conversations. That human work is data labelling, and AI companies pay for it every day. This guide shows you how to turn that work into a service, from picking one type of labelling to finding your first paying client.</p>

<h2 id="what-ai-data-labelling-is">What AI data labelling is</h2>
<p>Data labelling, also called data annotation, means adding tags to raw data so a machine learning model can learn from it. Raw data is the unlabelled material: photos, videos, audio clips, text documents, or sensor readings. A label is the tag you add, such as "cat", "stop sign", or "angry customer". The model studies thousands of labelled examples and learns the pattern, so it can label new data on its own later.</p>
<p>This is different from data analysis. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-offer-ai-data-analysis-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to offer AI data analysis services</a> covers analysing data to answer business questions. Labelling is the step before that: you prepare the training data that makes the AI work at all. A good label is accurate, consistent, and follows the client's rules exactly. A bad label teaches the model the wrong thing, which is why clients pay for careful human work instead of trusting fully automatic labelling.</p>
<p>You do not need a degree in computer science to do this work. You need attention to detail, patience, and the ability to follow a written guide exactly. Most labelling work is learned on the job in a few days, and the tools are free to start with.</p>

<p>Image labelling is the easiest type to start with, because the work is visual and the tools are simple. Text annotation is also a good start if you write well and read fast. Audio and document labelling pay more in some cases, but they need more care and sometimes specialist knowledge. AI evaluation is growing fast, because every company that builds a chatbot needs humans to check its answers.</p>
<h2 id="who-buys-labelling-services">Who buys labelling services</h2>
<p>Three kinds of buyers pay for labelling. AI startups are the most common. A small team building an app needs labelled data but cannot hire a full labelling team, so they buy the work from a freelancer or a small agency. Research teams at universities and large companies also buy labelling for one-off projects. And labelling platforms sometimes hire freelancers to handle overflow work, though their rates are usually lower than direct clients.</p>
<p>The best clients are small AI companies with a real product and a real budget. They need labels every week, not just once, so one good client can give you steady work for months. Avoid clients who cannot explain what they want labelled, or who refuse to give you a written labelling guide. Without a written guide, you cannot prove your work is right, and disputes follow.</p>
<h2 id="skills-and-tools-you-need">Skills and tools you need</h2>
<p>You need four skills to start. First, attention to detail, because a label that is a few pixels off can still count as wrong. Second, the ability to follow a written guide exactly, even when the guide feels fussy. Third, basic computer skills, including file management and spreadsheets. Fourth, clear written English, because you will ask clients questions and write short reports about your work.</p>
<p>You need two tools to start. A labelling tool is the software where you draw boxes and add tags. <a href="https://labelstud.io/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Label Studio</a> is a free open source labelling tool that handles images, text, audio, and video, and it runs in your browser. <a href="https://www.cvat.ai/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CVAT</a> is another free open source platform, built for image and video work, and it includes built-in quality checks. Learn one of them well before you look for clients. A spreadsheet is the second tool, for tracking how many items you labelled, how long each batch took, and what errors you found.</p>
<p>A computer made in the last few years is enough. You do not need a powerful graphics card, because the labelling happens in your browser, not on your machine. A second monitor helps, but it is not required at the start.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-pick-one-type-of-labelling">Step 1: Pick one type of labelling</h2>
<p>Pick the type that matches what you already do well. If you are visual and patient, pick image labelling. If you read fast and write clearly, pick text annotation. If you have a background in law, medicine, or finance, pick document labelling in that field, because specialist labels pay more.</p>
<p>Do not offer all six types at once. Clients trust a specialist more than a generalist, and each type needs its own samples and its own pitch. You can add a second type later, once the first one brings in work.</p>
<p>Write down your choice in one sentence, such as "I label product photos for online shops" or "I annotate customer support chats for chatbot teams". That sentence becomes the first line of your profile and your pitch.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-learn-one-tool-well">Step 2: Learn one tool well</h2>
<p>Download Label Studio or CVAT and work through its own getting started guide. Then label 100 practice items from a public dataset. Good practice sources include free image sets from open data sites, or photos you take yourself. The point is not the data, it is the routine: open the task, read the guide, label, save, and check your own work.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ai-data-labelling-annotation-work.jpg" alt="Hands using a mouse to draw a polygon outline around a car in a street photo on a computer screen, with annotation software open on a tidy desk in soft daylight" /></p>
<p>Time yourself. If 100 simple image labels take you two hours, your speed is 50 labels an hour. That number matters when you set prices later. Keep a short note of the mistakes you make in practice, because those are the mistakes you must catch in paid work too.</p>
<p>Learn the export formats your tool offers. Clients usually want labels in a standard format such as COCO or YOLO for images, or CSV for text. Both Label Studio and CVAT export in these formats. Test an export before your first client asks for one, so you are not learning it on a deadline.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-build-sample-work">Step 3: Build sample work</h2>
<p>Clients buy proof, not promises. Build three small samples that show your work clearly. Each sample should include the original data, the labelled version, and a one-page note that explains the rules you followed and the quality checks you ran.</p>
<p>A good sample set for image labelling is 20 product photos with boxes drawn around each product, plus a short guide that says how you handled unclear photos. For text annotation, a good sample is 30 short customer messages tagged by topic and feeling, with a note on how you handled sarcasm and mixed feelings. Label every sample clearly as sample work made by you, and never present someone else's labelled data as your own.</p>
<p>Put the samples on a simple page or in a PDF you can send to a client. You do not need a full website yet. A one-page document with your samples, your one-sentence service, and your contact details is enough to start.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-set-your-prices">Step 4: Set your prices</h2>
<p>Labelling is usually priced per item, per hour, or per project. Per-item pricing suits simple, repeatable work such as drawing boxes around products. Hourly pricing suits complex work such as document labelling, where each item takes a different amount of time. Project pricing suits a fixed batch with a clear scope, such as 5,000 labelled images in two weeks.</p>

Price from your timed speed, not from a guess. If you label 50 images an hour and charge 0.10 dollars per image, you earn 5 dollars an hour, which is too low. At 0.50 dollars per image, the same speed earns 25 dollars an hour. Always do this maths before you quote.</p>
<p>Offer a small paid pilot first, such as 200 labels for a fixed price, so the client can check your quality before committing to a large batch. A pilot protects both sides, and it is easier to sell than a big contract.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-find-your-first-clients">Step 5: Find your first clients</h2>
<p>Your first clients will come from direct outreach, not from waiting on a marketplace. Make a list of 30 small AI companies that build products in your chosen labelling type. You can find them on LinkedIn, on startup directories, and in the exhibitor lists of AI conferences. For each one, find the name of the person who runs the machine learning team, usually a CTO, a head of data, or a machine learning lead.</p>
<p>Send a short message to each person. Say what you label, show one sample, and offer a small paid pilot. Keep it under 100 words. Do not send a long sales letter. Most will not reply, and that is normal. Ten replies from 100 messages is a good result, and one or two of those can become clients.</p>
<p>Freelance marketplaces are a second channel. Create a profile on Upwork or Fiverr that names your labelling type in the title, shows your samples, and states your per-item price. Marketplace rates are lower, but they can fill gaps between direct clients. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-start-freelancing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start freelancing</a> covers profiles and first pitches in detail, and our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-set-freelance-rates" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to set freelance rates</a> explains how to price your time.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-set-up-quality-checks">Step 6: Set up quality checks</h2>
<p>Quality is the whole product in this business. A client who finds errors in your labels will not come back, and will tell others. Set up a simple quality system before your first paid batch.</p>
<p>First, label a small gold set yourself. A gold set is a small batch of items that you label very carefully and keep as the correct answer. Use it to test anyone who helps you label later. Second, check a sample of every batch before you send it. For a batch of 1,000 labels, check at least 100 at random, and fix every error you find. Third, keep an error log. Write down each type of error, how often it happens, and what you changed to stop it. Fourth, ask the client to check a sample too, and agree on an accuracy target in writing, such as 95 percent agreement on a shared test set.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ai-data-labelling-quality-check.jpg" alt="Two people reviewing a printed checklist and a laptop showing a labelled dataset at a shared desk, one pointing at the screen in a calm professional office" /></p>
<p>Agree the rules in writing before you start. The written agreement should say what a correct label looks like, how edge cases are handled, what accuracy target you must hit, and what happens if you miss it, such as re-labelling at no charge. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/freelance-contracts-explained" rel="noopener" target="_blank">freelance contracts explained</a> covers the clauses that protect you, and our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-offer-ai-data-analysis-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to offer AI data analysis services</a> shows how data skills fit into paid AI work.</p>
<h2 id="worked-example-what-a-small-labelling-job-could-earn">Worked example: what a small labelling job could earn</h2>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.</p>
<p>Imagine a client asks you to label 5,000 product photos, one box per photo, at 0.20 dollars per photo. The fee is 5,000 multiplied by 0.20, which is 1,000 dollars. If you label 60 photos an hour, the work takes 5,000 divided by 60, which is about 83 hours. If you also spend 7 hours on quality checks, messages, and delivery, the total time is 90 hours. Your effective rate is 1,000 divided by 90, which is about 11 dollars an hour. That is low, so you would either raise the per-photo price or speed up with practice and better tools.</p>
<p>Now imagine the same job at 0.40 dollars per photo. The fee is 2,000 dollars for the same 90 hours, which is about 22 dollars an hour. The lesson is simple: small changes in the per-item price change your hourly rate a lot, so always do the maths before you accept a job. Results vary with your speed, the complexity of the labels, and the client.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-offer-ai-data-labelling-services-what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">How to offer AI data labelling services: what it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>You can start for almost nothing. Label Studio and CVAT are free, and you already own a computer. A realistic startup cost is 0 to 50 dollars, for a second monitor stand or a better mouse if you want them. Your main cost is time: about 20 to 30 hours to learn a tool, build samples, and send your first 50 outreach messages.</p>
<p>A realistic timeline is 4 to 8 weeks to your first paid job, and 3 to 6 months to steady work, if you send outreach every week. Some people find a client faster, and many take longer. Income before that point is usually zero, so do not quit a job to start labelling. Treat it as a side service that grows into steady work.</p>
<p>Data rules differ by country. In the UK and the EU, the GDPR data protection law limits how personal data can be handled, and in the US, health data has its own rules under HIPAA. If a client sends you personal data, ask in writing how it must be stored and deleted, and do not keep client data after delivery unless the contract says so. If you are unsure, check your local data protection authority or a qualified adviser.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Offering every type of labelling at once instead of picking one and doing it well.</li>
<li>Quoting a per-item price without timing your own speed first.</li>
<li>Starting paid work without a written labelling guide from the client.</li>
<li>Skipping quality checks to finish faster, which costs you the client.</li>
<li>Labelling from memory instead of reading the guide for every batch.</li>
<li>Keeping client data after delivery without permission.</li>
<li>Promising 100 percent accuracy, which no human team can guarantee.</li>
<li>Underpricing the first jobs so low that the work cannot pay for your time.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Labelling has real risks. The first is data security. You may handle photos, documents, or recordings that belong to a client, and some of it may be personal or confidential. Use a password manager, turn on two-factor authentication, and never share client data with anyone else. Ask the client for a non-disclosure agreement, called an NDA, before they send sensitive data.</p>
<p>The second risk is scope creep. A client may keep adding new label types or new rules after you agreed a price. Stop this with a written scope that says what is included and what counts as extra work. If the rules change, the price changes too.</p>
<p>Watch out for scams aimed at new labellers. A common one is a "client" who asks you to pay for training, software, or a starter kit before you can start work. A real client pays you, not the other way round. Another is a job post that asks for your bank details or ID documents before any work is agreed. Never pay to get work, and never send ID documents to a stranger. If a listing feels wrong, check it against our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-spot-online-job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to spot online job scams</a>, or walk away.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="what-is-ai-data-labelling">What is AI data labelling?</h3>
<p>AI data labelling means adding tags to raw data so a machine learning model can learn from it. You might draw boxes around objects in photos, tag feelings in text, or transcribe speech in audio clips. The labels become the training data that teaches the model what to recognise.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-do-data-labelling-services-cost">How much do data labelling services cost?</h3>
<p>It depends on the work. Simple image labels often cost 0.03 to 0.50 dollars per label, by complexity, and specialist work such as medical or legal labelling costs more. Hourly rates for labelling work often start at 15 to 40 dollars an hour, by skill needed. These are starting ranges from competitor guides, not fixed rates, so quote from your own timed speed.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-experience-to-offer-data-labelling">Do I need experience to offer data labelling?</h3>
<p>No formal experience is needed, but you need proof you can do the work. Label 100 practice items, build three samples, and show them to clients. Most clients care more about your sample quality and your quality checks than about your CV.</p>
<h3 id="what-tools-do-data-labellers-use">What tools do data labellers use?</h3>
<p>Label Studio and CVAT are the most common free tools, and both handle images, text, audio, and video. Many clients also use paid platforms such as Labelbox, and some have their own in-house tools. Learn one free tool well first, then adapt to the client's tool.</p>
<h3 id="can-ai-replace-human-data-labellers">Can AI replace human data labellers?</h3>
<p>Not fully. AI can pre-label simple items, and that is already common, but humans still check the results, handle edge cases, and label work that needs judgement. The job is changing towards checking and fixing AI labels, which still needs a careful human.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Learning how to offer AI data labelling services in 2026 comes down to picking one type of labelling, learning one tool well, building samples that prove your quality, and pricing from your timed speed. Add a written quality system and a simple contract, then reach out to small AI companies with a short pitch and a paid pilot offer. Steady work follows quality, not volume.</p>
