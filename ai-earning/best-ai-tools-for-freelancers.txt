22:Tb21,<h2 id="what-ai-tools-are-actually-good-for">What AI tools are actually good for</h2>
<p>AI tools are strongest at first drafts, summaries, and repetitive work. They are weakest at judgement: knowing what a client actually wants, what is true, and what is good taste. Use them for the first 70% of a task, then bring your own expertise to the last 30%.</p>
<h2 id="tools-freelancers-use-every-day">Tools freelancers use every day</h2>
<h3 id="writing-and-editing">Writing and editing</h3>
<p>General-purpose AI assistants can draft outlines, rewrite awkward sentences, and summarise long documents. They also invent facts confidently, so every claim in client work must be checked against a real source. Never paste a client's confidential material into a tool without checking its privacy terms.</p>
<h3 id="research">Research</h3>
<p>AI search tools can gather background quickly, but they are a starting point, not a source. Follow the links, read the original page, and cite that — not the AI summary.</p>
<h3 id="design-and-images">Design and images</h3>
<p>Image generators are useful for mockups, mood boards, and placeholder art. For final client work, check the tool's licence terms and the client's policy on AI-generated images before delivering.</p>
<h3 id="audio-and-video">Audio and video</h3>
<p>Transcription tools turn interviews and meetings into searchable text. AI editing tools can cut silences and generate captions, which saves hours on video work.</p>
<h3 id="admin">Admin</h3>
<p>AI can draft invoices, proposal templates, and follow-up emails. Keep a human review step: a wrong price or a wrong name in an automated email costs more than the time it saved.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-charge-for-ai-assisted-work">How to charge for AI-assisted work</h2>
<p>Clients pay for the result, not for the hours you spent. If a tool lets you deliver faster, that is a reason to take on more work or improve quality — not a reason to cut your price in half. Be transparent if a client asks how the work was produced, and never present AI output as original research or original photography.</p>
<h2 id="a-simple-workflow">A simple workflow</h2>
<ol>
<li>Brief the tool with the client's goal, audience, and constraints.</li>
<li>Generate a draft, then rewrite it in the client's voice.</li>
<li>Check every fact, name, number, and link.</li>
<li>Run your own quality pass before delivery.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="what-to-avoid">What to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Delivering unedited AI output as finished work.</li>
<li>Using AI to fake expertise you do not have.</li>
<li>Uploading client data to tools with unclear data policies.</li>
<li>Building your whole business on one tool's pricing or availability.</li>
</ul>
<p>AI tools reward freelancers who already know their craft. Learn the skill first; let the tool remove the repetitive parts.</p>
