32:T47c7,<h2 id="how-to-offer-ai-recruitment-services-to-small-businesses-in-2026">How to Offer AI Recruitment Services to Small Businesses in 2026</h2>
<p>Small businesses often hire without a human resources team. The owner writes the job advert, reads every CV, and tries to interview between other tasks. When applications arrive in large numbers, good candidates get missed and the process drags on.</p>
<p>AI recruitment services use software to help with the heavy sorting work. The software can summarise CVs, match applicants against agreed criteria, draft screening questions, and organise interview scheduling. You review the output, apply human judgement, and hand the client a clear shortlist. This guide shows how to offer that service honestly and safely.</p>

<h2 id="what-ai-recruitment-services-are">What AI recruitment services are</h2>
<p>Recruitment support means helping a business move from job advert to interview shortlist. AI tools can read CVs faster than a person, pull out key facts, and rank applicants against criteria the client sets. They can also draft job adverts, screening questions, and polite rejection messages.</p>
<p>The tool does not decide who gets the job. AI systems can reflect bias from their training data and from the criteria they are given. A person must review the shortlist, check that criteria relate to the job, and make sure no group is unfairly screened out. The client makes the final hiring decision, and you should say so in every agreement.</p>

If you want that wider model, read the guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-an-online-recruitment-agency" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online recruitment agency</a>. This guide stays on one offer: AI-assisted screening and shortlisting delivered by you to small businesses.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ai32-recruitment-screening.jpg" alt="A person reviewing candidate profiles on a computer screen with a notebook beside" /></p>
<h2 id="step-1-learn-fair-hiring-basics-first">Step 1: Learn fair hiring basics first</h2>
<p>Before you touch any tool, learn how fair hiring works. Every criterion you use must relate to the actual job. If a requirement does not predict ability to do the work, it should not be used to screen people out.</p>
<p>Learn the basic rules that apply where your clients hire. In the United States, federal guidance explains that employers can be responsible for discrimination caused by automated tools they use, even when a vendor built the tool. Guidance also warns against screening that creates unjustified adverse impact on protected groups. Rules differ by country and region, so check official guidance for your client's location and advise clients to take qualified legal advice for their hiring process.</p>
<p>Also learn privacy basics. CVs contain personal data. Collect only what you need, store it securely, share it only with the client, and delete it when the work ends. Tell candidates how their data will be used where the law requires it.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-choose-your-tools-and-set-up-a-safe-workflow">Step 2: Choose your tools and set up a safe workflow</h2>
<p>Pick a small set of tools and learn them well. You may use an applicant tracking system, a CV summarising tool, and a scheduling tool. Check official pricing and data terms on the vendor's own pages before you promise a client anything, because plans and prices change often.</p>
<p>Build a clear workflow:</p>
<ol>
<li>Agree the role requirements with the client in writing. Separate must-have skills from nice-to-have skills.</li>
<li>Draft or review the job advert so it matches the real duties.</li>
<li>Collect applications in one place with the client's permission.</li>
<li>Use AI to summarise each CV and score it against the agreed criteria.</li>
<li>Review every score yourself. Read the CVs of anyone near the cut-off line.</li>
<li>Prepare a shortlist pack with evidence for each candidate and honest gaps.</li>
<li>Support scheduling and candidate messages, then hand over to the client.</li>
</ol>
<p>Keep a record of the criteria you used and any changes the client requested. That record protects the client, the candidates, and you. If you also build broader AI workflows for clients, the guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-build-ai-agents-for-small-businesses" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to build AI agents for small businesses</a> covers related ground.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-build-a-sample-shortlist-you-can-show-clients">Step 3: Build a sample shortlist you can show clients</h2>
<p>Clients will not trust you with hiring because a tool looks clever. They need to see your judgement. Build a sample using invented candidates for a fictional role. Write ten short fictional CV summaries. Score them against clear criteria. Produce a shortlist pack with evidence notes, gaps, and suggested interview questions.</p>
<p>Label the sample clearly as fictional. Never use real candidate data in a sample, even with names removed. A good sample shows three things: clear criteria, honest evidence, and a human review step that catches tool mistakes.</p>
<p>If you also help job seekers, keep the two services separate. Candidate-facing work is covered in the guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-offer-ai-resume-writing-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to offer AI resume writing services</a>. Mixing employer screening and candidate coaching for the same role creates a conflict of interest.</p>

<p>Price by project or by role, not by results you cannot control. You cannot promise that a hire will succeed. You can promise a clear process, a reviewed shortlist, and agreed turnaround times.</p>

<p>If you find work through a freelance platform, include the platform fee in your price. As of November 2026, Upwork states on its official pricing page that the freelancer service fee ranges from 0 percent to 15 percent per contract. Check the current wording on <a href="https://www.upwork.com/pricing/freelancer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Upwork's official freelancer pricing page</a> before you quote.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-find-your-first-small-business-clients">Step 5: Find your first small business clients</h2>
<p>Look for firms that hire often but have no HR staff. Local trades, clinics, shops, and growing online firms fit this profile. Introduce a narrow offer: reviewed shortlists for one role at a fixed price, with clear criteria agreed in advance.</p>
<p>Freelance platforms can bring early work, but expect strong competition. Some beginners first gain experience in a recruiter role. The guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-work-as-a-remote-recruiter" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a remote recruiter</a> covers that route. Experience inside a real hiring process teaches you what owners actually need.</p>
<p>Do not offer free screening for real roles. A paid pilot for one role, with a clear applicant limit, is a fair way to start. It protects your time and shows the client you run a real service.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-deliver-shortlists-and-support-interviews">Step 6: Deliver shortlists and support interviews</h2>
<p>Delivery quality decides whether clients return. Your shortlist pack should name the criteria, show the evidence for each candidate, and list honest gaps or questions to explore at interview. Flag any place where the AI score and your own reading disagreed, and explain which you trusted and why.</p>
<p>Support the client through scheduling, but keep the hiring decision with the client. Send polite messages to unsuccessful candidates if the client asks you to. People remember how they were treated, and small firms hire in small communities.</p>
<p>After each role, review what worked. Which criteria predicted good interviews? Where did the tool rank people poorly? Update your workflow notes so the next shortlist is better. If a client later needs ongoing candidate outreach, that is a different service. The guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-offer-ai-sales-outreach-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to offer AI sales outreach services</a> covers outreach and appointment setting, which you can adapt for candidate contact only with proper consent and care.</p>

<h2 id="worked-example-pricing-a-screening-package">Worked example: pricing a screening package</h2>
<p>Example only. These figures are invented to show the maths. They are not market rates and not a promise of income.</p>
<p>Suppose a small firm hires one office administrator and expects about 40 applicants. You estimate the work as follows. The criteria meeting and advert review take 1 hour. Reviewing AI summaries and reading borderline CVs takes 3 hours. Writing the shortlist pack takes 1.5 hours. Candidate messages and scheduling take 1 hour. Total time is 6.5 hours.</p>
<p>You value your time at 30 dollars per hour for this example. Labour cost is 6.5 times 30, which equals 195 dollars. Your tool cost share for this project is 20 dollars. Your total cost base is 195 plus 20, which equals 215 dollars.</p>
<p>You add a 20 percent margin for admin and overruns. Twenty percent of 215 dollars equals 43 dollars. Your package price is 215 plus 43, which equals 258 dollars. You round to 260 dollars and state that the package covers up to 50 applicants.</p>
<p>If the work came through a platform charging a 10 percent fee in this example, the fee on 260 dollars equals 26 dollars, and you would receive 234 dollars. Recheck: 260 minus 26 equals 234. Price with the fee included from the start.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Your costs are tool subscriptions, a reliable computer, and learning time. Some applicant systems charge per user or per job, so check official pricing before each quote. Budget time for learning fair hiring rules in the places your clients operate.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Owners trust hiring help slowly, because a bad hire is costly. Early work may come one role at a time, with gaps between projects. There is no guaranteed income. Income depends on your skill, your reputation, and how often local firms hire.</p>
<p>You must handle tax on your own earnings. In the United States, the IRS states that gig and freelance income is taxable and must be reported, even for part time or side work. See the <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gig-economy-tax-center" rel="noopener" target="_blank">IRS gig economy tax centre page</a> for details. Rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority and keep payment records.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Letting the AI rank candidates with no human review. Tools miss context and can reflect bias.</li>
<li>Using criteria that do not relate to the job, such as age, school name, or address, to screen people out.</li>
<li>Promising a successful hire. You control the process, not the final outcome.</li>
<li>Keeping candidate data longer than needed, or sharing it beyond the client.</li>
<li>Writing job adverts that oversell the role. Misleading adverts waste everyone's time.</li>
<li>Screening for the same role while also coaching candidates for that role. That is a conflict of interest.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Watch for clients who ask you to screen out protected groups or to hide the real criteria. Refuse that work. In the United States, the <a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/sites/default/files/2024-04/20240429_What%20is%20the%20EEOCs%20role%20in%20AI.pdf" rel="noopener" target="_blank">EEOC guidance on AI in employment decisions</a> states that discrimination laws apply to AI tools just as they apply to other employment practices, including unjustified disparate impact. Watch for job posts that ask you to pay for training, software, or access to candidate lists before you can earn. The FTC warns that legitimate employers do not charge upfront fees for a job. Read the signs on the <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">FTC job scams page</a>.</p>
<p>Also watch for fake candidate schemes, where someone offers to sell you CVs or asks you to forward candidate data to unknown parties. Use written agreements, verify who you are working for, and keep candidate data inside agreed systems only.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-ai-decide-who-gets-hired">Can AI decide who gets hired?</h3>
<p>No. AI can summarise and rank, but it can also make mistakes and reflect bias. A person must review the shortlist, check that criteria relate to the job, and the client must make the final decision. Never sell AI as a replacement for human judgement in hiring.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-hr-qualifications-to-offer-this-service">Do I need HR qualifications to offer this service?</h3>
<p>You do not need a formal HR title to offer screening support in many places, but you do need real knowledge of fair hiring and privacy duties. Rules vary by country and region. Check official guidance where your clients hire, and advise clients to take qualified legal advice.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-stop-the-tool-from-screening-out-good-candidates-unfairly">How do I stop the tool from screening out good candidates unfairly?</h3>
<p>Agree job related criteria in writing, review every score, and read the CVs near the cut-off line yourself. Check whether any criterion removes one group far more than others without a job related reason. Record your checks so the client can see them.</p>
<h3 id="how-should-i-price-when-applicant-numbers-are-unknown">How should I price when applicant numbers are unknown?</h3>
<p>Set an applicant limit in the package and a clear price for extra volume. For example, cover up to 50 applicants, then charge a stated amount per extra block. Put the limit in the agreement before the advert goes live.</p>
<h3 id="what-candidate-data-can-i-keep">What candidate data can I keep?</h3>
<p>Keep only what you need to complete the work, store it securely, and delete it when the agreed period ends. Share data only with the client named in the agreement. Follow the privacy rules that apply where the candidates and client are based.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-before-this-service-earns-steady-money">How long before this service earns steady money?</h3>
<p>There is no fixed timeline. Hiring work often comes in bursts, and trust builds slowly. Treat early projects as reputation building, and do not rely on this income until you have repeat clients.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>AI recruitment services work when fast software supports careful human judgement. Learn fair hiring first, agree clear criteria, review every score, and deliver shortlists with honest evidence. Keep the final hiring decision with the client and protect candidate data at every step.</p>
