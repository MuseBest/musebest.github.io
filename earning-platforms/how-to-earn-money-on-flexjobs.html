How to Earn Money on FlexJobs in 2026

To earn money on FlexJobs in 2026, you pay for a subscription, search a screened list of remote and flexible jobs, and apply directly to the employer who posted the role. FlexJobs does not pay you, and it does not find you a job. It gives you a cleaner list of jobs to apply for, and the employer pays you if you are hired.

What FlexJobs is, and what it is not

FlexJobs is a paid job board for remote, part time, freelance, and flexible schedule jobs. It was founded in 2007 by Sara Sutton, and it has run for almost twenty years. Its About FlexJobs page says its team hand screens every listing, and that it has served more than 10,000,000 job seekers, with more than 244,000 verified jobs listed and around 16,000 companies hiring.

FlexJobs is not a freelance marketplace. On a marketplace such as Upwork or Fiverr, clients post projects, freelancers bid, and the platform takes a fee and holds the payment in escrow. On FlexJobs, employers post jobs, you apply directly to the employer, and if you are hired the employer pays you directly. There is no bidding, no platform fee on your earnings, and no escrow. That is a strength, because you keep everything you earn, and a weakness, because there is no payment protection.

FlexJobs is also not a recruitment agency. Nobody on the team applies for you, interviews for you, or finds you a job. The service gives you access to screened listings, search tools, job alerts, and career resources. You still have to do the work of searching, applying, interviewing, and following up.

What FlexJobs costs in 2026

FlexJobs is not free. You can browse job categories without paying, but you need an active subscription to see full job details and to apply. The prices below are the US dollar prices shown on the official FlexJobs pricing page, as confirmed on 2026-11-23. Prices can change, so check the pricing page before you pay.

Plan What you pay What it renews at
14 day full access $2.95 once $23.95 billed every 4 weeks
3 month full access $29.85 up front ($9.95 a month) $29.85 every 3 months
Annual full access $71.40 up front ($5.95 a month) $71.40 every year

Read the renewal terms with care, because the trial is where most people lose money. The $2.95 trial is not a free trial. It lasts 14 days, and on day 15 it renews automatically at $23.95, billed every four weeks. There are 13 four week periods in a year, not 12, so a year on the rolling plan costs $311.35 if you let it run. If you try the service for two weeks, set a reminder to cancel on day 12 or 13 if you do not want to continue.

The three month plan suits most job seekers, because a real search often takes two to three months. Do not buy a year up front on your first day.

FlexJobs also offers a satisfaction guarantee. Its pricing page says that if you are unhappy for any reason during the first 14 days, you can contact the team and they will refund your money. Ask for the refund by phone, email, or chat within 14 days of account creation or renewal.

How the screening process works

Screening is the main reason FlexJobs charges a fee. According to FlexJobs, its research team checks each employer and each job posting before it goes live. The team removes scams, multi level marketing offers, commission only roles, and junk listings. It also checks that the role offers some flexibility, such as remote work or part time hours.

The team checks that the company is real and that the listing describes the work honestly.

Screening reduces risk, but it does not remove it. A screened job is not a promise that the employer will reply or that the role is still open. Research each employer yourself and ask clear questions in the interview about pay, hours, and the contract.

How to earn money on FlexJobs in 7 steps

Follow these steps in order.

Step 1: Start with the 14 day trial and search before you commit

Pay $2.95 for the 14 day trial, then spend your first day searching, not applying. Search for your job title and skills, and count how many roles you could apply for from your country. If you find fewer than ten roles that fit, do not buy a longer plan. Set a reminder for day 12 to decide whether to continue.

Step 2: Build your profile and resume first

Upload a current resume in a clean, single column layout with no graphics or tables, because many employers use software that reads resumes. Write a short headline that names your role and your strongest skill, such as "Customer Support Specialist, Email and Chat, 3 Years Experience". Add a brief summary that names one problem you solved and one result you achieved.

Step 3: Set up saved searches and job alerts

Use the filters to narrow the board to roles that fit you: job type, schedule, career level, and remote level. Save your best searches and turn on email alerts, so new roles reach you on the day they are posted. Apply soon after a role is posted, because good roles attract many applicants fast.

Step 4: Read the location line on every listing

This step matters most for readers outside the United States. Many listings are open only to people who live in the United States, or who already have United States work authorisation. Others are open worldwide, or open to contractors in any country. Read the location line first and skip United States only roles if you live elsewhere.

Step 5: Apply with a short, specific cover note

Apply only to roles that match your real skills. For each application, write a short cover note of three paragraphs: the employer's problem, your relevant result, and a request for an interview. Change the note for every role, because a generic letter will be ignored. Track each application, follow up once after a week, then move on.

Step 6: Prepare for video interviews in the employer's time zone

Most interviews happen by video call. Test your camera, microphone, and internet connection before the call, and choose a quiet room with a plain background. Be ready for interviews in the employer's time zone, which can mean late evening or night in your country. Prepare short answers for common questions about working from home.

Step 7: Agree pay and terms in writing before you start

Agree the pay rate, pay schedule, currency, payment method, and hours in writing before you start work. Employees are usually paid through payroll, which may only be open to people in some countries. Contractors should agree the payment method in writing and confirm who pays the transfer fees.

If you want short gigs, such as one logo or one article, a freelance marketplace will suit you better, because FlexJobs lists jobs and contracts, not one off tasks.

Pay varies by role, and FlexJobs publishes no pay scale, so read the pay on each listing.

How you get paid

FlexJobs does not pay you, and it takes no fee from your earnings. The employer pays you directly, so your pay, pay schedule, and contract all come from the employer, not from FlexJobs.

Employees are usually paid through payroll, with tax taken off where the law requires it. Contractors usually send an invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service, and they handle their own tax. Ask in the interview how often you will be paid, in which currency, and by which method, and agree it in writing.

There is no escrow, so there is no payment protection. If an employer pays late, FlexJobs cannot release your money, because it never held it.

What it costs and what to expect

One hire should cover the subscription cost many times over, but a hire is not certain, and a search can take weeks or months.

Example only: suppose you find a part time customer support contract at an assumed rate of $18 per hour, and you work an assumed 20 hours per week. One week pays 20 times $18, which is $360, and four weeks pay $1,440. After the $29.85 cost of a three month plan, that leaves $1,410.15 before tax and transfer fees. These figures are assumed. Real pay rates vary by role, employer, and country.

Expect a slow start. Most applications get no reply, and that is normal. A realistic first month is week one for your profile and saved searches, weeks two and three for five to ten applications per week, and week four for interviews. If you apply to 30 roles and hear back from three or four, that is normal. If you hear back from none, review your resume and cover notes before you buy another month.

Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses, and platform fees.

Who FlexJobs suits, and who it does not suit

FlexJobs suits you if you want a steady remote job, part time or full time, with one employer. It suits career changers, parents who need flexible hours, and workers in professional fields such as marketing, project management, software, finance, and customer service.

FlexJobs does not suit you if you want quick cash, short one off gigs, or work with no skills and no experience. Employers still expect a resume, an interview, and proof that you can do the work. It does not suit you if you cannot afford the subscription, or if most listings are closed to your country, so test the 14 day trial first and only continue if the number of roles open to you is healthy.

If your goal is freelance project work rather than a job, a guide on how to work as a freelance project manager may fit your plans better than a job board subscription.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying for a year on your first day, before you know whether your field is listed in numbers.
  • Forgetting that the $2.95 trial renews automatically at $23.95 every four weeks.
  • Applying before your resume and profile are ready, and sending weak applications to the best roles first.
  • Applying to every listing instead of applying well to a smaller number of roles that fit.
  • Sending the same generic cover letter to every employer.
  • Ignoring the location line and wasting time on roles that need United States work authorisation you do not have.
  • Starting work before pay, hours, and the payment method are agreed in writing.
  • Treating a screened listing as a promise and skipping your own research into the employer.

Risks and scams to watch for

FlexJobs screens its own listings, but scams still reach job seekers through other channels, and criminals misuse the FlexJobs name.

First, beware of copycat sites and fake emails. Only use the real FlexJobs website, and type the address yourself or use a bookmark. Do not click a link in an email or message that claims to be from FlexJobs. FlexJobs will never ask you to pay a fee to an employer, buy equipment before you start, or send money to receive a job offer.

Second, beware of job offers that arrive too fast. A real employer interviews you and sends a written contract. An offer after one short chat, with high pay for simple work and no interview, is a warning sign. Never pay for training, equipment, or a background check before you start, and never cash a cheque from an employer and send part of it back.

Third, protect your personal details. Do not share bank details or copies of identity documents until you have a written contract with an employer you have verified. If a listing asks you to contact the employer on a messaging app, or to pay anything, report it to FlexJobs and do not continue.

Fourth, remember that screening is not a promise. A listing can pass screening and still come from an employer with poor pay practices or unclear hours. Check the company website and ask direct questions in the interview. If the answers are vague, walk away.

Frequently asked questions

Is FlexJobs free?

No. FlexJobs charges job seekers a subscription: $2.95 for 14 days of full access, $29.85 up front for three months, or $71.40 up front for a year. The trial renews automatically at $23.95 every four weeks unless you cancel.

Is FlexJobs legit?

Yes. FlexJobs has run since 2007, and its team hand screens every listing before it goes live. That makes it a legitimate job board, but it does not mean every employer on the board is honest, so verify each employer yourself before you share personal details or start work.

How much does FlexJobs cost in 2026?

FlexJobs costs $2.95 for a 14 day trial, $29.85 up front for three months, or $71.40 up front for a year, in US dollars. The trial auto renews at $23.95 billed every four weeks.

Does FlexJobs guarantee a job?

No. FlexJobs lists screened jobs, but it cannot guarantee you will be hired, and it publishes no income figures. Your skills, your applications, and the employer's needs decide the outcome.

How does FlexJobs pay you?

FlexJobs does not pay you. The employer pays you directly, usually through payroll for employees, or by bank transfer or a payment service for contractors. FlexJobs takes no fee from your earnings, and it holds no payment in escrow, so agree the pay method in writing before you start.

Can I cancel my FlexJobs subscription?

Yes. You can cancel in your account settings at any time, but cancel before the renewal date, because all plans renew automatically. If you subscribed through the mobile app, cancel in your app store payment settings instead.

Conclusion

FlexJobs is worth the subscription only if you want a steady remote job and your field is listed there in numbers. The screening saves you time and the trial costs little, but the board cannot find you a job or protect your pay. Test it for 14 days and cancel on day 12 if the numbers do not add up.
