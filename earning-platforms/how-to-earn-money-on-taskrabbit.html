How to Earn Money on TaskRabbit in 2026

Learning how to earn money on TaskRabbit is a practical choice if you are good with your hands, you live in a busy city, and you want flexible local work. TaskRabbit connects people who need help with everyday tasks to local workers called Taskers. You set your own rates, you choose the jobs you take, and you keep 100 percent of the rate you set plus tips. The catch is that income is uneven, demand depends on your city, and you need to pass a background check and pay a registration fee before you start.

This guide is for people in the United States and the other countries where TaskRabbit operates who want a hands on side hustle. You will learn how TaskRabbit works, what it costs, how to become a Tasker, which jobs pay best, and how to build reviews that bring steady bookings.

What TaskRabbit is and who it suits

TaskRabbit is a gig platform where you get paid for local tasks such as moving, cleaning, furniture assembly and handyman work. It is owned by Ingka Group, the largest IKEA retailer, which is one reason furniture assembly is one of its most popular services. Clients search for Taskers by task, place and date, then invite the Taskers they like. You cannot reach out to a client first: work is client initiated, so your profile has to do the selling.

TaskRabbit suits you if you have practical skills, your own tools, a car or a van for moving jobs, and you live in or near a city where TaskRabbit is active. Guides report it now operates in all 50 states of the United States and in seven other countries, but demand still depends heavily on your local market. A Tasker in a large city can be booked most days. A Tasker in a small town may wait weeks between jobs.

It does not suit you if you want remote work, a fixed salary, or you cannot do physical work. If you want online work instead, read how to find part time online jobs.

How earning on TaskRabbit works

TaskRabbit works differently from most freelance platforms: it takes no cut from your pay. You keep 100 percent of the hourly rate you set, plus 100 percent of any tips. TaskRabbit makes its money by charging the client a service fee and a Trust and Support fee on top of your rate.

There are three pricing models, and the one that applies depends on the task and the area. Most tasks use self set hourly rates, where you choose your price per hour. Some tasks use pre-set hourly rates, where the platform sets the rate, usually when a client hires you directly. Some partner jobs, including some IKEA assembly work, use fixed pricing per task, so you earn the same amount even if you finish early, but you earn no more if the job takes longer.

Rates vary by task, city and experience. The ranges below come from guides, not from TaskRabbit, which does not publish a platform wide pay range.

Task type Reported hourly rate range
Moving help 40 to 60 US dollars
Furniture assembly 50 to 60 US dollars
Cleaning 25 to 40 US dollars
Handyman work 35 to 80 US dollars and above
Yard work 30 to 70 US dollars

New Taskers typically start at 15 to 25 US dollars an hour to win their first reviews, according to guides, while experienced Taskers in skilled categories report 60 to 75 US dollars an hour or more in some markets.

Pricing model How you are paid Where it applies
Self set hourly Your rate per hour Most tasks
Pre-set hourly Platform set rate Direct hires
Fixed per task Flat fee per task Some IKEA assembly jobs

You are an independent contractor, not an employee. That means TaskRabbit does not deduct tax for you, does not provide benefits, and does not guarantee you work. You invoice through the app when a task is done, and you are paid by direct deposit. Clients can reimburse expenses through the app, and they can tip you for 24 hours after receiving your invoice, but only after they have left you a review.

Step 1: Check you are eligible

Before you apply, check the requirements. Guides agree on the list: you must be at least 18 years old, you must be able to work in a city where TaskRabbit operates, you must pass a background check, and you need a United States Social Security number, a checking account for direct deposit, a credit card to pay the registration fee, and a smartphone for the Tasker app. In Canada, where TaskRabbit also operates, the minimum age is 19 and a Social Insurance Number is needed instead.

You also need the right tools and transport for the work you offer. Moving help needs a vehicle and strength. Furniture assembly needs a drill, an Allen key set, and a basic toolkit. TV mounting needs a drill, a level, and a stud finder. Do not offer a category you cannot do properly, because a bad first review follows you for a long time.

Step 2: Create your Tasker profile

Sign up on TaskRabbit, then download the Tasker app to finish your registration and build your profile. The app walks you through verification, your photo, your services, and your rates.

Your profile is your shop window, and it needs to sell you in seconds. Upload a clear, friendly photo of yourself, not a logo and not a blurry selfie. Write a short pitch for each service you offer, in two or three lines, that says what you do, what tools you bring, and what experience you have. A furniture assembly pitch that says "I assemble IKEA furniture fast, I bring my own tools, and I have built over 100 pieces" is far stronger than "I am a hard worker".

Complete the background check and identity verification. Guides report most Taskers are ready within four business days. Once approved, you pay the one time, non-refundable registration fee of 25 US dollars and set up direct deposit at the same time.

Step 3: Set your rates and availability

TaskRabbit offers pricing guidance based on your location, task category and experience level, so start with the rate it suggests. New Taskers usually start slightly below the local average to win their first bookings, then raise their rates as reviews arrive. A cleaner who starts at 25 US dollars an hour and rises to 35 after ten good reviews earns more over a year than a cleaner who starts at 40 and waits weeks for a first booking.

Set your weekly availability honestly and keep it current. Offer at least 2.5 hour blocks, keep dates open up to 17 days ahead, and opt in to same day jobs at first, because they are the fastest way to get booked and build reviews.

Choose your categories with care. Offer the tasks you are genuinely good at, and add more categories only when you can do them well. A Tasker who offers five strong categories gets more bookings than a Tasker who offers fifteen weak ones. Skilled categories such as handyman work, TV mounting and appliance installation pay more than general cleaning or waiting in line, because fewer Taskers can do them well.

Step 4: Win your first jobs

Your first jobs are about reviews, not money. Price competitively, respond fast, and over deliver on the small things. Reply to invitations within an hour, even if you decide not to take the job, because leaving an invite pending can hurt your ranking in client searches. Confirm the scope by message before you arrive, so there are no surprises about what the job includes.

On the day, show up on time, dress neatly, and bring your tools. Take photos of your work as you finish each task, so you can add them to your profile and show the next client what you do. Ask the client to leave a review when you send the invoice, while the good work is fresh in their mind. One early review changes everything, because clients filter by ratings and a Tasker with no reviews looks risky next to one with ten.

Step 5: Grow your reviews and your rates

Once you have ten or more good reviews, raise your rates in small steps. Guides report that TaskRabbit itself suggests higher rates as your record improves, and clients accept a higher rate from a Tasker with strong reviews far more readily than from a new profile. Raise one category at a time, watch whether bookings fall, and adjust.

Finally, treat it like a business. Track your hours, your fuel, your tools, and your income, because you will need those records at tax time. In the United States, TaskRabbit sends a 1099 form if you earn over 600 US dollars in a calendar year, and you are responsible for your own tax. Set aside part of every payment for tax, and do not spend money you will owe later.

Worked example: what a part time Tasker could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can test your own figures.

Imagine a Tasker in a mid sized city who assembles furniture at 40 US dollars an hour, working 10 hours a week. That is 400 US dollars a week, or 1,600 US dollars in a four week month. TaskRabbit takes no cut, so the full 1,600 US dollars is hers before her own costs, and clients can tip on top.

Now subtract the real costs. Fuel and travel for the month might be 120 US dollars. Tool wear and replacement might be 30 US dollars. The 25 US dollar registration fee is a one off cost in the first month. That leaves 1,425 US dollars before tax in the first month, and 1,450 in later months without the registration fee. She must also set aside money for tax, because she is self employed. If she sets aside 20 percent, that is 320 US dollars, leaving 1,130 US dollars after costs and tax in a later month.

Recheck: 10 times 40 is 400. 400 times 4 is 1,600. 1,600 minus 120 minus 30 minus 25 is 1,425. 1,600 minus 120 minus 30 is 1,450. 20 percent of 1,600 is 320, and 1,450 minus 320 is 1,130. The maths checks out.

What it costs and what to expect

Starting costs are small but real. The registration fee is a one time 25 US dollars, non-refundable, and it varies by location. You also need a smartphone, a checking account, and your own tools, which can cost 50 to 200 US dollars if you are starting from scratch. There is no subscription fee and no commission on your earnings.

Expect uneven income. Guides report new Taskers earning around 1,000 to 1,500 US dollars a month, but that is the guides' estimate and it may not be realistic for everyone. Your income depends on your city, your categories, your reviews, and how many hours you make yourself available.

Results vary with location, demand, ratings, and effort. TaskRabbit can be a useful side hustle or a full time living for skilled Taskers in busy cities, but it is not a guaranteed paycheck, and you should not rely on it for rent until you have several months of steady bookings behind you.

Common mistakes

  • Offering categories you cannot do well, then earning a bad first review.
  • Setting your rate too high as a new Tasker with no reviews, then waiting weeks for a booking.
  • Leaving invitations pending for hours, which hurts your ranking in client searches.
  • Arriving without the right tools, then wasting the client's time and your own.
  • Forgetting to ask for a review after a good job, which slows your growth.
  • Taking payment off the platform, which removes your protection and your record.
  • Spending your full earnings without setting money aside for tax.
  • Ignoring your availability calendar, so clients see you as unavailable when you are free.

Risks and scams to watch for

TaskRabbit is a legitimate platform, but its local, in person nature creates risks that online platforms do not. Never take a job that feels unsafe. If a client's messages are aggressive, if the address looks wrong, or if they ask you to come alone to an isolated place at night, cancel the task and report it. Your safety matters more than any booking.

Be careful with payment. All payment should go through the app, by direct deposit after you submit your invoice. Never accept cash for a TaskRabbit job and never pay a client for "materials" upfront with your own money unless the expense is agreed in the app, where the client can reimburse you. A client who asks you to buy expensive items with your own cash and promises to pay you back later is a warning sign.

TaskRabbit will not ask you to pay a fee by gift card, wire transfer, or a link in a text message. The only fee is the 25 US dollar registration fee paid through the app.

Frequently asked questions

How much can you make on TaskRabbit?

It depends on your city, your category, your rate, and your reviews. New Taskers typically start at 15 to 25 US dollars an hour, and experienced Taskers in skilled categories report 60 to 75 US dollars an hour or more. Average figures conflict, with TaskRabbit reporting 47 US dollars an hour and ZipRecruiter reporting 17.53, so do not plan your budget on any single average.

Is TaskRabbit worth it?

It can be, if you live in an active market and you have hands on skills. It is less useful if you live in a quiet area, if you want predictable income, or if you prefer remote work.

How do I become a Tasker?

Sign up on the TaskRabbit website, download the Tasker app, complete verification and a background check, build your profile with your services and rates, and pay the 25 US dollar registration fee once approved. Guides report most Taskers are ready within four business days.

Does TaskRabbit take a cut of your pay?

No. TaskRabbit charges the client a service fee and a Trust and Support fee on top of your rate. You keep 100 percent of the hourly rate you set, plus 100 percent of any tips. The only fee you pay is the one time 25 US dollar registration fee.

Do you need experience to work on TaskRabbit?

Not always formal experience, but practical skill matters. A Tasker with real experience in moving, mounting, assembly or repairs will find it easier to get hired and to charge more. Informal experience counts if you can show photos of finished work.

Can you make 1,000 US dollars a week on TaskRabbit?

Some Taskers can, but it is not typical or guaranteed. It usually needs a strong local market, good reviews, high demand categories, and 30 or more hours a week. For most part time Taskers, a few hundred US dollars a week is a more realistic aim.

Conclusion

To earn money on TaskRabbit, check you meet the requirements, build a profile with a clear photo and honest pitches, start with competitive rates in one or two strong categories, respond fast, over deliver on your first jobs, and raise your rates as reviews arrive. You keep 100 percent of your rate plus tips, and the only fee is the 25 US dollar registration, but income is uneven and demand depends on your city, so build slowly and keep your expectations honest.
