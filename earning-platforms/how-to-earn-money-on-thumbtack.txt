<p>If you have a practical skill, such as cleaning, handyman work, photography, or personal training, you may be able to turn it into steady local income. Learning how to earn money on Thumbtack is a good start, because Thumbtack is one of the largest local services marketplaces in the United States, and it works differently from most gig apps.</p>
<p>On Thumbtack, customers pay nothing to use the platform. Service professionals, called pros, pay for leads. You buy credits, you spend them to contact customers, and you keep the full amount you charge for the job. There is no commission on completed work, but you pay whether or not the lead turns into a job.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-thumbtack-and-how-does-it-work">What is Thumbtack and how does it work</h2>
<p>Thumbtack, at <a href="https://www.thumbtack.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">thumbtack.com</a>, is an American online marketplace that connects customers with local service professionals. It was founded in 2008 and is based in San Francisco. Its homepage lists more than 300,000 local pros, 15 million or more reviews, and 100 million or more home projects. It operates in all 50 US states and covers more than 1,100 service categories, from home cleaning and plumbing to photography, tutoring, and personal training.</p>
<p>The process is simple from the customer side. A customer describes a project, answers a few questions about scope, location, timing, and budget, and submits the request. Thumbtack matches the request with relevant pros in the area. The customer sees profiles, reads reviews, compares quotes, and hires the pro they like.</p>
<p>From the pro side, the process is different. You create a profile, pass a background check, and wait for matching leads. When a customer request matches your profile, you get a notification. You can then spend credits to unlock the customer details and send a quote. If the customer hires you, you do the work and the customer pays you directly.</p>
<p>Thumbtack takes no commission on the completed job. That is the key difference from most marketplaces. The risk is that you pay for the lead before you know whether you will win the job. Repeat customers and referrals are free, because Thumbtack only charges for the first contact with a new customer.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-join-as-a-pro-and-pass-the-background-check">Step 1: Join as a pro and pass the background check</h2>
<p>Joining Thumbtack as a pro is free, but you must pass a background check before your profile goes live. Have your ID and business details ready. The check protects customers, and it also protects serious pros, because it keeps unreliable operators off the platform.</p>
<p>You also need the basics for your trade. That means the right tools, transport, and any licence or insurance your state or city requires. For example, electricians and plumbers often need a licence, while cleaners may need liability insurance. Check your local rules before you apply, because Thumbtack cannot fix a missing licence for you.</p>
<p>Thumbtack is only for work in the United States. If you live outside the US, you cannot use it to find local jobs. That is a hard limit, not a setting you can change.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-build-a-profile-that-wins-trust">Step 2: Build a profile that wins trust</h2>
<p>Your profile is your shop window. Customers compare several pros at once, so a complete profile wins more work than a thin one.</p>
<p>Start with a clear description of your services. Say exactly what you do, which areas you serve, and what a typical job includes. Add high quality photos of your past work. A cleaner should show spotless rooms. A handyman should show finished repairs. A photographer should show real sessions, not stock photos.</p>
<p>Add your credentials, licences, and insurance details. Add a friendly introduction that says who you are and how you work. Customers hire people they trust, and a human introduction builds trust faster than a list of services.</p>
<p>Ask for reviews from the start. Your review score is one of the strongest factors in winning jobs. A steady stream of five star reviews helps customers trust you and often lets you charge more. After each job, politely ask the customer to leave a review. Most happy customers will do it if you ask at the right moment.</p>
<p>Thumbtack also offers a guarantee to customers. Its homepage says that if a job is not done as agreed, the customer could get up to $2,500 back, with terms applying. That guarantee helps customers hire with confidence, which helps good pros win work.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-buy-credits-and-choose-your-leads">Step 3: Buy credits and choose your leads</h2>
<p>Leads are the heart of Thumbtack, and they are also its biggest cost. You buy Thumbtack credits in advance, and you spend them to contact customers. When you send a quote, the credit is spent, whether or not the customer hires you.</p>
<p>Lead prices vary by service type, location, job size, and competition. A lead for a plumbing emergency in a big city costs more than a lead for a photography session in a small town. The table in the pay section below shows the credit prices reported by independent reviews.</p>
<p>Set a weekly budget for leads and stick to it. Thumbtack lets you control how many leads you pursue, so start small while you learn which leads convert. A lead is only worth buying if the job is big enough, close enough, and likely enough to win. Check the job size, the budget listed, the distance you must travel, and whether the customer seems ready to hire before you spend credits.</p>
<p>Thumbtack also offers Promote, a paid visibility option. Promoted profiles rank higher in search results, and Thumbtack reports a discount of about 20% on credit bundles for Promote users. Paid visibility can bring more leads, but it also brings more spending, so test it with a small budget first.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/earning-platforms-thumbtack-quote-lead.jpg" alt="A home service professional in a work van typing a job quote on a smartphone" /></p>
<h2 id="step-4-send-a-quote-and-win-the-job">Step 4: Send a quote and win the job</h2>
<p>Speed matters. Customers often hire the first pro who replies, and Thumbtack tracks your response time. Aim to reply within minutes when you can, especially during working hours.</p>
<p>Write a quote that answers the customer's real question. Say what you will do, how long it will take, and what it will cost. Keep it short and specific. A quote that says "I can do this for $120 on Saturday, including supplies" beats a vague message that says "Contact me for a price."</p>
<p>Set your price with care. If you price too low, you limit your income and attract the hardest customers. If you price too high without enough reviews, customers will skip your profile. Most pros start in the middle range and raise prices as their reputation grows. You can charge by the hour, by the project, or by a flat rate, depending on the service.</p>
<p>Follow up once if you hear nothing, then move on. Chasing a cold lead wastes time you could spend on a warm one.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-do-great-work-and-turn-jobs-into-repeat-clients">Step 5: Do great work and turn jobs into repeat clients</h2>
<p>The real money on Thumbtack comes from repeat clients. A one-time cleaning can become a weekly booking. A small repair can become a regular maintenance contract. Repeat customers are free on Thumbtack, because you only pay for the first contact, so they are the most profitable jobs you will ever get.</p>
<p>Do the basics well. Arrive on time, communicate clearly, send updates or photos when helpful, and leave the job cleaner or more organised than you found it. Those small touches earn five star reviews and tips, especially in cleaning, home services, moving, personal training, and event work.</p>
<p>Ask for reviews after every job, and keep your profile updated with new photos and services. A profile that grows with your business wins more leads at a lower cost per job.</p>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Thumbtack is a legitimate platform, but the lead model has real risks.</p>
<p>The biggest risk is paying for leads that never convert. If you buy 20 leads and win only two jobs, your cost per job is very high. Track your win rate every week. If a category or an area never converts, stop buying those leads.</p>
<p>Watch for fake customers and off-platform scams. If a customer asks you to pay a fee before a job, buy gift cards, or move payment outside Thumbtack in a strange way, walk away. Real Thumbtack work is quoted and booked through the platform. Thumbtack's help centre at <a href="https://help.thumbtack.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">help.thumbtack.com</a> explains how leads, refunds, and payments should work.</p>
<p>Check your insurance before your first job. Your regular car or home insurance may not cover you while you work for pay. Tell your insurer what you do, and ask what cover you need. Keep records of your income and expenses, because you are self-employed and you handle your own tax.</p>
<p>Account problems can also hurt. Low ratings, late replies, and cancelled jobs can reduce your visibility. Protect your rating by communicating delays early, following the job details exactly, and fixing problems fast when they happen.</p>

<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-thumbtack-legit-for-pros">Is Thumbtack legit for pros?</h3>
<p>Yes. Thumbtack is a real marketplace founded in 2008, with more than 300,000 pros and more than 15 million reviews listed on its homepage. It is free to join as a pro. That said, you pay for leads whether or not you win the job, so it is not free to use. Judge it by your cost per booked job, not by the sign-up price.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-can-i-earn-on-thumbtack">How much can I earn on Thumbtack?</h3>
<p>It depends on your service, area, prices, and reviews. Pro reports list home cleaning at $25 to $50 an hour, handyman work at $40 to $80 an hour, and photography at $50 to $150 per session. Your net pay is lower than your gross, because you pay for leads, fuel, supplies, and insurance. Track all of those costs before you judge your income.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-do-thumbtack-leads-cost">How much do Thumbtack leads cost?</h3>
<p>Leads are paid with prepaid credits. Independent reviews report a credit at about $1.42 to $1.50, with bundles of about 12 credits for $18, 24 for $35, or 60 for $85. The cost per lead varies by service, location, and demand, and a single lead can cost several credits. Check the current credit prices in your pro account before you buy.</p>
<h3 id="does-thumbtack-take-a-commission-on-my-jobs">Does Thumbtack take a commission on my jobs?</h3>
<p>No. Thumbtack takes no commission on completed jobs. You keep the full amount you charge. You pay for leads instead, and some categories may carry a service fee when customers pay through Thumbtack's in-app payments. Repeat customers are free.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-a-licence-to-work-on-thumbtack">Do I need a licence to work on Thumbtack?</h3>
<p>It depends on your trade and your state. Some trades, such as electrical and plumbing work, often need a licence. Others, such as cleaning, may need insurance instead. Check your state and city rules before you apply, and add your licence and insurance details to your profile.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-use-thumbtack-outside-the-united-states">Can I use Thumbtack outside the United States?</h3>
<p>No. Thumbtack covers all 50 US states, but it does not offer local service jobs in other countries. If you live outside the US, look at online freelance platforms instead, such as the ones in our <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms" rel="noopener" target="_blank">earning platform guides</a>.</p>
