<h2 id="what-kolabtree-is-and-how-to-earn-money-on-kolabtree">What Kolabtree is, and how to earn money on Kolabtree</h2>
<p>Kolabtree is a freelance marketplace for scientists, researchers, and technical experts. Businesses, academics, and startups post projects that need specialist knowledge, and experts bid for the work. Kolabtree states that it has over 20,000 experts across more than 170 countries. Its healthcare pages state a smaller figure of more than 15,000 freelance scientists. Either way, the pool is large, and it is made of credentialed experts rather than general freelancers.</p>
<p>Kolabtree suits PhD holders, postdoctoral researchers, university lecturers, industry scientists, and consultants with deep subject knowledge. It suits people who can write grants, analyse data, or advise on a technical problem. It also suits researchers who want part time work alongside an academic post, because projects are usually short or medium term.</p>
<p>It does not suit everyone. If you do not have a research background or a higher degree, Kolabtree will be hard to join, because clients choose experts by their credentials. It also does not suit people who need fast, simple gig work. If your skills are general rather than scientific, a general marketplace is a better fit. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-make-money-on-upwork" rel="noopener" target="_blank">making money on Upwork</a> covers broader freelance work, and our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-data-analyst" rel="noopener" target="_blank">working as a freelance data analyst</a> covers a related skill.</p>

Healthcare clients also buy medical device development and healthcare app development. Outside healthcare, clients buy food science work, such as product formulation and chemical engineering advice.</p>

<h2 id="step-1-join-as-an-expert-and-get-your-profile-approved">Step 1: Join as an expert and get your profile approved</h2>
<p>Learning how to earn money on Kolabtree starts with an expert profile. Kolabtree states that your profile must be complete and approved before you can submit proposals, so treat the profile as your application. Fill in every section. List your degree, your university, your field, your publications, your work history, and your skills. Add your services, your rates, and your availability. A half finished profile will not be approved.</p>
<p>Be precise about your credentials. Clients on Kolabtree check degrees and institutions, so state your highest degree, your subject, and where you studied. List publications with their titles and journals, because they prove you can do the work. If you have industry experience, describe the projects and the results, without sharing confidential client details.</p>
<p>One fact this guide could not verify is the exact approval criteria beyond a complete and approved profile. Kolabtree does not publish a checklist, and it does not state whether experts without a PhD can join. Its pages call on postdocs and PhDs to join, and state that most experts hold PhDs. If you do not hold a PhD, apply with a strong profile and a clear record of research work, but be aware that approval is not certain. Do not pay anyone who offers to get you approved.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-find-projects-and-submit-proposals">Step 2: Find projects and submit proposals</h2>
<p>Kolabtree <a href="https://www.kolabtree.com/how-it-works" rel="noopener" target="_blank">states that there are four ways</a> for an expert to submit proposals. First, a client may invite you directly, after finding your profile. Second, the Kolabtree team may invite you to a project that fits your profile. Third, you can visit the Browse Projects page and submit proposals directly. Fourth, Kolabtree may assess your proposal and take it forward. Most new experts will rely on the third route until they build a reputation.</p>

<h2 id="how-projects-work-statement-of-work-milestones-and-payment">How projects work: statement of work, milestones and payment</h2>
<p>When a client accepts your proposal, you agree a statement of work. A statement of work is a written agreement that states the deliverables, the timeline, and the fee. Read it before you accept it, and make sure it says what you agreed. If anything is missing or unclear, ask for it to be fixed before you start.</p>
<p>Payment on Kolabtree works through an escrow style system. The client deposits funds securely once they select you, and the funds are released only when the client approves the deliverables. You can be paid per project or by the hour, using milestones and timesheets to track progress and make partial payments. A milestone is a stage of the project with a payment attached, and a timesheet is a record of hours worked. This protects both sides.</p>
<p>Kolabtree also promises a 100% satisfaction guarantee for clients, with a refund if the client is unhappy, and it says it will intervene if an expert cannot deliver as stated in the statement of work. That means you must deliver what you agreed, or tell the client early if a problem arises. If you cannot finish a project, say so at once, because Kolabtree may assign another freelancer to redo the work.</p>
<p>Confidentiality is built into the platform. Kolabtree's terms include a default confidentiality clause and a default non disclosure agreement, which is a legal promise not to share the client's private information. The terms also state that freelancers may not claim ownership of client intellectual property. Respect those rules, and do not share client work as a portfolio sample without written permission.</p>
<h2 id="setting-your-rates">Setting your rates</h2>
<p>Kolabtree publishes hourly rates for some featured experts, and those figures give a useful starting point. On its <a href="https://www.kolabtree.com/industry/healthcare" rel="noopener" target="_blank">healthcare page</a>, a biostatistician with a PhD in neuroscience from the University at Buffalo lists a rate of 80 US dollars per hour, and a medical device expert with a PhD in oncology from the University of Cambridge lists a rate of 100 US dollars per hour. These are published rates for named experts, not averages.</p>
<p>Other rate claims exist, but they are not verified by Kolabtree. A course provider aimed at Indian PhD scholars claims that Indian PhDs on Kolabtree charge 30 to 100 US dollars per hour or more, and that grant writing pays 75 to 150 US dollars per hour. Those figures come from a sales page for a paid bootcamp, not from Kolabtree, so treat them as reported claims, not facts. Set your rate from your costs, your experience, and the published rates of experts in your field.</p>
<p>For fixed price projects, quote from the deliverable, not from your hours. A systematic review that takes two weeks should be priced as a project, with a clear list of what is included. State how many revision rounds are included, and charge for extra work. For hourly work, keep a timesheet, and agree the hours and the rate in the statement of work before you start.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-kolabtree-without-a-track-record">How to earn money on Kolabtree without a track record</h2>
<p>A new expert on Kolabtree faces a real problem. Clients prefer experts with completed projects and reviews, and you have none yet. The way through is to make your first applications as strong as possible. Apply for smaller projects first, such as a literature search or a short statistical review, where the client can judge your work quickly. Write each proposal for that specific project, and show that you understand the brief. Mention one publication or past project that matches the work.</p>
<p>Keep your profile focused on the services that clients need often. If you can do systematic reviews, say so clearly, because that is a common first project. Respond to invitations fast, and be available for a call. Clients often choose the expert who replies quickly, especially when several experts have similar credentials.</p>
<p>If you are a PhD student, be honest about your status and your time. Kolabtree projects can fit around research work, and one course provider suggests starting at 10 to 15 hours a week alongside a PhD. Check that your university or funding rules allow outside freelance work before you take a project, because some funding agreements limit outside earnings.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Learning how to earn money on Kolabtree costs nothing for experts. There is no subscription fee stated for experts, and clients post projects for free. Your real costs are the time to build a profile, write proposals, and do the work. There may also be costs for software or tools your field needs, such as statistical software.</p>
<p>Kolabtree is a slow start for most new experts. Your profile must be approved first, then you must win a first project. That can take weeks or months. Income grows as you complete projects and collect reviews. Do not expect a full income in your first month, and keep other work going while you build your record.</p>
<p><strong>Worked example, example only: these numbers are not typical results.</strong> Imagine you win a statistical review project priced at 500 US dollars, like the pre clinical review in Kolabtree's own success stories, and it takes you one week. If you work 10 hours on it, your effective rate is 50 US dollars per hour. If it takes you 20 hours, your effective rate is 25 US dollars per hour. The project price is fixed, so the hours you spend decide what you earn per hour. Always estimate your hours before you quote a fixed price.</p>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>The main risk in learning how to earn money on Kolabtree is a slow start with no income while you wait for approval and a first project. Plan for it by keeping other income while you build your record. A second risk is scope creep, where a client asks for more work. Prevent this with a clear statement of work and a limit on revision rounds.</p>
<p>Watch for scams that use Kolabtree's name. Real Kolabtree work runs through the platform, with a statement of work and funds held securely. Be wary of anyone who claims to be a Kolabtree client but asks you to work outside the platform, pay a fee to receive a project, or share bank details before any work is agreed. Never pay for a project, and never send work before the statement of work is agreed. If you are unsure about a message, contact Kolabtree support before you share personal details. In the United States, you can report fraud to the Federal Trade Commission at <a href="https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ReportFraud.ftc.gov</a>.</p>
