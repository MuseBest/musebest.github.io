How to Earn Money on Turing in 2026

You want to earn money on Turing in 2026, but you are not sure how the platform works or whether you can pass its tests. The honest answer is simple. Turing hires experienced engineers and subject experts as contractors for remote software work and AI training work. You apply to a listing, you pass proctored tests and interviews, and Turing pays you monthly for approved hours if a client selects you. This guide shows you each step, what it costs, and what to check before you rely on it for income.

What Turing is, and what it is not

Turing began in 2018 as a marketplace for remote software engineers, and it now also supplies coding and reasoning data to AI labs. Its platform runs at work.turing.com, where you make one profile and apply to listings one at a time. Most listings are full time contracts, typically 40 hours a week, with overlap with US Pacific time. Turing says all work is paid, with no unpaid trials, as of November 2026.

Turing is not a freelance marketplace. On Turing, you apply to a specific listing, state your hourly rate, take the required tests, and work on one engagement at a time. Turing charges no joining fee. Its model is a margin between client pay and your rate, which it does not publish.

You work as an independent contractor, so do not expect employee benefits such as paid leave. Contracts are fixed term and can end when client needs change. Passing the tests does not guarantee a project.

Who Turing suits, and who it does not

Turing suits experienced software engineers and STEM specialists who can pass timed, proctored tests. STEM means science, technology, engineering and mathematics. Roles also exist for experts in medicine, finance, law and languages. Clear written English matters, because much of the work involves explaining reasoning in writing.

Turing does not suit beginners, generalists looking for small tasks, or people who need quick income. Vetting is selective, reviews can take weeks, and applicants who are not picked often hear nothing. If you want short projects with payment protection, a freelance marketplace will suit you better. How to earn money on Turing in 7 steps

Follow these steps in order. Set aside a quiet day for the tests, because they are timed and recorded.

Step 1: Check that you meet the basic requirements

You must be 18 or over, with a desktop or laptop, a webcam and a stable internet connection, because phones cannot take the tests. You need a valid physical passport or driving licence for identity checks, and a bank account in your own name. Country eligibility is set listing by listing, so read the location line on each listing. Never use a VPN to get around a country restriction.

Step 2: Create your account and submit your profile

Create an account at work.turing.com and upload your CV in English. Complete your details, education, skills and experience, then press Submit Profile, because you cannot apply until your profile is submitted. List only skills you can be tested on, and keep to one account.

Step 3: Apply to one listing and state your hourly rate

Filter listings by skill or category, and read the hours and time zone overlap before you apply. Choose Apply Now and complete the short form, which includes your hourly rate. Decide your rate first, because the form cannot be changed after you submit. Pay is often shared later, not in the listing, so confirm the rate before you onboard.

Step 4: Take the proctored assessments

Most listings require a proctored assessment, a timed test with your screen, webcam and microphone recorded. Turn off extensions and VPNs, share your entire screen, and use a connection of at least 4 Mbps. A finished assessment cannot be retaken, and if you fail you must wait 90 days. Applications are not reviewed until every assessment is done.

Step 5: Complete interviews and wait for review

Some roles add an AI video interview or a live interview. Treat the AI interview as a single attempt. Turing says reviews take 48 hours to six weeks, and only people moving forward are contacted. Shortlisted does not guarantee selection.

Step 6: Confirm terms and onboard

If Turing contacts you, confirm your rate, availability, start date, hours and time zone overlap. Onboarding starts only once the client chooses you, and includes terms, ID verification, a tax form and payment details. Some projects onboard through Deel.

Step 7: Log hours accurately and protect your quality scores

Read the project guidelines end to end and log your hours accurately, because pay follows approved hours. Turing treats misuse of AI tools and falsified work as grounds for removal, which can mean no pay for the work concerned and a permanent ban.

How you get paid

Turing pays contractors monthly for manager approved hours. Its official how to get paid help page says payments are processed on the 5th calendar day of each month, bank processing typically takes 2 to 3 days after payment is sent, and funds may take up to 72 business hours to appear. Other Turing help pages describe pay by the 5th business day of the next month into a wallet or bank account, and some projects pay through Deel. Because pages differ, check the pay cycle in your own contract during onboarding.

Bank transfer is the supported withdrawal method, by local bank transfer or SWIFT. PayPal and Airtm are not supported for withdrawals, according to the help page checked as of November 2026. Your bank account must be in your own name and match the full legal name on your Turing profile. As an independent contractor, you are responsible for taxes in your country, and you do not pay taxes to Turing.

Turing charges you no explicit fee to join or apply. The margin between client billing and your rate is built into client pricing and is not disclosed. Some contractors report late or stuck withdrawals, so keep records of your hours and approvals, and contact support through official channels if pay is late.

What it costs and what to expect

Joining, applying and taking assessments on Turing cost you nothing in fees. Your real costs are time, equipment and patience. You need a reliable computer, stable internet and a quiet place for timed tests.

Example only: suppose you are selected for a contract at an assumed rate of 25 US dollars per hour, and you work an assumed 40 hours per week for four weeks. That is 160 hours times 25, which is 4,000 US dollars before tax and transfer fees. These figures are assumed for the example. Real rates vary by project, role and location, and many applicants never get selected at all.

Expect a slow start. A realistic first month is week one for your profile and first application, week two for assessments, and weeks three and four for review and interviews. Reviews can take up to six weeks, and you may hear nothing if you are not picked. Passing tests does not guarantee paid work, and contracts can end early when client needs change, so do not quit other income until you have a signed agreement and a first payment.

Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses and platform fees. Tax rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.

Common mistakes

  • Listing skills you cannot be tested on, so you get matched to roles that do not fit.
  • Submitting an application form with a rushed hourly rate that cannot be changed later.
  • Taking proctored tests on a phone, with a VPN on, or with extensions running.
  • Applying to listings without reading the country, hours and time zone overlap rules.
  • Creating a second account, which causes identity verification to fail.
  • Assuming that passing tests guarantees paid work or a long contract.
  • Using AI tools on project tasks in ways the project rules forbid, which can end the engagement.

Risks and scams to watch for

Turing is a real company with a public job board and help centre, but its name is misused by scammers, and its contractor model carries real risks. Learn the warning signs before you apply.

First, never pay to get work. Genuine Turing roles are listed on work.turing.com and handled through your Turing account. Treat any request for a fee, or a recruiter who works only over WhatsApp or Telegram, as a red flag. The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same warning in its job scams advice.

Second, verify every offer. Turing does message people on WhatsApp in some flows, so check any offer against your account at work.turing.com before you share documents or bank details. Onboarding mail should come from official Turing addresses, and support runs through official channels.

Third, protect your identity and your account. Never use a VPN or another person identity to bypass country rules, because accounts caught doing so are closed, often with unpaid earnings. Keep your profile name, date of birth and bank account name consistent, or identity checks will fail.

Fourth, understand the income risk. Contracts are fixed term, can end early, and carry no paid leave. Pay waits on manager approval, and some contractors report withdrawals stuck for weeks. Keep other income where you can, log hours on time, and keep copies of your agreements and approvals. Our guide on how to spot online job scams lists the full set of checks.

Frequently asked questions

Is Turing free to join?

Yes. Creating an account, applying to listings and taking assessments cost you nothing. Turing makes its money from client contracts, through a margin it does not publish. Never pay anyone who claims to sell you access to Turing work.

How does Turing pay you?

Turing pays monthly for manager approved hours, processed on the 5th calendar day of each month according to its help page, with bank transfer as the supported method. Some projects pay through Deel. Check the pay cycle in your own contract.

How much can you earn on Turing?

There is no single rate. Pay is typically hourly and set per project, and many listings share the rate only after you apply. Independent trackers report wide ranges, but those are estimates, not promises. Confirm your rate before you onboard.

Is Turing legit?

Yes. Turing is a real, established company that hires contractors through a public board and documented onboarding. The main risks are selective vetting, silent rejections, hidden pay on many listings, and fixed term contracts that can end early.

How hard is it to get accepted on Turing?

It is selective. You must pass proctored technical or subject tests and often interviews, and the client makes the final decision. Reviews take 48 hours to six weeks, and only selected candidates are contacted.

Can I work part time on Turing?

Most listings ask for about 40 hours a week with time zone overlap, so part time work is limited. Read the hours on each listing before you apply, and do not apply if you cannot meet them.

Conclusion

Turing is worth applying to if you are an experienced engineer or expert who can pass proctored tests and commit to full time contractor hours. It charges no joining fee, it pays monthly for approved hours, and the work is close to real engineering and expert reasoning. It cannot guarantee selection, steady work or long contracts, so your test preparation, your stated rate and your written agreement do the real work.
