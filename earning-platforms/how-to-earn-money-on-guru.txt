2b:T4908,<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-guru-in-2026">How to Earn Money on Guru in 2026</h2>
<p>Learning how to earn money on Guru is a good choice if you want a quieter marketplace than Upwork or Fiverr, and you are willing to bid carefully for each job. Guru.com is one of the oldest freelance platforms, and it works around two ideas: a WorkRoom for every project, and SafePay, an escrow service that holds the client's money until the work is done. Its fee system is more complicated than most platforms, and that is the first thing to understand before you join.</p>
<p>This guide is for freelancers in writing, design, programming, admin, finance and other professional services who want long term clients rather than one off gigs. You will learn how Guru works, what each membership costs, how to bid well, how SafePay protects you, and what a realistic first six months looks like.</p>

<h3 id="what-guru-is-and-who-it-suits">What Guru is and who it suits</h3>
<p>Guru.com is an online marketplace where employers, as Guru calls its clients, post jobs and freelancers send quotes to win them. It has operated since 1998, which makes it older than Upwork and Fiverr, and it has a smaller, more professional community than either of them.</p>
<p>Guru covers a wide range of work: programming and web development, design, writing, admin support, finance and accounting, marketing, and more. It suits you if you have a clear skill, you are comfortable bidding for work, and you want repeat clients who hire you again. It suits you less if you want clients to come to you without bidding, or if you want quick gig sales, because Guru is built around active bidding and employers choosing from quotes.</p>
<p>Guru is quieter than the big platforms, and that cuts both ways. You face fewer bids per job, often 15 to 20 instead of 50 or more, so a good quote stands out. But there is also less total work, so a slow week on Guru can mean no income at all.</p>

<p>One figure needs a warning. Guides disagree on the Basic+ job fee: some list 9 percent and others list 7 percent, so we give the full range of 5 to 9 percent and flag the Basic+ figure as varying by source. Annual billing lowers the monthly prices, with guides reporting savings of up to 27 percent. Employers also pay a 2.9 percent handling fee on each invoice, which does not come out of your pay, although an employer can agree to cover part of your job fee.</p>
<p>Every project runs in a WorkRoom, a private workspace for you and the employer. Files, messages, deadlines and payments all live there. SafePay is Guru's escrow service: the employer funds SafePay before you start, Guru holds the money, and you request release when a milestone is done. If you work without SafePay funded, you are working on trust alone, and guides are blunt that hope is not a business strategy.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-your-account-and-choose-a-membership">Step 1: Create your account and choose a membership</h3>
<p>Sign up on <a href="https://www.guru.com" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Guru</a> with your email address and create your freelancer profile. Joining is free, and the Basic membership costs nothing. You can browse jobs and send up to 10 bids a month on Basic, enough to test the platform before you spend anything.</p>
<p>Start on Basic, send your 10 bids carefully, and see whether you get replies. If you win work and run out of bids, then consider Basic+ at 11.95 US dollars a month. The higher tiers only make sense once your monthly billings are large enough that the fee saving covers the subscription. For example, Professional costs 21.95 US dollars a month and saves 2 percentage points against Basic, so it only pays for itself once you bill about 1,100 US dollars a month. Do the same maths for your own figures before you upgrade.</p>
<p>Watch the auto renewal. Guides report paid memberships renew automatically, are non-refundable, and drop you back to Basic on expiry. Check the current terms on <a href="https://www.guru.com/pricing-freelancer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Guru's pricing page</a> before you choose a plan.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-build-a-profile-that-wins-trust">Step 2: Build a profile that wins trust</h3>
<p>Your Guru profile is your storefront. Employers see your total earnings and your feedback score, so a new profile with zero earnings looks risky to them. Your job in the first months is to change that, one small project at a time.</p>
<p>Pick one narrow niche and build the whole profile around it. Do not list yourself as a virtual assistant, logo designer and SEO expert at once. Employers hire specialists. If you write, be a technical writer for software companies, or a product description writer for online stores. If you design, be a brand identity designer for food businesses.</p>
<p>Write a bio that says what the employer gets, not how many years you have worked. "I help small business owners save 10 hours a week by managing their social media" is stronger than "I have 5 years of experience in social media". Use short paragraphs and bullet points so it reads well on a phone, and name the real tools you use, such as WordPress, Trello or Slack.</p>
<p>Build a portfolio even if you have no clients yet. Writers can upload three strong sample articles as PDFs. Developers can link to a GitHub repository or a live demo. Designers can upload concept work. An employer wants to see that you can do the work, and does not always care who paid for it first.</p>

<h3 id="step-3-find-jobs-worth-bidding-on">Step 3: Find jobs worth bidding on</h3>
<p>Guru gives you a limited number of bids each month, so every bid is precious. The biggest mistake new freelancers make is burning all 10 free bids in two days on jobs they cannot win.</p>
<p>Before you bid, check the employer. Look at their profile: do they have a verified payment method, have they hired people before, and do their past jobs have feedback? If an employer has posted 50 jobs and hired nobody, they are likely price shopping and will not hire you either. Skip them and save the bid.</p>
<p>Look for jobs that fit your niche exactly, where you can show relevant work in your quote. A job that asks for "email newsletter setup" is a better bid for a marketer than a job that asks for "marketing expert", because the first one has a clear beginning and end and you can prove you have done it before.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-write-quotes-that-get-replies">Step 4: Write quotes that get replies</h3>
<p>Guru employers are often small business owners who have used the platform for years. They can spot a copied template in seconds, and a generic quote goes straight to the archive. Treat each bid like a short consultation, not a lottery ticket.</p>
<p>Open with their problem, not your history. If they need a WordPress site fixed, mention the specific plugin that might be causing the issue, or ask a question about their hosting. That proves you read the job post, which most bidders do not do. Then give a clear price and a clear scope, broken into milestones. For a 400 US dollar project, suggest 100 US dollars for research, 200 for the first draft, and 100 for final revisions. Milestones make the employer feel safer, because they are not committing the whole amount at once, and they get you paid faster.</p>
<p>Do not lowball. A winning pitch that explains why your skills fit the job beats a cheap price, so price within a competitive range and explain what the employer gets.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-work-in-the-workroom-and-get-paid-with-safepay">Step 5: Work in the WorkRoom and get paid with SafePay</h3>
<p>When an employer accepts your quote, agree the milestones in the WorkRoom before you start. For a 50 US dollar first draft, the employer funds 50 US dollars in SafePay, you do the work, you upload the file to the WorkRoom, and the employer releases the payment. That is the whole flow, and it only protects you if you follow it.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/guru-safepay-workroom.jpg" alt="A freelancer managing project milestones on a laptop, the screen showing a simple project dashboard with a checklist and progress bar, files and a calendar on the desk" /></p>
<p>Keep every message and file in the WorkRoom. If anything goes wrong, Guru uses the messages and files there to decide who is right. If you move to WhatsApp or Telegram, Guru cannot help you, and that is exactly where most Guru scams happen. If a client asks to move off the platform, refuse and report it.</p>
<p>Getting paid means withdrawing from Guru to your own account. Guides report PayPal and bank wire as the main methods, with Guru taking 3 to 5 days to process withdrawals. One guide reports that Guru stopped Payoneer withdrawals in October 2023, so do not plan on Payoneer until you confirm it in your own account.</p>

<h3 id="worked-example-what-a-guru-project-really-pays">Worked example: what a Guru project really pays</h3>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can test your own figures.</p>
<p>Imagine a writer on the free Basic plan who wins a 400 US dollar fixed price project, split into two milestones of 200 US dollars each. Guru's job fee on Basic is 9 percent, so 36 US dollars comes off the 400, leaving 364 US dollars. The employer pays a 2.9 percent handling fee of 11.60 US dollars on their side, which does not reduce the writer's pay.</p>
<p>Now imagine the same writer on Professional at 21.95 US dollars a month, where the job fee is 7 percent. The fee is 28 US dollars, leaving 372 US dollars, which is 8 US dollars more than on Basic. But the membership costs 21.95 US dollars, so the writer is worse off on this single project, by 13.95 US dollars. Professional only pays for itself when monthly billings pass about 1,100 US dollars, where the 2 point saving equals the subscription.</p>
<p>Recheck: 9 percent of 400 is 36, and 400 minus 36 is 364. 7 percent of 400 is 28, and 400 minus 28 is 372. 372 minus 364 is 8. 21.95 minus 8 is 13.95. 21.95 divided by 0.02 is 1,097.50, so about 1,100 US dollars. The maths checks out.</p>
<h3 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h3>
<p>Joining Guru is free, and you can start on the free Basic plan with 10 bids a month. Paid plans run from 11.95 to 49.95 US dollars a month, and the job fee runs from 5 to 9 percent. Your main cost is time: writing careful quotes, waiting for replies, and doing the first small jobs that build your feedback score.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Guides suggest many beginners earn 0 to 150 US dollars in total in their first three months, while spending about 12 US dollars a month if they upgrade. By months 3 to 6, as your profile shows history, guides report 200 to 500 US dollars a month as a realistic range for part time effort, with higher skill niches moving faster. Treat those figures as one guide's estimates, not a promise, because your niche and your bidding skill decide the outcome.</p>
<p>Results vary with your niche, your quote quality, your reviews, and how many bids you can send. Guru rewards patience and repeat clients. If you need income this week, it is the wrong platform to rely on alone.</p>
<h3 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Burning all 10 free bids in two days on jobs you cannot win.</li>
<li>Sending the same copied quote to every job, which employers spot at once.</li>
<li>Bidding on jobs from employers who have never hired anyone.</li>
<li>Working before SafePay is funded, which means working on trust alone.</li>
<li>Moving the conversation to Telegram or WhatsApp, where Guru cannot protect you.</li>
<li>Promising a 3 day job in 24 hours, then earning a low first review for being late.</li>
<li>Paying for a membership before your monthly billings cover the subscription.</li>
<li>Forgetting that paid memberships auto renew, and paying for a month when you are on a break.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h3>
<p>The most common Guru scam is the off platform move. A fake employer hires you, then asks to continue on Telegram or WhatsApp for "faster communication". Once you are there, they ask for a security deposit, or ask you to work without SafePay. Never leave the platform for payments or project tracking. If it is not in the WorkRoom, it did not happen.</p>
<p>Watch for employers who ask for free sample work that is really the whole job. A short sample is normal. A full project delivered before any SafePay funding is not a test, it is unpaid work. Agree a small paid milestone first, even for a test.</p>
<p>Be careful with your own money too. Guru memberships auto renew, and guides report they are non-refundable. Check your renewal date every month, and cancel before you take a break. For a broader check before you commit to any platform, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-choose-earning-platform" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose an earning platform</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/freelance-contracts-explained" rel="noopener" target="_blank">freelance contracts explained</a>.</p>
<h3 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>How much can you earn on Guru?</h4>
<p>It depends on your niche, your reviews, and how much you bid. One guide estimates 0 to 150 US dollars in total in the first three months, rising to 200 to 500 US dollars a month by months 3 to 6 for part time effort. Those are the author's estimates, not a guarantee.</p>
<h4>Is Guru legit?</h4>
<p>Yes. Guru.com has operated since 1998 and is a real freelance marketplace with escrow payments through SafePay. It is smaller and quieter than Upwork or Fiverr, and its fee system is more complicated, but it is not a scam. Always keep payments inside the platform.</p>
<h4>What is SafePay on Guru?</h4>
<p>SafePay is Guru's escrow service. The employer funds SafePay before you start, Guru holds the money, and you request release when a milestone is done. If the employer does not fund SafePay, do not start the work, because you have no payment protection.</p>
<h4>How much does Guru charge freelancers?</h4>
<p>Guru charges a job fee of 5 to 9 percent of each invoice, depending on membership. Basic is free with a 9 percent fee and 10 bids a month. Paid plans cost 11.95 to 49.95 US dollars a month and lower the fee to as little as 5 percent. Guides disagree on whether Basic+ charges 7 or 9 percent, so check the current pricing page.</p>
<h4>How do you get paid on Guru?</h4>
<p>You withdraw from Guru by PayPal or bank wire, with Guru taking 3 to 5 days to process withdrawals according to guides. One guide reports Payoneer withdrawals ended in October 2023. Check the withdrawal options in your own account, because methods and fees change.</p>
<h4>Can beginners earn on Guru?</h4>
<p>Yes, but slowly. Beginners usually start with small tasks to build reviews. Pick one narrow niche, write careful quotes, and expect the first 90 days to be about building a feedback score rather than earning serious money.</p>
<h3 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h3>
<p>To earn money on Guru, start on the free Basic plan, build a narrow specialist profile with real samples, check each employer before you bid, write short quotes that solve the employer's problem, and run every project through the WorkRoom with SafePay funded before you start. The fees are higher than they first look, and the first months are slow, but patient freelancers who turn one off jobs into recurring agreements can build steady income.</p>
