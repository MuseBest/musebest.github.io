<h2 id="what-outsourcely-is-and-how-to-earn-money-on-outsourcely">What Outsourcely is, and how to earn money on Outsourcely</h2>
<p>Outsourcely connects employers with remote workers for long term, dedicated roles. A company posts a job, searches worker profiles, and hires one person for an ongoing role. That is different from Fiverr or Upwork, where work is usually project based and short term.</p>
<p>Employers pay a subscription to use Outsourcely, and they can post unlimited jobs and message candidates directly. Third party reports state that employer pricing starts from 19 US dollars per month with a free trial, but that figure comes from comparison pages, not an official page, so treat it as a reported figure. Employers pay no per hire fees and no commission on salaries, according to the same reports. That model matters for workers, because the employer is not paying a fee on your salary, and your pay is agreed directly with them.</p>

<h2 id="who-outsourcely-suits">Who Outsourcely suits</h2>

It suits virtual assistants, customer support workers, social media managers, content writers, designers, and developers, because those are the roles employers hire for most often. It suits people who can work regular hours for one employer, communicate well, and commit to a role for months.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-outsourcely-the-two-ways-to-earn">How to earn money on Outsourcely: the two ways to earn</h2>
<p>There are two distinct ways to learn how to earn money on Outsourcely. The first is getting hired for a long term remote role. You create a worker profile, apply for jobs, attend interviews, and, if hired, work for that employer and get paid by them directly. This is the main route.</p>
<p>The second is the affiliate programme. You promote Outsourcely to employers, for example through a blog or a website, and you earn a commission when an employer signs up through your link. A third party affiliate listing reports that the programme pays 50% per sale, but that figure is reported by Lasso, not confirmed on an Outsourcely page opened for this guide, and the cookie duration is unknown. The affiliate route suits people who already run a website about remote work. Do not treat the 50% figure as a promise, because it could not be confirmed officially.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-create-a-worker-profile-that-employers-notice">Step 1: Create a worker profile that employers notice</h2>
<p>Your worker profile is how employers find you, so build it as a job application. Start with a professional photo and a clear headline that states your role, such as "Virtual Assistant for Ecommerce Support" rather than just "Virtual Assistant". Write a summary that says what you do, in plain words. Add your skills, your tools, your languages, and your work history.</p>
<p>Add a portfolio where it fits your role. Designers should show design work, writers should show writing samples, and developers should show projects or code. Virtual assistants and support workers can describe the systems they used and the tools they know. Third party reports describe vetting as a portfolio review, interviews, and skills tests, but that description comes from a company profile seen only in a search result, not from an official page, so the exact vetting steps could not be confirmed.</p>
<p>Set your availability and expected pay honestly. Employers hire for long term roles, so they need to know your hours, your time zone, and whether you can work their business hours. If your hours do not match, say so early. Keep your profile up to date, and respond to employer messages fast, because employers often contact several candidates at once.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-find-roles-apply-and-handle-interviews">Step 2: Find roles, apply, and handle interviews</h2>
<p>Outsourcely lists remote jobs that you can browse and apply for, and employers can also search worker profiles and contact you directly. Use both routes. Browse new listings often, and apply for roles that match your skills and your availability. Read each job post with care, and write each application for that specific role. Mention one detail from the post, explain why you fit it, and state your expected pay and hours clearly.</p>
<p>If an employer contacts you, expect a hiring process like a normal job application. Third party reports describe video interviews, skills tests, and direct messaging between employers and candidates, with application tracking and interview scheduling tools on the platform. The exact steps could not be confirmed officially. Prepare as you would for any remote job interview. Test your internet, camera, and microphone before the call. Prepare examples of your past work, and questions about the role, the hours, the pay, and how you will be paid. Take notes on what was agreed about the role, hours, pay, and start date.</p>

<h2 id="how-pay-works-and-what-is-not-verified">How pay works, and what is not verified</h2>
<p>Pay on Outsourcely is agreed directly between you and the employer. Third party reports state that workers pay no platform fees and negotiate their pay with the employer, and that the platform generally does not handle payment processing. The employer pays you through a method you agree together, such as bank transfer, PayPal, or Wise. This guide could not confirm any of that from an official source, so confirm the pay method, the pay date, and the currency with the employer before you start work.</p>
<p>Because the platform does not handle payment, you have no payment protection from Outsourcely. That is the biggest difference from a marketplace such as Upwork, where the platform holds funds. On Outsourcely, you must judge the employer yourself. Ask how and when you will be paid before you start, get the agreement in writing, and be wary of any employer who will not put pay terms in writing. Start with a short trial period where you can, so you can check that payment arrives as agreed.</p>
<p>Salary levels on Outsourcely could not be verified either. No reliable source found in the research states what workers typically earn, and any salary figure you see in a third party article should be treated as a reported claim, not a fact. Your pay will depend on your role, experience, country, and the employer's budget. Research the market rate for your role before you negotiate. Our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-negotiate-salary-for-a-remote-job" rel="noopener" target="_blank">negotiating salary for a remote job</a> explains how to prepare for that conversation.</p>
<h2 id="fees-what-workers-pay-and-what-employers-pay">Fees: what workers pay and what employers pay</h2>
<p>According to third party reports, workers pay no platform fees on Outsourcely. You do not pay to create a profile, apply for jobs, or get hired, and no commission is taken from your pay. Employers pay a subscription, reported to start from 19 US dollars per month, with unlimited job postings and no per hire fees. Neither figure could be confirmed from an official Outsourcely page, so check the pricing page on the Outsourcely site before you rely on them.</p>
<p>That fee model is good for workers in one clear way. Because the employer pays a flat subscription rather than a fee on your salary, there is no platform cut reducing your pay. It also means Outsourcely has no reason to protect your payment. Keep that in mind when you agree pay terms, and get them in writing.</p>

<h2 id="the-affiliate-programme-50-per-sale-as-reported">The affiliate programme: 50% per sale as reported</h2>
<p>The Outsourcely affiliate programme is the second earning route, and it works like most affiliate programmes. You apply to the programme, receive a unique referral link, and add that link to content about remote hiring, such as a blog post or a resource page. When an employer signs up through your link, you earn a commission. A third party affiliate listing reports the commission at 50% per sale, and states that applications are approved or denied within a few days. The cookie duration is unknown, and the 50% figure is reported by Lasso, not confirmed on an Outsourcely page.</p>
<p>The affiliate route only works if you have an audience of employers. A blog about remote work, a YouTube channel about hiring, or a newsletter for founders could all work. A link shared with no audience will earn nothing. The same listing is honest that succeeding without a website is very hard. If you do promote Outsourcely as an affiliate, mark the link clearly, because readers should know you may earn from it. This article itself contains no affiliate links.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Learning how to earn money on Outsourcely costs nothing according to third party reports. Your real costs are the time to build a profile, apply for roles, and attend interviews, plus a reliable internet connection, a computer, and a headset for video calls. If you take the affiliate route, the cost is running a website or channel that reaches employers.</p>
<p>Outsourcely is a slow route to income, because long term hiring takes time. You may apply for many roles before one employer replies, and the interview process can take weeks. A first role may also be part time or a trial before it becomes full time. Keep applying while you wait, and keep other income channels open until a role is confirmed and your first payment has arrived.</p>
<p><strong>Worked example, example only: these numbers are not typical results.</strong> Imagine you are hired as a virtual assistant for 20 hours a week at 8 US dollars per hour, a rate you agreed with the employer. Your weekly pay is 160 US dollars, and your monthly pay is about 640 US dollars for a four week month. Because no platform fee is reported for workers, you would keep the full 640 US dollars before taxes and payment service fees. Now imagine the employer pays monthly in arrears, which means you are paid at the end of the month for work already done. You would need to cover your own costs for the first month before any pay arrives. Always ask whether pay is weekly or monthly, and in arrears or in advance, before you start.</p>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>The main risk in learning how to earn money on Outsourcely is the lack of payment protection. Because the platform reportedly does not handle payment or contracts, you must protect yourself. Get pay terms in writing, and test payment with a short trial period. If an employer will not put pay terms in writing, do not start work for them.</p>
<p>Watch for scams that target remote job seekers. Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay a fee to secure a job, asks for your bank details or identity documents before an interview, offers a very high salary for simple work with no interview, or pressures you to start at once without a written agreement. Real employers describe the role, interview you, discuss pay, and agree terms in writing. Outsourcely itself is a real platform, and third party reports give it an aggregated review score of 3.7 out of 5, which suggests mixed experiences, but that score is aggregated by a third party and could not be confirmed officially. If something feels wrong, stop and check the employer before you share personal details or start work. In the United States, you can report fraud to the Federal Trade Commission at <a href="https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ReportFraud.ftc.gov</a>.</p>
