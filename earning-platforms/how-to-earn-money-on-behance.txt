30:T4b23,<h1>How to Earn Money on Behance in 2026</h1>
<p>To earn money on Behance, you need a strong portfolio first, then a clear way for clients to hire you or buy from you. Behance does not pay you for views, likes or appreciations. It gives you a public place to show your best work. It also gives clients a way to contact you, buy a digital asset, or hire you for a freelance service. Treat it as a shop window, not as a shop.</p>
<p>In this guide, you will learn what Behance is, how the money routes work, how to build a portfolio that wins clients, how to list a service, how to sell digital assets, how to use the job board, what Behance charges, and which scams to avoid.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-behance-and-what-is-it-not">What is Behance, and what is it not</h2>
<p>Behance is a portfolio platform owned by Adobe. Designers, illustrators, photographers and motion artists publish projects there, with images, process shots and a short case study. Other members can follow you, save your work and send you a message. Clients can search by skill, field and location.</p>
<p>Behance is not a job marketplace in the usual sense. It does not assign you work, and it does not promise you clients. It has a job board and service listings, but most value comes from discovery. A client finds your work, likes the style, checks your profile, and then contacts you. Quality matters more than quantity.</p>
<p>A Dribbble vs Behance comparison is only a keyword line in the existing Dribbble guide, not a full Behance guide. In simple terms, Dribbble is known for short shots and quick ideas, while Behance is known for full projects and case studies. Many designers use both. Read <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-earn-money-on-dribbble" rel="noopener" target="_blank">our guide to earning money on Dribbble</a> for the Dribbble side. For a contest style platform, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-earn-money-on-designcrowd" rel="noopener" target="_blank">our guide to earning money on DesignCrowd</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-earning-on-behance-works">How earning on Behance works</h2>
<p>There are four main money routes. You can win freelance client work, sell digital assets, apply for jobs, and send traffic to your own website or shop. A fifth route, subscriptions from followers, is mentioned by Behance for exclusive content. Availability and terms can vary by account and country, so check your own account before you plan income around it.</p>
<p>None of these routes pays you for posting alone. You get paid when a client hires you, when a buyer pays for an asset, or when an employer hires you. Payments for services and asset sales go through Stripe or PayPal. Behance takes a platform fee on some sales, and Stripe or PayPal take a processing fee too. The split depends on whether you have a free account or a Pro account.</p>
<p>Think of the flow in three steps. First, a stranger finds your work through search, a gallery, a tag, or a share. Second, that stranger reads your case study and decides if your style fits. Third, that stranger contacts you, buys an asset, or hires you. Make each step easy, with clear titles and a hire or buy link a busy client can find fast.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-build-a-portfolio-that-can-win-work">Step 1: Build a portfolio that can win work</h2>
<p>Start with six to ten strong projects, not thirty weak ones. A client will judge you on your weakest visible project. Remove work that does not match the work you want. If you want logo work, show logo work. If you want editorial work, show editorial work.</p>
<p>Each project needs a clear structure. Show the finished work first, then the problem, then your process, then the result. Use short captions. A client should understand the project in under a minute. Add the tools you used, the year, and the client type if you can share it. If it was a personal project, say so.</p>
<p>Use tags with care. Add the field, the skill and the style, for example brand identity, logo design and minimal. Do not add tags that do not match the work. Use a cover image that reads well at a small size. Publish one strong project every two or three weeks.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/behance-portfolio-earnings.jpg" alt="A designer presenting portfolio work to a client at a desk with printed design boards and a laptop" /></p>
<h2 id="step-2-list-a-freelance-service-with-a-clear-price-and-scope">Step 2: List a freelance service with a clear price and scope</h2>
<p>Behance lets you list freelance services in your profile. This turns a viewer into a buyer. A listing should name the service, the deliverables, the timeline, the number of revisions, and the starting price. Vague listings get vague enquiries.</p>
<p>Start with two or three services you can deliver well. A logo designer might list a logo package, a brand style guide and a social media kit. A web designer might list a landing page and a small business website. If you are still building web skills, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-web-development-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn web development online</a> before you offer web work to paying clients.</p>
<p>Set your price from your costs and your time. Work out how many hours the work takes, what your hourly target is, and what expenses you have, such as software, fonts and tax. Add a margin for revisions. State what is included, what is not included, and what an extra revision costs.</p>
<p>When a client contacts you, reply fast and ask for the brief in writing. Ask what they need, when they need it, and what their budget range is. Send a short proposal with the price, the timeline and the deliverables. Ask for a deposit before you start. Many freelancers ask for 50 percent up front. Put it in writing. For a wider view, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-graphic-designer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a freelance graphic designer</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-sell-digital-assets-that-solve-a-real-problem">Step 3: Sell digital assets that solve a real problem</h2>
<p>Digital assets are files you make once and sell many times. On Behance, you can attach files to a project, set a price and a licence type, and sell them to buyers who find your work. Common assets include logo templates, social media templates, presentation templates, fonts, textures, icons, Lightroom presets and illustration packs. The best assets solve a clear problem for a clear buyer.</p>
<p>Behance allows up to five files per project for assets, according to the Behance getting started guide. Sell one focused pack and show it in use, on a phone, a poster and a slide. Price it for the buyer. A low price can bring more sales, while a high price needs a strong reason, such as a full commercial licence. State whether the buyer can use it for personal work, client work, or resale. If your asset uses a file you did not create, check its licence first.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-use-your-profile-to-win-work-off-behance-too">Step 4: Use your profile to win work off Behance too</h2>
<p>Many clients will find you on Behance and hire you off Behance. That is normal, and it is often where larger projects come from. Add a short bio that states what you do, who you help, and where you are based. Add links to your website and your email. Add your availability, and update it when you are full.</p>
<p>Link to your own website when you have one. It gives you more control over pricing, terms and contact forms. Adobe Portfolio is included with Behance Pro, and it builds a website from your Behance projects. Keep your profiles consistent, with the same name, photo and service list.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-apply-for-jobs-on-the-behance-job-board">Step 5: Apply for jobs on the Behance job board</h2>
<p>Behance has a job board where companies post full time, freelance and remote roles for creative work. You can apply with your Behance profile as your portfolio. Search by role, field and location, and read each listing in full before you apply.</p>
<p>Tailor each application. Link to two or three projects that match the role, not to your whole profile. Write a short note that names the role and links to the matching work. For UX roles, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-find-remote-ux-design-jobs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to find remote UX design jobs</a>. Treat the job board as one channel, not your only channel. Keep your freelance listings live while you apply.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-teach-and-offer-subscriptions-with-care">Step 6: Teach and offer subscriptions with care</h2>
<p>If you have a skill other creatives want, you can offer teaching or mentoring, such as a short course, a workshop, or a portfolio review. It works best when your own projects prove the skill. Behance also mentions subscriptions for exclusive content. This suits a creator with a loyal audience, not a beginner. Check the current terms and country availability in your own account first. Do not plan income around sponsored content alone, because it needs a large audience. Most creators earn more from client work and asset sales.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Behance is free to join and free to publish a portfolio. You can list services, sell assets and apply for jobs without Pro. Costs come from fees on sales, payment processing, software and time. Behance Pro is a paid subscription. It adds advanced analytics, project boosting, scheduled publishing, password protected projects, profile customisation and an Adobe Portfolio website. The Pro price is not stated here, because it was not verified on an official Behance page in full. Check the current price on Behance before you pay.</p>
<p>On a standard account, Behance takes a 30 percent platform fee when you sell a digital asset. Stripe takes 2.9 percent plus 0.30 dollars per sale. PayPal takes a fee that varies by country. If you are a Behance Pro subscriber, the 30 percent platform fee is waived, according to the Behance help centre FAQ on fees, but Stripe or PayPal processing fees still apply. Check the <a href="https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/13356478351387-FAQ-What-are-the-fees" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Behance help centre FAQ on fees</a> before you set a price. The <a href="https://www.behance.net/blog/behance-pro" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Behance Pro announcement</a> also states 0 percent platform fees for Pro members.</p>

<h3 id="worked-example-example-only">Worked example: Example only</h3>
<p>This example shows how fees change your net income. It is not a promise of sales. Suppose you sell a template pack for 20 dollars, and you sell 10 copies in a month. Gross sales are 10 times 20 dollars, which is 200 dollars. On a free account, the Behance fee is 30 percent of 200 dollars, which is 60 dollars. Stripe processing is 2.9 percent of 200 dollars, which is 5.80 dollars, plus 0.30 dollars per sale for 10 sales, which is 3.00 dollars. Total Stripe fees are 8.80 dollars. Net income is 200 dollars minus 60 dollars minus 8.80 dollars, which is 131.20 dollars, before tax. On a Pro account, the Behance fee is 0 dollars. Net income is 200 dollars minus 8.80 dollars, which is 191.20 dollars, before tax and before the Pro subscription cost. The Pro saving is 60 dollars, but subtract the Pro cost for that month to see if Pro pays for itself.</p>
<p>In your first 90 days, publish six strong projects, list two services, publish one asset pack, and apply for five matched jobs. Then improve your best project and remove your weakest one. Results vary with experience, location, demand, time, competition, expenses and platform fees. Plan for months, not days.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>You publish many weak projects instead of a few strong ones, so a client judges you on the weakest work.</li>
<li>You use vague service listings with no price, no scope and no timeline, so clients do not know what they are buying.</li>
<li>You price from a guess instead of from your hours, your costs and the platform fee, so you earn less after fees.</li>
<li>You start work without a deposit or a written agreement, so you risk unpaid revisions and scope creep.</li>
<li>You buy appreciations or likes to look popular, which breaks Behance rules and can put your account at risk.</li>
<li>You leave your availability out of date, so a client who wants to hire you moves on to someone else.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Behance does not pay you for appreciations. No honest buyer pays you to click Appreciate on other people's work. Behance community guidelines ban artificial appreciations and third party services that inflate them. Accounts that break the rules can be disabled. If a site offers a few cents to like a project, the money is small and the risk to your account is real. Do not take that work.</p>
<p>Watch for fake client messages. A real client will give a clear brief, a timeline and a budget range, and will accept a written agreement and a deposit. A scammer may ask you to work for free, pay a fee to start, share your password, or pay through an odd link. Do not share your password, do not pay to get work, and do not start large work without a deposit.</p>
<p>Watch for copyright traps. Do not sell an asset that uses a font, photo or graphic you do not have the right to sell. Do not copy another designer's project. Keep your source files and licences. Stripe and PayPal are not available in every country, so check payment terms first. Keep records of every sale, fee and payout for tax.</p>
<h2 id="who-behance-suits-and-who-it-does-not-suit">Who Behance suits, and who it does not suit</h2>
<p>Behance suits designers, illustrators, photographers and motion artists who have strong work to show and want clients to find them through search. It suits freelancers who can write a clear service list, price their work, and reply fast.</p>
<p>Behance does not suit a writer or a coder with no visual work to show. It does not suit someone who wants quick cash for clicks or likes, because Behance does not pay for engagement. It does not suit a beginner with no finished work yet, because an empty profile will not win clients. Build three or four strong personal projects first, then publish them.</p>

<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="can-you-earn-money-on-behance">Can you earn money on Behance?</h3>
<p>Yes, through freelance client work, digital asset sales, job applications and off platform hires. Behance itself does not pay you for views, likes or appreciations. You get paid when a client hires you, a buyer pays for an asset, or an employer hires you.</p>
<h3 id="does-behance-pay-you-directly">Does Behance pay you directly?</h3>
<p>No, Behance does not pay you a salary or a fee for posting. Payments for services and asset sales go through Stripe or PayPal, which you connect to your account. Behance takes a platform fee on standard accounts, and Stripe or PayPal take a processing fee, so check the current fee page before you set a price.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-you-get-clients-on-behance">How do you get clients on Behance?</h3>
<p>Publish strong projects with clear case studies, list services with a price and scope, use accurate tags, and keep your bio and availability up to date. Clients find you through search, galleries and shares, then they contact you. A steady rhythm of strong work helps more than a burst of weak work.</p>
<h3 id="is-behance-pro-worth-it">Is Behance Pro worth it?</h3>
<p>Behance Pro can be worth it if you sell enough for the 0 percent platform fee saving to cover the subscription cost. Pro also adds analytics, an Adobe Portfolio website, project boosting and scheduling. It is not worth it if you sell little or nothing, because the subscription cost will exceed the saving. Check the current Pro price and work out the maths for your sales level.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-behance-charge">How much does Behance charge?</h3>
<p>On a standard account, Behance charges a 30 percent platform fee on digital asset sales, according to the Behance help centre FAQ on fees. Stripe charges 2.9 percent plus 0.30 dollars per sale, and PayPal charges a fee that varies by country. Behance Pro waives the 30 percent platform fee, but processing fees still apply. Fees can change, so check the live help page.</p>
<h3 id="can-a-beginner-make-money-on-behance">Can a beginner make money on Behance?</h3>
<p>A beginner can make money on Behance, but it takes time and strong work. Start with three or four strong personal projects, then add two freelance services, then add one digital asset. Expect weeks or months before regular enquiries arrive. Results vary with experience, location, demand, time, competition, expenses and platform fees.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Behance can help you earn money in 2026 if you treat it as a portfolio first and a sales channel second. Build six to ten strong projects, list two or three clear services, and sell one useful digital asset. Check the fees before you price, ask for a deposit before you start, and avoid paid like schemes.</p>
