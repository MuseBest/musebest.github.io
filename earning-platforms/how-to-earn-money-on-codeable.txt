37:T4d3b,<h1>How to Earn Money on Codeable in 2026</h1>
<p>To earn money on Codeable, you need strong WordPress skills, a clear portfolio, and the patience to pass a strict vetting process. Codeable is a vetted network for WordPress developers, not an open marketplace where anyone can bid. Clients use it because they want screened experts for WordPress and WooCommerce work. If you pass, you can see a steady flow of projects at rates of $80 to $120 per hour.</p>
<p>In this guide: what Codeable is, who it suits, the kinds of work, steps 1 to 7 from application to paid work, how much you can earn, what it costs, payout rules, downsides, mistakes, risks, and FAQs.</p>
<h2 id="what-codeable-is-and-how-it-works-for-developers">What Codeable is and how it works for developers</h2>
<p>Codeable is a marketplace that focuses only on WordPress. Clients do not post a job and wait for dozens of bids. Codeable matches a client with a small group of screened experts, who talk with the client in a shared chat room. The experts then give estimates, and Codeable shows the client one price, based on the average of the estimates, plus a service fee. See the <a href="https://www.codeable.io/freelance-wordpress-developer-jobs/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Codeable freelance jobs page</a> for the official description.</p>
<p>You work as an expert, not as a bidder. You review jobs that fit your skills and your availability. You join a conversation with the client and up to three or four other experts. You ask questions until the scope is clear. You then give an estimate and apply for the job. The client reviews the profiles of the experts who gave an estimate, and chooses one. You then move to a private chat room with the client and deliver the project.</p>
<p>Codeable also offers coaching, training videos, and a private community for experts.</p>

<h2 id="who-codeable-suits-and-who-it-does-not-suit">Who Codeable suits and who it does not suit</h2>
<p>Codeable suits experienced WordPress developers who can show real work and explain their choices. It suits you if you know PHP, WordPress themes and plugins, and WooCommerce, and if you can fix, build, and improve live sites without close supervision. It also suits you if you like clear scopes, written estimates, and direct talks with clients. For a wider view, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-web-developer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a freelance web developer</a>.</p>
<p>Codeable does not suit beginners, developers who work mainly in other stacks, or people who want to bid on many jobs at low prices. If you are still learning, start with <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-web-development-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn web development online</a>. It may not suit you if you need income this week, because the application can take up to four weeks.</p>
<h2 id="what-kinds-of-work-you-can-do-on-codeable">What kinds of work you can do on Codeable</h2>
<p>Codeable lists three main kinds of work. Short term jobs are one off tasks, such as fixing a site, updating a plugin, moving a site, or cleaning a hacked site. These jobs often last up to one week.</p>
<p>Complex projects are larger builds. You may create a custom plugin, build a custom theme, design a full site, or connect WordPress to another system through an API. These projects often last one to six months. They suit front end, back end, and full stack developers, as well as agencies and web designers.</p>
<p>Ongoing retainers are long term partnerships. A client pays in advance for a block of hours each month, and you handle maintenance, new features, and improvements. These often last three to six months on average.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/codeable-wordpress-developer.jpg" alt="A developer fixing a WordPress website, with code on the laptop screen" /></p>
<p>Common tasks include plugin, theme, WooCommerce, speed, security, migration, and maintenance work. State a clear strength in your profile.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-check-that-you-are-ready-to-apply">Step 1: Check that you are ready to apply</h2>
<p>Start with an honest skills check. Can you build a WordPress theme from scratch, or do you only edit ready made themes? Can you write a simple plugin, use hooks and filters, and work with WP_Query? Can you debug a white screen, a plugin conflict, or a slow database query? If you answer yes to most of these, you are closer to ready.</p>
<p>Check your client skills too. Codeable work involves written chats, clear estimates, and honest updates. You need to ask good questions and explain a technical problem in plain words. Check your time as well. You need time for the application, the quiz, the test project, and the interview.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-prepare-your-portfolio-and-your-profile-story">Step 2: Prepare your portfolio and your profile story</h2>
<p>Your portfolio is your proof. Choose three to five projects that show range and depth. Include a custom theme or plugin if you have one. Include a WooCommerce store if you work with shops. For each project, write a short note: what was the problem, what did you build or fix, and what changed for the client.</p>
<p>Make sure your live sites work. Check every link and form before you apply. A broken demo will hurt you more than a missing project. If you want to build a WordPress site to practise on, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-start-a-blog-on-wordpress" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start a blog on WordPress</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-apply-and-complete-the-introduction-stage">Step 3: Apply and complete the introduction stage</h2>
<p>Codeable says the application starts with your application, then a short video about Codeable and some questions about what you learned. This stage checks that you understand how the platform works, not just that you can code. Watch the video with care, take notes, and answer in your own words.</p>
<p>Your application should be complete and honest. Give your real skills, your real experience, and links that work. If you have a gap in your skills, say so. Codeable wants experts who know their limits.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/codeable-application-process.jpg" alt="A developer preparing a job application and portfolio on a laptop" /></p>
<p>Codeable then reviews your application. If you do not pass, work on the weak area, build more proof, and apply again later if Codeable allows it.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-pass-the-coding-quiz-and-the-test-project">Step 4: Pass the coding quiz and the test project</h2>
<p>The next stage is a brief coding quiz. Codeable says this quiz tests your WordPress knowledge. Revise hooks and filters, WP_Query, custom post types, taxonomies, and WordPress coding standards before you apply. Practise with real code, not just by reading notes.</p>
<p>After the quiz comes a test project. Codeable says you can choose a front end or a back end test project. Treat this project as a real client job. Read the brief twice, plan before you code, write clean code, and test your work in a browser and on a phone. Your test project should be complete, not just nearly done. Include a short note on how you tested it.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-complete-the-interview-and-onboarding">Step 5: Complete the interview and onboarding</h2>
<p>After the test project, Codeable holds an interview with a staff member. Be ready to explain your test project in plain words. Say what you built, why you chose that approach, and what problems you faced. Be ready to talk about a hard client situation too, and how you handled it.</p>
<p>Ask questions in the interview as well. Ask how jobs are matched, how estimates work, and what support is available when a project goes wrong. If you pass, you will complete onboarding and fill out your expert profile. Codeable says the whole process can take up to four weeks.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-find-projects-give-estimates-and-win-work">Step 6: Find projects, give estimates, and win work</h2>
<p>After onboarding, you start to review jobs that match your criteria. Do not try to take every job. Choose jobs where you can add clear value, and where the scope fits your skills. A good fit will lead to better work, better reviews, and repeat clients.</p>
<p>When you engage with a job, you join a shared chat room with the client and up to three or four other experts. Ask about the goal, not just the feature. Once the scope is clear, you give an estimate and apply. Codeable shows the client one price, based on the average of the estimates, plus the service fee.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-deliver-the-work-get-paid-and-build-your-name">Step 7: Deliver the work, get paid, and build your name</h2>
<p>Delivery starts with a clear plan. Agree the scope in writing, list what is included, and list what is not included. Set milestones for larger projects, and share updates on a regular basis. If a problem comes up, tell the client early, explain the options, and agree on the next step.</p>
<p>Test your work before you hand it over. Check it on desktop and on mobile, test forms and checkout flows, and check for errors. Clean up test data, remove debug code, and write a short handover note. Codeable says its escrow service means you are paid on time without having to deal with client billing yourself. Keep your own records of hours, milestones, and messages.</p>
<h2 id="how-much-you-can-earn-on-codeable">How much you can earn on Codeable</h2>
<p>Codeable publishes a rate band of $80 to $120 per hour for experts. That is a rate band, not a promise of income. Your actual income depends on how many hours you work, how many projects you win, how much time you spend on talks and estimates, and how much time you spend on admin and learning. You also need to allow for gaps between projects, for tax, and for your own costs.</p>
<h3 id="example-only-a-small-project-at-100-per-hour">Example only: a small project at $100 per hour</h3>
<p>Example only. This is a worked example to show the maths, not a promise of what you will earn.</p>
<ul>
<li>Hours worked: 10 hours</li>
<li>Hourly rate: $100 per hour</li>
<li>Project estimate: 10 x $100 = $1,000</li>
<li>Client service fee: 17.5% of $1,000 = $175</li>
<li>Total paid by the client: $1,000 + $175 = $1,175</li>
<li>Your share: shown in your Codeable estimate, before any payment processing or currency costs. Codeable does not publish an expert side fee percentage on the public pages checked, so do not assume a fixed share.</li>
</ul>
<p>This example shows why you must read the estimate. The client total is clear, but your net pay is a separate figure.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Codeable does not charge you to apply, based on the public pages checked. The main cost is your time, plus the tools you need to do the work. You may need a good laptop, reliable internet, a local WordPress setup, and access to test sites. Check who pays for paid plugins or tools before you start.</p>
<p>The main fee you will see as a developer is in the project estimate. Codeable publishes a 17.5% fixed service fee, added on top of the estimate, and paid by the client. That fee covers expert vetting and training, 24 hour customer service, and platform management. See the <a href="https://codeable.io/pricing/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Codeable pricing page</a> for the official figures. Do not confuse the client fee with your own share.</p>

<h2 id="payout-rules-on-codeable">Payout rules on Codeable</h2>
<p>Codeable says it uses a secure escrow service, and that this service ensures you are paid on time without having to deal with client billing yourself. Escrow means the client funds the project before work starts, and the money is released when the work is done. That reduces the risk that a client will not pay, but you should still follow the platform rules for milestones and delivery.</p>
<p>The public pages checked on 2026-11-23 do not publish payout methods, payout timing, or any minimum payout. Check your expert dashboard, your contract, and the Codeable terms before you accept a project. Keep your own records for every project.</p>
<h2 id="downsides-of-codeable">Downsides of Codeable</h2>
<p>Codeable has real downsides, and you should weigh them before you apply. The vetting process is strict, and many developers will not pass. That can feel harsh, but it is also what keeps rates high. If you do not pass, you lose the time you spent on the application and the test project.</p>
<p>The work is WordPress only. If you want to work in other stacks, you will need other platforms or direct clients as well. You do not control the client price directly, because Codeable shows one price based on the average of estimates. You will also lose some jobs to other experts. That is part of the model, and it can be frustrating at first.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>You apply before you have enough WordPress proof, so your portfolio looks thin.</li>
<li>You give a low estimate to win a job, then you work long hours for little pay.</li>
<li>You skip the scoping chat, so the client expects more than you planned to deliver.</li>
<li>You take on work outside your skills, so you learn on a live client project.</li>
<li>You write a vague profile, so clients cannot tell what you do best.</li>
<li>You go quiet when a problem appears, so the client loses trust.</li>
<li>You forget to test on mobile, so a bug appears after handover.</li>
<li>You do not read the estimate and payout terms, so you are surprised by the net pay.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Codeable itself is a real platform with a public site, a pricing page, and a jobs page. That does not mean every message that uses its name is real. Scammers often copy the name of a known platform to trick developers. Be careful with any message that asks you to pay to join, to pay for a test, or to move a conversation to a private chat before you have applied through the official site. Codeable does not show a joining fee on the public pages checked, so a fee to apply is a warning sign.</p>
<p>Do not share passwords, one time codes, or bank details with anyone who claims to be from Codeable. Use the official site and support channel, and check the web address before you log in. If a message asks you to work outside the platform, to accept gift cards, or to pay for software first, stop and check with Codeable support. A fake test can also be a way to get free work.</p>
