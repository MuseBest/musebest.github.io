<h3 id="what-contra-is-and-who-it-suits">What Contra is and who it suits</h3>
<p><a href="https://contra.com" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Contra</a> is a commission free platform for independent professionals, launched in 2019. Guides report it has grown to more than one million independent professionals worldwide, working in design, development, writing, marketing, video and more. It works as three things at once: a visual portfolio website, a place where vetted clients discover freelancers, and a workspace for contracts, invoices and payments.</p>
<p>Contra suits you if you have a strong portfolio and you want to keep all of what you charge. It suits designers and developers best, because clients browse portfolios by look and feel, but writers, marketers and other professionals use it too.</p>
<p>Unlike open marketplaces, Contra does not run on bidding wars. Clients browse portfolios in a Discover feed, or post briefs that freelancers can apply to. That is good news if you hate writing proposals all day, and bad news if your portfolio is thin, because a thin portfolio simply does not get picked. If you are starting from zero, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-make-money-on-fiverr" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to make money on Fiverr</a> first to build paid samples, then bring your best work to Contra.</p>

<p>Two things need care. First, the flat per payment fee on the free plan is reported by some guides and not mentioned by others, so treat it as reported, not confirmed. Second, guides disagree on the annual Pro price, with one listing 199 US dollars a year and another listing 144 US dollars a year, so we quote only the monthly price of 29 US dollars here.</p>
<p>You can earn in three ways on Contra: clients can discover your portfolio, you can apply to briefs on the Opportunities board, which needs Pro for full access, or you can bring your own clients and use Contra for contracts and invoicing, which costs you nothing in commission.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-your-account">Step 1: Create your account</h3>
<p>Sign up on <a href="https://contra.com/sign-up" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Contra's sign up page</a> and choose "I am an independent". You can register with a Google account or an email address. The sign up is free, and the basic plan costs nothing.</p>
<p>Guides report that anyone aged 16 or above can join, and that identity verification is required before you can receive payments. Have a photo ID ready, and use your real name and a professional email address.</p>
<p>Once your email is confirmed, Contra walks you through onboarding. A complete profile takes about an hour to set up properly, and guides report that complete profiles get far more visibility in discovery.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-build-a-portfolio-that-sells">Step 2: Build a portfolio that sells</h3>
<p>On Contra, your profile is not a resume. It is a visual portfolio website, and it is the main reason a client contacts you. Clients judge the quality of your actual work, not your job title, so this step decides almost everything.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/contra-portfolio-profile.jpg" alt="A designer building an online portfolio on a laptop, the screen showing a grid of project thumbnails with colourful design work, a tablet and printed design samples on the desk" /></p>
<p>Start with three to five featured projects. Each one should show the problem, what you delivered, and the outcome, in that order. A logo on its own tells a client little. A logo with the brief, two early concepts, the final design in use, and a short note on what changed for the client tells them a great deal. If you have no paid client work yet, build concept projects: redesign a real product's landing page, rebuild a local business website, or create a brand identity for a made up startup. Label concept work honestly as concept work, and make it good enough that the label does not matter.</p>
<p>Write a headline that names your niche and your outcome, not your job title. "Brand designer for early stage startups" is stronger than "freelance designer". "I build fast Webflow landing pages for software startups" is stronger than "web designer and developer". One clear sentence helps the right clients find you and helps the wrong ones move on.</p>
<p>Add your services with starting prices. You can charge per project, at a fixed weekly rate, or on a flexible basis depending on the project. Clear prices filter out clients who cannot afford you and save you both time.</p>
<p>Finally, add skills and tools. Contra has a verified expert network for tools such as Figma, Framer and Spline, and getting verified on a tool you use daily can boost your visibility.</p>
<h3 id="step-3-list-services-with-clear-prices">Step 3: List services with clear prices</h3>
<p>Contra lets you list fixed price service packages, similar to products in an online store. This is one of its most useful features, because a client can buy a defined service without a long negotiation.</p>
<p>Keep your entry package between 250 and 750 US dollars if you are new to the platform, as guides suggest. Make the deliverables exact. "Three page mobile responsive landing page built in Framer in 48 hours" is a service a client can buy. "Web design services" is not. List what is included, how many revisions are included, and what costs extra.</p>
<p>Share your Contra profile link in your email signature, your LinkedIn bio, your X bio, and your own website, because the Discover feed will not send you work while your profile is new.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-find-your-first-client">Step 4: Find your first client</h3>
<p>Work comes to you on Contra in two ways, and you should use both. The first is passive discovery. Clients browse the Discover feed and search by skill tags. A complete profile with strong projects, clear services and the right tags is what gets you found. Keep your availability status current and update your projects regularly, because guides report the platform favours active profiles.</p>
<p>The second is active applying, which needs Pro for full access to the Opportunities board. If you are on the free plan, you can still receive inbound interest and work with clients you bring yourself.</p>
<p>When you apply to a brief, keep your pitch short and specific. Do not open with your life story. Open with the client's problem and one useful observation. Guides suggest a message shorter than a short social media post: name the problem you spotted, show a quick example of how you would fix it, and link to one relevant project. A client who sees you have already understood their problem will hire you over a freelancer with five star reviews and a generic message.</p>

<h3 id="step-5-run-the-project-and-get-paid-safely">Step 5: Run the project and get paid safely</h3>
<p>Once a client hires you, everything moves inside a project workspace. You can chat, share deliverables for approval, and manage milestones without switching tools. Keep all communication and payments inside Contra. Off platform deals remove the protections Contra provides, and if a dispute arises, you will want the platform on your side.</p>
<p>Use milestone based contracts. Never start a project with 100 percent payment on delivery, and never demand 100 percent upfront when you have no reviews. A common split is 30 percent upfront, 40 percent at the mid point, and 30 percent on delivery. The client funds each milestone, Contra holds the money in escrow, and you are paid when the milestone is approved.</p>
<p>Getting paid works like this: the client pays the full project fee upfront into escrow, you deliver the work, and Contra releases the funds to you once the client approves. Guides report payouts by direct bank deposit, PayPal and USDC, a US dollar stablecoin, with money reaching your account in 2 to 3 business days. Payment processing of about 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars applies to card payments, and an optional instant payout costs about 1.5 percent extra according to one guide.</p>
<p>When the project ends, ask for a review within a day, while the client is still happy.</p>
<h3 id="worked-example-what-a-contra-project-really-pays">Worked example: what a Contra project really pays</h3>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. They show how the maths works, so you can test your own figures.</p>
<p>Imagine a designer who sells a brand identity package for 1,000 US dollars through Contra on the free plan. Contra takes no percentage commission, so the invoice is 1,000 US dollars. The client pays by card, so Stripe processing of 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars takes 29.30 US dollars. The free plan per payment fee, as reported by guides, is 29 US dollars on a payment of 500 US dollars or more. That leaves 941.70 US dollars.</p>
<p>Now compare the same project on Upwork, which charges a 10 percent fee. There the designer would keep 900 US dollars before processing, which is 41.70 US dollars less than on Contra's free plan. On Pro, where the per payment fee is reported as removed, the designer would keep 970.70 US dollars, which is 70.70 US dollars more than on Upwork. The Pro plan costs 29 US dollars a month, so it pays for itself with one project a month at this size.</p>
<p>Recheck: 2.9 percent of 1,000 is 29, plus 0.30 is 29.30. 1,000 minus 29.30 minus 29 is 941.70. 1,000 minus 29.30 is 970.70. 970.70 minus 900 is 70.70. The maths checks out.</p>

<h3 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h3>
<p>Joining Contra is free, and the core tools, portfolio, contracts, invoicing and commission free payments, are free too. The optional Pro plan costs 29 US dollars a month, and the annual price varies by source, so check the current pricing page. Your main cost is time: about an hour to build a strong profile, plus the hours to create concept projects if you have no client work yet.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Contra will not send you work on day one. Your first client usually comes from your own outreach, or from a client you invite yourself, not from the Discover feed. Guides suggest it takes weeks of visibility and a few completed projects before inbound interest becomes regular.</p>
<p>Results vary with your portfolio quality, your niche, how active you are, and whether you bring your own clients. Contra suits independents who already have work to show and clients to invite. It is not a job board that hands out work to new profiles.</p>
<h3 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Publishing a profile with one weak project and expecting clients to find you.</li>
<li>Writing a generic headline such as "freelance designer" instead of naming your niche.</li>
<li>Listing services with no price, no scope and no revision limit.</li>
<li>Waiting for Contra to send clients instead of sharing your profile and applying to briefs.</li>
<li>Taking communication and payment off the platform, which removes your protection.</li>
<li>Starting work before the client has funded the first milestone in escrow.</li>
<li>Paying for Pro before your portfolio is strong enough to convert the leads it brings.</li>
<li>Forgetting that card processing fees still apply, even though commission is zero percent.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h3>
<p>Contra is a legitimate platform, but scammers use its name and the names of real clients. Never pay anyone to get work on Contra, and never send money to a "client" for equipment, software, or a security deposit. Real clients pay you, not the other way round.</p>
<p>Watch for fake briefs that ask you to move to Telegram or WhatsApp for "faster communication". Once you leave the platform, Contra cannot protect your payment or help in a dispute. Keep every message, file and payment inside Contra, and if a client refuses, walk away.</p>
<p>Be careful with the zero commission message itself. It is true that Contra takes no percentage cut, but card processing fees, the reported flat per payment fee on the free plan, and the Pro subscription are real costs. Work out your net pay on every project before you quote, or you will quote a fee that looks good and pays less than you expected. For a broader comparison before you commit, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-choose-earning-platform" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose an earning platform</a>.</p>
<h3 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is Contra free to join?</h4>
<p>Yes. Signing up and creating your profile is free, and core features such as commission free payments, invoicing and contracts are available on the free plan. Full access to the Opportunities job board needs Contra Pro, reported at 29 US dollars a month.</p>
<h4>Does Contra really take no commission?</h4>
<p>Contra charges no percentage commission on freelancer earnings, which every guide confirms. But card processing of about 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars still applies, and guides report a flat fee of 15 to 29 US dollars per payment on the free plan.</p>
<h4>How do you get clients on Contra?</h4>
<p>Clients find you through the Discover feed, you apply to briefs on the Opportunities board, or you bring your own clients and use Contra for contracts and payments. A strong visual portfolio is the main factor in all three routes, and new profiles usually win their first client through their own outreach.</p>
<h4>Is Contra legit?</h4>
<p>Yes. Contra launched in 2019 and reports more than one million independent professionals. It is a real platform with escrow, contracts and client vetting, but some guides note poor public review scores, so keep your work and payments inside the platform.</p>
<h4>Do you need Pro to earn on Contra?</h4>
<p>No. The free plan gives you a portfolio, unlimited service listings, direct proposals and commission free contracts. Pro adds the full job board, custom domain, branding tools and analytics. Skip Pro until your portfolio is strong and you are applying to briefs regularly.</p>
<h4>How long does it take to get paid on Contra?</h4>
<p>Guides report payouts in 2 to 3 business days after a milestone is approved, by bank deposit, PayPal or USDC. The client funds the milestone upfront, so the money is held in escrow before you start the work, which is the main protection the platform offers.</p>
<h3 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h3>
<p>To earn money on Contra, build a visual portfolio with three to five strong projects, write a headline that names your niche, list fixed price services with clear scope, share your profile link widely, apply to briefs with short specific pitches, and run every project on milestones inside the platform. The zero commission model means you keep more of each fee than on Upwork or Fiverr, but only if your portfolio is strong enough to win the work in the first place.</p>
