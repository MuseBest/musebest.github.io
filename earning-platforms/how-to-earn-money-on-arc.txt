32:T552f,<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-arc-in-2026">How to Earn Money on Arc in 2026</h2>
<p>If you are an experienced developer, you may be tired of bidding against hundreds of people for low paid work. Learning how to earn money on Arc could change that, because Arc works the other way round. You pass a strict vetting process first, and then companies apply to interview you.</p>
<p>Arc is a remote hiring marketplace for developers and other tech talent. It is owned by Codementor, a developer mentorship platform. Arc says its talent pool is the top 2% of applicants, and independent reviews estimate acceptance near 2.3%. That low rate is the whole point. Companies trust the pool, so they pay more, and developers keep the full rate they agree.</p>
<p>This guide explains what Arc is, how the vetting works, how much developers can earn, what it costs, and who should not apply. All facts about fees and pay were checked on 15 November 2026.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-arc-and-how-does-it-work">What is Arc and how does it work</h2>
<p>Arc, at <a href="https://arc.dev/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">arc.dev</a>, is a global marketplace of vetted remote talent. Its homepage says it gives companies access to 450,000 talent in 190 countries, and that it covers freelance and full-time roles for developers, designers, marketers, product managers, project managers, assistants, and more. Developers are the core group, and this guide focuses on them.</p>
<p>The model is simple. You create a profile with your skills, work history, and expected rate. Arc screens you. If you pass, you enter the talent pool. Arc uses its HireAI matching system to show your profile to companies that need your skills. Companies then invite you to interview. You do not bid on jobs, and you do not pay Arc a fee.</p>
<p>There are two ways to work through Arc. Freelance contracts are paid by the hour, and Arc says hiring can take 72 hours once a match is made. Full-time remote hires are paid a salary, and Arc says hiring can take 14 days. Arc also offers a risk-free trial of up to two weeks, so a company can test the fit before it commits.</p>
<p>Arc is not a job board. You cannot browse hundreds of open jobs and apply to each one. You pass the gate first, and then the work comes to you. That is good news if you are senior and patient, and bad news if you need income this week.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-build-a-profile-that-passes-screening">Step 1: Build a profile that passes screening</h2>
<p>Your profile is your first test. Recruiters read it before they invite you to any assessment, so it must show real production experience.</p>
<p>Start with your strongest skills. List the languages, frameworks, and tools you have used in paid work, not the ones you tried once in a tutorial. Arc focuses on full-stack, mobile, cloud, and backend work, so make your depth in one of those areas clear.</p>
<p>Write your work history as results, not duties. Say what you built, which stack you used, and what changed for the product or the team. Keep each job to three or four short lines. Recruiters scan fast, and long paragraphs hide your best work.</p>
<p>Set an honest expected rate. Independent reviews report that Arc developers typically charge $60 to $110 an hour, with experienced developers charging more. If you set your rate far below the market, you may look junior. If you set it far above your experience, you may get no matches. Check the pay section below, then pick a rate that fits your skill level and your location.</p>
<p>Add a short video introduction. Arc asks candidates to record one, and it is used to judge remote readiness, English fluency, and communication style. Record it in a quiet room with good light. Speak slowly and clearly. Say who you are, what you build best, and what kind of work you want. Keep it under two minutes.</p>
<p>Finally, link to real proof. A GitHub profile with clean, recent code helps. So does a live project or a short case study that explains a problem you solved. Do not link to tutorial clones. Recruiters have seen them all before.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-pass-the-vetting-process">Step 2: Pass the vetting process</h2>
<p>Arc uses a four stage vetting process. Independent reviews describe the same four stages, and Arc confirms on its site that vetted talent have passed communication and technical assessments.</p>
<p>Stage one is profile screening. Recruiters check your training, skills, and professional background. Most applicants fail here, because Arc wants at least three years of professional experience. Junior developers with less than three years of production work rarely pass.</p>
<p>Stage two is a coding assessment. This is usually an automated test that checks how you solve problems in code. Practise the basics before you start. Clean code, clear logic, and working tests matter more than clever tricks.</p>
<p>Stage three is a live technical interview or pair programming session with a senior engineer. You will talk through a real problem, write code live, and explain your choices. The interviewer is judging how you think, not only whether your answer works. Talk out loud. Ask questions when the problem is unclear. Say what trade-offs you are making.</p>
<p>Stage four is an English and behavioural evaluation. This may be a live interview or a recorded video interview. Arc wants developers who can work with remote teams across time zones, so clear written and spoken English matters as much as code.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/earning-platforms-arc-vetting-process.jpg" alt="A software developer preparing for a technical interview with a laptop, checklist, and headphones on the desk" /></p>
<p>A final review team then checks every stage for consistency before you enter the pool. The whole process can take one to two weeks. That sounds slow, but it is the reason companies pay the rates they do.</p>
<p>Prepare properly. Review the fundamentals of your main stack. Practise live coding with a timer. Record yourself answering simple questions about your past work, and listen back for unclear speech. If you fail, you can use the feedback to prepare for a later attempt, but do not rush back in unprepared.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-get-matched-and-interview-with-companies">Step 3: Get matched and interview with companies</h2>
<p>Once you are in the pool, Arc matches you with companies. You will see the salary range or contract terms before you agree to a call. That transparency is one of Arc's best features, because you never waste time on a role that pays far below your rate.</p>
<p>Companies on Arc include high growth startups and established tech brands. Arc says its clients include Spotify and Automattic, plus venture backed startups. You interview directly with the company, usually over video. Expect questions about your past projects, your way of working, and how you handle remote communication.</p>
<p>Many US employers ask for three to four hours of daily overlap with US Eastern or Pacific time. If you live in South Asia, that can mean working late afternoons or evenings. Check the time zone needs before you accept an interview, because a great rate is less useful if the hours do not fit your life.</p>
<p>You can also say no. If the role, the stack, or the pay does not fit, decline it and wait for a better match. That is the advantage of a vetted pool. You are not competing for every job, so you can be selective.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-agree-your-rate-and-start-work">Step 4: Agree your rate and start work</h2>
<p>When a company wants to hire you, you agree the terms. For freelance work, you agree an hourly rate and the expected hours. For a full-time role, you agree a salary. Arc says developers keep 100% of the rate they agree, because Arc charges its fee to the hiring company, not to you.</p>
<p>Read the contract before you sign it. Check the hours, the time zone overlap, the notice period, and who owns the code you write. For full-time international hires, Arc sends clients to third-party Employer of Record partners, which handle local payroll and compliance. Make sure you understand who your legal employer is and how your tax will be handled.</p>
<p>Arc says it handles secure freelancer payments. Confirm the payment schedule, the currency, and the payment method in writing before your first day. If anything is unclear, ask Arc support to explain it. A short email now can prevent a payment dispute later.</p>

<li>Companies come to you, so you skip public bidding wars.</li>

<li>US time zone overlap is often required.</li>

<h2 id="who-arc-suits-and-who-it-does-not-suit">Who Arc suits and who it does not suit</h2>
<p>Arc suits senior developers with three or more years of production experience, strong English, and a clear specialism such as full-stack, backend, mobile, or cloud work. It suits people who want remote work with US or European companies and who can wait for the right match.</p>
<p>Arc does not suit beginners, career changers with no paid experience, or developers who need income this week. It does not suit people who want many small side jobs at once, because part-time mode limits you to one engagement. It also does not suit developers who cannot offer any overlap with US working hours.</p>
<p>If you are junior, build two or three years of real experience first, then apply. Use the time to learn <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-coding-for-beginners" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn coding for beginners</a> if you are still early in your journey, and to build a portfolio of real projects.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Applying with less than three years of experience and expecting to pass.</li>
<li>Listing skills you have never used in paid work.</li>
<li>Writing a long profile that hides your best results.</li>
<li>Setting a rate far above or below your real market value.</li>
<li>Skipping practice for the live coding interview.</li>
<li>Recording a rushed video intro in a noisy room.</li>
<li>Accepting a role without checking the time zone overlap.</li>
<li>Signing a contract without reading the payment and notice terms.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Arc itself is a legitimate platform, but the remote hiring world has scams, so stay alert.</p>
<p>Never pay anyone to join Arc, to pass vetting, or to get matched. Arc is free for developers. If someone asks for a fee, it is not Arc.</p>
<p>Watch for fake recruiters who contact you outside the platform and ask you to move to chat apps, pay for training, or share bank details. Real Arc communication happens through Arc's own system. Check any message against the official site at <a href="https://arc.dev/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">arc.dev</a>.</p>
<p>Be careful with contracts that ask you to work before the agreement is signed. Do not start work until the rate, hours, and payment terms are in writing. Keep copies of every agreement and every invoice.</p>
<p>Your account can also be delayed in review. Some developers report long waits with little feedback from support. If that happens, keep records of your application, stay polite with support, and keep other job options open. Do not depend on one platform alone.</p>

<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-arc-legit-for-developers">Is Arc legit for developers?</h3>
<p>Yes. Arc is a real hiring marketplace owned by Codementor, and its clients include known tech companies. It is free for developers, and it charges its fees to hiring companies. That said, acceptance is very low, and passing vetting does not guarantee work. Treat it as one channel among several, not as a job promise.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-can-i-earn-on-arc">How much can I earn on Arc?</h3>
<p>Independent reviews report full-time salaries from $60,000 to $160,000 a year and hourly contract rates from $40 to $120 an hour, with typical rates more often $60 to $110 an hour. Your rate depends on your stack, seniority, location, and the client. Arc takes no fee from developers, so you keep the rate you agree.</p>
<h3 id="how-hard-is-it-to-get-accepted-on-arc">How hard is it to get accepted on Arc?</h3>
<p>It is hard. Arc describes its pool as the top 2% of applicants, and independent reviews estimate acceptance near 2.3%. You need at least three years of professional experience, plus a profile screen, a coding assessment, a live technical interview, and an English evaluation. Prepare well before you apply.</p>
<h3 id="does-arc-charge-developers-any-fee">Does Arc charge developers any fee?</h3>
<p>No. Arc is free for developers. There is no sign-up fee, no monthly fee, and no commission on your pay. Arc charges the hiring company instead. For full-time hires, third-party reviews report a fee near 20% of the first year salary. Confirm the current terms on the <a href="https://arc.dev/pricing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Arc pricing page</a> before you sign anything.</p>
<h3 id="can-beginners-earn-money-on-arc">Can beginners earn money on Arc?</h3>
<p>Not usually. Arc is built for experienced developers, and junior developers with less than three years of production experience rarely pass the first screen. Beginners should build real experience first, through a job, freelance projects, or open source work, and then apply when they have proof of their skills.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-get-hired-on-arc">How long does it take to get hired on Arc?</h3>
<p>Vetting takes about one to two weeks. After that, matching time varies. Arc says freelance hiring can happen in 72 hours once a match is made, and full-time hiring can take 14 days. Some developers wait longer for the right role, so do not stop other job searching while you wait.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Arc can be a strong route to well paid remote work if you are an experienced developer with three or more years of production experience. You pay no platform fee, companies come to you, and the rates are higher than on open marketplaces. The price is a tough vetting process, a slow start, and no guarantee of work.</p>
<p>If that fits your situation, build a sharp profile, practise for the technical interview, record a clear video intro, and apply with patience. If you are still junior, build your experience first and come back when you have proof to show.</p>
