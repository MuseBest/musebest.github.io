How to Earn Money on Catalant in 2026

You can earn money on Catalant if you are an experienced consultant with a clear record of results for large clients. Catalant is not a general freelance site. It is a curated marketplace for independent consultants and business experts, and it serves large companies and private equity firms. This guide explains how it works, who it suits, what it pays, and what to do if you are not ready for it yet.

What Catalant is

Catalant connects independent consultants with large organisations that need expert help for a set period. The company was founded in 2013 as HourlyNerd and renamed Catalant in 2016. It is based in Boston, in the United States. Its clients are mainly large enterprises and private equity firms.

Catalant describes itself as fit to purpose consulting. It does not sell small tasks or quick gigs. It sells scoped consulting work that solves a real business problem, such as a market entry plan, a merger, or a cost reduction programme.

Catalant does both short calls and whole projects, but its main business is project work that lasts weeks or months.

Catalant covers eight industry areas, from Consumer to Technology, plus capabilities such as Digital and AI and Operations. Each area needs deep proof.

How Catalant differs from general freelance sites

On a general site, you bid for jobs and compete with many freelancers. On Catalant, you apply to join a vetted network first, then pitch for projects. The client list is smaller, the projects are fewer, and the rates are higher. Catalant is not a first step into freelancing.

Who Catalant suits

Catalant suits experienced professionals who can show real results: a former strategy consultant from a large firm, a senior manager who led change or cost projects, a finance or operations leader with a clear record, a specialist in a narrow area such as pricing, or a boutique consultancy that wants access to large clients.

The common thread is proof. You need case studies, outcomes with numbers, and references. A job title alone is not enough. Education can help, but it does not replace experience.

Who Catalant does not suit

Catalant does not suit beginners. You are unlikely to be a good fit if you have less than five years of relevant experience, if you have no case studies or measurable outcomes, if your work so far is general freelance work with no consulting focus, if you need steady weekly income from next month, or if you cannot work across US or European time zones.

None of this means your career is stuck. It means Catalant is a later step. Build your record elsewhere first, then apply. If you are based outside the United States or Europe, you can still apply, as there is no published country ban. But most clients are American and European.

How Catalant projects work

A Catalant project follows a clear path. The steps below come from the Catalant page for independent consultants, which was read on 2026-11-23.

Step 1: A client posts a project brief

A client describes the problem, the skills needed, the timeline, and the budget range. Projects last several weeks, months, or even years, and they may extend into follow on work.

Step 2: Catalant matches experts to the brief

Catalant software matches the brief with consultants in its network. Your profile is the tool the matching system reads. A vague profile fails, while a specific profile with a named result matches much better.

Step 3: You search and pitch

You can also search the project database yourself. When you find a fit, write a pitch with a proposed approach and relevant work experience. Clients review pitches from several experts and choose who to interview based on pitch quality.

Step 4: You interview, propose, and sign

If your pitch stands out, you meet the client. This is a real consulting interview. After the interview, you submit a proposal with your approach, deliverables, and timeline. The project agreement then sets out the timeline, approach, deliverables, and fees, plus confidentiality and work product ownership.

Step 5: You deliver the work and get rated

You deliver the agreed work, then you and the client rate and review the engagement. This is when you can collect testimonials and case studies to help you win more work.

How to apply to Catalant

Step 1: Check your fit honestly

Write down three projects where you led the work and can show a result with a number. If you cannot write three, Catalant is probably too early for you. Build those projects first, then come back.

Step 2: Apply to become a verified consultant

Apply through the Catalant site to become a verified independent consultant or boutique consultancy. Catalant says its team checks that your background aligns with the needs of its customers. This process takes 2 to 5 business days to complete, according to the official consultants page.

Step 3: Build a robust profile

Create a profile that clearly explains your value, with your work experience, capabilities, education, and key strengths. Preview it before you publish it, and keep it up to date.

Your profile should name your industries and capabilities, add your rate, and add two or three outcomes with numbers. Use one clear headline, a short summary, and three case studies. If you want to sharpen delivery skills first, you can learn project management online. If your niche is finance, read how to work as a freelance financial consultant.

Step 4: Pass the vetting checks

Catalant reviews your background, checks references, and may interview you. Other 2026 guides report an acceptance rate of about 10 to 15 per cent, so most applicants are turned away. That figure is not published by Catalant, so treat it as a rough guide. You may also need short compliance training on confidentiality and conflicts of interest.

Step 5: Search, pitch, deliver, and update

Once you are accepted, your profile is visible to clients. You still need to pitch. Search the database and pitch only when you are a strong fit. After each project, update your profile with the new case study, if the client allows it.

What it costs and what to expect

Catalant does not publish a single public fee percentage on its independent consultants page, which was read in full on 2026-11-23. The Catalant Terms of Use for Experts was also checked, but a direct open returned a rate limit error that day, so its full text could not be read here. The fee detail below comes from third party reviews, and those reviews do not all agree.

Most reviews report a markup of about 25 to 30 per cent on the client bill, with the consultant keeping their stated rate. Other 2026 reviews report a deduction of 20 to 30 per cent from total project fees. In that model, a 200 dollar rate could leave you with 140 to 160 dollars before tax.

These two versions cannot both be right for every project. The fee may vary by engagement or by client contract. You must check the fee shown in your own project agreement before you sign it. Ask Catalant in writing what fee applies to your project, and keep that answer with your contract.

There is no fee to create a profile or to apply. Catalant does not charge you to join. Anyone who asks you to pay to join Catalant is not acting for Catalant. The real cost to you is time: time to build a strong profile, time to pitch, and time spent waiting for a match.

Catalant handles contracting and payment, and its official page says it protects consultants from lengthy payment terms. Even so, other guides report slow payment, from two to six weeks, and in one case 60 days after a project ends. Plan your cash flow for a slow payout.

Payment is made in United States dollars, according to the expert terms summary found in search results. You are responsible for exchange rate changes and transaction fees. Catalant also has a 12 month non-circumvention rule. For 12 months after you first meet a client through Catalant, you must use Catalant as your only route to bid for work with that client. Read that clause in full before you sign.

How much can you earn on Catalant

Pay varies a lot, because it covers short calls and long projects, and because rates follow your skill, your niche, and your track record. The ranges below are reported by other 2026 guides, not published by Catalant. Treat them as reported ranges, not as a promise.

Work type Reported rate range
Short expert consultations 300 to 500 dollars an hour, often more
Project work, senior consultants 150 to 450 dollars an hour
Project work, mid level professionals 150 to 300 dollars an hour
Top experts in high demand niches 500 to 1,000 dollars an hour or more, rare

These rates look high, and for the right person they are real. But they do not mean steady income. Many accepted experts get little or no work, because the client picks from a shortlist and the projects are few. Your results will vary with your experience, location, demand, time, competition, expenses, and platform fees.

Worked example: Example only

This example shows how the maths can work. It is an example only. It is not a promise of income, and your real figures will differ.

Imagine you are a senior operations consultant. You quote 250 dollars an hour. You win a scoped project for 80 hours over eight weeks. Your gross fee is 80 times 250, which equals 20,000 dollars.

Now apply the two fee models reported by other guides. Model A, client side markup: the client pays 20,000 dollars plus 25 per cent, which is 5,000 dollars, so you keep 20,000 dollars before tax. Model B, deduction: Catalant takes 25 per cent, which is 5,000 dollars, so you keep 15,000 dollars before tax.

The gap is 5,000 dollars on the same project. That is why you must check your project agreement. If you set aside 30 per cent for tax, that is 6,000 dollars in Model A, or 4,500 dollars in Model B. After tax, Model A leaves 14,000 dollars and Model B leaves 10,500 dollars, before other costs. If you win none, your income from Catalant is zero.

Downsides of Catalant

First, acceptance is low, and even accepted experts may get little work. Second, work is uneven, so Catalant is a poor choice as your only income source. Third, payment can be slow, with one guide reporting payment 60 days after a project ends. Fourth, the fee is not clear from public pages, so you must read your project agreement. Fifth, the 12 month non-circumvention rule stops you taking a Catalant client direct for a year. Sixth, clients are large and demanding, and weak work will hurt your rating. Seventh, time zones can be hard, with many calls in your evening or night if you are in Asia.

Common mistakes

  • You apply with a vague profile that lists broad skills and no case studies, so the client cannot see what you actually deliver.
  • You set a low rate to win work, then find that tax, fees, and unpaid time leave you with little pay for senior work.
  • You pitch for every project, even when you are a weak fit, and your pitch quality falls.
  • You treat acceptance as income, and you stop looking for other clients, then months pass with no project and no pay.
  • You sign a project agreement without reading the fee and the 12 month non-circumvention clause.
  • You plan your cash flow around a fast payout, then a slow payment cycle leaves you short for bills and tax.
  • You use the same generic pitch for every client, instead of writing a clear approach for that client problem.

Risks and scams to watch for

Catalant itself is a legitimate company, founded in 2013 and based in Boston. A match through the official platform is a real, paid opportunity. But scams can still target consultants who want to join.

Anyone who asks you to pay to join Catalant, to pay for a profile, or to pay for a project is a scam. Catalant does not charge experts to join. A message that claims to be from Catalant but asks you to move off the platform to pay, or to share bank details by email, is a red flag. Use only the official Catalant site and platform.

A client who asks you to start work before you have a signed project agreement may leave you unpaid. Do not start delivery until the agreement is signed. A project that asks you to share confidential data from a past client, or to break a non-disclosure agreement, is a risk to your career. Say no. A pitch that asks you to do a large amount of free work as a test is a warning sign. A short case discussion is normal. A full unpaid project is not.

You are an independent consultant, not an employee. You must handle your own tax, insurance, and contracts. If you are unsure, speak to a qualified adviser in your country before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

Is Catalant legit?

Yes. Catalant is a legitimate company based in Boston, founded in 2013 as HourlyNerd and renamed in 2016. It works with large enterprises and private equity firms, and it handles contracting and payment. It never charges experts to join.

How much can you earn on Catalant?

You can earn high hourly rates if you are accepted and you win work, with other 2026 guides reporting 150 to 450 dollars an hour for project work and 300 to 500 dollars an hour for short calls. But work is uneven, and many accepted experts get little or no work.

Who can join Catalant?

Catalant accepts experienced independent consultants and boutique consultancies who can show senior experience and measurable results. Beginners and general freelancers are rarely accepted, and they should build a stronger record first.

Does Catalant charge experts a fee?

Catalant does not charge you to create a profile or to apply, but the exact fee on a project is not published as a single public rate. Other reviews report a client side markup of about 25 to 30 per cent, while other reviews report a deduction of 20 to 30 per cent from project fees. Check the fee in your own project agreement before you sign.

How long does Catalant take to pay?

Payment timing varies. Catalant says it protects consultants from lengthy payment terms. Other guides report a cycle of two to six weeks, a first payout in 30 to 45 days, and in one case payment 60 days after a project ends. Plan for a slow payout.

What should I do if Catalant rejects me?

Treat a rejection as a signal to build proof. Build case studies on other platforms or with direct clients, collect references, and sharpen one niche. Then apply again when you have three strong projects with measured outcomes to show.

Conclusion

Catalant is a strong option for senior consultants who have proof, patience, and other income while they wait for a match. It offers access to large clients and high reported rates, but it also brings strict vetting, uneven work, slow payment, and a fee you must check in your own contract.

If that profile fits you, apply with a clear niche and three strong case studies. If it does not fit you yet, build your record first, then apply when you have proof to show.
