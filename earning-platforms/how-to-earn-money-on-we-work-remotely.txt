<h2 id="what-we-work-remotely-is-and-what-it-is-not">What We Work Remotely is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>We Work Remotely is a job board dedicated only to remote work. It launched publicly in 2011, and its official <a href="https://weworkremotely.com/frequently-asked-questions" rel="noopener" target="_blank">frequently asked questions page</a> says more than 1,000 new positions are posted every month. Companies post listings, and job seekers browse the listings and apply. Most listings are full time jobs or longer contracts in programming, design, product, marketing, sales and customer support.</p>
<p>We Work Remotely is not a freelance marketplace. On a marketplace such as Upwork or Fiverr, clients post projects, freelancers bid or sell services, and the platform holds the payment in escrow and takes a fee. On We Work Remotely, your application goes straight to the employer, usually through the employer website, an applicant tracking system, or email. There is no bidding, no fee on your salary, and no escrow. That means you keep everything the employer pays you, and it also means the board cannot protect your pay.</p>
<p>We Work Remotely is not a recruitment agency. Nobody there applies or interviews for you. Most roles are employment or long contracts, not short gigs, so the board suits people who want steady remote work.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-we-work-remotely-in-7-steps">How to earn money on We Work Remotely in 7 steps</h2>
<p>Follow these steps in order. They take about an hour to set up, and then a short check a few times a week.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-a-free-basic-account">Step 1: Create a free Basic account</h3>
<p>Create a free account so you can save jobs, upload your CV and set alerts. Choose your categories, your regions and your time zones during setup, so your feed shows roles you can actually take. Turn on daily email notifications for your tailored search, so new roles reach you quickly.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-upload-your-cv-and-prepare-your-links">Step 2: Upload your CV and prepare your links</h3>
<p>Upload a current CV in a clean layout with clear headings. Remote employers look for clear written communication, because so much remote work happens in writing. Add links to your portfolio, your GitHub profile or your work samples, because the board does not store portfolios for you. Write a short headline that names your role, your strongest skill and your time zone.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-wwr-search.jpg" alt="A software developer reviewing remote job listings on a large monitor at a standing desk" /></p>
<h3 id="step-3-check-the-region-tag-and-the-full-description">Step 3: Check the region tag and the full description</h3>
<p>Every listing carries a regional tag, such as a country name, a group of countries, or Anywhere in the World. Read the tag first, then read the full description, because the real conditions are often in the text. Look for phrases about work authorisation, required time zone overlap, or hiring through a global payroll provider. A remote job is not always a job you can do from any country, so skip roles you are not eligible for. Check the employer website before you apply, so you know the role is real.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-apply-early-with-a-tailored-application">Step 4: Apply early with a tailored application</h3>
<p>Apply soon after a role is posted. Tailor your CV and cover note to the role. Name the company problem, give one relevant result, and mention your remote experience, time zone and availability. Highlight async communication, which means working through written messages rather than live meetings.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-track-applications-and-follow-up-once">Step 5: Track applications and follow up once</h3>
<p>Keep a simple list of where you applied, the date, and the reply. If you hear nothing, follow up once after about two weeks with a short, polite message. Then move on and keep applying. Remote hiring can take several rounds, including take home tasks, so plan your time for that.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-wwr-apply.jpg" alt="A remote team member on a video call with colleagues shown on a laptop screen in a home office" /></p>
<h3 id="step-6-prepare-for-remote-interviews">Step 6: Prepare for remote interviews</h3>
<p>Most interviews happen by video call. Test your camera, microphone and internet connection before the call, and choose a quiet room with a plain background. Be ready for questions about your home setup, your internet reliability, your time zone and your experience working independently. Prepare short, honest answers, and be ready for interviews in the employer time zone, which can mean late evening or night in your country.</p>
<h3 id="step-7-agree-pay-and-terms-in-writing-before-you-start">Step 7: Agree pay and terms in writing before you start</h3>
<p>Before you start work, agree the pay rate, the pay schedule, the currency, the payment method and the hours in writing. Employees are usually paid through payroll or a global payroll provider, which may only operate in certain countries. Contractors usually invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service. Confirm that the employer can legally pay someone in your country before you accept.</p>

<h2 id="how-you-get-paid">How you get paid</h2>
<p>We Work Remotely does not pay you, and it takes no fee from your earnings. The employer pays you directly, so your pay, your pay schedule and your contract all come from the employer.</p>
<p>Employees are usually paid through payroll or a global payroll provider, with tax handled where the law requires it. Contractors usually send an invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service, and they handle their own tax. Ask in the interview how often you will be paid, in which currency, and by which method, and agree it in writing.</p>
<p>There is no escrow, so there is no payment protection. If an employer pays late, the board cannot release your money, because it never held it. That is why the written agreement in Step 7 matters so much.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>The free Basic account costs you nothing, and you can browse and apply without paying. Your real cost is time. A focused search takes about thirty minutes a few times a week, and a good application takes twenty to thirty minutes to tailor properly.</p>
<p>Example only: suppose you find a remote customer support contract at an assumed rate of 25 US dollars per hour, and you work an assumed 30 hours per week. One week pays 30 times 25, which is 750 US dollars, and four weeks pay 3,000 US dollars before tax. These figures are assumed for the example. Real pay rates vary by role, employer and country, and many applicants never get hired at all.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Most applications get no reply, and that is normal on a popular remote board. A realistic first month is week one for your account, CV and alerts, weeks two and three for five to ten tailored applications per week, and week four for interviews and follow ups. If you apply to 30 roles and hear back from three or four, that is normal. If you hear back from none, improve your CV and cover notes before you apply to more roles.</p>
<p>Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses and platform fees. Tax rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Paying for a 12 month Pro plan on day one, before you know whether your field is listed in numbers.</li>
<li>Applying to roles restricted to other countries or time zones without reading the full description.</li>
<li>Trusting the Anywhere in the World tag and skipping the work authorisation lines in the text.</li>
<li>Sending the same generic CV to every employer, instead of tailoring it to the role.</li>
<li>Applying late to popular roles that already have hundreds of applicants.</li>
<li>Leaving your portfolio links out of your CV, so employers cannot see your work.</li>
<li>Starting work before pay, hours and the payment method are agreed in writing.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Employer paid listings reduce fake posts, but scams still reach job seekers, and criminals misuse well known board names. Learn the warning signs before you apply.</p>
<p>First, never pay to get hired. A real employer will not ask you for an upfront equipment fee, training costs or your passport at the start. The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same warning in its <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">job scams advice</a>.</p>
<p>Second, beware of offers that arrive too fast. An offer after one short chat, with a very high salary for a vague job, is a warning sign. We Work Remotely itself lists warning signs such as unprofessional posts, crazy high salaries with vague descriptions, and requests for confidential information.</p>
<p>Third, protect your personal details. Do not share bank details or copies of identity documents until you have a written contract with an employer you have verified. Never cash a cheque from an employer and send part of the money back, because that is a fake cheque scam.</p>
<p>Fourth, check the employer yourself. Search the company name plus the words scam, review or complaint. Confirm that the same job appears on the company website. Once you move to an external site, the board does not moderate the process, so your own checks matter. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-spot-online-job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to spot online job scams</a> lists the full set of checks.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-we-work-remotely-free-for-job-seekers">Is We Work Remotely free for job seekers?</h3>
<p>Yes. The Basic account is free, and you can browse jobs, save listings and upload your CV at no cost as of November 2026. Optional paid tiers exist, but you do not need them to see or apply for jobs.</p>
<h3 id="does-we-work-remotely-pay-you-directly">Does We Work Remotely pay you directly?</h3>
<p>No. We Work Remotely never pays job seekers. The employer who hires you pays your salary or contract fee directly, usually through payroll or by bank transfer. The board takes no fee from your pay.</p>
<h3 id="are-all-jobs-on-we-work-remotely-open-worldwide">Are all jobs on We Work Remotely open worldwide?</h3>
<p>No. Many roles are limited to certain countries or time zones, even when the tag says Anywhere in the World. Read the full description for work authorisation and time zone rules before you apply.</p>
<h3 id="is-we-work-remotely-legit">Is We Work Remotely legit?</h3>
<p>Yes. We Work Remotely has run since 2011, and employers pay $299 per month to post listings, which filters out many low effort posts. That does not mean every employer is honest, so verify each company yourself.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-find-freelance-work-on-we-work-remotely">Can I find freelance work on We Work Remotely?</h3>
<p>Some contract roles exist, but most listings are employment or long contracts. If you want short one off projects, a freelance marketplace will suit you better.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-it-cost-employers-to-post-on-we-work-remotely">How much does it cost employers to post on We Work Remotely?</h3>
<p>The base price is $299 per month for a 30 day listing, with optional upgrades for more visibility. Job seekers do not pay that fee.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>We Work Remotely is worth using if you want steady remote work in programming, design, product, marketing or support, and you can meet the location and time zone rules. It is free to start, employers pay to post, and new roles arrive every month. It cannot find you a job, guarantee a reply, or protect your pay, so your CV, your tailored applications and your written agreement do the real work.</p>
