38:T3f1a,<p>Fiverr is a real marketplace, and it can work for beginners, but only if you accept its hardest rule first. Fiverr keeps 20% of every completed order, including extras and tips, so you take home 80% of the price you set. That single fact shapes every other decision in this Fiverr review, from your prices to your patience in the first month. If you want a platform where buyers find you, where work comes in small fixed-price packages, and where you can start without writing cold proposals, Fiverr is worth a serious look. If you want to keep nearly all of what a client pays, or you sell long consulting projects, Fiverr will frustrate you.</p>
<h2 id="what-fiverr-is-and-how-it-works">What Fiverr is and how it works</h2>
<p>Fiverr is a marketplace for fixed-price services, which Fiverr calls Gigs. A Gig is a packaged service with a set scope, a set price, and a set delivery time. A buyer browses, buys the package, and pays upfront. You deliver the work, the buyer accepts it, and the order completes. That is the whole model, and it is different from proposal platforms in one important way. On Fiverr, buyers come to you. You do not bid for each job. Your job is to make a clear offer that a buyer can understand and buy without a long conversation first.</p>
<p>A Gig usually has three package tiers, called Basic, Standard, and Premium. Each tier adds more work, faster delivery, or extra features. You can also add Gig Extras, such as faster delivery, source files, or an extra revision, for an added price. Tips work the same way as order value for fees, which means Fiverr also keeps 20% of a tip. None of this is hidden, but many beginners still price as if the listed price were their income. It is not. Your income is 80% of the listed price, minus any withdrawal or currency costs.</p>
<h2 id="what-fiverr-costs-sellers">What Fiverr costs sellers</h2>
<p>The seller fee is the simplest part of Fiverr, and it is also the heaviest. Fiverr keeps a flat 20% at every seller level, with no volume discount and no loyalty tier that lowers it. The table below shows what that means in practice, before withdrawal and currency costs.</p>
<p>| Order value | Fiverr keeps (20%) | You receive |
| 25 dollars | 5 dollars | 20 dollars |
| 50 dollars | 10 dollars | 40 dollars |
| 100 dollars | 20 dollars | 80 dollars |
| 250 dollars | 50 dollars | 200 dollars |</p>
<p>Buyers pay their own separate fee at checkout. That fee is 5.5% of the purchase amount, plus a small-order fee on smaller orders. Sources disagree on the exact small-order amount and on the order value where it starts, so do not budget from a single figure you read in a review. Check the checkout total on a real order before you quote a buyer a total cost. The buyer fee does not come out of your earnings, but it does raise what your buyer pays, and that matters most on very small orders, where a few extra dollars can change a buying decision.</p>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine you sell a logo package for 100 dollars with a 20 dollar extra for source files, and the buyer adds a 10 dollar tip. The order total is 130 dollars. Fiverr keeps 26 dollars. You receive 104 dollars before withdrawal or currency costs. If that order took you four hours, your gross rate before other costs is 26 dollars per hour. That is the maths to do before you set any price. Divide the amount you want to keep by 0.80 to find the price you must list. To keep 80 dollars, you must list at 100 dollars. To keep 200 dollars, you must list at 250 dollars.</p>
<h2 id="getting-paid-clearance-withdrawal-and-conversion">Getting paid: clearance, withdrawal, and conversion</h2>
<p>Getting paid on Fiverr takes longer than most beginners expect. After an order completes, your earnings sit in a clearance period before you can withdraw them. The standard clearance period is 14 days. Top Rated sellers, Fiverr Pro sellers, and some paid membership tiers clear faster, at 7 days. Eligible sellers can also use an early payout option for a 1% fee on the amount cleared early, but eligibility depends on your level and account standing, so do not count on it in your first weeks.</p>
<p>Once earnings clear, you withdraw through methods that vary by country, commonly PayPal, Payoneer, or bank transfer. Fiverr-side withdrawal fees are commonly in the range of 1 dollar to 3 dollars depending on the method, and your payout provider may add its own currency conversion charge if you are paid in a currency other than your own. Those conversion charges are the quiet second cut. A seller outside the United States can lose a further few percent when dollars become local currency. Add that to the 20% platform fee, and your real take-home on a small order can sit closer to three quarters of the listed price than four fifths. Check the withdrawal fees shown in your own account before you set prices, because they vary by method and country.</p>

<h2 id="seller-levels-and-why-they-matter">Seller levels and why they matter</h2>
<p>Fiverr ranks sellers in levels: New Seller, Level 1, Level 2, and Top Rated Seller. Levels are based on completed orders, unique clients, earnings, average rating, response rate, and a Success Score that reflects order quality and client satisfaction. Higher levels bring more Gig slots, faster clearance, and better placement in search. Lower levels mean fewer slots and slower clearance.</p>
<p>Beginners should treat levels as a slow climb, not a sprint. Your first level change needs a handful of completed orders from different clients, a solid average rating, and a strong response rate. That means your first ten orders matter far more than your first ten Gig ideas. One cancelled order or one poor review early on hurts more than the same event later, because you have so little history to balance it. Answer messages fast, deliver on time, and say no to work you cannot do well, even when you are hungry for orders.</p>
<p>Exact level thresholds are published on Fiverr's official Level Overview page, and they can change, so check that page before you plan around a specific number. Do not trust a single review article for thresholds, because sources in this batch disagreed on extra day requirements for higher levels.</p>
<h2 id="what-sells-well-on-fiverr-and-what-does-not">What sells well on Fiverr, and what does not</h2>
<p>Fiverr suits services that can be packaged, repeated, and delivered without long discovery calls. Short-form video editing, social media graphics, logo design, voice work, virtual assistance tasks, proofreading, and simple website fixes all fit the model well, because a buyer can grasp the offer from the Gig page alone. Services that need deep client knowledge, such as business strategy, long consulting engagements, or open-ended retainers, fit badly. A buyer who needs three calls before you can even scope the work will not buy a fixed package happily.</p>
<p>Crowded categories are the other trap. General offers such as "I will do graphic design" disappear in search, because thousands of sellers say the same thing. Narrow offers stand out. A Gig that promises one clear outcome for one clear buyer, such as short captions for fitness coaches or product photos for candle sellers, is easier to find and easier to buy. If you are choosing a skill to sell, read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-start-freelancing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start freelancing</a> first, and use it to pick one service and one buyer type before you build anything on Fiverr.</p>
<h2 id="your-first-30-days-on-fiverr">Your first 30 days on Fiverr</h2>
<p>Set honest expectations for month one. Most new sellers get few orders at first, because they have no reviews and little search history. A realistic first month looks like this: week one, build one strong Gig and a complete profile. Week two, share the Gig where your buyers already gather, and keep improving the title, images, and packages. Week three, aim for your first orders, even at a modest price, and overdeliver on them. Week four, review what sold, what questions buyers asked, and what to change. If you reach the end of month one with two or three completed orders and good reviews, that is a solid start, not a failure.</p>
<p>Do not open five Gigs on day one. One strong Gig teaches you more than five weak ones, because you can see which title, thumbnail, and package buyers actually respond to. Add a second Gig only when the first one is bringing enquiries or orders. Price your first Gig to win reviews, not to maximise income. You can raise prices once reviews exist.</p>
<h2 id="packaging-your-gig-and-getting-found">Packaging your Gig and getting found</h2>
<p>A strong Gig answers four questions in seconds: what do I get, who is it for, how fast do I get it, and why should I trust this seller. Put the outcome in the title, not the tool. "Social media captions for busy gym owners" beats "professional writing services" every time, because the buyer sees themselves in it. Use your Gig images to show real samples of the work, not stock photos. Buyers judge quality from images before they read a word.</p>
<p>Packages need care too. Make the Basic tier a complete, useful result, not a teaser that forces an upgrade. Make the Standard tier the one most buyers should pick. Make the Premium tier genuinely bigger, with more deliverables or faster delivery, not just a higher price. Tags and category choice matter for search, so pick the category that matches your service most closely, and use tags that buyers would actually type. For portfolio samples that support your Gig, see our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-create-a-freelance-portfolio" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to create a freelance portfolio</a>.</p>

<h2 id="cancellations-disputes-and-off-platform-traps">Cancellations, disputes, and off-platform traps</h2>
<p>Cancellations happen, and they hurt more than lost income. A cancelled order can affect your completion rate and your level progress, so prevent cancellations before they start. Confirm the scope in the order chat before you begin, especially when a buyer request is vague. If a buyer asks for work far beyond the package, offer a Gig Extra or a custom offer instead of silently absorbing the extra work. If you cannot deliver on time, message the buyer early and agree an extension. Silence is what turns a late order into a cancelled order.</p>
<p>Fiverr has a Resolution Center for disputes, and payment protection applies when orders run through the platform properly. That protection disappears the moment you move payment off Fiverr. Buyers who ask to pay by bank transfer, or sellers who offer a discount for paying outside Fiverr, are asking you to give up your protection and risk your account. Fiverr treats off-platform payments as a serious rule breach, and accounts have been restricted for it. Keep every payment inside Fiverr, even when a regular buyer suggests otherwise. If a buyer behaves badly, keep messages polite, keep evidence in the order chat, and use the Resolution Center rather than arguing in private messages.</p>
<h2 id="who-fiverr-does-not-suit">Who Fiverr does not suit</h2>
<p>Fiverr does not suit everyone, and saying so plainly saves readers wasted months. Fiverr does not suit consultants who sell advice by the hour, because the package model fights hourly work. It does not suit sellers who need nearly all of what a client pays, because the 20% fee plus conversion costs will always bite. It does not suit people who dislike self-promotion, because Gig search, thumbnails, and reviews are the marketing, and nobody else does it for you. It does not suit sellers who want steady retainer income from day one, because Fiverr income rises and falls with search placement and order flow. If any of those describe you, read our <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/upwork-review-for-beginners" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Upwork review for beginners</a> or our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-choose-earning-platform" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose an earning platform</a>, which compares proposal platforms, packaged marketplaces, and direct clients.</p>
<h2 id="alternatives-to-fiverr">Alternatives to Fiverr</h2>
<p>If Fiverr feels wrong, you still have good options. Upwork suits longer proposal-based work and repeat clients, and it charges a variable fee per contract rather than a flat 20%, as stated on <a href="http://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062538-Freelancer-Service-Fee" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Upwork's official service fee page</a>. Freelancer.com suits bidders who are comfortable competing on price in an open bidding system. Direct clients, found through your own outreach, keep the most income but need you to do all the marketing yourself. None of these is free of costs or effort. The right question is not which platform is cheapest, but which platform matches how you like to sell. For pricing help across any platform, read our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-set-freelance-rates" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to set freelance rates</a>.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-fiverr-legit-for-beginners">Is Fiverr legit for beginners?</h3>
<p>Yes, Fiverr is a legitimate marketplace with buyer payments held through its own system until orders complete. Legitimate does not mean easy, because beginners still face strong competition and a 20% fee on every order.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-fiverr-take-from-sellers">How much does Fiverr take from sellers?</h3>
<p>Fiverr keeps 20% of every completed order, including Gig Extras and tips, so a seller keeps 80% of the order value before withdrawal or currency costs. There is no volume tier that lowers that percentage for standard sellers.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-get-paid-on-fiverr">How long does it take to get paid on Fiverr?</h3>
<p>Earnings normally clear 14 days after an order completes, or 7 days for Top Rated sellers, Fiverr Pro sellers, and some paid membership tiers. After clearance, withdrawal transfer times depend on the method you choose.</p>
<h3 id="can-beginners-make-money-on-fiverr">Can beginners make money on Fiverr?</h3>
<p>Beginners can earn on Fiverr, but early income is usually modest and uneven, because new accounts have no reviews and limited search visibility. Treat the first month as a reputation-building period, not as a full income month.</p>
<h3 id="is-fiverr-better-than-upwork">Is Fiverr better than Upwork?</h3>
<p>Neither platform is better in general, because they suit different ways of selling. Fiverr suits packaged services that buyers can purchase directly, while Upwork suits proposal-based work and longer client relationships. Choose by how you want to win work, not by headline fee alone.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion-should-you-start-on-fiverr">Conclusion: should you start on Fiverr?</h2>
<p>Start on Fiverr if you have one clear, packageable service, you can accept keeping 80% of each order, and you are willing to spend your first month earning reviews rather than chasing high prices. Build one strong Gig, price it with the 20% fee already counted, answer fast, and protect your rating from day one. If that plan sounds workable, open your account, check the current fees on <a href="https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/34069565843985-How-Fiverr-works-for-freelancers" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Fiverr's official help page for freelancers</a>, and publish your first Gig this week. For the full decision picture, browse all reviews on the <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Earning Platforms category page</a>. Earning results vary, and nothing on this site is financial advice.</p>
