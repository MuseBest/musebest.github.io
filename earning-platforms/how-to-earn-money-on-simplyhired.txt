<h2 id="what-simplyhired-is-and-what-it-is-not">What SimplyHired is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>SimplyHired is an online job search engine that aggregates listings from company career pages, other job boards and recruitment agencies across the web. Aggregation means it collects listings from many sources into one index, in the same way a web search engine collects pages. Job seekers can browse, filter and apply to as many jobs as they want for free. SimplyHired operates in 23 countries and 12 languages, according to its official <a href="https://www.simplyhired.com/brand-facts" rel="noopener" target="_blank">brand facts page</a>, checked as of November 2026.</p>
<p>SimplyHired and Indeed are both owned by Recruit Holdings, but each platform has its own matching and ranking system, so you will not see the exact same jobs in the same order on both. In practice, you sign in with an Indeed account, employers post jobs through an Indeed employer account, and some applications run through Indeed while others send you to the employer website. SimplyHired is a web only service with no native mobile apps, although the site works in mobile browsers.</p>
<p>SimplyHired is not a freelance marketplace. On a marketplace such as Upwork or Fiverr, clients post projects, freelancers bid or sell services, and the platform holds the payment in escrow and takes a fee. On SimplyHired, you search an index of jobs, you apply to the employer, and the employer pays you directly if you are hired. There is no bidding, no fee on your pay, and no escrow. That means you keep everything you earn, and it also means there is no payment protection.</p>
<p>SimplyHired is also not a recruitment agency. Nobody there applies for you or interviews for you. The service gives you search, filters, salary estimates and alerts. You still have to check each listing, apply, interview and agree terms yourself.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-simplyhired-in-6-steps">How to earn money on SimplyHired in 6 steps</h2>
<p>Follow these steps in order. They take about an hour to set up, and then a short daily check to run well.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-a-free-account-and-upload-your-cv">Step 1: Create a free account and upload your CV</h3>
<p>You can browse listings without an account, but you need a free account to apply. Sign in with your Indeed account or create one, then upload your CV. Use a clean layout with clear headings, because many employers use software to read CVs. Write a short headline that names the role you want and your strongest skill. Keep the layout clean and simple, with clear headings and no graphics.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-search-by-title-skill-and-location-then-filter-hard">Step 2: Search by title, skill and location, then filter hard</h3>
<p>Search by job title, keyword or location. Then use the filters for location, job type, salary estimate and date posted. For remote work, include the word remote in your search or use the Remote and Hybrid filters after your first search. For contract work, add words such as contract, temporary or freelance next to your skill. Sort or filter by date posted, and favour listings from the last seven days, because older aggregated listings may already be filled.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-simplyhired-search.jpg" alt="A person checking job alert emails on a smartphone at a bench with a laptop bag beside them" /></p>
<h3 id="step-3-check-every-listing-on-the-employer-website">Step 3: Check every listing on the employer website</h3>
<p>This step matters more on SimplyHired than on most boards. Because SimplyHired aggregates from many sources, some listings are old, duplicated, or posted by staffing agencies under different titles. Click through to the employer career page and read the original posting. Check that the role is still open, that it is truly remote if that matters to you, and that it accepts applicants from your country. If the job does not appear on the employer website, treat it with caution.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-apply-with-a-tailored-cv-and-cover-note">Step 4: Apply with a tailored CV and cover note</h3>
<p>Some listings let you apply through Indeed with your stored CV. Others send you to the employer website or an applicant tracking system, which is software employers use to manage applications. Either way, tailor your CV headline and write a short cover note for each role. Name the employer need, give one relevant result, and ask for an interview. A generic CV sent to fifty listings will lose to a tailored CV sent to five. Keep a simple list of applications, dates and replies.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-set-job-alerts-and-apply-early">Step 5: Set job alerts and apply early</h3>
<p>Set up alerts for your best searches, so new matches reach your inbox quickly. Apply soon after a role is posted, because employers often review early applicants first. Review your alerts each week and delete the ones that keep sending poor matches. Update your CV whenever your skills change, so matching stays accurate.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-simplyhired-apply.jpg" alt="A person filling in an online job application form on a laptop with a paper checklist nearby" /></p>
<h3 id="step-6-agree-pay-and-terms-in-writing-before-you-start">Step 6: Agree pay and terms in writing before you start</h3>
<p>Before you start work, agree the rate, the pay schedule, the currency, the payment method and the hours in writing. If the role comes through a staffing agency, ask who the end client is, how long the contract runs, and whether you will be an employee of the agency or an independent contractor, because that affects tax and benefits. Employees are usually paid through payroll. Contractors usually invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service.</p>

<h2 id="how-you-get-paid">How you get paid</h2>
<p>SimplyHired does not pay you, and it takes no fee from your earnings. The employer pays you directly, so your pay, your pay schedule and your contract all come from the employer or the staffing agency.</p>
<p>Employees are usually paid through payroll, with tax taken off where the law requires it. Contractors usually send an invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service, and they handle their own tax. Ask in the interview how often you will be paid, in which currency, and by which method, and agree it in writing.</p>
<p>There is no escrow, so there is no payment protection. If an employer pays late, SimplyHired cannot release your money, because it never held it. For a first contract with a new employer, ask for weekly payment or a small advance, rather than a single payment after a month of work.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Searching, filtering, using the salary estimator and applying on SimplyHired cost you nothing, and there is no subscription for job seekers. Your real cost is time, and the time needed to check listings carefully.</p>
<p>Example only: suppose you find a remote contract admin role at an assumed rate of 20 US dollars per hour, and you work an assumed 15 hours per week. One week pays 15 times 20, which is 300 US dollars, and four weeks pay 1,200 US dollars before tax. These figures are assumed for the example. Real pay rates vary by role, employer and country, and many applicants never get hired at all.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Most applications get no reply, and some listings will turn out to be closed when you check them. A realistic first month is week one for your account, CV and alerts, weeks two and three for five to ten checked and tailored applications per week, and week four for interviews and follow ups. Use SimplyHired alongside Indeed and one or two other boards, not as your only source.</p>
<p>Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses and platform fees. Tax rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.</p>

<li>Sending the same generic CV to every listing, instead of tailoring it to the role.</li>
<li>Treating a salary estimate as a real offer, instead of confirming the rate with the employer.</li>
<li>Ignoring the location line and applying to roles that need work authorisation you do not have.</li>
<li>Starting contract work before the rate, payment method and payment date are agreed in writing.</li>
<li>Trusting unsolicited messages from supposed recruiters without checking the company yourself.</li>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>SimplyHired says it uses the same safety technology as Indeed to remove unsafe postings, and you can report suspicious listings. Even so, aggregators can show stale listings, and scammers misuse job board names. Learn the warning signs before you apply.</p>
<p>First, never pay to get a job. A real employer will not ask you for an application fee, training costs or equipment money before you start. The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same warning in its <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">job scams advice</a>.</p>
<p>Second, beware of unsolicited outreach. SimplyHired does not recruit through WhatsApp or other messaging apps, and any outreach like that should be treated as suspicious. A real employer interviews you properly and sends a written contract. An offer after one short chat, with high pay for simple work, is a warning sign.</p>
<p>Third, protect your personal details. Do not share bank details, identity documents or tax numbers until you have a written contract with an employer you have verified. Never cash a cheque from an employer and send part of the money back, because that is a fake cheque scam.</p>
<p>Fourth, check the employer yourself. Search the company name plus the words scam, review or complaint. Confirm that the same job appears on the company website. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-spot-online-job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to spot online job scams</a> gives you the full checking routine.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-simplyhired-free-for-job-seekers">Is SimplyHired free for job seekers?</h3>
<p>Yes. Searching, filtering, viewing listings, using the salary estimator and applying are free for job seekers as of November 2026. You need a free account to apply, and you can apply to an unlimited number of jobs at no cost.</p>
<h3 id="is-simplyhired-the-same-as-indeed">Is SimplyHired the same as Indeed?</h3>
<p>No, but they are closely linked. Both are owned by Recruit Holdings, and SimplyHired runs as part of the Indeed network with shared sign in. Each platform has its own matching and ranking, so results differ between the two.</p>
<h3 id="does-simplyhired-pay-you-directly">Does SimplyHired pay you directly?</h3>
<p>No. SimplyHired never pays job seekers. The employer or staffing agency who hires you pays you directly, usually through payroll or by bank transfer. SimplyHired takes no fee from your pay and holds no money for you.</p>
<h3 id="is-simplyhired-legit">Is SimplyHired legit?</h3>
<p>Yes. SimplyHired is an established job search platform owned by the same parent company as Indeed. That does not mean every listing is current or honest, so check each listing on the employer website before you apply.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-find-remote-and-contract-work-on-simplyhired">Can I find remote and contract work on SimplyHired?</h3>
<p>Yes. SimplyHired lists full time, part time, contract, temporary and remote roles. Use the job type filters, add words such as contract or remote to your search, and read the location line on every listing.</p>
<h3 id="why-do-some-simplyhired-listings-get-no-reply">Why do some SimplyHired listings get no reply?</h3>
<p>Because SimplyHired aggregates from external sources, some postings may not update when a role is filled or paused. Response times also depend on the employer, not on SimplyHired. Favour recent listings and check the employer website.</p>
