How to Earn Money on Indeed in 2026

You want to earn money on Indeed in 2026, but you are not sure how the money part works. The honest answer is simple. Indeed does not pay you. Indeed is a job search engine, and the employer who hires you pays your wages or your contract fee. This guide shows you how to use Indeed well, so you find real part time, temporary, contract and remote work and give yourself the best chance of being hired.

What Indeed is, and what it is not

Indeed brings job postings from many places into one search. Employers post jobs directly, and Indeed also indexes jobs from company career pages. You can create a free account, upload your CV, search, save jobs, set alerts and apply. Indeed says its tools are free for job seekers, as of November 2026. See the official How Indeed works guide.

Indeed is not a freelance marketplace. On a marketplace such as Upwork or Fiverr, clients post projects, freelancers bid or sell services, and the platform holds the payment and takes a fee. On Indeed, employers post jobs, you apply for a job, and the employer decides whether to hire you. There is no bidding, no platform fee on your pay, and no escrow, which is money held safely by a third party until work is done. That means you keep everything the employer pays you, but it also means Indeed cannot protect your pay if an employer pays late.

Indeed is not a recruitment agency. Nobody there applies or interviews for you. You still have to search, apply, interview and follow up yourself.

You may see old guides about an Indeed affiliate programme that paid website owners per click. That programme was for job board owners, not job seekers. For a job seeker, the only honest route is to get hired into a real job.

How to earn money on Indeed in 7 steps

Follow these steps in order. They take a few hours to set up, and then about an hour a day to run well.

Step 1: Create a free account and complete your profile

Create a free account, then complete your profile in full. Add your job titles, skills, location, pay expectations, schedule and remote work preference. A half finished profile brings poor matches, so finish it properly on day one.

Step 2: Upload a clean CV and set your visibility

Upload your CV or build one on Indeed. Use a clean layout with clear headings, because many employers use software to read CVs. Write a short headline that names the role you want and your strongest skill, such as Customer Support Agent, Email and Chat, Two Years Experience. Then choose your visibility setting. If you select Employers can find you, employers who search the CV database can see your profile and contact you. If you select the private option, only employers you apply to will see it. If you need help with the CV itself, use a clean single column layout with clear headings and no graphics.

Step 3: Search with tight filters, not broad words

Search for your job title plus the words that matter, such as part time, contract, temporary or remote. Then use the filters for date posted, job type, salary, location, company and experience level. Sort by date so the newest roles come first, because old listings are often filled already. Save your best searches and turn on job alerts, so new matches reach your inbox on the day they are posted. Good roles attract many applicants fast, so speed helps.

Step 4: Read the full listing before you apply

Read the whole job description, not just the title. Check the location line first, because many remote roles require you to live in a certain country or hold work authorisation there. Check the hours, the pay if shown, and whether the role is a job, a contract, or agency work. Then read the company reviews and check the employer website before you apply.

Step 5: Apply with a short, tailored application

Apply only to roles that match your real skills. For each application, adjust your CV headline and write a short cover note of three paragraphs. Name the employer need, give one relevant result from your past work, and ask for an interview. Change the note for every role, because a generic letter is easy to ignore. Keep a simple list of where you applied, the date, and the reply. Follow up once after about a week, then move on.

Step 6: Reply fast and prepare for interviews

Employers may message you through Indeed after you apply. Reply promptly and proofread every message. Most first interviews happen by phone or video call, so test your camera, microphone and connection first and sit in a quiet room.

Step 7: Agree pay and terms in writing before you start

Before you start, agree the pay rate, pay schedule, currency, payment method and hours in writing. Employees are usually paid through payroll. Contractors invoice and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service, and handle their own tax.

How you get paid

Indeed does not pay you, and it takes no fee from your earnings. The employer pays you directly, so your pay, your pay schedule and your contract all come from the employer.

Employees are usually paid through payroll, with tax taken off where the law requires it. Contractors usually invoice the employer and are paid by bank transfer or a payment service. Ask in the interview how often you will be paid, in which currency, and by which method. Agree it in writing before you start.

There is no escrow on Indeed, so there is no payment protection. If an employer pays late or does not pay, Indeed cannot release your money, because it never held it. That is why the written agreement in Step 7 matters so much.

What it costs and what to expect

Searching, applying and using alerts on Indeed cost you nothing. Your real cost is time. A focused search takes about an hour a day, and a good application takes twenty to thirty minutes to tailor properly.

Example only: suppose you find a part time remote customer support job at an assumed rate of 15 US dollars per hour, and you work an assumed 20 hours per week. One week pays 20 times 15, which is 300 US dollars, and four weeks pay 1,200 US dollars before tax. These figures are assumed for the example. Real pay rates vary by role, employer and country, and many applicants never get hired at all.

Expect a slow start. Most applications get no reply, and that is normal on a large job board. A realistic first month is week one for your profile, CV and saved searches, weeks two and three for five to ten tailored applications per week, and week four for interviews and follow ups. If you apply to 30 roles and hear back from three or four, that is normal. If you hear back from none, improve your CV and cover notes before you apply to more roles.

Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses and platform fees. Tax rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.

Common mistakes

  • Applying to every listing with the same generic CV and cover letter, instead of applying well to fewer roles that fit.
  • Searching with broad words only and never using the date, job type and location filters.
  • Ignoring the location line and wasting time on roles that need work authorisation you do not have.
  • Applying to old listings that are already filled, instead of sorting by date and applying early.
  • Leaving your profile half finished, so employers who search the CV database never find you.
  • Starting work before pay, hours and the payment method are agreed in writing.
  • Treating a job listing as a promise and skipping your own research into the employer.

Risks and scams to watch for

Indeed monitors its platform for fraudulent postings, but scams still reach job seekers, because the board is very large. Learn the warning signs before you apply.

First, never pay to get a job. A real employer will not ask you for an application fee, training costs or equipment money before you start. The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same warning in its job scams advice, and it is worth reading in full.

Second, be careful with offers that arrive too fast. An offer after one short chat, with high pay for simple work and no real interview, is a warning sign. Never cash a cheque from an employer and send part of the money back, because that is a fake cheque scam and the bank will ask you to repay the full amount.

Third, protect your personal details. Do not share bank details, identity documents or tax numbers until you have a written contract with an employer you have verified. Be cautious if an employer insists on speaking only through chat or messaging apps, or uses a personal email address instead of a company one.

Fourth, check the employer yourself. Search the company name plus the words scam, review or complaint. Read the company reviews on Indeed, and check that the same job appears on the company website. If the answers are vague, walk away. Our guide on how to spot online job scams lists the full set of checks.

Frequently asked questions

Is Indeed free for job seekers?

Yes. Searching, applying, uploading a CV and using job alerts on Indeed are free for job seekers as of November 2026. Employers pay Indeed for sponsored postings and other services, and that is how the site makes its money.

Does Indeed pay you directly?

No. Indeed never pays job seekers. The employer who hires you pays your wages or your contract fee directly, usually through payroll or by bank transfer. Indeed takes no fee from your pay and holds no money for you.

Can I find remote and part time work on Indeed?

Yes. Indeed lists full time, part time, temporary, contract and remote roles in many fields. Use the job type and remote filters, sort by date, and read the location line on every listing before you apply.

Is Indeed legit?

Yes. Indeed is a large, established job search site used by employers in many countries. That does not mean every listing is honest, so verify each employer yourself before you share personal details or start work.

How long does it take to get hired through Indeed?

There is no fixed time. Some people hear back within days, and many searches take weeks or months. Your skills, your CV, the number of tailored applications you send and employer demand all affect the result.

Should I use Indeed or a freelance marketplace?

Use Indeed when you want a job or a longer contract with one employer. Use a freelance marketplace when you want short projects with many clients and payment protection. Many people use both at the same time.

Conclusion

Indeed is worth using if you want a real job, part time work or a longer contract, and you are willing to apply with care. It is free, it is large, and employers search its CV database every day. It cannot find you a job, guarantee an interview, or protect your pay, so your profile, your CV and your written agreement do the real work.
