30:T4b53,<h1>How to Earn Money on Twine in 2026</h1>
<p>To earn money on Twine, you need a strong portfolio, a clear skill, and a steady habit of pitching for the right jobs. Twine is a marketplace for creative freelancers, and it suits designers, animators, video editors, writers, and musicians. This guide explains how Twine works, what it costs, how you get paid, and the mistakes to avoid.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-twine">What is Twine?</h2>
<p>Twine is a community and freelance marketplace for creative, digital and marketing freelancers. Clients post projects, browse profiles, and hire people for one off work or for a team. You showcase your work in a portfolio, apply for jobs, chat with the client, and receive payment through Twine or by direct arrangement.</p>
<p>Twine says it has connected companies with creative and tech experts since 2016, from its base in Manchester in the United Kingdom, and that its network covers 1.4 million experts. Treat that number as Twine's own claim, not as a count of active freelancers.</p>
<p>Twine is built around creative work first. If your main skill is design, video, animation, music, audio, writing, or marketing, Twine fits your work. If not, you may find fewer jobs. See our <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms" rel="noopener" target="_blank">EarnQuill earning platforms guide</a> to compare platforms.</p>

<p>Twine follows a simple flow. You create a profile, you show your work, you apply for jobs, you agree terms, and you get paid.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-your-account-and-profile">Step 1: Create your account and profile</h3>
<p>Twine is an open platform, and the Help Centre says there is no application process to join. Freelancers of all skill levels are welcome. That open door helps beginners, and it also means your profile must prove your skill.</p>
<p>Add a clear photo, a short bio, and your main skill in plain words. Say what you do and who you do it for.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-build-a-portfolio-that-shows-real-work">Step 2: Build a portfolio that shows real work</h3>
<p>Your portfolio is the main reason a client will pick you. The Help Centre says you should add relevant examples of your work before you apply. A portfolio with no work, or with weak work, will struggle even if your pitch is good.</p>
<p>Choose six to ten of your best pieces that match the jobs you want. If you want logo work, show logos. If you want video work, show finished videos. If you have no client work yet, make three or four sample projects, such as work for a cafe or a small clothing brand, and label sample work as sample work. For each piece, add a two sentence note: what the brief was, and what you made. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-graphic-designer" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a freelance graphic designer</a> can help, and our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-motion-graphics-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn motion graphics online</a> can help you add motion skills.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/twine-creative-freelance.jpg" alt="A freelance animator working on a project, with a storyboard and a screen showing animation work" /></p>
<p>Keep your portfolio fresh. Add your best new work, and remove older work that no longer shows your current skill. Ask a client for permission before you share work you made for them.</p>
<h3 id="step-3-find-jobs-that-fit-your-skill">Step 3: Find jobs that fit your skill</h3>
<p>Twine offers two ways to find work. You can browse jobs posted on Twine and apply, and you can also use Ari, Twine's job assistant. Twine says Ari searches over 10 million jobs a month and brings you the ones that fit your profile. Ari searches Twine jobs and other job boards and company career pages.</p>
<p>Start with Twine jobs first. Read each brief in full. Check the skill, the deadline, the deliverables, and the budget if one is shown. Some clients do not show a budget, so ask about pay before you start work. Focus on jobs where your portfolio proves you can do the work, and apply early, because jobs often receive many pitches.</p>
<h3 id="step-4-write-a-pitch-that-answers-the-brief">Step 4: Write a pitch that answers the brief</h3>
<p>A good pitch is short, specific, and focused on the client. Start with one sentence that shows you read the brief. Then link to one or two portfolio pieces that match the job. Then say what you will deliver, when you can deliver it, and what you charge.</p>
<p>A pitch of 120 to 200 words is often enough. Do not copy the same pitch for every job. Change the first line, the portfolio links, and the price or timeline to fit the brief. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/freelancing/how-to-work-as-a-freelance-copywriter" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to work as a freelance copywriter</a> covers clear writing, and our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/ai-earning/how-to-offer-ai-video-editing-services" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to offer AI video editing services</a> can help video editors stand out.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-agree-terms-do-the-work-and-get-paid">Step 5: Agree terms, do the work, and get paid</h3>
<p>When a client replies, agree the scope in writing before you start. Scope means what you will deliver, how many revisions are included, when the work is due, and what you will be paid. Ask for the pay terms in the same message. If the client will pay through Twine Vault, ask how much will be held as a deposit, and when the rest will be released.</p>
<p>Update the client when you start and when you have a draft ready. If a deadline slips, tell the client early and give a new date. Send the final files only after the payment terms are met. Keep a copy of the brief, the agreed price, and the messages about payment.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Twine is free to join, and you can create a portfolio and apply for some jobs on the free plan. The Help Centre, updated on 11 November 2026, says a free account is topped up with one application per day. Once that limit is reached on a job, only Twine Pro members can pitch for it.</p>
<p>Twine Pro is an optional paid plan. The Help Centre says it costs from $9.49 per month when billed yearly, or $18.99 per month when billed monthly. It gives you up to 50 pitches per month, direct contact with clients, priority placement in client searches, and no transaction fees on payouts.</p>
<p>An older Twine blog post lists Twine Pro at $13.99 per month, or £11.99, which does not match the current Help Centre figure, so use the Help Centre price and check the pricing page before you subscribe.</p>
<h3 id="how-twine-vault-works">How Twine Vault works</h3>
<p>Twine Vault is Twine's payment protection system. When a client pays through Vault, Twine holds the funds until the work is done and the client releases them.</p>
<p>The commission for Vault payments is paid by the client, not by the freelancer. Twine's commission update, published on 14 March 2025 and updated on 31 March 2025, says the client pays 10 percent on Vault payments up to $10,000, and 5 percent over $10,000. Posting a job is free.</p>
<p>Freelancers do not pay that commission. The same Twine update says freelancers pay only transaction fees on some Vault payments, set by the payment provider, and the fee can be as low as 0 percent for some methods. Twine Pro members pay no transaction fees at all. If you bill a client directly outside Twine, you pay no Twine transaction fee, but you also lose Vault protection.</p>

<h3 id="how-you-get-paid">How you get paid</h3>
<p>Twine lists these payout methods: US bank transfer, UK bank transfer, EU bank transfer, international wire transfer, PayPal, and Payoneer. The method that works for you depends on your country and your bank, and PayPal does not operate in every country.</p>
<p>The Help Centre does not give a clear minimum payout amount or a payout time in the pages read for this guide, and that fact could not be verified on 23 November 2026. Ask Twine support, or read the payout page in the Help Centre, before you plan your cash flow around a Twine payment.</p>
<h3 id="worked-example-what-a-project-can-be-worth">Worked example: what a project can be worth</h3>
<p>Example only. This example uses simple numbers to show the maths. It is not a promise of income, and your real pay will depend on your skill, your price, and whether you win the job.</p>
<p>Imagine you quote $500 for a logo and brand pack. The client agrees to pay through Twine Vault. The client pays the Vault commission on top of your quote. The maths is: 10 percent of $500 is $50, so the client pays $550 in total. You receive $500 before any payout transaction fee. If you are a Twine Pro member, Twine says you pay no transaction fee.</p>
<p>Now imagine you win two projects like that in one month. Your gross pay is $500 plus $500, which is $1,000. If you pay for Twine Pro at $18.99 per month, your pay before transaction fees and tax is $1,000 minus $18.99, which is $981.01.</p>

<h2 id="the-downsides-of-twine">The downsides of Twine</h2>
<p>Twine has real limits. The first limit is the free plan. One application per day is a slow pace if you are looking for work full time. Twine Pro removes that limit in part, but it costs money before you have earned anything.</p>
<p>The second limit is competition. Twine is open to freelancers of all skill levels, and many people can apply for the same job. Clients often pick people with reviews and a strong portfolio, so new freelancers may need to apply for many jobs before they win one.</p>
<p>The third limit is pay clarity. Some clients do not show a budget, so ask about pay early, and do not start work until you have agreed a price in writing.</p>
<p>The fourth limit is that Twine does not promise work. Your results will vary with your experience, location, demand, time, competition, expenses, and platform fees. Treat Twine as one channel, not as your only source of income. Direct payment has no Twine commission, but it has no Vault protection either.</p>
<h2 id="who-twine-suits-and-who-it-does-not-suit">Who Twine suits, and who it does not suit</h2>
<p>Twine suits creative freelancers who have a portfolio and a clear skill, such as designers, video editors, animators, illustrators, writers, marketers, voice artists, and music makers. It suits people who can work in English with international clients, reply fast, and meet deadlines, and who want project work, not a full time job.</p>
<p>Twine suits you less well if you have no portfolio yet, or if your skill is not creative, digital, or marketing work. It also suits you less well if you need steady work at once, or if you cannot afford to spend time pitching without pay. In that case, build your portfolio first, or use a broader platform alongside Twine.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>You apply for every job, even when your portfolio does not prove the skill, so your pitches look generic.</li>
<li>You send the same copied pitch to every client, so no client feels that you read their brief.</li>
<li>You start work before you agree the price, the scope, and the number of revisions in writing.</li>
<li>You accept direct payment from a new client to save on fees, and you lose the protection that Twine Vault gives you.</li>
<li>You pay for Twine Pro before you have a strong portfolio, so the extra pitches do not lead to more hires.</li>
<li>You let your portfolio go stale, so clients see old work that no longer shows your current skill.</li>
<li>You quote a price that is too low to cover your time, your tools, and your tax.</li>
<li>You ignore client messages for a day or more, so the client hires someone who replied faster.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Twine is a real platform, but that does not mean every job on Twine is real. Fake clients can appear on any marketplace, so check each job with care before you apply or start work.</p>
<p>Never pay money to get a job. A real client pays you for work, not the other way round. If a client asks you to pay a fee, buy a tool, or send money before you start, stop and report the job to Twine. Never share your bank login, your password, or a code sent to your phone.</p>
<p>Be careful with jobs that ask you to move off Twine at once. That is not a scam by itself, because some clients prefer email or phone. It becomes a risk when a new client asks you to move off the platform before you have agreed terms, and then asks you to start work without a Vault payment. For a new client, keep the first payment inside Twine Vault if you can.</p>
<p>Check the brief for signs of fake work. A brief with no clear deliverable, no timeline, and no budget is a warning sign. A job that promises high pay for very little work is also a warning sign. If a client asks you to work for free as a test, ask for a small paid test instead.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-twine-legit-for-freelancers">Is Twine legit for freelancers?</h3>
<p>Yes, Twine is a real freelance marketplace for creative, digital and marketing work. Twine says it has connected companies with experts since 2016. You still need to check each job yourself, because fake clients can appear on any marketplace, and Twine does not promise that you will be hired.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-earn-money-on-twine">How do I earn money on Twine?</h3>
<p>You earn money on Twine by building a portfolio, applying for jobs that fit your skill, agreeing a price with the client, doing the work, and getting paid through Twine Vault or by direct payment. You set your own quote for each job. There is no fixed rate, and your pay depends on the project, your skill, and whether the client hires you.</p>
<h3 id="what-does-twine-charge-freelancers">What does Twine charge freelancers?</h3>
<p>Twine charges no commission to freelancers on Vault payments. The client pays 10 percent commission on Vault payments up to $10,000, and 5 percent on Vault payments over $10,000. You may pay a small transaction fee when you receive a payout, depending on your payout method. Twine Pro members pay no transaction fees, according to the Twine Help Centre.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-get-paid-on-twine">How do I get paid on Twine?</h3>
<p>Twine offers US, UK and EU bank transfer, international wire transfer, PayPal, and Payoneer. When a client pays through Twine Vault, Twine holds the funds until the work is done and the client releases them. The minimum payout and the payout timing could not be verified on the official pages read on 23 November 2026, so check the Help Centre before you rely on a payout date.</p>
<h3 id="is-twine-pro-worth-it">Is Twine Pro worth it?</h3>
<p>Twine Pro can be worth it if you have a strong portfolio and you apply for jobs often. It gives you up to 50 pitches per month, direct contact with clients, and no payout transaction fees, for $9.49 per month billed yearly, or $18.99 monthly. It is less useful if your portfolio is weak, because more pitches will not fix a weak portfolio.</p>
<h3 id="can-beginners-earn-money-on-twine">Can beginners earn money on Twine?</h3>
<p>Beginners can join Twine, because the Help Centre says there is no application process and freelancers of all skill levels are welcome. A beginner still needs a portfolio, even if it is made of sample projects. Start with small jobs, build reviews, and improve your portfolio with each project.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>To earn money on Twine, start with a focused portfolio, apply for jobs that fit your skill, and agree the price and scope in writing before you start. Use Twine Vault for new clients, and check the fee and payout pages before you rely on a payment date. Twine is a useful channel, but it is not a promise of work, and it works best alongside other ways to find clients.</p>
