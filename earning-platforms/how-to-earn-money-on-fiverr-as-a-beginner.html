How to Earn Money on Fiverr as a Beginner

Fiverr is one of the easiest places to earn your first money online — if you set up your profile and gigs the right way. Here is the full beginner process, from choosing a service to getting your first order.

What Fiverr is and how it works

Fiverr is a marketplace where freelancers sell services — called gigs — and buyers come to find them. Instead of applying for jobs, you list what you can do, set a price, and buyers order from you. Services range from writing and graphic design to voiceovers, video editing, data entry, and virtual assistance.

When a buyer orders, Fiverr holds the payment. You deliver the work, the buyer accepts it (or asks for a revision), and Fiverr releases the money to you after a clearance period. Fiverr takes a 20% commission on each order, so a $50 order earns you $40. Factor that into your pricing from the start.

What beginners can sell on Fiverr

You do not need ten years of experience. Beginners do well with services that are quick to learn and easy to deliver:

  • Writing: product descriptions, social media captions, blog outlines, proofreading.
  • Design: social media posts, simple logos, presentation slides, using tools like Canva.
  • Admin help: data entry, spreadsheets, research, virtual assistance.
  • Audio and video: voiceovers, podcast editing, short video captions.
  • Simple tech tasks: WordPress fixes, spreadsheet formulas, file conversions.

Pick one service you can already do reasonably well, or one you can learn in a week of focused practice. Trying to sell ten unrelated services at once is the most common beginner mistake.

Setting up a profile buyers trust

Buyers choose sellers they trust. A strong profile has:

  • A real photo of you, or a clean professional logo — not a stock image.
  • A one-line description that says what you do and who it is for, for example "I write clear product descriptions for online shops".
  • A short bio that mentions your skills, the tools you use, and how fast you reply.
  • Your language skills and time zone, so buyers know when to expect answers.

Fill in every section. Empty profiles look unfinished, and buyers skip them.

Creating your first gig

A gig is your shop listing. Each one needs:

  • A clear title that says the service and the result, for example "I will write 5 product descriptions for your online store".
  • A description that explains exactly what the buyer gets, how many revisions are included, and what you need from them to start.
  • A cover image that shows the work, not just text on a coloured background. If you have no past work yet, create one or two sample pieces to show.
  • Delivery time you can actually meet. A 2-day delivery you hit is better than a 1-day delivery you miss.

Fiverr lets you create several gigs, but start with one or two and make them good.

Pricing your first gigs

New sellers usually start low to win their first orders and reviews. That does not mean working for almost nothing — it means pricing below the established sellers in your category while you build a track record.

A practical approach: set your basic package at a price that feels slightly low but still worth your time, and offer standard and premium packages with more work for more money. As reviews arrive, raise your prices gradually. Sellers who never raise prices stay stuck at beginner rates.

Getting your first order

Fiverr's search favours gigs that are complete, active, and responsive. To get your first order:

  • Stay online and reply to messages quickly — response time matters for search ranking.
  • Share your gig where buyers already gather: relevant social media groups, forums, and your own profiles.
  • Consider the "buyer requests" section, where buyers post jobs they need done and sellers can offer their services.
  • Ask a past client or a friend who genuinely used your service to leave an honest review after a real order — never buy fake reviews, which can get your account banned.

Your first few orders are the hardest. After five to ten completed orders with good reviews, new orders start coming in more easily.

Fiverr fees and realistic expectations

Be honest with yourself about the numbers. Fiverr takes 20% of every order. Withdrawal options and their fees vary by country and method, so check what applies to you before you need the money. Payments take time to clear after delivery.

Fiverr will not make you rich in a week. Most beginners earn small amounts at first — a few orders a month — and grow from there. What it does give you is a place to earn your first money online, build a portfolio of real client work, and learn what buyers actually pay for.

Growing beyond your first orders

Fiverr has seller levels. New sellers can progress to Level One, Level Two, and Top Rated by completing orders on time, keeping high ratings, and staying responsive. Higher levels unlock more gigs, better placement in search, and extra tools.

To grow: deliver on time, communicate clearly, ask satisfied buyers for a review, and keep improving the service itself. One happy buyer who returns every month is worth far more than ten one-off orders.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Copying another seller's gig text word for word. Buyers notice, and Fiverr can remove copied listings.
  • Accepting work you cannot do, then delivering late or badly. One bad review early on is hard to recover from.
  • Ignoring messages for days. Slow replies push your gig down in search.
  • Underpricing permanently. Low prices win first orders, but raise them as your reviews grow.
  • Treating Fiverr as the whole business. Use it to find clients and build a reputation — and keep your own portfolio and client list outside the platform too.

Is Fiverr worth it for a beginner?

If you have a service people will pay for and you are willing to start small, yes. It is one of the few places where a complete beginner can get a first paying client without a portfolio, a website, or a professional network. The trade-off is the 20% commission and the competition. Go in with realistic expectations, deliver good work, and treat your first ten orders as the foundation — not the finish line.
