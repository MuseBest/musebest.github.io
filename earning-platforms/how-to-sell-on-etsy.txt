39:T57d0,<h2 id="how-to-sell-on-etsy-in-2026">How to Sell on Etsy in 2026</h2>
<p>Learning how to sell on Etsy starts with one honest fact. Etsy has millions of sellers, so a new shop is invisible at first. Buyers do not find you because you opened a shop. They find you because your listings match what they search for, your photos earn a click, and your first buyers leave good reviews. This guide shows you how to open your shop, list products buyers can find, price them so the fees leave you a profit, and turn your first sales into a shop that grows.</p>

<h3 id="what-you-can-sell-on-etsy">What you can sell on Etsy</h3>
<p>Etsy is a marketplace for handmade, vintage, and craft supplies, plus digital products. That focus is what makes it different from a general marketplace. Buyers come to Etsy for things that feel personal, handmade, or hard to find elsewhere.</p>
<p>Etsy's Creativity Standards, tightened in June 2025, set out what qualifies. Your items must fall into one of four categories: made by you, designed by you, handpicked by you (for vintage and some supplies), or sourced by you. Each listing must show a human touch in how it was made, designed, or chosen. You cannot resell generic commercial goods as if they were handmade. If you buy plain items and add your own design, say so clearly. If you work with a production partner, disclose it in your listing. Etsy checks listings against these standards, and shops that break them risk removal.</p>
<p>Digital products are a separate route worth knowing about. A printable, a template, or a digital planner needs no shipping, no stock, and no postage. The buyer downloads it at once, and you can sell the same file many times. The listing still costs 0.20 dollars, and the transaction fee still applies, but you skip packing and posting entirely. For a fuller guide to this route, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-sell-printables-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to sell printables online</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-create-your-first-digital-product" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to create your first digital product</a>.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-create-your-account-and-open-your-shop">Step 1: Create your account and open your shop</h3>
<p>You must have an Etsy account before you can open a shop, and you must set up the shop using a desktop web browser. Go to Etsy.com/sell and select Get started. Answer a few questions about your business experience, then choose your shop language, country, and currency. Pick your local currency where you can, because it makes your bookkeeping simpler. Note that you cannot change your shop language later, so choose carefully.</p>
<p>Choose your shop name next. Etsy's own Help Center lists the rules: the name must be 4 to 20 characters long, with no spaces or special characters, no profanity, not already in use by another member, and it must not infringe on another seller's trademark. Pick a name that fits what you sell and that you will not outgrow. A name tied to one product, such as Christmas mugs, will box you in if you later sell more. If your first choice is taken, Etsy suggests available alternatives. You can change your name later from the Shop Name tab, but it is easier to choose well now.</p>
<p>Etsy may charge a one-time set-up fee when you open a shop, and the amount varies by location. The official policy says the fee "may be required" and is shown during setup. Some guides quote 29 dollars as a US figure, but that is not a fixed global fee, so do not budget around it. Check what Etsy shows you during setup.</p>
<p>You will also verify your identity, usually with a government ID, and set up two-factor authentication. Etsy will not open an empty shop, so you need at least one live listing to finish. Prepare your first listings before you start the setup flow, so you can open the shop in one sitting.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-write-your-shop-policies-and-about-section">Step 2: Write your shop policies and About section</h3>
<p>Policies are not a formality. They tell buyers how you work, and they protect you when something goes wrong. Write them in plain words before your first sale.</p>
<p>Cover shipping first: how long you take to dispatch, which service you use, and whether tracking is included. Cover returns next: whether you accept them, in what time frame, and who pays the return postage. Cover custom orders if you offer them: how a buyer asks, how long they take, and whether they cost extra. Cover digital products separately if you sell them: the buyer gets a download, not a posted item, and downloads cannot be returned once accessed.</p>
<p>Use the About section to tell your story in a few short paragraphs. Say what you make, how you make it, and why you started. Add a photo of your workspace or your hands at work. Buyers on Etsy care about the maker, and a real story builds trust that a bare shop cannot. Keep it honest. Do not claim handmade work you did not make, and do not copy another seller's story.</p>
<h3 id="step-3-create-listings-that-sell">Step 3: Create listings that sell</h3>
<p>A listing is your product page, and it has four parts that decide whether a buyer clicks and buys: photos, title, description, and price. Get the photos right first, because buyers decide in seconds.</p>
<p>Take bright, clean photos on a plain background, with natural light where you can. Show the product from several angles, show it in use, and show its size beside a familiar object, such as a hand or a cup. A buyer cannot hold your product, so your photos must do that job. Take your own photos. Never copy another seller's images, and never use stock photos of a product you did not make.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/etsy-listing-photos.jpg" alt="Bright, clean product photos of handmade goods arranged on a light background, with a camera beside them" /></p>
<p>Write the title with the words buyers search for first. Put the most important words at the start, such as the product, the material, and who it is for. A title like "Hand-poured soy candle, lavender scent, gift for her" tells a buyer and the search engine what the item is. A title like "My beautiful candle, made with love" tells them nothing.</p>
<p>Write the description for a buyer who is deciding, not for a search engine. Open with what the item is and who it suits. Then give the facts: size, weight, materials, colours, and what is included. Then say how it is made, how it is packaged, and how long dispatch takes. Use all 13 tags Etsy gives you, with phrases buyers actually search for, and fill in the attributes Etsy offers, such as colour, material, and occasion. Tags and attributes are how Etsy search finds your listing, so do not waste them on words nobody searches for. For photo skills, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-photo-editing-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn photo editing online</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-skills/how-to-learn-graphic-design-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to learn graphic design online</a>.</p>

<p>Price your product to cover materials, your time, and all the fees. Add the listing fee, the transaction fee on the price plus shipping, and the processing fee. Then add a margin for your time. If you price only to cover materials, you work for free.</p>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine you sell a handmade item for 40 dollars, with 6 dollars shipping. The buyer pays 46 dollars. The transaction fee is 6.5 percent of 46 dollars, which is 2.99 dollars. The listing fee is 0.20 dollars. Recheck this maths yourself, because your shipping and your country's processing fee will differ.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-set-up-payments-and-billing">Step 5: Set up payments and billing</h3>
<p>Etsy pays sellers through Etsy Payments, which lets buyers pay by card, PayPal, or digital wallets in their own currency. You will link a bank account so Etsy can send you your money. Set this up during shop creation, and check that the account details are correct, because a wrong account delays your payout.</p>
<p>Etsy deducts its fees from your sales automatically, so you do not pay the listing fee or the transaction fee separately. Your payout is the sale price minus the fees, sent to your bank on Etsy's payout schedule. Payout timing varies by country, so check the schedule shown in your Shop Manager. Currency conversion applies if your bank account is in a different currency from your shop, and the conversion cost comes out of your payout.</p>
<p>Set up a billing method for the fees you owe, usually a card. Etsy charges listing fees and other costs to that card when your sales do not cover them. Keep a card on file that will not expire mid-month, and check your payment account each week in your first months, so no fee surprises you.</p>
<h3 id="step-6-launch-and-get-found-in-etsy-search">Step 6: Launch and get found in Etsy search</h3>
<p>Etsy search decides which listings buyers see. The ranking factors are not a mystery, but no seller controls all of them. Focus on the ones you can control.</p>
<p>Relevance comes first. Your title, tags, and attributes should match the words buyers type. Use Etsy's own search bar to test this: type a word, and watch the suggestions that appear. Those suggestions are what real buyers search for. Listing quality matters next. Listings with clear photos, complete descriptions, and fair prices rank better than thin listings. Recency helps too, so a new listing gets a short boost. Shipping price matters: listings with low or free shipping rank better, but only offer free shipping if your price still covers it. Shop performance matters most over time: fast replies, good reviews, and on-time shipping all lift your shop.</p>
<p>Aim for 10 to 15 listings before you judge your shop. A shop with three listings gives buyers little reason to stay and gives search little to rank. A shop with 15 listings covers more search terms and looks like a real shop. Build those listings in your first two weeks, not over two months.</p>
<h3 id="getting-your-first-sale">Getting your first sale</h3>
<p>Expect a slow first few weeks. A new shop has no reviews, no sales history, and no search ranking, so your first sale usually comes from promotion outside Etsy, not from search. Share your listings where your buyers already spend time, such as Pinterest, Instagram, or a craft group that allows it. Tell friends and family the shop is open, and ask them to share it, not to buy out of kindness. A real buyer who finds you through a share is worth more than a pity purchase.</p>
<p>Etsy Ads can help, but keep the budget small. A budget of 1 to 3 dollars a day is enough to test which listings get clicks. Watch which listings earn clicks and which earn sales, and stop advertising the ones that only eat money. The Share and Save programme gives you a small discount on the transaction fee when a buyer reaches your shop through a link you shared, so use your own links when you promote.</p>
<p>Do not pay for fake reviews, and do not ask friends to leave reviews for items they did not buy. Etsy detects fake reviews, and the damage lasts far longer than the short boost. One honest review from a real buyer is worth more than ten fake ones.</p>
<h3 id="after-the-first-sale-shipping-reviews-and-returns">After the first sale: shipping, reviews, and returns</h3>
<p>Ship on time, every time. Pack the item well, include tracking where you can, and send the buyer a short message when it is on its way. A small thank-you note in the parcel costs almost nothing and earns goodwill. Buyers can leave a review for 100 days after purchase, so good service has a long window to pay off.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/etsy-first-sale.jpg" alt="A small parcel wrapped for shipping on a table, with a thank-you card and a laptop showing a new sale" /></p>
<p>When a review arrives, thank the buyer. When a problem arrives, fix it fast. Answer messages within a day. If an item arrives damaged, offer a replacement or a refund promptly, and do not argue about whose fault it was. If a buyer asks for a return, follow the policy you wrote in Step 2, and handle it politely even when you disagree.</p>
<h3 id="growing-the-shop">Growing the shop</h3>
<p>Grow by adding listings, not by adding gimmicks. A shop with 30 listings earns more search traffic than a shop with 10, because each listing is a new chance to be found. Mix evergreen products that sell all year with seasonal products that spike at holidays. Watch which listings sell, and make more of what works, in new colours or sizes.</p>
<p>Aim for the Star Seller badge as a growth target. Etsy awards it to shops with fast replies, high ratings, on-time shipping with tracking, and a steady sales record. The exact requirements are listed on Etsy's Star Seller page, so check them there. The badge tells buyers your shop is reliable, and it is worth working toward once you have your first sales.</p>
<p>Etsy Plus, at 10 dollars a month, gives you 15 listing credits and 5 dollars in Etsy Ads credits, plus extra shop tools. It is worth trying once your shop earns regularly, but not before. Do not pay for it in your first month, when your money is better spent on materials and photos.</p>
<h3 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Opening with too few listings, which gives buyers little reason to stay and search little to rank.</li>
<li>Using poor photos, such as dark, blurry, or cluttered images that hide the product.</li>
<li>Writing titles that say nothing buyers search for, such as "my beautiful creation".</li>
<li>Ignoring the fees, and pricing only to cover materials, so you work for free.</li>
<li>Copying another seller's photos, titles, or designs, which breaks Etsy's rules and earns complaints.</li>
<li>Offering free shipping without raising the price to cover it.</li>
<li>Leaving Offsite Ads on without understanding the 15 percent fee.</li>
<li>Expecting sales in the first week, and giving up before the shop has had time to rank.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="how-to-sell-on-etsy-what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">How to sell on Etsy: what it costs and what to expect</h3>
<p>Starting an Etsy shop costs very little. A few listings at 0.20 dollars each, plus a set-up fee that varies by location, means you can open a shop for under 20 dollars. Your real costs are materials, your time, packaging, postage, and the fees on each sale. There is no monthly charge unless you choose Etsy Plus.</p>
<p>Expect a slow first few weeks. Your first sale may take days or weeks, and that is normal. Your first month is for building listings, learning what buyers search for, and improving your photos. Your second and third months are for earning your first reviews and finding which products sell. Nobody can promise you a monthly income figure, because earnings depend on your products, your prices, your photos, and how much time you give it.</p>
<p>Tax rules differ by country, and Etsy reports seller income in some countries. Keep records of every sale, fee, and cost from day one, and talk to a qualified accountant if you are unsure how your income is taxed. For the wider picture of the platform, read our <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/etsy-seller-review" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Etsy seller review</a> and <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-choose-earning-platform" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose an earning platform</a>.</p>
<h3 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>How much does it cost to sell on Etsy?</h4>
<p>It costs 0.20 dollars to list an item, and the listing lasts four months. When you sell, Etsy takes a 6.5 percent transaction fee on the price plus shipping and gift wrapping, plus a payment processing fee that varies by country. A one-time set-up fee may apply, and the amount varies by location. You can open a shop with a few listings for under 20 dollars, as of November 2026.</p>
<h4>Do I need a business licence to sell on Etsy?</h4>
<p>Etsy does not require a business licence to open a shop, but your country or local area may require one, and tax rules differ by place. Check your local rules before you sell, and talk to a qualified accountant if you are unsure. If you sell as a business rather than as an individual, say so in your shop settings, because it affects how Etsy handles your payments and taxes.</p>
<h4>How long does it take to make my first sale on Etsy?</h4>
<p>It usually takes days to weeks, and sometimes longer. A new shop has no reviews and no search ranking, so the first sale often comes from promotion outside Etsy rather than from search. Shops with 10 to 15 listings, clear photos, and prices that cover the fees tend to sell sooner than shops with three listings and weak photos. Be patient, and keep adding listings while you wait.</p>
<h4>Can I sell digital products on Etsy?</h4>
<p>Yes. Digital products such as printables, templates, and planners are allowed on Etsy, and they need no shipping or stock. The buyer downloads the file at once, and you can sell the same file many times. The listing fee and the transaction fee still apply. Say clearly in the listing that the product is a digital download, and that no physical item will be posted.</p>
<h4>Is selling on Etsy worth it?</h4>
<p>Etsy is worth trying if you make handmade, vintage, or digital products that fit its marketplace, and you have patience for a slow start. It gives you access to millions of buyers who already shop for handmade goods, and it costs very little to start. It is less suited to sellers of generic commercial goods, or to anyone who wants instant sales without building listings and reviews.</p>
<h4>What sells best on Etsy?</h4>
<p>What sells best changes with trends and seasons, so no honest guide can name a fixed list. Evergreen categories such as personalised gifts, home decor, jewellery, and craft supplies sell steadily, while seasonal items spike at holidays. The safest choice is a product you can make well, in a niche with real buyers, rather than chasing a trend you saw in a headline.</p>
<h3 id="conclusion-open-the-shop-list-well-and-earn-the-first-review">Conclusion: open the shop, list well, and earn the first review</h3>
<p>Selling on Etsy comes down to a simple method. Open your shop with a clear name and honest policies, create listings with bright photos and titles buyers actually search for, price them to cover the 6.5 percent fee and your time, and promote your first listings outside Etsy while search ranking builds. Then ship on time, answer fast, and let each good review make the next sale easier. Start today by photographing your best product and writing its listing.</p>
