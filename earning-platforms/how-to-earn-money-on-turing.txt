<h2 id="what-turing-is-and-what-it-is-not">What Turing is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>Turing began in 2018 as a marketplace for remote software engineers, and it now also supplies coding and reasoning data to AI labs. Its platform runs at work.turing.com, where you make one profile and apply to listings one at a time. Most listings are full time contracts, typically 40 hours a week, with overlap with US Pacific time. Turing says all work is paid, with no unpaid trials, as of November 2026.</p>
<p>Turing is not a freelance marketplace. On Turing, you apply to a specific listing, state your hourly rate, take the required tests, and work on one engagement at a time. Turing charges no joining fee. Its model is a margin between client pay and your rate, which it does not publish.</p>
<p>You work as an independent contractor, so do not expect employee benefits such as paid leave. Contracts are fixed term and can end when client needs change. Passing the tests does not guarantee a project.</p>
<h2 id="who-turing-suits-and-who-it-does-not">Who Turing suits, and who it does not</h2>
<p>Turing suits experienced software engineers and STEM specialists who can pass timed, proctored tests. STEM means science, technology, engineering and mathematics. Roles also exist for experts in medicine, finance, law and languages. Clear written English matters, because much of the work involves explaining reasoning in writing.</p>
<p>Turing does not suit beginners, generalists looking for small tasks, or people who need quick income. Vetting is selective, reviews can take weeks, and applicants who are not picked often hear nothing. If you want short projects with payment protection, a freelance marketplace will suit you better. If you want vetted developer work with a different model, compare <a href="https://earnquill.com/earning-platforms/how-to-earn-money-on-toptal" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to earn money on Toptal</a>.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-earn-money-on-turing-in-7-steps">How to earn money on Turing in 7 steps</h2>
<p>Follow these steps in order. Set aside a quiet day for the tests, because they are timed and recorded.</p>
<h3 id="step-1-check-that-you-meet-the-basic-requirements">Step 1: Check that you meet the basic requirements</h3>
<p>You must be 18 or over, with a desktop or laptop, a webcam and a stable internet connection, because phones cannot take the tests. You need a valid physical passport or driving licence for identity checks, and a bank account in your own name. Country eligibility is set listing by listing, so read the location line on each listing. Never use a VPN to get around a country restriction.</p>
<h3 id="step-2-create-your-account-and-submit-your-profile">Step 2: Create your account and submit your profile</h3>
<p>Create an account at work.turing.com and upload your CV in English. Complete your details, education, skills and experience, then press Submit Profile, because you cannot apply until your profile is submitted. List only skills you can be tested on, and keep to one account.</p>
<h3 id="step-3-apply-to-one-listing-and-state-your-hourly-rate">Step 3: Apply to one listing and state your hourly rate</h3>
<p>Filter listings by skill or category, and read the hours and time zone overlap before you apply. Choose Apply Now and complete the short form, which includes your hourly rate. Decide your rate first, because the form cannot be changed after you submit. Pay is often shared later, not in the listing, so confirm the rate before you onboard.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-turing-test.jpg" alt="A person taking an online technical assessment on a laptop with a webcam in a quiet room" /></p>
<h3 id="step-4-take-the-proctored-assessments">Step 4: Take the proctored assessments</h3>
<p>Most listings require a proctored assessment, a timed test with your screen, webcam and microphone recorded. Turn off extensions and VPNs, share your entire screen, and use a connection of at least 4 Mbps. A finished assessment cannot be retaken, and if you fail you must wait 90 days. Applications are not reviewed until every assessment is done.</p>
<h3 id="step-5-complete-interviews-and-wait-for-review">Step 5: Complete interviews and wait for review</h3>
<p>Some roles add an AI video interview or a live interview. Treat the AI interview as a single attempt. Turing says reviews take 48 hours to six weeks, and only people moving forward are contacted. Shortlisted does not guarantee selection.</p>
<h3 id="step-6-confirm-terms-and-onboard">Step 6: Confirm terms and onboard</h3>
<p>If Turing contacts you, confirm your rate, availability, start date, hours and time zone overlap. Onboarding starts only once the client chooses you, and includes terms, ID verification, a tax form and payment details. Some projects onboard through Deel.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ep31-turing-work.jpg" alt="A data specialist reviewing charts and code on two monitors at a home office desk with headphones on" /></p>
<h3 id="step-7-log-hours-accurately-and-protect-your-quality-scores">Step 7: Log hours accurately and protect your quality scores</h3>
<p>Read the project guidelines end to end and log your hours accurately, because pay follows approved hours. Turing treats misuse of AI tools and falsified work as grounds for removal, which can mean no pay for the work concerned and a permanent ban.</p>

<h2 id="how-you-get-paid">How you get paid</h2>
<p>Turing pays contractors monthly for manager approved hours. Its official <a href="https://help.turing.com/articles/9695053382-how-to-get-paid" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to get paid help page</a> says payments are processed on the 5th calendar day of each month, bank processing typically takes 2 to 3 days after payment is sent, and funds may take up to 72 business hours to appear. Other Turing help pages describe pay by the 5th business day of the next month into a wallet or bank account, and some projects pay through Deel. Because pages differ, check the pay cycle in your own contract during onboarding.</p>
<p>Bank transfer is the supported withdrawal method, by local bank transfer or SWIFT. PayPal and Airtm are not supported for withdrawals, according to the help page checked as of November 2026. Your bank account must be in your own name and match the full legal name on your Turing profile. As an independent contractor, you are responsible for taxes in your country, and you do not pay taxes to Turing.</p>
<p>Turing charges you no explicit fee to join or apply. The margin between client billing and your rate is built into client pricing and is not disclosed. Some contractors report late or stuck withdrawals, so keep records of your hours and approvals, and contact support through official channels if pay is late.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Joining, applying and taking assessments on Turing cost you nothing in fees. Your real costs are time, equipment and patience. You need a reliable computer, stable internet and a quiet place for timed tests.</p>
<p>Example only: suppose you are selected for a contract at an assumed rate of 25 US dollars per hour, and you work an assumed 40 hours per week for four weeks. That is 160 hours times 25, which is 4,000 US dollars before tax and transfer fees. These figures are assumed for the example. Real rates vary by project, role and location, and many applicants never get selected at all.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. A realistic first month is week one for your profile and first application, week two for assessments, and weeks three and four for review and interviews. Reviews can take up to six weeks, and you may hear nothing if you are not picked. Passing tests does not guarantee paid work, and contracts can end early when client needs change, so do not quit other income until you have a signed agreement and a first payment.</p>
<p>Results vary with your experience, your location, demand for your skills, the time you spend, competition, your expenses and platform fees. Tax rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant.</p>

<li>Creating a second account, which causes identity verification to fail.</li>

<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Turing is a real company with a public job board and help centre, but its name is misused by scammers, and its contractor model carries real risks. Learn the warning signs before you apply.</p>
<p>First, never pay to get work. Genuine Turing roles are listed on work.turing.com and handled through your Turing account. Treat any request for a fee, or a recruiter who works only over WhatsApp or Telegram, as a red flag. The US Federal Trade Commission gives the same warning in its <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">job scams advice</a>.</p>
<p>Second, verify every offer. Turing does message people on WhatsApp in some flows, so check any offer against your account at work.turing.com before you share documents or bank details. Onboarding mail should come from official Turing addresses, and support runs through official channels.</p>
<p>Third, protect your identity and your account. Never use a VPN or another person identity to bypass country rules, because accounts caught doing so are closed, often with unpaid earnings. Keep your profile name, date of birth and bank account name consistent, or identity checks will fail.</p>
<p>Fourth, understand the income risk. Contracts are fixed term, can end early, and carry no paid leave. Pay waits on manager approval, and some contractors report withdrawals stuck for weeks. Keep other income where you can, log hours on time, and keep copies of your agreements and approvals. Our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-spot-online-job-scams" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to spot online job scams</a> lists the full set of checks.</p>
