<h2 id="what-you-need-before-you-start">What you need before you start</h2>
<p>Gather these five things before you sign up, and the setup will take hours instead of days. This checklist is the unglamorous part of how to start an ecommerce store with Shopify, and skipping it is why setups drag on for days.</p>
<ul>
<li>A product to sell, or a clear decision about what you will sell first. One product is enough to launch.</li>
<li>Clear photos of each product, taken in good light against a plain background. Phone photos are fine if they are sharp and honest.</li>
<li>A business name, and a shortlist of domain names in case your first choice is taken.</li>
<li>A payment method for your own Shopify bill, such as a card.</li>
<li>The legal details for your country, such as whether you need to register the business or charge tax. Rules differ by country, so check your local tax authority.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="step-1-create-your-account-and-start-the-free-trial">Step 1: Create your account and start the free trial</h2>
<p>Go to the <a href="https://www.shopify.com/pricing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Shopify pricing page</a> and start the free trial. Shopify usually offers a short free trial followed by a low cost introductory period for the first few months. The exact offer changes, so check the live pricing page before you sign up, and do not rely on a figure from an old guide.</p>
<p>Sign up with your email address and a password. You do not need a credit card to start the trial. Answer the short setup questions about what you plan to sell. Your answers shape the suggestions Shopify shows you, but you can change every setting later, so do not worry about getting them perfect.</p>
<p>You will land in the Shopify admin, which is the control panel for your store. Take five minutes to look around. The left menu holds Products, Orders, Customers, Content, Discounts, and Settings. You will use Products and Settings most during setup.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-set-up-the-basics-in-the-admin">Step 2: Set up the basics in the admin</h2>
<p>Go to Settings, then Store details. Enter your store name, your business address, and your contact email. The store name is what customers see in emails and on receipts, so use your real business name.</p>
<p>Next, set your currency. Go to Settings, then Store details, and choose the currency your customers will pay in. Changing currency later is possible but messy, because it affects prices and reports, so choose carefully now.</p>
<p>Add your business details in Settings, then Plan and billing, so invoices go to the right place. Then go to Settings, then Locations, and add the place you will ship from, such as your home or a warehouse. Shopify uses this address to work out shipping rates and taxes.</p>
<p>Finally, set up your sender email. Go to Settings, then Store details, and check the sender email address. Customers will see this address on order emails, so use a business address such as hello at your domain, not a personal inbox, once your domain is connected.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-choose-and-customise-a-theme">Step 3: Choose and customise a theme</h2>
<p>A theme is the design of your store: the layout, colours, fonts, and how products are shown. Go to Online Store, then Themes, to browse the theme store. Shopify offers free themes, including Horizon, a free theme released in 2025, and more than 20 paid themes in the theme store, with paid themes usually costing about 100 to 500 US dollars as a one time payment.</p>
<p>Start with a free theme. A free theme is enough to launch, and you can change themes later without losing your products. Pick a theme that fits your product type. A theme built for fashion suits clothing. A theme built for single products suits one hero product. Do not pick a theme for its demo photos. Pick it for its layout.</p>
<p>Click Try theme, then Customise, to open the theme editor. Change the colours to match your brand, using two or three colours at most. Set your fonts, one for headings and one for body text. Add your logo, or a simple text logo with your store name, if you have no logo yet. Check the mobile preview on every change, because most of your visitors will shop on a phone.</p>
<p>Keep the homepage simple. It needs: a clear headline that says what you sell, one strong product image, a short line about why buyers should trust you, and your best products. Remove every section that does not help a visitor buy. A tidy store sells better than a busy one.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-add-your-products-and-write-descriptions-that-sell">Step 4: Add your products and write descriptions that sell</h2>
<p>Go to Products, then Add product. Shopify's own help centre explains the same steps in its <a href="https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-your-store" rel="noopener" target="_blank">guide to setting up your store</a>, which is worth keeping open while you work. For each product, add a clear title, at least two photos, a price, and a description. Then add inventory, variants, and shipping details.</p>
<p>Variants are the options for a product, such as size or colour. Add only the variants you truly stock. A product with 20 variants looks impressive and creates 20 chances to run out of stock. Start small.</p>
<p>Write descriptions that help a buyer decide. A good description answers four questions: what is it, who is it for, what does it do that matters, and what should the buyer check before ordering, such as size, material, or care instructions. Write in short sentences and short paragraphs, because shoppers scan.</p>
<p>Here is a short example for a handmade candle. Weak version: "Beautiful candle, great scent, buy now." Strong version: "A soy wax candle with a 40 hour burn time. It suits small rooms and quiet evenings. The scent is cedar and amber, with no added colour. Each candle is poured by hand and ships in a gift ready box." The strong version gives facts a buyer can use.</p>
<p>Shopify Magic can draft product descriptions for you, and Sidekick, Shopify's AI assistant, can help with admin tasks. Both are useful for a first draft, but always check AI written copy before you publish it. AI can invent a feature your product does not have, and a wrong claim can cost you a refund and a bad review. Keep your own voice in the final text.</p>
<p>Organise products into collections, which are groups such as New arrivals, Sale, or Bestsellers. Go to Products, then Collections, then Add collection. Collections make the store easier to browse and give you pages to share on social media. Add each product to at least one collection, and add the collections to your main menu under Content, then Menus.</p>

If Shopify Payments is available in your country, turn it on first. It lets customers pay by card, and it powers Shop Pay, which is Shopify's fast one page checkout that remembers a buyer's details. Check the official Shopify page for your country before you rely on it, because Shopify Payments is not available everywhere, and the card rates differ by country.</p>

Note the extra fee: on Basic, using a third party provider adds a 2 percent fee on top of the provider's own fee. That is worth knowing before you choose.</p>
<p>In Settings, then Checkout, check that Shop Pay is on, and that customers can check out as guests without creating an account. Forcing an account at checkout loses sales. Also turn on abandoned checkout recovery, which emails customers who leave items in their cart. Go to Settings, then Checkout, and confirm the recovery email is set.</p>

<h2 id="step-6-configure-shipping-and-taxes">Step 6: Configure shipping and taxes</h2>
<p>Go to Settings, then Shipping and delivery. Create shipping zones, which are the areas you will ship to, such as your home country first, then other countries later. For each zone, set a rate. Common choices are free shipping over a set order value, a flat rate per order, or a rate based on weight. Start with one zone and one simple rule, then add more as you learn what shipping really costs.</p>
<p>If you use a courier, check whether Shopify Shipping, which buys labels at a discount inside Shopify, is available in your country. It can cut label costs, and it lets you print labels from the admin. Also set up local delivery or pickup if you sell to people nearby. Both are options in the same Shipping and delivery settings.</p>
<p>For taxes, go to Settings, then Taxes and duties. Shopify Tax can work out sales tax for US stores automatically. For other countries, set the tax rules for your region, and check whether you need to register for VAT or a local sales tax before you sell. Tax rules differ by country, and getting them wrong can cost you later. If you are unsure, ask a local accountant before your first sale.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-add-a-custom-domain-and-the-essential-pages-and-policies">Step 7: Add a custom domain and the essential pages and policies</h2>
<p>Your store starts on a free address such as yourstore.myshopify.com. That works, but a custom domain such as yourstore.com looks more professional and is easier to share. Go to Settings, then Domains, and buy a domain through Shopify, usually about 11 to 20 US dollars a year, or connect a domain you already own. After you connect it, set it as the primary domain, and check that the store loads on it.</p>
<p>Next, add the essential pages. Go to Online Store, then Pages, then Add page, for each of these:</p>
<ul>
<li>About: who you are, what you sell, and why buyers should trust you.</li>
<li>Contact: an email address and a contact form, plus how fast you reply.</li>
<li>FAQ: delivery times, returns, and payment questions, answered in plain words.</li>
</ul>
<p>Then add the policy pages, which Shopify can generate as drafts. Go to Settings, then Policies, and fill in each one:</p>
<ul>
<li>Refund policy: what can be returned, within how many days, and who pays return shipping.</li>
<li>Privacy policy: what customer data you collect and how you use it.</li>
<li>Terms of service: the rules of buying from your store.</li>
<li>Shipping policy: delivery times, costs, and which countries you ship to.</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not publish empty policy pages. Read each one, fill in your real rules, and keep the wording honest. Buyers check these pages when they are unsure, and a clear refund policy can be the difference between a sale and an abandoned cart. Add the pages to your footer menu under Content, then Menus, so buyers can find them.</p>
<h2 id="step-8-test-everything-then-launch">Step 8: Test everything, then launch</h2>
<p>Before you launch, place a test order. Shopify's help centre explains the test order method using the Bogus Gateway, a test payment provider that lets you place an order without real money. Follow those steps in the <a href="https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-your-store" rel="noopener" target="_blank">guide to setting up your store</a>, then check the whole journey as a customer would.</p>

<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-much-does-a-shopify-store-cost">How much does a Shopify store cost?</h3>
<p>The Basic plan costs 39 US dollars a month, or 29 US dollars a month when billed yearly, as listed on Shopify's pricing page on 2026-11-12. Add a domain at about 11 to 20 US dollars a year, plus card fees on each order. A realistic first month total is about 31 to 50 US dollars before stock and shipping, if you use a free theme and no paid apps.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-coding-skills-to-set-up-a-shopify-store">Do I need coding skills to set up a Shopify store?</h3>
<p>No. The theme editor, product editor, and settings all work without code. You can change colours, fonts, layouts, and sections by clicking. Coding helps only if you want a custom design that no theme offers.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-set-up-a-shopify-store">How long does it take to set up a Shopify store?</h3>
<p>A simple store with a few products can be ready in one or two days if your photos and product details are ready. A larger store with many products and custom policies takes longer, usually a week of part time work. The product photos and descriptions take more time than the Shopify setup itself.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-set-up-a-shopify-store-for-free">Can I set up a Shopify store for free?</h3>
<p>You can start with a free trial, but a paid plan is needed to take real orders. The trial lets you build the store and test it. After the trial, the Basic plan is the lowest cost way to go live.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-shop-pay">What is Shop Pay?</h3>
<p>Shop Pay is Shopify's fast checkout. It remembers a buyer's details from past Shopify purchases, so repeat buyers can check out in fewer steps. It is turned on by default when Shopify Payments is active, and it can raise checkout completion because it removes friction.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-test-my-shopify-store-before-launch">How do I test my Shopify store before launch?</h3>
<p>Use the Bogus Gateway test payment provider to place a test order without real money, following the steps in Shopify's help centre guide to setting up your store. Check the order email, the payment record, and the store on a phone before you remove the password page and go live.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion-how-to-start-an-ecommerce-store-with-shopify-in-2026">Conclusion: how to start an ecommerce store with Shopify in 2026</h2>
<p>Learning how to start an ecommerce store with Shopify comes down to this: gather your products, photos, and policies first, then create your account, set up the basics, choose a free theme, add products with honest descriptions, turn on payments, set shipping and taxes, connect a custom domain, and test everything with a test order before you launch. Start on the Basic plan, skip paid apps until you need them, and spend your first 30 days chasing the first sale and fixing friction.</p>
