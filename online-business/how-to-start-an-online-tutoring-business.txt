<h2 id="what-an-online-tutoring-business-is-and-who-pays-for-it">What an online tutoring business is, and who pays for it</h2>
<p>An online tutoring business sells one-to-one or small-group lessons over video call, usually as a package with a set number of sessions and a clear outcome, such as a better exam grade or a passed language test. You are the teacher and the business owner at first, and later you may hire other tutors or sell recorded courses alongside live lessons.</p>
<p>The buyer is often not the student. For school-age tutoring, the parent pays, the student attends, and the parent judges value by grades, test scores, and whether homework fights stopped. For exam candidates, the student pays and cares about the deadline. For adult learners, the student pays and cares about convenience and pace. Sell to the person who pays, and teach the person who attends.</p>
<p>This guide is about running the business. If you want the job side first, such as finding tutoring work on a platform, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-jobs/how-to-find-online-tutoring-jobs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to find online tutoring jobs</a>. The business route gives you more control over prices and students, but it also means you do your own marketing, admin, and tax.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-choose-one-subject-one-level-one-outcome">Step 1: Choose one subject, one level, one outcome</h2>
<p>"Tutoring everything for ages 6 to 18" is hard to market and hard to prepare for. Pick one lane and expand later. A good niche reads like a sentence: "GCSE maths for Year 10 and 11 students who need extra support before exams" or "IELTS speaking practice for adults who need band 7". The subject, the level, and the outcome are all in one line.</p>
<p>Choose from what you know best. That knowledge may come from a degree, a professional qualification, or years of practical experience. Then check that you can answer questions at that level, explain tricky ideas in more than one way, and plan useful lessons. Be realistic: if your highest French qualification is a school exam from years ago, convincing advanced students that you are the tutor for them will be an uphill task.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-check-demand-before-you-build-anything">Step 2: Check demand before you build anything</h2>
<p>Talk to ten people in your target group before you design a website. Ask what they use now, what they pay, what frustrates them, and what result would make them renew. Look up the exam calendar for your subject so you know the selling seasons, because demand peaks in the weeks before major exams. Check whether tutors in your subject on the big marketplaces show full calendars or waitlists. If three or more parents say they would book next week, you have enough signal to proceed. Those ten conversations are the cheapest market research in how to start an online tutoring business, and they cost nothing but time.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/online-tutoring-business-booking.jpg" alt="A laptop showing a simple online booking calendar with lesson time slots, beside a notebook and a small clock" /></p>
<h2 id="step-3-design-packages-not-hours">Step 3: Design packages, not hours</h2>
<p>Hourly tutoring invites cancellations, makes income unpredictable, and leaves no room for the work between sessions that produces results. A package has a name, a length, a session count, a defined outcome, and support between sessions. Here are three package shapes that work for most subjects:</p>

<p>Add one entry point: a 20 minute diagnostic call at no charge, where you assess the student level with a few questions, explain what you would work on, and recommend one package. That call is the main thing your website, social posts, and referral asks will point to. Put a simple cancellation rule in writing now, such as 24 hours notice to reschedule, with a late cancellation counting as a used session, so you never have to argue about it with a parent.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-set-prices-from-an-income-target">Step 4: Set prices from an income target</h2>
<p>Start from the income you need, then work out how many students and live hours each package requires. That keeps you from picking a rate because it feels right and then discovering you need 35 teaching hours a week to cover your rent.</p>
<p><strong>Example only: these numbers are not typical results.</strong> Suppose your monthly income goal is 2,000 US dollars and you sell a term plan at 320 dollars a month with four sessions per student. You would need about 7 students to reach 2,240 dollars of revenue, and about 7 live teaching hours a week. Add preparation, marking practice sets, and parent updates, which can add 30 to 60 minutes per student each week, so your real working week is closer to 12 hours for those 7 students. That is the honest maths: price the whole job, not just the hour on camera.</p>
<p>Before you settle on a number, subtract what you do not keep. Card processing fees come out of each payment, and self-employed tax applies in most countries, so set aside a fixed share of every payment in a separate account and ask an accountant what share fits your situation. Compare your prices with tutors at a similar level in your subject, but do not copy the cheapest rate. A rate that covers your preparation time and your tax is a rate you can keep.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-set-up-the-business-legally">Step 5: Set up the business legally</h2>
<p>Set up the business before your first student pays you. The exact steps differ by country, so use this list as a checklist to take to a local professional, not as legal advice.</p>
<p>Choose a business name and check that the domain is free. Choose a business structure. In the United States, the common choices are a sole proprietorship or a limited liability company, known as an LLC. The US Small Business Administration explains <a href="https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to choose a business structure</a> and the trade-offs of each option. Many tutors start as sole proprietors to test the idea and form an LLC once income is steady. If you are in the United States and you form an LLC, you can apply for an employer identification number, known as an EIN, free from the IRS. The IRS explains <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to apply for an EIN online</a> and warns that you never have to pay a fee for one.</p>
<p>Write a tutoring agreement and have it signed before the first paid session, by the parent when the student is a minor. It should cover what the package includes and its length, payment terms and refunds, the cancellation rule, communication rules, whether sessions are recorded and who can access recordings, and how either side ends the arrangement. Have a local attorney review the template once, because it is a small cost compared with a dispute.</p>
<p>Get professional liability insurance and ask the insurer specifically about tutoring minors online, because some policies treat work with children differently. Open a separate business bank account and keep accurate records from the start, because your future self will thank you at tax time.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-choose-your-tools-and-booking-system">Step 6: Choose your tools and booking system</h2>
<p>You need tools for six jobs: a website, a booking page, payments, agreements, a place to deliver homework and practice between sessions, and a record of each student progress. You can assemble these from separate tools or run them from one platform. Start simple and add tools only when a real problem appears.</p>
<p>A lean starter stack is a video call app such as Zoom or Google Meet, a booking page such as Calendly or Acuity, a payment method such as Stripe or PayPal, a shared drive for worksheets, and a spreadsheet for student records. Check the free plan limits before you choose, especially the time limit on free video calls. As you grow, a single tutoring platform that bundles booking, payments, agreements, and practice tracking can save you several logins and several subscriptions, but do not pay for it before you have students.</p>
<p>Set your booking rules on day one: buffers between sessions so you are not running from one student to the next, a minimum notice so nobody books you for 7 a.m. tomorrow at 11 p.m. tonight, and time-zone detection if you teach students in other countries. Add intake questions to the booking, such as the student year or grade, the subject, the next exam date, and what the parent has tried already.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-build-a-small-website-that-books-calls">Step 7: Build a small website that books calls</h2>
<p>Your website has one job: to book diagnostic calls. Three to five pages is enough at launch. The home page should answer three questions in the first screen: who you tutor, what changes for the student, and how to start, with one button to book a diagnostic call. Add your qualifications, your academic integrity rule, and a short note on how you work. Testimonials can come later, and only real ones with the parent permission.</p>
<p>Use four pages: Home, How it works, Packages, and About. On the Packages page, show the three packages with prices, session counts, and what is included, so parents can choose before the call. On the About page, say who you are, what you teach, and how you teach it. Keep the design plain and fast, and make sure the booking button works on a phone, because many parents will find you on one. For a wider view of starting an online business from nothing, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-online-business-beginner" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online business for beginners</a>.</p>
<h2 id="step-8-find-your-first-ten-students">Step 8: Find your first ten students</h2>
<p>Your first ten students come from people who already trust you or trust someone who knows you, not from ads. Make a list of 50 names and organisations, contact them over three weeks, and send every interested person to the diagnostic call.</p>
<p>Channels that work for tutors include your own network of former colleagues and friends with school-age children, school counsellors and teachers who are asked for tutor recommendations constantly, parent groups on Facebook and WhatsApp, homeschool co-ops that often need a subject specialist for a whole term, and past students if you taught or tutored before. Answer questions helpfully in parent groups for a few weeks before you mention your services, and follow each group rules about promotion.</p>
<p>Marketplaces such as Preply, Wyzant, and iTalki can send you students when you are starting with no audience, but they take a large share of every lesson, so treat them as a launchpad while you build channels of your own. After the diagnostic call, follow up the same day with a short note: what you saw, what you recommend, and the link to book and pay. If you wait a week, the parent will have booked someone else.</p>
<h2 id="step-9-deliver-lessons-that-keep-students-enrolled">Step 9: Deliver lessons that keep students enrolled</h2>
<p>Families renew when they see steady progress and the student practises between sessions without being chased. Build that into the package: a weekly structure, a small daily habit, a quick check-in, and a regular note to the parent.</p>
<p>Turn each package into a programme with weekly goals and daily tasks. For an exam sprint, week one might be diagnosing gaps, with daily tasks of ten practice questions, and week four is the first timed mock. A daily practice habit, such as 20 minutes of practice questions, gives the student a small win each day and gives you evidence when the parent asks how things are going. Send the parent a three-line note every two weeks: what you covered, what improved, and what to watch. Keep session notes on the client record so you can write that note in two minutes.</p>
<p>Put recurring explanations into a short recorded course so you stop re-teaching the same concept to every student. For small group classes, a moderated group space where students post questions between classes keeps momentum and lets stronger students explain to others, which helps both. If you plan to add recorded lessons or group teaching later, our guide on <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-online-coaching-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online coaching business</a> covers the programme format in more detail.</p>

<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Selling hours instead of packages, which makes income unpredictable and cancellations easy.</li>
<li>Tutoring every subject and age, which is hard to market and hard to prepare for.</li>
<li>Forgetting the parent, who pays and decides whether to renew.</li>
<li>Having no cancellation rule in writing, so every late cancellation becomes a negotiation.</li>
<li>Doing the homework for the student, which feels helpful and costs you your reputation with schools.</li>
<li>Promising score jumps you cannot guarantee, which breaks trust and can breach advertising rules.</li>
<li>Collecting data from children directly, instead of booking and paying through the parent.</li>
<li>Running five separate tools when one platform with one login for the family would do.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-safety-rules-to-watch-for">Risks and safety rules to watch for</h2>
<p>Tutoring is academic help. It is not a diagnosis of learning disabilities, not therapy, and not a substitute for the school own support process. If you suspect a student has an undiagnosed learning difference or a difficulty that blocks learning, tell the parent what you have observed and suggest they ask the school or a licensed specialist for an evaluation. Write this scope line into your agreement and your website.</p>
<p>Set an academic integrity rule from day one. You explain, model, and check, but you do not complete graded homework, essays, or tests for the student. Put it in writing, because it protects the student and your reputation with schools that may later refer families to you.</p>
<p>If you tutor minors, many parents will ask whether you have had a background check. Getting one voluntarily, and saying so, removes a common objection. In the UK, for example, self-employed tutors can apply for an enhanced background check through an umbrella body, and the level available depends on the role. Collect booking and payment details from the parent, not the child, and keep only what you need: name, level, goals, session notes, and the parent contact details. If you record sessions, get written consent first, from the parent when the student is a minor, and state in your agreement who can access recordings and how long you keep them.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="do-i-need-a-teaching-licence-to-start-an-online-tutoring-business">Do I need a teaching licence to start an online tutoring business?</h3>
<p>Licence and permit rules for private tutoring depend on your location and your activity, so there is no single answer. In most places you do not legally need a teaching qualification to offer private tuition, but it is a strong marketing asset when you have one.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-charge-per-hour-or-per-package">Should I charge per hour or per package?</h3>
<p>Per package, in most cases. Packages earn more per live hour, because you get paid for the structure around the sessions, and they make cancellations less tempting. Keep an hourly rate only as a fallback for one-off help, and price it higher than the per-session cost inside a package.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-students-can-one-online-tutor-handle">How many students can one online tutor handle?</h3>
<p>It depends on your format and your available hours. Add each student live hour to their preparation, marking, and parent-update time, then divide your available weekly hours by that total. Small group classes let you serve more students in fewer hours.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-tutor-students-in-other-countries">Can I tutor students in other countries?</h3>
<p>Yes. Use a booking page with time-zone detection, price in one currency, and accept card payments. Data rules such as GDPR apply to students in the EU and UK, and sales tax or VAT may apply depending on where students live, so check with an accountant before you sell internationally.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-insurance-to-tutor-online">Do I need insurance to tutor online?</h3>
<p>It is strongly advised. Professional liability insurance protects against financial loss from errors and negligence for businesses that provide services. Ask insurers specifically about online tutoring and working with minors, because some policies treat work with children differently.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-start-an-online-tutoring-business">How long does it take to start an online tutoring business?</h3>
<p>Plan on two to four weeks of setup: a few days for the niche and packages, one to two weeks for legal filings, and a few days for the website and booking page. Signing the first ten students usually takes longer and depends on your network and the exam calendar.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Learning how to start an online tutoring business comes down to a clear sequence. Pick one subject, one level, and one outcome, check demand with ten real conversations, sell packages instead of hours, and set prices from an income target. Then build a small website that books diagnostic calls, contact your list of 50 names, and deliver lessons with practice plans and parent updates that keep families renewing.</p>
