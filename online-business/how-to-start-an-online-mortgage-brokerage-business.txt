<h2 id="what-an-online-mortgage-brokerage-is-and-what-it-is-not">What an online mortgage brokerage is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>A mortgage brokerage helps a borrower choose and apply for a mortgage. You collect facts about income, debts, savings and the property, then match the borrower with a lender and send the application to that lender. The lender checks the file, values the property and decides whether to lend.</p>
<p>You are not a lender. A lender lends money and carries the risk. You earn a fee for arranging the loan, often paid by the lender after closing. An online brokerage does this work through a website, video calls and secure uploads. Online does not mean unlicensed. You still need the right licence for every state where you work.</p>
<p>This is a regulated business, because mortgage advice affects a family home and a large debt. This guide gives general information only. It is not legal advice and it is not financial advice, and it does not tell any reader which mortgage to take. Licences, exams and rules differ by country and by US state. Check your regulator before you act, for example the Nationwide Multistate Licensing System, known as NMLS, in the United States, and get local legal advice.</p>
<p>A brokerage is not the same as promoting mortgage offers as an affiliate. An affiliate shares links and earns a small fee for clicks or leads. A broker gives regulated advice and sends a full application to a lender. If you only want to promote offers, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/affiliate-marketing/how-to-earn-with-mortgage-affiliate-programs" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to earn with mortgage affiliate programs</a> and compare the models. Do not give mortgage advice without a licence.</p>

<p>Before you spend on a logo or ads, test whether local borrowers need another broker. A clear niche, such as first time buyers, helps you plan your licence and your website.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-get-licensed-to-originate-mortgages">Step 1: Get licensed to originate mortgages</h2>
<p>You start by getting licensed, not by taking clients. In the United States, the Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing Act, known as the SAFE Act, was passed in 2008. It set a nationwide system for licensing originators through NMLS. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains the system in its <a href="https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervision-examinations/secure-and-fair-enforcement-for-mortgage-licensing-safe-act-examination-procedures/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SAFE Act examination procedures</a>. NMLS is run by the Conference of State Bank Supervisors, known as CSBS, for state supervisors.</p>
<p>As an individual, you complete pre licensing education, pass a national test, pass background and credit checks, and apply through NMLS for a state licence. CSBS states that originators need at least 20 hours of pre licensing education and 8 hours of continuing education each year. See the CSBS note on <a href="https://www.csbs.org/newsroom/states-sanction-mortgage-loan-originator-safe-act-violation" rel="noopener" target="_blank">SAFE Act education and enforcement</a>. Your state may add rules, so check the state page in NMLS before you book a course.</p>
<p>Consumer Financial Protection Bureau Regulation H covers registration for originators at banks and similar firms, and it sets the idea of a unique identifier for each originator. See <a href="https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1008/103/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Regulation H on mortgage loan originator registration</a>. At a non bank brokerage, you normally need a state licence plus NMLS registration and a unique identifier before you originate. You must renew each year and complete continuing education.</p>
<p>Many states also license the company. Rules can cover a surety bond, net worth and remote office rules, and they vary by state. Check your regulator through NMLS and ask a local lawyer to confirm your model. Outside the United States, you need permission where the borrower and property sit. If you work from home, check remote office rules in writing.</p>

<h2 id="step-2-set-up-the-business-on-paper">Step 2: Set up the business on paper</h2>
<p>Choose a name that does not mislead borrowers. Do not suggest that you are a bank or a lender if you are a broker. Check the name with your state registry and your regulator, because mortgage names often need approval. Register the business as your lawyer and accountant advise, open a business bank account and set up bookkeeping from day one. Keep personal and business money apart.</p>
<p>Write a simple plan that names your niche, service area and client source. Build your mortgage brokerage startup costs list from regulator fees and quotes. Compare this model with <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-an-online-tax-preparation-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online tax preparation business</a> and you will see the same need for licences and secure records.</p>
<p>Buy insurance for your state and model, such as professional liability cover and cyber cover. Ask a broker who knows mortgage firms to explain limits in plain words. Keep policies, licences and lender agreements in one secure folder with renewal dates on a calendar.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-get-approved-by-lenders">Step 3: Get approved by lenders</h2>
<p>A licence alone does not let you place loans. You also need approval from lenders or from a wholesale lender or network. Lenders check your licence, experience, finances and compliance record. New firms often start with a small panel and add lenders as quality grows.</p>
<p>Prepare a clean pack with your licences, insurance proof, business plan, sample documents and data security steps. Learn each lender rule set before you advise, because lenders differ on credit scores, deposits and self employed income proof. Do not promise a rate or a yes before the lender checks the file. If you join a network, get legal advice before you sign and check who owns the client relationship.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-build-online-systems-and-stay-compliant">Step 4: Build online systems and stay compliant</h2>
<p>Borrowers share payslips, bank statements and identity documents, so your system must protect data and leave a clear file trail. Use a secure portal for uploads, strong passwords, two step sign in where you can, and access limits. You need a customer record system, e signature tools that your lenders accept, and one checklist per case. Test the full journey as a borrower before you launch.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ob32-mortgage-consult.jpg" alt="A mortgage adviser taking notes during a video call with a home buyer shown on a laptop screen" /></p>
<p>State on your website who you are, where you are licensed and what you do. Show licence details where the rules require them. Do not promise approval or the lowest rate. Use standard fact find forms, disclosure letters and file notes for every call. Keep records for the period your state requires. Back up files and write a simple data incident plan. If you work from home, use a private room for calls and lock paper files away.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-get-your-first-clients-online">Step 5: Get your first clients online</h2>
<p>Clients come from trust, not from hype. Start with estate agents, builders, solicitors and accountants who meet home buyers. If you work near property sales, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-start-an-online-real-estate-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start an online real estate business</a> to see what a fair referral deal looks like. Put deals in writing and follow disclosure rules.</p>
<p>Write website guides on deposits, credit checks and the steps from offer to closing, in plain words. Add clear contact forms and a short privacy notice. Use the same basics as any service firm. Read <a href="https://earnquill.com/online-business/how-to-get-first-customers-online-business" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to get first customers for an online business</a> and apply the parts on fast replies and simple follow up. Reply on the same working day when you can, send a written summary after each call, and collect reviews only from real clients. Log where each lead came from.</p>
<h2 id="step-6-run-cases-well-and-get-paid-after-closing">Step 6: Run cases well and get paid after closing</h2>
<p>Start each case with a full fact find on income, debts, savings and goals. Check identity and documents early, because late requests cause most delays. Send a clean file to the lender with labelled, readable scans and a short cover note for unusual facts. Answer lender questions fast and keep the borrower updated. Do not hide facts to force a yes, because a hidden fact can kill the loan and end your licence.</p>
<p><img src="/covers/ob32-mortgage-documents.jpg" alt="A stack of mortgage application papers and a calculator on a desk beside a laptop" /></p>
<p>You get paid only after closing in most models, and only if the loan completes. You can work for weeks and earn nothing if the borrower withdraws or the lender declines, so keep cash reserves. Disclose fees in writing before the borrower commits. After closing, save the final disclosures and your notes in order. A tidy file protects you if a complaint comes later.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>Costs include education, tests, licence fees, setup, legal advice, insurance, any required bond, technology and marketing. No honest guide can give one total for every state. Build your budget from regulator pages and quotes, and keep a reserve for several months.</p>
<p>Example only: suppose you close one loan with an assumed loan amount of 200,000 US dollars. Suppose the lender pays an assumed compensation rate of 1.5 percent for this example. Step one, 200,000 times 1.5 percent equals 3,000 US dollars. Step two, suppose you pay an assumed network split of 20 percent, which is 600 US dollars, so you keep 2,400 US dollars. Step three, suppose assumed direct case costs total 400 US dollars, so you keep 2,000 US dollars before tax and overheads. Recheck: 3,000 minus 600 equals 2,400, and 2,400 minus 400 equals 2,000. Every number is assumed for learning only. Real pay varies by lender, loan and state rules, and many cases never close.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Your first months go on tests and lender approval. Your next months go on cases that take weeks to close. Plan for late pay, not fast income.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>You take enquiries before you hold the right licence for the borrower state.</li>
<li>You copy bond or commission figures from a blog instead of checking your regulator and lender contracts.</li>
<li>You promise a rate or an approval before the lender checks the file.</li>
<li>You collect documents by normal email without a secure portal.</li>
<li>You send messy files with missing pages and no cover note.</li>
<li>You spend your reserve on ads before you secure lender approval.</li>
<li>You keep poor file notes, so you cannot prove what you advised.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>If you originate without a licence or hide facts from a lender, you can face fines, licence loss and legal action. Keep a licence calendar and complete continuing education every year. Mortgage files hold identity and bank data, so use secure systems and never store files on personal drives. If you suspect a breach, follow your incident plan at once.</p>
<p>Watch for firms that sell useless lead lists or fake lender access. Check suppliers with your lender panel before you pay. Never pay a large upfront fee for guaranteed clients. Stop if a document looks false.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-mortgage-brokerage">How much does it cost to start a mortgage brokerage?</h3>
<p>Costs include education, tests, licence fees, setup, insurance, technology and marketing. The total varies by state and bond rules, so use regulator fees and real quotes. Keep a reserve, because you get paid after closing.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-a-licence-to-start-a-mortgage-business">Do I need a licence to start a mortgage business?</h3>
<p>Yes, in most places you need a licence to advise on or arrange mortgages. In the United States you normally need state licensing through NMLS, plus company licensing where required. Check your regulator and get local legal advice.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-it-take-to-get-licensed-as-a-mortgage-broker">How long does it take to get licensed as a mortgage broker?</h3>
<p>Time varies by state and application checks. Education, testing, background checks and lender approval each take time. Plan for months of setup, and do not take cases until every licence is in place.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-run-a-mortgage-brokerage-online-without-an-office">Can I run a mortgage brokerage online without an office?</h3>
<p>You can do much work online, but office rules depend on your state. Some states allow a home office with strict privacy rules, and some require a licensed address. Confirm your setup with your regulator in writing.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-a-mortgage-broker-and-a-mortgage-lender">What is the difference between a mortgage broker and a mortgage lender?</h3>
<p>A broker advises the borrower and sends the application to a lender. A lender provides the money and carries the loan risk. Lenders set credit rules and make the final decision.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-start-with-no-mortgage-experience">Can I start with no mortgage experience?</h3>
<p>You can start the licence path with no experience in many states, but lenders may require supervision. Many new brokers first work under an experienced firm to learn clean file work.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>You can build an online brokerage from home in some states, but you must earn the right to do the work first. Get licensed, set up the company with legal advice, win lender approval, build secure systems and run clean cases. Disclose fees in writing and never promise a result that only a lender can decide.</p>
