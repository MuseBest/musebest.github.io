How to Start an Online Insurance Agency in 2026

To start an online insurance agency, you need a licence, a carrier that will let you sell its policies, and a steady way to find people who need cover. Many beginners think the hard part is building a website, but the real work is licensing, carrier appointments and renewals, which are the steps that decide whether the agency survives. This guide walks you through the models, the licence, the appointments, the costs, the software and a realistic first year, so you can launch in the right order.

The four insurance models compared

An online insurance agency is a business that sells insurance policies to customers over the internet. The agency does not carry the risk itself. The insurer, also called the carrier, pays the claims, and the agency earns a commission on each policy it sells and renews. The model you choose decides how much freedom you have and how much support you get.

Model Who it suits What you sell Main trade off
Independent agency Sellers who want choice Policies from several carriers You must win each carrier appointment yourself
Captive agency Sellers who want support Policies from one carrier only You cannot offer a rival carrier when the price is better
Brokerage Experienced sellers Policies placed with the best carrier for the client More paperwork and more responsibility to the client
Aggregator or network member New sellers who need access Policies through a network that holds the appointments You share commission with the network

An independent agency gives you the most freedom, because you can compare carriers and find the best price for each customer. A captive agency gives you training, marketing help and a known brand, but you can only sell that one carrier. A network or aggregator is a common first step for a new agency, because it lets you sell through the network's appointments while you build enough volume to win your own.

Step 1: Choose captive or independent

The captive versus independent choice is the first real decision, and it shapes everything else. A captive agent works with one carrier, such as a large national insurer, and the carrier often provides training, office support and leads. The trade off is that you cannot shop around, so if that carrier raises its prices, your customers may leave and you cannot offer them a better deal.

An independent agent can sell for many carriers, which means you can compare prices and keep more customers. The trade off is that you must win an appointment with each carrier, and a new agency with no sales history will find that hard. Many new agents solve this by joining a network or cluster, which is a group of agencies that shares appointments and commission, so a small agency can sell for carriers it could not reach alone.

Step 2: Pick a niche and product lines

A new agency that tries to sell every type of insurance will struggle, because each product line has its own rules, carriers and customers. Pick one niche and one or two product lines to start, and add more once you have steady sales.

Personal lines cover people and their homes and cars, and they include home insurance, car insurance and personal liability cover. Commercial lines cover businesses, and they include general liability, professional indemnity and commercial property cover. Life and health lines cover people against death, illness and medical costs, and they often pay higher commissions but need more advice and more compliance checks.

Good starting niches include small business owners who need liability cover, landlords who need property cover, freelancers who need professional indemnity, and families who need home and car cover. Pick the group you understand best and can reach easily. A niche also helps with carrier appointments, because a carrier is more likely to appoint an agency that brings it the customers it wants.

Step 3: Get licensed

You cannot sell insurance without a licence, and licensing rules differ by country. In the United States, insurance is regulated by each state, and you need a producer licence for each line of authority you sell, such as property, casualty, life or health. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners supports state regulators, and its site at naic.org links to each state insurance department, where you can check the exact steps. In the United Kingdom, the Financial Conduct Authority regulates insurance firms, and you can check its rules on the FCA website. In India, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India sets the rules. Check the regulator in your own country before you spend any money.

The usual steps are similar in most places. First, complete a pre-licensing study course for the lines you want to sell. Second, pass the licensing exam. Third, apply for the licence and pass a background check. Fourth, if you run an agency rather than selling as an individual, apply for an entity licence for the business as well. Some countries also require continuing education each year to keep the licence active, so budget time and money for that.

Step 4: Register the business and sort insurance

Register the agency as a business in the same way as any other online business. In the United States, the Small Business Administration explains how to register a business and the structures available, including sole proprietorships, partnerships and limited liability companies. A limited liability company is a common choice for an agency because it separates your personal assets from the business. In the United Kingdom, the government explains how to set up as a sole trader or a limited company. Check whether your regulator also requires the agency itself to be registered or authorised, because the business registration and the insurance authorisation are two different steps.

Errors and omissions insurance is essential for an agency. It is insurance that pays if a client claims you gave wrong advice or missed a cover they needed. Most carriers will not appoint an agency that does not hold it, and most networks require it too. Get quotes from two or three insurers, and check the cover limit, the excess and whether the policy covers all the product lines you sell.

Step 5: Get carrier appointments

A carrier appointment is the carrier's formal agreement that your agency may sell its policies. Without appointments, you have nothing to sell, and this is the step that surprises most beginners, because carriers do not appoint every agency that asks.

Carriers look at three things. First, your licence and your errors and omissions insurance. Second, your business plan and your niche, because they want agencies that bring them the customers they want. Third, your expected volume, because appointments cost the carrier time and money. A new agency with no sales history will often be turned down for a direct appointment, and that is normal, not a failure.

Apply to two or three carriers that fit your niche, and be honest about your volume. Show them a simple plan: your niche, your target customers, how you will reach them and how many policies you expect to sell in the first year. Follow up politely, and ask what volume or experience they need before they will appoint you. If they say no, ask what would change the answer, and work toward that number through a network.

Step 6: Set up your technology

An online agency runs on a small set of tools. An agency management system is the centre of the agency. It holds client details, policies, renewal dates, documents and commission records in one place, and it reminds you when a renewal is due. Well known systems include Applied Epic, AMS360 and HawkSoft, and each one offers a demo, so test two before you pay. Pick the one that makes renewals easy to see, because renewals are where the profit lives.

Step 7: Build your website and online quote flow

Your website has one job: to turn a visitor who needs cover into a quote request. The home page should say who you help, which types of cover you sell and how to get a quote, all in the first screen. A page for each product line should explain what the cover does, who it suits and what it costs in general terms. Do not promise a price on the page, because the real price depends on the customer's details and the carrier's underwriting.

Make sure the site loads fast on a phone, because many customers will ask for a quote from one. Add your licence number and your regulator's name in the footer, because it builds trust and it is required in some countries.

Step 8: Find clients and keep them

Your first clients will come from people you know and from local partnerships, not from search alone, because a new site takes months to rank. Tell accountants, mortgage advisers, estate agents, car dealers and small business groups that you have opened an agency and what you cover. These people meet customers who need insurance at exactly the right moment, such as when they buy a house, a car or a business policy. See also our insurance affiliate guide.

Renewals are the profit engine of an agency. A customer who renews each year pays commission again, and it costs far less to keep a customer than to win a new one. Contact every customer at least 30 days before renewal, check whether their cover still fits, and compare prices across your carriers. A customer who feels looked after will renew and will recommend you to others. For ideas on keeping clients happy, read our guide on how to keep freelance clients, because the same habits apply.

Worked example: what a small online agency could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.

Imagine a new independent agency that sells home insurance online. The average annual premium is 800 dollars, and the agency earns 12 percent commission on each new policy, which is 96 dollars per policy. In a month, the agency sells 25 new policies. The new business commission is 25 multiplied by 96, which is 2,400 dollars.

The agency also renews 40 policies from earlier months, and it earns 12 percent on each renewal, which is another 96 dollars per policy. The renewal commission is 40 multiplied by 96, which is 3,840 dollars. The total commission for the month is 2,400 plus 3,840, which is 6,240 dollars.

Monthly costs are 150 dollars for the management system, 100 dollars for insurance spread across the month, 60 dollars for the website and email, and 200 dollars for leads and marketing. That is 510 dollars in total. The profit before tax is 6,240 minus 510, which is 5,730 dollars.

That example assumes 65 policies in force and steady renewals, which takes a year or more to build. In the first months, the same agency might sell 5 policies a month, which is 480 dollars of commission against the same 510 dollars of costs. The lesson is that an agency loses money at first and becomes profitable as renewals build up, so plan your finances for a slow first year.

How to start an online insurance agency: what it costs and what to expect

A lean launch is realistic, because you do not need an office. The table below shows typical ranges to check before you budget. Prices vary by country and by provider, so confirm each one before you spend.

Item Typical range Notes
Pre-licensing course and exam 200 to 500 dollars Per line of authority in many places
Licence application fees 50 to 300 dollars Per licence and per state or region
Business registration 50 to 500 dollars Depends on country and structure
Errors and omissions insurance 500 to 2,000 dollars per year Required by most carriers and networks
Agency management system 100 to 400 dollars per month Test demos before you pay
Website, domain and email 100 to 400 dollars per year Keep it simple at the start
Marketing and lead buying 0 to 500 dollars per month Referrals first, paid leads later

Expect the first year to be slow. A realistic plan is to spend the first two months on licensing and registration, the next two months on appointments and technology, and the rest of the year on winning your first 50 to 100 policies. Most new agencies take 12 to 24 months to reach a steady income, because renewals build slowly. Keep another income source until the agency can support you, and do not borrow heavily to buy leads in the first months.

Common mistakes

  • Selling insurance in a place where you do not hold a licence.
  • Joining a network without reading how much commission it keeps and how you can leave.
  • Trying to sell every product line at once, which leads to weak advice and lost customers.
  • Ignoring renewal dates, which lets customers drift away to a rival agency.
  • Taking a long time to reply to quote requests, while faster agencies win the sale.
  • Buying large numbers of shared leads before testing whether they convert.
  • Working without errors and omissions insurance to save money in the first month.
  • Promising a customer a price or a cover level before the carrier has confirmed it.

Risks and scams to watch for

The biggest risk is a client claiming you sold them the wrong cover or missed a risk they needed. Clear records, written confirmations of what was sold and what was not, and good errors and omissions insurance are your main protection. Send every customer a short summary of their cover in writing, and keep a copy.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start an insurance agency?

A lean launch often costs between 2,000 and 6,000 dollars in the first year, including licensing, registration, insurance, software and a website. The exact figure depends on your country, how many licences you need and whether you join a network, so get real quotes before you budget.

Can I start an insurance agency from home?

Yes, in most places, as long as you hold the right licence, you have errors and omissions insurance, and you meet your regulator's rules for working from home and storing customer data. A home office is normal for a new online agency, and carriers care more about your licence and your plan than about your address.

Do I need experience to start an insurance agency?

Experience helps, but many new agents start with no sales history and learn through a network, a captive carrier or a mentor. What you cannot skip is the licence, the appointments and a clear niche, because those are the steps that let you sell at all.

What is the difference between an insurance agent and a broker?

An agent usually sells for one or more carriers under an appointment, while a broker acts for the customer and places cover with the best carrier for that customer. The exact legal difference depends on the country, and some places use the two words for the same role, so check your local rules.

How do insurance agencies make money?

Agencies earn commission on each policy they sell, usually a percentage of the premium, and they earn commission again when the policy renews each year. Some agencies also earn fees for extra services where the rules allow it, but commission and renewals are the main income.

Conclusion

To start an online insurance agency, pick one niche and one or two product lines, get licensed in every place you plan to sell, register the business, arrange errors and omissions insurance, then win carrier appointments through a direct application or a network. Set up a management system, a simple quote flow and a renewal routine, and let referrals and partnerships bring your first customers while renewals build your income over time.
