How to Create a Digital Product Bundle in 2026

Learning how to create a digital product bundle is the fastest way to earn more from products you already have. A bundle is a set of two or more digital products sold together at one price, which is lower than the price of buying each product on its own. The buyer gets a clear saving, and you get a higher sale value from one order, without making a new product from scratch.

This guide shows you how to pick the right products, name the bundle after the result, price it fairly, build it on Gumroad, Etsy, or Payhip, deliver it cleanly, and tell whether it is working. It also explains why the discount advice in different guides disagrees, and how to choose a discount that fits your products.

What a bundle is, and what it is not

A bundle is a pre-packaged set of products sold as a group, at a combined price. That is the definition used by Salesforce, which describes bundle pricing as selling multiple products together for a combined price, often at a discount. A bundle can be a fixed set, such as an ebook plus a template pack plus a short course, or it can be a set the buyer customises, such as "pick any three templates". It can also be a tiered offer, with a small bundle, a medium bundle, and a complete collection.

A bundle is not a clearance table. Throwing ten unrelated files together at a deep discount does not make a bundle. It makes a confusing offer that buyers ignore. A good bundle has one buyer, one goal, and products that work together. If the products do not serve the same goal, they do not belong in the same bundle.

A bundle is also not a replacement for your single products. Keep selling each product on its own, at its own price, and offer the bundle as a better value choice for buyers who want the full set. That way, a buyer who only wants one item can still buy it, and a buyer who wants everything can save money.

Why bundles work

Bundles work for three reasons. First, they raise the average order value, which is the average amount a buyer spends in one order. A buyer who came for a 15 dollar ebook may spend 59 dollars on a bundle that includes the ebook, a template pack, and a short course. Second, they reduce decision fatigue, which is the tired feeling buyers get when they must choose between many similar products. One bundle, with one price, is easier to choose than five separate products. Third, they move slow sellers. A template pack that rarely sells on its own may sell well inside a bundle, because the buyer sees it as part of a useful set.

Bundles also give you a new offer without new work. You already made the products. The bundle is packaging, pricing, and a sales page, not a new product. That makes it a good second step after you have three or more products in one niche. For the first product, read how to create your first digital product.

Step 1: Pick one buyer and one outcome

Every bundle starts with one sentence: "This bundle helps [one buyer] to [one outcome]." For example: "This bundle helps a new freelance writer to find clients, track pitches, and send invoices." Or: "This bundle helps a bride to plan her wedding, track the budget, and organise the guest list." If you cannot write that sentence, you do not have a bundle yet. You have a pile of files.

The buyer and the outcome decide everything else: which products to include, what to call the bundle, what price to set, and where to sell it. A bundle for a freelance writer needs a pitch tracker, an invoice template, and a rate guide. A bundle for a bride needs a budget tracker, a guest list, and a supplier list. The same products in a different bundle would serve a different buyer, and they would need a different name and a different price.

Step 2: Choose 3 to 5 products that serve that outcome

Choose three to five products that serve the same outcome, in the same order the buyer will use them. Three products is enough for a starter bundle. Five products is enough for a complete collection. More than five products makes the bundle hard to explain, and buyers stop reading the list.

There are three ways to build the set. A sequence set follows the buyer's journey: learn, plan, then act. For example, an ebook that teaches the skill, a template that plans the work, and a checklist that checks the result. A format mix set gives the buyer the same content in different forms: a video lesson, a PDF workbook, and a spreadsheet tracker. A level mix set gives the buyer a beginner version, an intermediate version, and an advanced version of the same tool.

Set type Example Best for
Sequence Ebook, then template, then checklist Buyers who are learning a new skill
Format mix Video lesson, workbook, tracker Buyers who learn in different ways
Level mix Beginner, intermediate, advanced templates Buyers who want to grow into the tool

Check each product against one test: if you removed this product, would the bundle still solve the problem? If the answer is yes, the product is filler, and you should remove it. A weak product in a bundle pulls down the perceived value of the strong ones, which one competitor guide calls the Presenter's Paradox. One strong product plus two weak ones sells worse than one strong product on its own.

Step 3: Name the bundle after the result, not the file count

Name the bundle after the outcome, not after the number of files. "The Freelance Starter Kit" is better than "Five digital products bundle", because it tells the buyer what they will be able to do. "The Wedding Planning Collection" is better than "Wedding bundle 2026", because it sounds like a finished set, not a sale bin.

Use names that work across niches. A planner plus a budget tracker plus a habit tracker could be "The Organised Life Bundle". An ebook plus a template pack plus a short course could be "The Complete Beginner Kit". A preset pack plus a Lightroom guide plus a photo checklist could be "The Photo Editing Bundle". The name should make the buyer think of the result, not of the files.

Step 4: Price it

Pricing a bundle is where the guides disagree, and the disagreement is useful once you understand it. One guide suggests 15 to 30 percent off the total of the single prices. Another suggests 20 to 40 percent off. A third suggests 30 to 60 percent off. They disagree because they serve different sellers: a seller clearing old stock can afford a deep discount, while a seller with new products cannot.

Use a decision rule instead of one number. If the products in the bundle sell well on their own, discount less, at 15 to 25 percent off, because the buyer is already willing to pay for them. If the products are new, or slow sellers, discount more, at 25 to 40 percent off, because the bundle needs a clear reason to buy. If the bundle is a launch offer or a clearance offer, you can go deeper, but only for a short time, and only if you say so. Never discount so deeply that the bundle earns less than the single product the buyer would have bought anyway.

Pricing method How it works Best for
Fixed price One price for the whole set, such as 59 dollars Simple bundles, clear value
Percentage off A set discount, such as 25 percent off the total Buyers who want to see the saving
Build your own Buyer picks three items for a set price Shops with many similar products

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine a bundle with an ebook priced at 15 dollars, a template pack priced at 25 dollars, and a mini course priced at 40 dollars. The total of the single prices is 80 dollars. You price the bundle at 59 dollars. The buyer saves 21 dollars, which is 26 percent off the total. If you sell ten bundles in a month, that is 590 dollars in sales. If you had sold the same products singly to the same buyers, you might have sold only the ebook to most of them, at 15 dollars each, which is 150 dollars. The bundle earns more per buyer, but only if the buyer would not have bought all three products singly anyway. That is the cannibalisation question, which is covered below.

One competitor guide gives a similar worked example: a fitness coach bundle with a total of 83 dollars, priced at 59 dollars, saving 24 dollars, which is about 29 percent off. That example is from the Constants guide, and the maths checks out: 24 divided by 83 is about 29 percent.

For the single-product pricing method, read how to price digital products.

Step 5: Build it on your platform

Each platform builds a bundle in a different way. Gumroad lets you create a product that contains several files, or a bundle of existing products, and it handles the checkout and the delivery in one place. Etsy does not have a built-in bundle tool, so you create one listing, upload the files as a ZIP file, or upload a PDF with download links to the files inside. The official Etsy fee page lists the listing fee and the transaction fee that apply to that listing. Payhip lets you create a bundle product and add existing products to it, and it handles the checkout. Shopify needs an app for digital bundles, such as the Alva app described by one competitor guide, which creates file packs that generate a ZIP file at once and update it when you change a file.

Platform How to build the bundle What to check
Gumroad Create a bundle product, add the files or link existing products Check the fee on the live pricing page before you set the price
Etsy One listing, ZIP file or PDF with download links Check the 20 MB file limit and the five file limit per listing
Payhip Create a bundle, add existing products Check the fee and the payout rules on the live page
Shopify Use a bundle app, such as Alva Check the app cost and the file pack limits

Gumroad, Payhip, and Shopify app fees were not verified from official pages in this run, so check the live pricing pages before you set your bundle price. The Etsy file limits are from the Etsy fee and listing rules as reported by competitor guides: five files per listing, 20 MB per file. For the Etsy method, read how to sell digital products on Etsy in 2026, and for the Gumroad method, read how to launch a digital product on Gumroad.

Step 6: Deliver it

The buyer should receive everything in one place, at one time, with one clear list. If the bundle has a PDF, a video, and a spreadsheet, put them in one ZIP file, or in one download page with a link to each file. Do not make the buyer click through five emails to find five files.

Test the delivery before you publish. Buy your own bundle, download it, and open every file. Check that the video plays, that the spreadsheet opens, and that the PDF links work. If the bundle has mixed formats, state in the listing what each file is and what the buyer needs to open it. If the bundle includes a live call or a coaching session, state when it happens and how the buyer books it.

Step 7: Write the bundle page

The bundle page must show the value in five seconds. Put the bundle name at the top, then one sentence that states the outcome. Then list each product in the bundle, with its own price next to it. Then show the total, the bundle price, and the saving, in large text. Then add a short list of who the bundle is for, and who it is not for.

Example page structure:

  • Bundle name: The Freelance Starter Kit
  • Outcome: Find clients, track pitches, and send invoices in one place.
  • Inside: Pitch tracker template, 25 dollars; invoice template, 15 dollars; rate guide ebook, 20 dollars.
  • Total if bought singly: 60 dollars. Bundle price: 45 dollars. You save 15 dollars, which is 25 percent off.
  • For: new freelance writers who want a simple system. Not for: writers who already have a client system they like.

Do not hide the saving. If the buyer cannot see the saving at once, they will not feel that the bundle is good value. Do not use a fake deadline, such as "offer ends tonight", unless the offer really does end tonight. Fake urgency damages trust, and buyers notice it.

Bundle types to try first

Bundle type What it is Best first use
Starter bundle The three products a beginner needs New buyers in your niche
Launch bundle A new product plus two older products at a launch price Launching a new product
Upgrade bundle A basic product plus the advanced version Buyers who already own the basic product
Complete collection Every product in one niche at one price Loyal buyers who want everything

Start with a starter bundle of three products. It is the easiest to explain, the easiest to price, and the easiest to test. Add a complete collection later, once you have five or more products in the same niche.

Launch it

Launch the bundle to a warm audience first, which means people who already know you: your email list, your Pinterest followers, or your Etsy buyers. Tell them what is inside, what it costs, and what they save. Give them a clear end date if the launch price is limited, and keep to that date.

Add the bundle everywhere your single products appear: on your shop page, in your email welcome message, and as a link in the description of each single product in the bundle. A buyer who finds one product should be able to see the bundle as a better value choice.

How to tell if it works

Check three numbers after a month. First, bundle share, which is the share of your orders that are bundles rather than single products. Second, revenue per customer, which is the average amount each buyer spends. Third, item pull, which is which products in the bundle buyers actually use. If the bundle share is rising and the revenue per customer is rising, the bundle is working. If buyers download only one item from the bundle and ignore the rest, that item is filler, and you should replace it.

Also check for cannibalisation, which is when a bundle eats sales you would have made anyway. If a buyer would have bought all three products singly at full price, and now buys the bundle at a discount, you earn less. That is the cost of the bundle. It is worth paying if the bundle brings in buyers who would not have bought anything, or if it brings in buyers who buy more than they would have. It is not worth paying if it only discounts sales you already had. Compare your total revenue before and after the bundle launch, not just the bundle sales on their own.

What it costs and what to expect

A bundle costs almost nothing to make, because the products already exist. Your costs are the platform fees on the sale, and the time to write the bundle page and test the delivery. There is no new product to design, and no new file to make.

Income varies, and no honest guide can promise a figure. A bundle can raise the amount each buyer spends, but only if buyers want the full set. A bundle of unrelated products will not sell, no matter how deep the discount is. Expect to test the price and the product mix for a month before you know whether the bundle works. Do not judge it on the first week.

Common mistakes

  • Bundling unrelated products, so the buyer cannot see who the bundle is for.
  • Adding filler products that nobody uses, which pulls down the value of the strong ones.
  • Discounting so deeply that the bundle earns less than the single product the buyer would have bought.
  • Hiding the saving, so the buyer cannot see why the bundle is good value.
  • Using a fake deadline to push sales, which damages trust.
  • Making a mega bundle of 100 products, which confuses buyers and hides the good products.
  • Forgetting to test the delivery, so the buyer cannot open one of the files.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch for refund requests from buyers who only wanted one item from the bundle. State clearly in the listing that the bundle is sold as a set, and that you do not offer a partial refund for one item. Help the buyer first if a file does not open, and only then consider a full refund.

Watch for licence risk. Do not bundle products made by other creators unless you have the right to sell them together. A bundle of your own products is safe. A bundle that includes someone else's work needs their written permission.

Also watch for platform rules. Etsy, Gumroad, and Payhip each have their own rules for digital delivery and refunds. Check the rules for your platform before you launch, and state your refund terms clearly on the bundle page.

Frequently asked questions

How many products should be in a digital bundle?

Three to five products is the best range for most bundles. Three is enough for a starter bundle, and five is enough for a complete collection. More than five makes the bundle hard to explain, and buyers stop reading the list.

What is the best discount for a product bundle?

There is no single best discount. Use 15 to 25 percent off if the products sell well on their own, and 25 to 40 percent off if the products are new or slow sellers. Check the total revenue before and after the launch, not just the bundle sales.

What digital products are good to bundle?

Products that serve the same buyer and the same goal work best. An ebook plus a template plus a checklist for one skill is a good bundle. A planner plus a budget tracker plus a habit tracker for one kind of buyer is a good bundle. Unrelated products do not work.

Should I still sell the products individually?

Yes. Keep selling each product on its own, at its own price, and offer the bundle as a better value choice. A buyer who only wants one item should still be able to buy it.

Can I bundle products made by other creators?

Only if you have their written permission to sell their work in your bundle. Do not bundle someone else's products without permission, because it breaks their licence and can get your shop closed.

When is the best time to launch a bundle?

Launch to a warm audience first, such as your email list or your existing buyers, when you have three or more products in one niche. A launch price with a real end date can help, but do not use a fake deadline.

Do bundles reduce the value of my products?

They can, if the discount is too deep or the bundle includes weak products. A bundle with a fair discount and strong products raises the perceived value, because the buyer sees a complete system. A bundle with a deep discount and filler products lowers it.

Conclusion: bundle what you already have, price it fairly, test it

Learning how to create a digital product bundle comes down to a simple sequence. Pick one buyer and one outcome, choose three to five products that serve that outcome, name the bundle after the result, price it with a fair discount, build it on one platform, test the delivery, and write a page that shows the saving clearly. Then watch the numbers for a month, and change the mix if buyers ignore one of the items.
