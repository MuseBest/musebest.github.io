<h2 id="what-an-ebook-is-and-how-selling-one-works">What an ebook is and how selling one works</h2>
<p>An ebook is a book sold as a digital file. A reader buys it online and reads it within minutes on a phone, tablet, computer, or Kindle e-reader. There is no printing, no stock to store, and no postage. You create the file once, and the same file can be sold many times.</p>
<p>The money side is simple. A store such as Amazon lists your book, takes the reader's payment, delivers the file, and keeps a share of the price. You receive the rest as a royalty, which is your percentage of each sale. Your job is to make a book worth buying and to help readers find it. Everything after this section is the detail of how to create and sell ebooks online, one stage at a time.</p>
<p>People buy ebooks because they want a problem solved faster than free searching allows. A good ebook saves time, organises scattered advice into one clear path, and gives steps in the right order.</p>
<p>You can start with no investment. Claude has a free plan, Canva has a free plan, and a KDP account costs nothing. Amazon only takes its share when a book sells. Paid extras, such as a Canva Pro plan or a hired cover designer, can wait until a book earns something.</p>
<h2 id="the-types-of-ebooks-that-sell-well">The types of ebooks that sell well</h2>
<p>Short, focused non-fiction sells best for beginners, because a clear promise is easy to understand in search results. Fiction can also sell well, but it usually needs several books in a series before income grows. These types are proven starting points:</p>
<ul>
<li>How-to guides that solve one problem, such as passing an exam, learning a software tool, or starting a small business.</li>
<li>Workbooks and planners with exercises, checklists, and trackers.</li>
<li>Cookbooks and meal plans for a clear audience, such as busy parents or people with a specific diet.</li>
<li>Language and study guides for students and job seekers.</li>
<li>Money, career, and freelancing guides based on real, checkable knowledge.</li>
<li>Children's picture books, which suit a visual tool like Canva.</li>
<li>Low-content books such as journals and log books, although competition there is now heavy and quality matters more than it did.</li>
</ul>
<p>Profitable niches share three traits. People search for the topic, people already pay for solutions, and you can say something accurate and useful about it. A niche is simply a narrow topic for a clear group of readers.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-choose-a-topic-readers-already-want">Step 1: Choose a topic readers already want</h2>
<p>More ebooks fail at this step than at any other. A book on a topic nobody searches for will not sell, however well it is written. Start from demand, then match it to what you know.</p>
<ol>
<li>List problems you can genuinely help with, from work, study, hobbies, or life experience.</li>
<li>Search the Kindle Store for each idea. Look for books with steady reviews, which show that readers buy in this niche.</li>
<li>Read the one star and three star reviews of competing books. Complaints show what readers wanted and did not get, and that gap is your book's angle.</li>
<li>Check that the topic is narrow. "Fitness" is too broad. "A twelve week home workout plan for busy parents with dumbbells" names a reader, a situation, and a result.</li>
<li>Write one sentence that names your reader, their starting point, and the result your book delivers. If you cannot write that sentence, the idea is not ready.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you are still deciding what to make first, our guide on <a href="/digital-products/how-to-create-your-first-digital-product" rel="noopener" target="_self">how to create your first digital product</a> walks through choosing and testing an idea before you build it.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-write-the-ebook-with-claude-ai">Step 2: Write the ebook with Claude AI</h2>
<p>Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic. You chat with it in plain language, and it can help you plan, draft, and improve text. Used well, it speeds up the slow parts of writing. Used carelessly, it produces flat, repetitive text that readers dislike and that can contain wrong facts. The workflow below keeps you in charge.</p>
<h3 id="set-up-claude">Set up Claude</h3>
<ol>
<li>Open claude.ai in your browser.</li>
<li>Create a free account with your email address or a Google account, and confirm your email if asked.</li>
<li>Start a new conversation. The free plan has usage limits that reset over time, so work chapter by chapter rather than asking for a whole book at once.</li>
<li>Keep one document on your computer as the master manuscript. Paste finished sections into it as you go, so your book never lives only inside a chat.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="the-writing-workflow">The writing workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li>Ask Claude to research your topic with you: the audience, their main problems, and the questions they ask most.</li>
<li>Ask for an outline with chapter titles and the goal of each chapter. Edit the outline yourself before any drafting starts.</li>
<li>Draft one chapter at a time. Give Claude the chapter goal, the key points, and the tone you want.</li>
<li>Ask for an introduction that states the reader's problem and the book's promise, and a conclusion that sums up the steps and gives a next action.</li>
<li>Ask for practical extras for each chapter: examples, short exercises, checklists, and common mistakes.</li>
<li>Proofread everything yourself. Check every fact, number, name, and instruction, because AI tools can state wrong things with confidence.</li>
<li>Cut repetition. AI drafts often say the same point three ways. Keep the clearest version and delete the rest.</li>
<li>Read the full manuscript aloud once. Awkward sentences are easy to hear, and easy to fix.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="copy-and-paste-prompts-you-can-use">Copy and paste prompts you can use</h3>
<p>For ideas:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Act as a research assistant. My knowledge areas are [your topics]. Suggest 10 specific ebook topics for beginners in [country or audience] that solve a clear problem. For each one, name the reader, the problem, and the result the book would deliver."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>For research:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"I am writing an ebook for [reader] about [topic]. List the 15 questions this reader asks most often, the mistakes they make, and the terms I should explain simply."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>For an outline:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Create a chapter outline for a beginner ebook called [working title]. The reader is [reader] and the result is [result]. Give each chapter a title, a one line goal, and three key points."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>For a chapter:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Write a draft of chapter [number], titled [title]. The goal is [goal]. Cover these points: [points]. Use simple English, short paragraphs, one practical example, and end with a short checklist. Do not invent statistics or quotes."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>For the introduction and conclusion:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Write an introduction for this ebook outline: [paste outline]. Start with the reader's problem, say what the book will help them do, and who it is for. Then write a conclusion that recaps the steps and gives one clear next action."</p>
</blockquote>
<p>For proofreading:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Check this chapter for repeated ideas, unclear sentences, and claims that need a source. List the problems first. Then give me a cleaner version that keeps my meaning and tone: [paste chapter]."</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="use-ai-responsibly">Use AI responsibly</h3>
<p>You own the final book, so you are responsible for everything in it. Never copy text from other books or websites, and rewrite AI output in your own voice with your own examples. Do not present AI guesses as facts. Amazon also has a rule you must know before you publish: if AI generated text, images, or translations in your book, you must tell Amazon during the publishing process, even if you edited the material heavily. Help with your own writing, such as brainstorming or grammar checks on your own text, counts as AI-assisted and does not need that disclosure. Part 6 of this guide shows where the disclosure question appears.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-edit-and-design-the-ebook-in-canva">Step 3: Edit and design the ebook in Canva</h2>
<p>Canva is a free design tool that runs in the browser. It can hold your manuscript, style it, and produce both your cover and a PDF edition. Kindle needs one extra conversion step after Canva, which Part 4 explains.</p>
<ol>
<li>Create a free account at canva.com.</li>
<li>Search Canva for "ebook" templates and pick a clean layout with readable body text. Avoid decorative designs that make long reading hard.</li>
<li>Design the cover first, because it sets your colours and fonts. A Kindle cover should use a custom size of 2560 by 1600 pixels, which is the size Amazon recommends. Keep the title large enough to read as a small thumbnail, use strong contrast, and add your author name.</li>
<li>Build the front pages: a title page with the book title, subtitle, and author name, then a copyright page with your name, the year, and a short "all rights reserved" line.</li>
<li>Add a table of contents page that lists your chapters, and keep the chapter titles identical everywhere.</li>
<li>Create one chapter page style and reuse it: the same heading font, body font, and spacing for every chapter. Consistency is what makes a self-made book look professional.</li>
<li>Add images and simple graphics only where they help the reader, such as a diagram or a checklist box, and check that every image is sharp at full size.</li>
<li>Use one or two readable fonts and a small colour palette across the whole book. Serif fonts suit long reading; clear sans serif fonts suit headings and workbooks.</li>
<li>Add page numbers in a PDF edition for direct sales. Kindle ebooks do not use fixed page numbers, because readers can change the text size.</li>
<li>Paste in your finished manuscript chapter by chapter, and check headings, lists, and spacing as you go.</li>
<li>Read the whole book inside Canva one last time, checking for missing pages, repeated pages, and broken sentences.</li>
<li>Export with Share, then Download, choosing PDF Print for the best quality PDF.</li>
</ol>
<p>The free plan covers a full ebook project. Canva Pro adds premium photos and elements and is optional. Respect Canva's licence rules: some elements carry conditions, designs built from stock content cannot be trademarked, and you should never use images you found randomly on the web.</p>

Amazon accepts PDF uploads, but the result is often poor, and Amazon itself recommends a reflowable format.</p>

<h2 id="step-5-publish-on-amazon-kdp-step-by-step">Step 5: Publish on Amazon KDP step by step</h2>
<p>Kindle Direct Publishing is Amazon's self-publishing platform. Publishing is free, you keep your rights, and your book can sell in Amazon stores around the world. One account covers ebooks and print books.</p>
<h3 id="create-your-account">Create your account</h3>
<ol>
<li>Go to kdp.amazon.com and sign in with an Amazon account, or create one. You must be at least 18 years old, and each person may hold only one KDP account.</li>
<li>Complete your account details with your legal name and address, exactly as they appear on your documents.</li>
<li>Turn on two-step verification to protect the account.</li>
<li>Add your payment details under the Getting Paid section. Part 7 of this guide explains exactly what to enter for Pakistan, India, and Bangladesh.</li>
<li>Complete the tax interview before you publish. Non-US authors fill in a short form called a W-8BEN inside the interview, and Amazon applies a default 30 percent withholding to US royalties until it is complete.</li>
</ol>
<h3 id="add-your-book">Add your book</h3>
<ol>
<li>From your Bookshelf, choose Create, then Kindle eBook.</li>
<li>Enter the book title and subtitle. Write them for a human first: the problem or result, then the audience.</li>
<li>Write the description in short paragraphs. State who the book helps, what it covers, and what the reader will be able to do. Never paste fake reviews or earnings claims into a description.</li>
<li>Choose two categories that match your topic closely, and enter seven keywords that readers would type into Amazon search. Use phrases from your research, not single vague words.</li>
<li>Upload the manuscript file (EPUB or the Kindle Create file) and then upload your cover image.</li>
<li>Answer the AI-generated content questions honestly. If Claude or another AI tool generated text or images that appear in the book, say so for each type: text, images, and translations. This information goes to Amazon and is not shown to readers on the book page.</li>
<li>Use the online previewer to check the whole book on phone, tablet, and Kindle views. Fix layout problems before you continue.</li>
<li>Set your price and royalty option, which Part 6 explains, and confirm your distribution territories.</li>
<li>Click Publish. Amazon reviews every book before it goes live. As of November 2026, Amazon says most ebooks are reviewed within 72 hours, although checks can take longer.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="step-7-how-you-get-paid-including-pakistan-india-and-bangladesh">Step 7: How you get paid, including Pakistan, India, and Bangladesh</h2>
<p>This is the part most guides skip, so read it carefully. KDP pays royalties monthly, roughly 60 days after the end of the month in which the sale was reported. Sales made in January are normally paid at the end of March. You can watch sales and estimated royalties in the Reports section of your KDP account at any time.</p>
<p>Amazon pays by bank transfer. It does not pay by PayPal. There are three methods, and the one you can use depends on where your bank account is:</p>

<td>The fastest route, paid into a bank account in a supported country</td>

<p>Thresholds are counted per marketplace, so small balances from smaller stores can take longer to reach you. As of November 2026, Amazon's payment options list covers direct deposit for bank accounts in many countries, including Pakistan and India. Bangladesh is not on that direct deposit list. Here is what that means in practice:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pakistan:</strong> You can link a normal Pakistani bank account and receive direct deposits. In the Getting Paid section, choose Pakistan as the bank country and enter your IBAN, which starts with PK, plus your bank's details as KDP asks for them. Your bank converts the payment into rupees at its own rate. If you already hold a Payoneer account, its receiving account details are an alternative, but a local IBAN account is now the simpler route for most authors.</li>
<li><strong>India:</strong> You can link an Indian bank account and receive direct deposits in rupees. Enter the account number and bank codes exactly as your bank gives them, and make sure the account holder name matches your KDP account name.</li>
<li><strong>Bangladesh:</strong> There is no local direct deposit option, so authors usually receive royalties through a payment service provider. Services such as Payoneer or Wise give you receiving account details in US dollars or other currencies, which you enter in KDP as the payment account. Since August 2026, Amazon only pays providers that belong to its Payment Service Provider Programme and have completed identity checks, and Payoneer and Wise are both on that list. Fees apply when you later move money to your local bank, so compare them before you commit.</li>
</ul>
<p>About tax: the tax interview in your account decides how much US tax Amazon withholds from your US royalties. Without a completed interview, withholding is 30 percent. Pakistan and India both have tax treaties with the United States, so authors there can claim a reduced treaty rate inside the interview; Pakistan authors will need their National Tax Number, and Indian authors their PAN. Bangladesh has no such treaty, so Bangladeshi authors should expect the full 30 percent withholding on US sales. Tax rules are personal, they change, and this guide is not tax advice. Check your position with your local tax authority or an accountant, and read Amazon's own guidance in the <a href="https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help" rel="noopener" target="_blank">KDP Help pages</a>.</p>
<p>If a payment is late, check three things in order. First, confirm the 60 day window has passed for that month's sales. Second, open Reports and then Payments in your KDP account to see whether a payment was issued. Third, check that your bank details and tax interview are still complete, because a wrong digit in an IBAN quietly blocks payment. If all three look right, contact KDP support from inside your account.</p>

<h2 id="ebook-earnings-a-worked-example">Ebook earnings: a worked example</h2>
<p>Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are not a promise. They show how the maths works so you can test your own prices.</p>
<p>Imagine a short guide priced at $4.99 on Amazon.com, on the 70 percent option, with a file size of about 1 megabyte. The delivery fee is about $0.15, so your royalty per sale is ($4.99 minus $0.15) multiplied by 70 percent, which is about $3.39.</p>
<ul>
<li>Selling 1 copy a day pays about $3.39 a day, or about $101.70 across a 30 day month.</li>
<li>Selling 5 copies a day pays about $16.95 a day, or about $508.50 a month.</li>
<li>Selling 10 copies a day pays about $33.90 a day, or about $1,017.00 a month.</li>
</ul>
<p>On the 35 percent option, a $2.99 book pays about $1.05 per sale, with no delivery fee. Five sales a day would pay about $157.50 a month. Notice what the example leaves out: tax withholding, bank conversion rates, and refunds all reduce the amount that reaches you. Also notice the harder truth: most new ebooks sell fewer than one copy a day at first. The examples show the ceiling of simple maths, not a forecast.</p>
<h2 id="step-8-market-your-ebook-and-help-readers-find-it">Step 8: Market your ebook and help readers find it</h2>
<p>Publishing is the halfway point. Readers cannot buy a book they never see, so give your book several honest routes to attention:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Amazon search:</strong> Your title, subtitle, seven keywords, and categories decide which searches show your book. Use the words readers type, and keep them accurate.</li>
<li><strong>Your launch price:</strong> A fair price and, if you enrol in KDP Select, its free promotion days can gather early readers. Never buy reviews or swap reviews, because Amazon bans both and can remove your book and account.</li>
<li><strong>Pinterest:</strong> Create pins for your book cover and for tips taken from its chapters, linked to the book page. How-to topics do well there because people save ideas to read later.</li>
<li><strong>Short video:</strong> Post short TikTok videos, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels that teach one tip from the book and mention where the full guide lives.</li>
<li><strong>Facebook:</strong> Share genuinely useful posts in groups where your readers spend time, follow each group's rules, and answer questions instead of dropping links.</li>
<li><strong>An email list:</strong> Offer a free chapter or checklist in exchange for an email address, then tell subscribers when new books appear. Our guide on <a href="/digital-products/how-to-sell-digital-products-with-email-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_self">how to sell digital products with email marketing</a> shows a simple setup.</li>
<li><strong>Your own website:</strong> A simple page for your book, written for search engines, keeps working while you sleep. If you later want to sell the PDF edition directly as well, our guide on <a href="/digital-products/how-to-launch-digital-product-on-gumroad" rel="noopener" target="_self">how to launch a digital product on Gumroad</a> covers that route.</li>
<li><strong>Genuine reviews:</strong> Ask early readers for honest reviews and say plainly that honesty is what you want. A small number of real reviews beats a large number of suspicious ones.</li>
</ul>
<p>Marketing works by repetition. Share one useful idea from the book each day where your readers already are, and let the book be the deeper answer.</p>
<h2 id="common-beginner-mistakes">Common beginner mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Choosing a topic with no demand, then blaming the platform when the book does not sell.</li>
<li>Publishing raw AI output without editing, fact checking, or adding anything of your own, which readers spot quickly and Amazon's quality checks can flag.</li>
<li>Uploading a fixed PDF as the Kindle file and leaving readers with tiny, unreadable pages.</li>
<li>Designing a cover whose title cannot be read at thumbnail size.</li>
<li>Ignoring copyright: copying text, using images you do not own, or using Canva elements outside their licence.</li>
<li>Pricing with no logic: copying another book's price instead of testing your own.</li>
<li>Skipping the payment and tax setup, then wondering why no money arrives.</li>
<li>Expecting instant sales and quitting before the book has had time to be found.</li>
<li>Breaking KDP rules, such as hiding AI-generated content or chasing fake reviews, which risks the book and the account.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="your-seven-day-action-plan">Your seven day action plan</h2>
<p>This plan turns the full process of how to create and sell ebooks online into one focused week. Each day ends with a short checklist.</p>
<p><strong>Day 1: Choose your niche.</strong> List your knowledge areas, check demand in the Kindle Store, read competitor reviews, and write your one sentence that names the reader and the result. Checklist: topic chosen, reader named, result written down.</p>
<p><strong>Day 2: Research and outline.</strong> Use the research prompt with Claude, gather the 15 questions readers ask, and build your chapter outline. Checklist: outline finished, chapter goals written, examples planned.</p>
<p><strong>Day 3: Write the ebook.</strong> Draft chapter by chapter with Claude, add your own examples, then proofread and cut repetition. Checklist: full draft saved in your master document, facts checked, introduction and conclusion written.</p>
<p><strong>Day 4: Edit and design in Canva.</strong> Build the cover at 2560 by 1600 pixels, add title and copyright pages, style every chapter, and export the PDF. Checklist: cover readable as a thumbnail, contents page matches chapter titles, whole book read once inside Canva.</p>
<p><strong>Day 5: Prepare the Kindle file.</strong> Run the PDF or manuscript through Kindle Create, fix chapter breaks and the contents list, and preview on phone and tablet views. Checklist: file exported, preview checked page by page, no fixed layout problems.</p>
<p><strong>Day 6: Create the KDP account and publish.</strong> Complete your details, bank information, and tax interview first. Then add the book, answer the AI content questions honestly, set your price and royalty option, and publish. Checklist: payment method saved, tax interview complete, book submitted for review.</p>
<p><strong>Day 7: Start marketing.</strong> Make your first pins and short videos, tell your email list or followers, and line up one useful post per day for the next two weeks. Checklist: three pieces of content published, book link checked, review requests sent to genuine early readers only.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-it-too-late-to-learn-how-to-create-and-sell-ebooks-online-in-2026">Is it too late to learn how to create and sell ebooks online in 2026?</h3>
<p>No. The market is more crowded than it was, which means weak books fail faster, but readers still buy clear, specific, well made books every day. A narrow topic, a readable cover, and honest marketing beat publishing early.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-really-start-an-ebook-business-with-no-money">Can I really start an ebook business with no money?</h3>
<p>Yes. Claude, Canva, Kindle Create, and KDP all have free routes, and Amazon charges nothing to publish. Your real investment is time.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-to-be-an-expert-or-a-professional-writer-to-sell-ebooks">Do I need to be an expert or a professional writer to sell ebooks?</h3>
<p>No. You need accurate, useful knowledge about one narrow topic and the discipline to check your facts. Many beginner ebooks teach a practical skill learned through work, study, or a hobby.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-should-my-first-ebook-be">How long should my first ebook be?</h3>
<p>A focused non-fiction ebook of about 10,000 to 20,000 words is a sensible first target. Length matters less than completeness: the book must deliver the result its title promises, with no padding.</p>
<h3 id="will-amazon-accept-a-book-written-with-ai-help">Will Amazon accept a book written with AI help?</h3>
<p>Yes, with a condition. Amazon allows AI-generated content, but you must disclose it during publishing when AI created text, images, or translations that appear in the book, even if you edited them. Using AI to brainstorm or polish your own writing counts as AI-assisted and does not need disclosure.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-upload-my-canva-pdf-straight-to-kdp">Can I upload my Canva PDF straight to KDP?</h3>
<p>You can, but you should not for a normal ebook. A PDF is a fixed layout, so Kindle readers cannot adjust the text, and the reading experience is poor. Build a reflowable Kindle file with Kindle Create or EPUB instead, and keep the PDF for direct sales.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-amazon-kdp-charge-to-publish">How much does Amazon KDP charge to publish?</h3>
<p>Nothing. There is no sign-up fee, listing fee, or monthly charge. Amazon earns its share from each sale through the royalty split you choose.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-difference-between-the-35-percent-and-70-percent-royalty-options">What is the difference between the 35 percent and 70 percent royalty options?</h3>
<p>The 70 percent option pays more per sale but requires your price to sit inside a set band for each store, and it subtracts a small delivery fee based on file size. The 70 percent band on Amazon.com is $2.99 to $12.99 as of November 2026. The 35 percent option works at any price, in every territory, with no delivery fee.</p>
<h3 id="how-and-when-does-kdp-pay-me">How and when does KDP pay me?</h3>
<p>KDP pays monthly by bank transfer, roughly 60 days after the end of the month of the sale. Direct deposit has no minimum balance on Amazon.com, while wire transfers and cheques wait until your balance reaches $100 per marketplace. KDP does not pay through PayPal.</p>
<h3 id="can-authors-in-pakistan-india-and-bangladesh-receive-kdp-payments">Can authors in Pakistan, India, and Bangladesh receive KDP payments?</h3>
<p>Yes, but the route differs. Pakistan and India are on Amazon's direct deposit list, so a local bank account works: an IBAN account in Pakistan and a standard account in India. Bangladesh is not on the direct deposit list, so authors there usually receive royalties through a payment service such as Payoneer or Wise, using the receiving account details those services provide.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-amazon-take-to-review-and-publish-a-new-ebook">How long does Amazon take to review and publish a new ebook?</h3>
<p>Amazon says most ebooks are reviewed within 72 hours of submission, as of November 2026. Reviews can take longer if Amazon needs to check content, rights, or quality issues, so publish before any launch date you announce.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-sell-the-same-ebook-on-amazon-and-on-my-own-website">Can I sell the same ebook on Amazon and on my own website?</h3>
<p>Yes, unless you enrol the ebook in KDP Select. Select asks for 90 days of exclusivity for the ebook edition, during which it cannot be sold or given away elsewhere in digital form. Outside Select, selling a PDF edition from your own page or a platform like Gumroad is allowed.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-copies-will-my-first-ebook-sell">How many copies will my first ebook sell?</h3>
<p>Nobody can promise a number, and you should distrust anyone who does. Many first books sell a handful of copies in their first month. Topic demand, your cover, your price, and steady marketing decide the rest.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>You now have the full route: pick a topic readers already want, draft it with Claude while keeping your own judgement in charge, design it cleanly in Canva, build a proper Kindle file, publish on KDP with your payment and tax details set up first, and market it a little every day. That is how to create and sell ebooks online without a publisher, a budget, or technical skills. Start with Day 1 of the action plan today, and let your first book teach you the process.</p>
