This guide explains what sells, how to build a sheet buyers can use, how delivery works for Excel and Google Sheets, and where to list your work.</p>

<h2 id="why-spreadsheets-sell">Why spreadsheets sell</h2>
<p>People pay for spreadsheets because building formulas takes time and skill. A buyer who wants to track a budget does not want to learn how to write a SUMIF formula or build a chart. They want to type in their numbers and see the answer. Your job is to do the hard part once, then sell the finished sheet many times.</p>
<p>Spreadsheets also solve a clear problem, which makes them easy to market. A mortgage calculator answers one question: what will I pay each month. A budget tracker answers another: where does my money go. When the problem is clear, the buyer can decide in seconds whether the sheet is worth buying.</p>
<h2 id="what-sells-trackers-calculators-and-dashboards">What sells: trackers, calculators and dashboards</h2>
<p>Budget trackers are the most common first product, because almost everyone needs one. Mortgage and loan calculators come next, because buyers want to test different rates and terms before they borrow. Business dashboards, invoice trackers, expense trackers, habit trackers and project trackers all have steady buyers too.</p>

<p>Pick one type first, and build it well. A budget tracker with clear categories, a yearly summary and a simple chart will outsell a folder of ten half finished sheets. Look at what sells on Etsy, read the reviews, and note what buyers praise and what they complain about, then build something better in the same niche.</p>
<h2 id="excel-or-google-sheets-which-to-build">Excel or Google Sheets: which to build</h2>
<p>Excel and Google Sheets look similar, but they behave differently, and buyers care about the difference. Excel files download to the buyer's computer, work offline, and support macros, which are small programs that automate tasks inside a workbook. Google Sheets lives in the browser, works on any device, and lets several people edit the same sheet at once.</p>
<p>Offer both versions where you can. Build the sheet in one app, then convert it to the other and test it, because conversion is not perfect. Notes can become comments, checkboxes can become the words TRUE or FALSE, and colours can shift. If a feature only works in one app, such as a macro that only runs in Excel, say so in the listing and sell the versions separately.</p>
<p>If you must pick one, pick the app your buyers use. State the format clearly in the title and the description, so a Google Sheets buyer does not buy an Excel only sheet.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-build-a-spreadsheet-buyers-can-use">How to build a spreadsheet buyers can use</h2>
<p>A good spreadsheet is easy to read and hard to break. Mark the input cells clearly, for example with a light colour and a label that says "Type your number here". Lock the formula cells, so a buyer cannot delete a formula by accident. Add an instructions tab that explains what to type, what each sheet does, and what the results mean.</p>
<p>Use sample data that looks real. A budget tracker with $0 in every cell tells the buyer nothing, while one filled with example rent, food and transport costs shows at once how it works. Add a chart that updates from the data, because a picture of the result is more useful than a table of numbers. Keep the design simple: one font, a few colours, and clear headings.</p>
<p>Example only: imagine a freelance project quote calculator. The buyer types 12 hours into one cell, a rate of $60 per hour into the next cell, and $50 of expenses into a third cell. The total cell multiplies 12 by $60, which is $720, then adds $50, which gives $770. If the buyer changes the hours to 15, the total becomes 15 times $60, which is $900, plus $50, which is $950. The maths is simple, but the buyer does not have to build it, and that is what they pay for. These numbers are an example only, and they are not typical results.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-test-before-you-sell">How to test before you sell</h2>
<p>Test every formula before you list. Check the normal case, such as a typical month of spending, and the edge case, such as a month with no income or a loan with a zero interest rate. Save the file, close it, reopen it, and check the totals again. A formula that works once but breaks after a save will cause refund requests.</p>
<p>Test the copy link from a fresh Google account. Open the link in a private browser window while signed in to a different account, and check that you can make a copy, that the formulas still work, and that no private data is visible. Check hidden tabs, because buyers can unhide them, and check any IMPORTRANGE formula, which pulls data from another sheet, because it will break if that sheet is private. Remove your own real data before you test, and use sample data instead.</p>
<h2 id="how-buyers-receive-their-copy">How buyers receive their copy</h2>
<p>Delivery differs for Excel and Google Sheets, and buyers get confused if you do not explain it. An Excel file is simple: the buyer downloads the XLSX file after payment, opens it in Excel, and saves their own copy. A Google Sheets template needs a link. You share the master sheet as view only, and the buyer makes their own copy from it.</p>
<p>To make a copy link, take the share link for your Google Sheet and change the ending from /edit to /copy. When a buyer opens that link, Google asks them to make a copy in their own Drive. Google's own help pages explain how to share files from Google Drive and control whether people can view, comment or edit, and you can read them in <a href="https://support.google.com/docs/answer/2494822" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Google's guide to sharing files from Drive</a>.</p>
<p>Most sellers deliver through a PDF. The PDF contains the download link for the Excel file, the copy link for the Google Sheet, and the instructions. On Etsy, the buyer downloads the PDF after payment. On your own store, you can email the same PDF after payment. Test the PDF yourself after you publish the listing, and check that both links work.</p>

Spreadsheet Nut is a niche marketplace that only sells spreadsheets, so its buyers arrive ready to buy, but it is smaller than Etsy. Your own store, built with WordPress and Easy Digital Downloads, or with Gumroad or Payhip, gives you control and the customer list, but you must bring your own traffic.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-price-your-spreadsheets">How to price your spreadsheets</h2>
<p>No reliable pricing range for spreadsheets was found in the research for this guide, so this section gives no price figures. Price by comparing similar sheets in your niche, and by thinking about how much work the sheet saves. A simple tracker with a few formulas is worth less than a dashboard with many sheets, charts and a mortgage calculator built in.</p>
<p>Bundles work well. A set of five related sheets, such as a budget tracker, a debt payoff sheet and a savings goal sheet, can sell as one pack, and buyers feel they get better value. Offer the Excel and Google Sheets versions together where you can, because buyers like having both. Do not set a price until you have looked at what similar sheets sell for on the same marketplace, and do not underprice, because a very low price signals low quality.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-write-a-listing-that-sells">How to write a listing that sells</h2>
<p>A good listing shows the sheet in use. Use screenshots with sample data, not empty cells, and show the chart and the summary page. Add a short video that shows you typing a number and watching the total change, because it proves the formulas work. State clearly whether the buyer gets an Excel file, a Google Sheets link, or both.</p>
<p>The description should list what is included, the number of sheets, the features, and what the buyer needs, such as Excel or a free Google account. Say what the sheet does not do, for example that it is not tax advice and does not replace an accountant. For a related product, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-sell-templates-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to sell templates online</a>, and for planners, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-create-and-sell-digital-planners-online" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to create and sell digital planners online</a>.</p>

<h2 id="how-to-protect-your-work">How to protect your work</h2>
<p>Lock the formula cells, so buyers can type in the input cells but cannot delete the formulas. Hide the workings sheets if you like, but remember that buyers can unhide them, so do not put anything private in a hidden tab. Add a terms of use file that says the buyer may not resell or share the sheet, and that they may use it for their own business.</p>
<p>Add a short note in the sheet itself that says who made it and that it may not be redistributed. If you find your sheet for sale by someone else, report it to the marketplace with proof, such as the original file with its creation date. Keep your working files, because they are the best proof of ownership.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-market-your-templates">How to market your templates</h2>
<p>Show the sheet solving a real problem. A short video that types in a monthly income and shows the savings goal update is more convincing than a product photo. Offer a free mini template, such as a simple weekly spending tracker, in exchange for an email address, then tell the list when you launch a new sheet.</p>
<p>Write a blog post or a video that teaches one skill the sheet uses, such as how to plan a monthly budget, and link to the sheet at the end. Buyers who learn from you are more likely to buy from you. For pricing help, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-price-digital-products" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to price digital products</a>, and for email marketing, read <a href="https://earnquill.com/digital-products/how-to-sell-digital-products-with-email-marketing" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to sell digital products with email marketing</a>.</p>
<h2 id="support-and-updates">Support and updates</h2>
<p>Buyers will ask questions, and most of them are the same: the link does not work, the formulas show an error, or the sheet looks different in their app. Reply within a day, ask which app and version they use, and send a fresh link or a short video that shows the fix. Keep a list of common questions and add the answers to your instructions tab.</p>
<p>When you improve a sheet, tell past buyers. Buyers do not get updates automatically from a Google Sheets copy, because their copy is separate from your master sheet, so say so in the listing and explain how updates work.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-what-to-expect">What it costs and what to expect</h2>
<p>You can start with free tools. Google Sheets is free, and LibreOffice Calc is a free alternative to Excel. A Microsoft 365 plan costs money each month. Etsy charges $0.20 per listing, and you may pay a one-off shop set up fee depending on your country, so check <a href="https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Etsy's Fees and Payments Policy</a> before you open a shop.</p>
<p>Expect a slow start. Most new shops take weeks or months to get regular sales, because they have no reviews and little search history. Plan to list at least 10 to 15 sheets before you judge the shop. Sales often come in waves, and income is lumpy at first. Do not expect steady daily income in the first months.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes">Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
<li>Leaving your own private data in the sheet, which buyers can see when they open it.</li>
<li>Using an IMPORTRANGE formula that points at a private sheet, so the buyer's copy shows an error.</li>
<li>Sharing the edit link to your master sheet instead of a copy link, which lets buyers change your original.</li>
<li>Skipping the fresh account test, so the first buyer finds a broken copy link.</li>
<li>Locking the input cells as well as the formulas, so buyers cannot type their numbers in.</li>
<li>Selling only an Excel version to buyers who use Google Sheets, or the reverse, without saying so.</li>
<li>Forgetting the instructions tab, so buyers do not know what to type or what the results mean.</li>
<li>Claiming the sheet gives tax or financial advice, when it is only a tracking tool.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="risks-and-scams-to-watch-for">Risks and scams to watch for</h2>
<p>Be careful of buyers who ask you to send the sheet before they pay, or who ask to move the sale off the marketplace to avoid fees. Both are against marketplace rules, and you lose all protection if you agree. Keep every sale inside the marketplace until the buyer has paid.</p>
<p>Watch for messages that claim your listing breaks a rule and ask you to click a link to fix it. These are often phishing messages. Only follow links from the official marketplace site or app. Do not pay anyone who offers guaranteed sales for a fee, because no one can guarantee sales.</p>

<h3 id="can-i-sell-spreadsheets-as-a-product">Can I sell spreadsheets as a product?</h3>
<p>Yes, you can sell spreadsheets as a digital product, and budget trackers, mortgage calculators and business dashboards are common examples. You deliver an Excel file, a Google Sheets copy link, or both, usually inside a PDF that the buyer downloads after payment. State the format clearly in the listing.</p>
<h3 id="is-it-profitable-to-sell-spreadsheets">Is it profitable to sell spreadsheets?</h3>
<p>It can be profitable over time, but earnings vary widely by niche, price, number of listings and marketing. Most new shops take weeks or months to get regular sales, because they have no reviews and little search history. Treat it as a slow business that grows with each new listing and each good review.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-let-buyers-copy-my-google-sheet">How do I let buyers copy my Google Sheet?</h3>
<p>Share the master sheet as view only, then change the share link ending from /edit to /copy. When a buyer opens that link, Google asks them to make a copy in their own Drive. Test the link from a fresh Google account before you list it, and check that the formulas still work in the copy.</p>
<h3 id="what-is-the-best-platform-to-sell-spreadsheet-templates">What is the best platform to sell spreadsheet templates?</h3>
<p>Etsy is the most common first choice, because buyers already search there for budget trackers and planners. Spreadsheet Nut is a niche marketplace for spreadsheets only. Your own store gives you control and the customer list, but you must bring your own traffic. Start with one channel, then expand once you have sales.</p>

A dashboard with many sheets and charts is worth more than a simple tracker, and a bundle of related sheets can sell as one pack.</p>
