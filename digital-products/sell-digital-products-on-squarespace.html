How to Sell Digital Products on Squarespace in 2026

To sell digital products on Squarespace, you need a paid plan with commerce features, a store page, and a product set to the Download type. You upload one file of up to 300 MB, set a price, and publish. Squarespace then sends the buyer a download link by email after checkout. This guide walks through the plan choice, the setup, the fees that catch people out, and the limits you need to know before you commit.

What you can sell on Squarespace

Squarespace supports a wide range of digital products. You can sell ebooks and guides as PDFs, printables and worksheets, photo presets, design templates, fonts, stock photos, music and audio files, and video content. You can also sell online courses through Course pages, membership access through Member Sites, and video libraries through paywalled video pages.

Each product type works differently. A download product sends the buyer a file by email. A course page gives the buyer access to lessons they work through on your site. A Member Site gates a collection of content behind a pricing plan. A video page can paywall a whole collection or single videos. Pick the type that matches how the buyer will use what you sell, because you cannot change the product type after you create it.

Squarespace also lets you sell services, such as coaching or consultancy, and you can take bookings through Acuity Scheduling. That is useful if your digital product comes with a live session, but the rest of this guide focuses on files and courses.

Step 1: Choose the right plan

You cannot sell on a free trial alone. You need a paid website plan, and the plan you pick changes what each sale costs you. Squarespace has four plans, and the prices below are the monthly rates when billed yearly, as shown on the pricing page in November 2026. Check the live pricing page before you buy, because plan prices change from time to time.

Plan Monthly cost, billed yearly, as of November 2026 Online store transaction fee Digital Product fee Card processing, domestic cards
Basic 19 US dollars 2 percent 7 percent 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars
Core 29 US dollars 0 percent 5 percent 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars
Plus 49 US dollars 0 percent 1 percent 2.7 percent plus 0.30 US dollars
Advanced 99 US dollars 0 percent 0 percent 2.5 percent plus 0.30 US dollars

The fee structure is the part most sellers miss. There are three layers: the plan price, the Digital Product fee on each digital sale, and the card processing fee. The Digital Product fee applies to download products, courses, and memberships. On Core it is 5 percent, which is a real cost on every sale, even though the online store transaction fee is 0 percent. Squarespace lists the full fee schedule in its official guide to choosing the right Squarespace plan.

For most people selling digital downloads, Core is the sensible starting point. It removes the 2 percent store fee, adds customer accounts and discounts, and costs 10 US dollars a month more than Basic. Plus only makes sense once you sell enough digital content for the drop from a 5 percent to a 1 percent Digital Product fee to cover the extra 20 US dollars a month. Advanced is for high volume stores.

Start with the 14 day free trial and build the whole shop before you pay for a plan. That way you only commit once the store is ready to sell.

Step 2: Connect a payment processor

Before you can sell anything, connect a payment processor. Squarespace gives you three options: Squarespace Payments, Stripe, and PayPal. Squarespace Payments is the native option and accepts cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Afterpay, Klarna, and ACH bank payments in the United States. PayPal can be connected alongside it so buyers can pay with PayPal or Venmo.

Two things are worth knowing before you choose. If you already use Stripe and you connect Squarespace Payments, it replaces Stripe as the processor for your Squarespace site. You can run Squarespace Payments alongside PayPal, but not alongside Stripe. And if you are new to Squarespace Payments, there is an 8 to 12 day holding period before you receive your first payout, after you connect your bank account, so factor that into a launch date.

Open the Payments settings panel, click Connect under your chosen processor, and follow the prompts. Compare the fees across the options first, because the total cost can vary with how and where you sell.

Step 3: Add your store page

If this is your first product, add a Store page to your site navigation. Open the Pages panel, click the plus icon, and choose Store. In version 7.1, click Products, Services, or Group Events, then click Store at the top of the window. Drag the page into your main navigation where you want it to appear.

Your download products will populate on this page automatically. If you sell more than a handful of products, set up product categories, such as Templates, Presets, and Guides, so buyers can browse. A buyer should reach any product within 2 to 3 clicks from your homepage.

Step 4: Add a download product

Open your store page, click the plus icon, and select Download as the product type. Remember that you cannot change the product type later, so be sure before you continue.

Upload your file. Each file can be up to 300 MB, and you can attach only one file per download product. If you sell a bundle, zip everything together into one file before you upload, as long as the total stays under 300 MB. If your file is larger, such as a long video course, host it on a cloud service such as Google Drive or Dropbox and deliver the access link through the product description or a follow up email instead.

Fill in the product details. Use a specific name, such as "Social Media Template Bundle for Canva", rather than a vague one such as "Template Pack". Write a description that says what is inside, who it is for, what software they need, and what they get after purchase. Set the price, and add a sale price if you want to show a discount. Upload preview images or mockups, because people cannot hold a digital file before they buy, so the images are the product experience.

After a buyer pays, Squarespace sends them an email with a download link. The link expires 24 hours after purchase. If a buyer clicks an expired link, Squarespace sends a new one automatically to the email used at checkout. If a buyer still has trouble, you can resend the link yourself from the order screen. Buyers with a customer account can also re-download from their account.

Step 5: Write a product page that sells

A good product page answers the buyer's questions before they ask them. Include the file format, the number of pages or templates, the file size, the software needed, and the delivery method. Add a short FAQ on the product page or the shop page: what software do I need, can I get a refund, how do I access my files, and what happens if my download link expires.

Use real preview images. Flat lays, mockups, screenshots of the product in use, and before and after images all help a buyer picture what they get. Write the product URL slug by hand, so it reads cleanly, such as shop/canva-social-media-templates, rather than a default address with a product number.

Add customer reviews if you have them. Squarespace has a product review feature for download products, and honest reviews build trust faster than any sales copy. Never invent reviews or pay for them, because that breaks trust and can break the platform's rules.

If you need help shaping the product itself, read our guide on how to create your first digital product, and if you need help with pricing, read our guide on how to price digital products.

Step 6: Test the checkout and launch

Before you announce anything, place a test order through Squarespace's test mode. Check that the checkout works smoothly, the download email arrives, the link works and delivers the right file, and the product images and description look right on desktop and on a phone.

Then launch. Share the product where your audience already is, and send an email to your list. Squarespace has built in Email Campaigns, discount codes, and announcement bars, so you can run the launch without extra tools. If you want to reach buyers through search and social media, read our guide on how to sell digital products with Pinterest, and compare Squarespace with marketplaces in our guides on how to sell digital products on Etsy and how to sell digital products on Shopify.

Squarespace also explains the whole process in its own guide to selling digital products on Squarespace, which covers courses, Member Sites, and video pages in more detail.

Worked example

Example only: these numbers are not typical results.

Imagine you sell a Canva template pack for 25 US dollars on the Core plan, and a buyer pays by card through Squarespace Payments in the United States.

  • Sale price: 25.00 US dollars
  • Digital Product fee at 5 percent: 1.25 US dollars
  • Card processing at 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars: 0.73 plus 0.30, which is 1.03 US dollars
  • Total fees: 1.25 plus 1.03, which is 2.28 US dollars
  • You keep per sale: 25.00 minus 2.28, which is 22.72 US dollars

With 20 sales in a month, you keep about 454.40 US dollars, minus the 29 US dollar plan cost, which is about 425.40 US dollars. On the Basic plan, the same 20 sales would keep about 434.40 US dollars before the 19 US dollar plan cost, because Basic adds a 2 percent store fee and a 7 percent Digital Product fee. At 20 sales a month, Core already comes out ahead by about 10 US dollars, and the gap grows as sales rise.

What it costs and what to expect

The Core plan costs 29 US dollars a month when billed yearly, or 39 US dollars a month when billed monthly, as of November 2026. On top of that, each digital sale costs you the 5 percent Digital Product fee plus card processing. There is no free plan, though the 14 day trial lets you build before you pay.

Expect to spend a weekend building the product and the shop, and a few hours on the launch. After that, each sale is automatic, though you will still answer buyer emails, especially about expired download links.

Be honest about the timeline. Squarespace brings you no buyers. Your sales come from your own audience, your email list, and search traffic. Most new sellers make a small number of sales in the first month, then grow slowly as reviews and search rankings build. A Squarespace store is a good home for your products, but it is not a source of traffic on its own.

Tax needs care. Squarespace automates United States sales tax, but European VAT for digital products needs manual configuration or a third party tax service. The UK government guidance on VAT rules for digital services explains how the place of supply rules work for digital products sold to consumers. Rules differ by country, so check with a qualified accountant before you sell into a new market.

Common mistakes

  • Picking the Basic plan to save money, then paying a 2 percent store fee and a 7 percent Digital Product fee on every sale.
  • Forgetting the Digital Product fee exists, and pricing as if only card processing applies.
  • Uploading a file larger than 300 MB, then finding out at the last step that it does not fit.
  • Trying to attach two files to one download product, when Squarespace only allows one file per listing.
  • Skipping the test order, then finding out on launch day that the download email or the link fails.
  • Writing a vague product name and description, when buyers need to know the format, the contents, and the software required.
  • Choosing Stripe and Squarespace Payments together, then finding that connecting Squarespace Payments replaces Stripe.
  • Launching before the payout holding period ends, and wondering why the first payout has not arrived.

Risks and scams to watch for

The main risk with Squarespace is the platform's limits, not a scam. The 300 MB file cap, the single file per listing, the 24 hour download link expiry, and the Digital Product fee are all real constraints. If your product is a large video course, Squarespace may be the wrong delivery method, and you should host the files elsewhere and deliver access links instead.

Watch for people who offer to build or grow your Squarespace store for a fee paid in advance, or who promise a set number of sales. No one can guarantee sales, and a real freelancer charges for work done, not for a promise. Never share your Squarespace password, and never give a stranger access to your payment processor account.

If you lose money to a scam, report it to your payment provider first, because they are the ones who may be able to reverse a payment. In the United States, you can also report fraud to the Federal Trade Commission, and the FTC explains how to report fraud on its consumer advice site. Readers elsewhere should use their own country's consumer protection body.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell digital products on Squarespace?

Yes. Squarespace supports digital downloads, online courses, memberships, ebooks, music, templates, software, and other digital files. Products are hosted directly on Squarespace with automatic delivery after purchase. You need a paid plan to sell, and the Core plan is the usual starting point for digital sellers.

What is the file size limit for digital products on Squarespace?

Each file can be up to 300 MB, and you can attach only one file per download product. If you sell a bundle, zip the files together into one file under 300 MB. For larger files, such as long video courses, host the file on a cloud service and deliver the access link after purchase.

Does Squarespace charge transaction fees on digital products?

Yes, in two layers. The Basic plan charges a 2 percent online store transaction fee and a 7 percent Digital Product fee. Core charges no store fee and a 5 percent Digital Product fee. Plus charges a 1 percent Digital Product fee, and Advanced charges none. Card processing fees apply on every plan, starting at 2.9 percent plus 0.30 US dollars on domestic cards.

How does digital product delivery work on Squarespace?

After purchase, Squarespace automatically sends the customer an email with a secure download link. The link expires 24 hours after purchase, and Squarespace sends a new link automatically if the buyer clicks after expiry. Customers with accounts can also access downloads through their account dashboard.

Can I sell courses on Squarespace?

Yes. Squarespace Courses lets you create structured lessons with video, audio, or text, organised into modules. Students can track progress and work at their own pace, and you can add quizzes, assignments, and downloadable resources. Course sales carry the same Digital Product fee as download products on your plan.

Do I need a website already to sell digital products on Squarespace?

No. Squarespace is a website builder and a store in one, so you can build the site and the shop together. Start with the 14 day free trial, build your store page and your first product, then choose a paid plan when you are ready to launch. If you already have a website elsewhere, you can still use Squarespace as a separate store.

Conclusion

To sell digital products on Squarespace, choose the Core plan for its 0 percent store fee, connect Squarespace Payments or Stripe, add a store page, upload one file under 300 MB as a Download product, write a clear product page with real previews, and test the checkout before you launch. Remember the Digital Product fee on every sale, and the 24 hour download link expiry, and price with both in mind.
