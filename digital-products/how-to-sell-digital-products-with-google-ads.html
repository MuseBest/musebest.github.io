Learn how to sell digital products with Google ads in 2026, from Search campaigns and keywords to landing pages, budgets, and tracking sales.

Learning how to sell digital products with Google ads starts with one useful fact: Google Ads reaches people who are already searching for what you sell, while most other ads interrupt people who were doing something else. That makes Google Ads powerful, but easy to waste money on, because every click costs you whether the visitor buys or not. This guide shows you how to set up a Search campaign, choose keywords, write ads, build a landing page, and judge the results honestly.

How Google Ads work for digital products

Google Ads shows your ad when someone searches for words related to your product. You bid on those words, and you pay each time someone clicks your ad. That cost depends on how many advertisers want the same words and how relevant your ad and landing page are.

For digital products, the buyer is often ready to buy. Someone who searches "buy wedding Lightroom presets" is closer to a purchase than someone who sees a preset ad while scrolling. That is the main strength of Google Ads, and it is why a small, well-chosen set of keywords can beat a large, sloppy one.

The risk is the cost. A click on a popular keyword can cost several US dollars, and a low-priced product may need many clicks to get one sale. That maths is why this guide starts with product fit, not with setup.

Before you start: is Google Ads right for your product?

Google Ads suits products that people search for by name or by problem. Ebooks, courses, templates, and presets all have search demand, because buyers type phrases such as "how to" and "best" plus the product type. It suits higher-priced products best, because a 100 US dollar course can afford a 2 US dollar click.

Check the demand before you spend anything. Type your product keywords into Google and see whether ads appear. If ads appear, read them and note what the advertisers promise, because those are your competitors.

You also need a product page that is ready for buyers. If you do not have one yet, build it first with our guide to create your first digital product, and price it with our guide on how to price digital products. Ads cannot fix a weak product page.

The Shopping ads warning (what Google does not allow)

This is the most important section in this guide, and none of the top guides for this keyword mention it. Google Shopping ads do not support most digital products. Google's own Merchant Center help page states that ebooks and digital books, not including audiobooks, are not allowed in Shopping ads, and that violations can lead to disapproval.

That means you must not list your ebook or digital download in Google Shopping or Merchant Center as if it were a physical product. Sellers who try have had their Merchant Center accounts suspended. Use Search ads for digital products instead, and read the Google Merchant Center help page on unsupported Shopping content before you touch Shopping at all.

Step 1: Set up your account and conversion tracking

Create a Google Ads account with the same Google account you use for your business, and set your currency and time zone carefully, because you cannot change the currency later. Add a payment method you control, and turn on two-factor authentication.

Set up conversion tracking before you launch your first campaign. A conversion is the action you want, which is usually a purchase. Set it up with the Google tag or Google Tag Manager, and test it with a real purchase before you spend money.

Check that the purchase event fires correctly, and check it again after any change to your checkout. Ads without conversion tracking are flying blind, because you cannot tell which keywords earn money and which only cost money.

If you need help with setup, the Google Ads Help centre is the official source for account setup, campaign types, and policy questions, and it is the first place to check when something does not work.

Step 2: Choose your campaign type (start with Search)

Google Ads offers four main campaign types for digital products: Search, Display, Video, and Performance Max. Start with Search, because it reaches people who are already looking for your product, and it is the easiest type to control and to judge.

Search campaigns show a text ad above or below the organic results when someone searches your keywords. Display campaigns show image ads on other websites, and they suit awareness more than direct sales. Video campaigns show ads on YouTube, and they suit products that need a visual demo. Performance Max uses automation across all of Google's channels, and it needs conversion data and a real budget to work well.

Campaign type Best for Start here?
Search Buyers who search for your product Yes, start here
Display Awareness and retargeting Later, for retargeting
Video Products that need a visual demo Later, if you have video
Performance Max Scaling a proven product Only after Search works

Step 3: Find keywords buyers actually type

Keywords are the words and phrases buyers type into Google, and choosing the right ones is the most important skill in Google Ads. Start with the words you would type if you wanted your own product, then check them with free tools.

Use Google's own suggestions: type your main phrase into Google and read the autofill suggestions and the related searches at the bottom of the results page. Then use Keyword Planner, Google's free keyword tool inside Google Ads, to check search volume and bids.

Choose a mix of keyword types. Exact match only triggers your ad for that exact phrase, phrase match triggers it for close variations, and broad match triggers it for related searches. Start with exact and phrase match, because broad match can spend your budget on searches that do not match your product.

Add negative keywords from the start. Negative keywords are words you do not want to trigger your ad, such as "free" if you sell a paid product, or "jobs" if you sell a course. A good negative keyword list saves more money than any other single setting.

Keyword type Example When to use it
Exact match Buy wedding Lightroom presets High intent, tight control
Phrase match Lightroom presets for weddings Close variations, still controlled
Broad match Wedding photo editing Discovery, use with care
Negative Free, jobs, torrent Block searches that will not buy

Step 4: Write ads that match the search

Your ad must match the search that triggered it. If someone searches "buy wedding Lightroom presets", your ad headline should say that you sell wedding Lightroom presets, and it should link to the wedding preset page, not to your homepage.

Write three headlines and two descriptions for each ad group, and let Google test which combination works best. Use plain words, name the benefit, and include the price if it helps the buyer decide. Do not make claims you cannot prove, because Google will disapprove ads with those claims.

Step 5: Build a landing page that converts

Your landing page is the page the ad links to, and it must match the ad exactly. If the ad promises wedding presets, the page must show wedding presets, with before and after samples, the price, and a clear buy button.

Keep the page focused on one product and one action. Show the product, name the benefit, answer the main objections, and make the buy button easy to find on a phone.

Make the page fast. A slow page loses buyers before they see the product, and Google also charges more for clicks to slow pages through Quality Score, which is Google's rating of how relevant your ad, keywords, and landing page are to each other.

Step 6: Set a test budget you can afford to lose

Your test budget is money you may lose completely, so set it at an amount you can afford. Seller guides disagree on the figure: one says 5 to 10 US dollars a day, while another says 10 to 50 US dollars a day. Both are starting ideas, not facts.

Work it out from your own numbers. If your product costs 25 US dollars and a click costs 1 US dollar, a test of 10 US dollars a day buys 10 clicks a day, so 7 days costs 70 US dollars for 70 clicks. If your cost per click is higher, you need a bigger test or a cheaper keyword.

Example only: at 1 US dollar per click, 70 US dollars buys 70 clicks. If 2 in 100 clicks buy a 25 US dollar product, that is about 1.4 sales, or 35 US dollars in revenue. 70 multiplied by 0.02 equals 1.4, and 1.4 multiplied by 25 equals 35. That test loses 35 US dollars. That is an example only, not a promise of results.

Do not raise the budget on a campaign that loses money. Fix the keywords, the ad, or the page first, and only scale a campaign that already earns more than it costs.

Step 7: Read the numbers and decide what to do next

Let your campaign run for at least a week before you judge it. Watch click-through rate, cost per click, conversion rate, and cost per acquisition, which is the cost of each sale.

Use simple decision rules. If your click-through rate is below 1 percent, rewrite the headlines. If your cost per click is high and your Quality Score is low, improve the landing page and the keyword match. If you get clicks but no sales after 50 to 100 clicks, the problem is usually the landing page, not the ad.

Pause keywords that spend money without sales, and pause ads that get clicks but no conversions. Pausing is not failure. It is the test doing its job, and the money you save can fund the next test.

Bidding strategies, in plain words

A bidding strategy tells Google how to spend your budget. Maximise Clicks suits a new campaign with no sales data. Maximise Conversions suits a campaign that already has sales data. Target CPA and Target ROAS aim for a cost or return you set, but both need enough sales data to work well.

Start with Maximise Clicks or a manual bid, so you control the cost per click while you learn. Move to Maximise Conversions once you have a steady flow of sales, and only use Target CPA or Target ROAS when you have enough data to set a realistic target.

Retargeting people who did not buy

Retargeting, also called remarketing, shows ads to people who visited your site but did not buy. It is one of the cheapest uses of Google Ads, because the audience already knows your product. Set up a Display retargeting campaign for visitors to your sales page, and show them a simple ad that reminds them of the product.

Keep the retargeting audience small and recent, such as visitors from the last 30 days, and limit how often the same person sees the ad. Retargeting works best when the product page is good and the visitor simply left to think about it.

Google Ads compared with Facebook Ads

Google Ads and Facebook Ads suit different buyers. Google Ads reaches people who search for your product, so the intent is high and the clicks cost more. Facebook Ads reach people based on interests, so the intent is lower and the clicks cost less.

Choose Google Ads when buyers search for your product by name or by problem, and when your product price can afford the cost per click. Choose Facebook Ads when your product is visual, when buyers do not search for it yet, or when you want to build an audience with video. Many sellers use both. What it costs and what to expect

Your costs are the ad spend, the product cost, and your time. Ad spend is the main cost, and it can be lost with no sales if the test fails. A first test of 5 to 10 US dollars a day for a week costs 35 to 70 US dollars, and you should expect to lose some or all of it while you learn what works.

Expect a slow start. Most first campaigns lose money, because the keywords, the ad, and the page all need testing. Sellers who succeed usually test for weeks before they find a combination that earns more than it costs. If you need sales this week, ads are the wrong choice, and our guides to promote digital products with Pinterest and sell digital products with email marketing offer slower but cheaper routes.

Do not believe the claim that Google Ads is low cost, zero risk, and high return. That claim appears in one of the top guides for this keyword, and it is not true. A test budget can be lost with no sales, and a beginner who bids on popular keywords can spend fast.

Common mistakes

  • Listing a digital product in Google Shopping, which does not allow ebooks and digital books.
  • Sending ad traffic to your homepage instead of the specific product page.
  • Using broad match keywords with no negative keywords, so the budget goes to irrelevant searches.
  • Starting with Performance Max before you have any conversion data.
  • Setting a Target CPA or Target ROAS with no sales data, so the ads stop showing.
  • Judging a campaign after one day, before Google has had time to learn.
  • Ignoring Quality Score, so you pay more for every click than you need to.
  • Raising the budget on a losing campaign instead of fixing the keywords, the ad, or the page.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be careful with anyone who promises to run your Google Ads for a fee and guarantees sales or a top ranking. No one can guarantee sales from ads, and a "guaranteed results" offer is a warning sign. Ask any agency for real references.

Watch out for courses that promise a secret Google Ads method. The basics in this guide are the method: the right keywords, a matching ad, a focused landing page, and careful testing. A course that hides its method behind a paywall is selling the promise, not the skill.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on Google Ads to get started?

Start with a test you can afford to lose, such as 5 to 10 US dollars a day for a week for a low-priced product. Higher-priced products may need 10 to 50 US dollars a day. Work the budget out from your product price and your cost per click, and only scale a campaign that already earns more than it costs.

Can I sell digital products with Google Shopping ads?

No, not in most cases. Google's own Merchant Center help page states that ebooks and digital books, not including audiobooks, are not allowed in Shopping ads, and violations can lead to disapproval. Use Search ads for digital products instead, and do not list a digital product in Merchant Center as if it were physical.

What is the best Google Ads campaign type for digital products?

Search is the best first campaign type, because it reaches people who are already searching for your product. Display suits retargeting, Video suits products that need a visual demo, and Performance Max suits scaling a product that already sells.

How do I track digital product sales from Google Ads?

Set up conversion tracking with the Google tag or Google Tag Manager before you launch, and test it with a real purchase. Compare Google Ads figures with your own checkout records. Without conversion tracking, you cannot tell which keywords earn money.

Why is my Google Ads campaign not converting?

The most common causes are a mismatch between the ad and the landing page, keywords that are too broad, or a slow or unfocused landing page. Check the search terms report for irrelevant searches, add negative keywords, and test the landing page before you spend more.

Conclusion: how to sell digital products with Google Ads in 2026

That is how to sell digital products with Google Ads in 2026: check that buyers search for your product, stay away from Shopping ads for digital products, set up conversion tracking first, start with a Search campaign, choose exact and phrase match keywords with negative keywords, write ads that match the search, send traffic to a focused landing page, and run a small test budget you can afford to lose. Read the numbers, fix the weak link, and scale only what already earns.
