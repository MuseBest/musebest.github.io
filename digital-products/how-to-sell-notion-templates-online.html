How to Sell Notion Templates Online in 2026

Learning how to sell Notion templates online is different from selling a file, because a Notion template is not a file at all. It is a ready-made workspace inside Notion, which is a note-taking and planning app. The buyer clicks a link, duplicates the workspace into their own Notion account, and starts using it. There is nothing to download, nothing to print, and nothing to install. That makes the product easy to deliver, but it also creates problems that file sellers never face: a shared link can be passed on, a database relation can break when it is duplicated, and a buyer can ask for edit access to your master copy.

This guide shows you how to build a template that works in three minutes, how to publish it so buyers can duplicate it, where to sell it, how to price it, how to protect the link, and how to get paid. It also covers the rules for sellers outside the United States, which most guides skip.

What a Notion template is, and why it is different from a file

A Notion template is a page, or a set of pages, inside Notion, with databases, views, and instructions already set up. A database in Notion is a table, or a board, that stores linked items, such as tasks, clients, or habits. A rollup is a field that pulls a value from a linked database, such as the total of all tasks in a project. When a buyer duplicates your template, they get a copy of all of it in their own workspace, which is their own private Notion account area.

That is different from a PDF or a spreadsheet. A file sits on the buyer's computer. A template lives in the buyer's Notion account, and it keeps working as long as they use Notion. It is also different from a digital planner, which is a PDF used in a tablet app. For the planner route, read how to create and sell digital planners online in 2026.

Buyers choose Notion templates because they want a system, not a blank page. A student wants a study system. A freelancer wants a client tracker. A small business owner wants a content calendar. Your job is to build the system so well that the buyer can use it in three minutes, without watching a long video first.

What makes a template sell

A template sells when it solves one clear problem for one clear person. "A content calendar for solo YouTubers" sells better than "the ultimate life OS", because the buyer knows at once who it is for and what it does. A template that tries to do everything confuses the buyer, and a confused buyer does not buy.

A good template has five parts. First, a navigation page that shows the buyer where everything is. Second, one or two databases that hold the real work, such as tasks or clients, linked together so that a change in one place shows up in the other. Third, a short onboarding page that tells the buyer what to do first, in three steps. Fourth, example content that shows how the template works, such as three sample tasks already filled in. Fifth, a clean design, with consistent colours and no clutter.

Do not build a template with ten databases and fifty pages. Buyers open it, feel lost, and ask for a refund. Build the smallest template that solves the problem well, then add one extra feature only if it helps the buyer finish the job faster.

Step 1: Build it

Start with the buyer, not with the features. Write one sentence: "This template helps a freelance writer track clients, pitches, and invoices in one place." Then list the three things the buyer must be able to do: add a client, add a pitch, and see which invoices are unpaid. Build only what serves those three things.

Build the databases first. A client database holds names, contact details, and rates. A pitch database holds the pitch title, the client it belongs to, the status, and the fee. Link the two, so that each pitch shows its client, and each client shows its pitches. Add a rollup that totals the unpaid invoices for each client, so the buyer can see at a glance who owes them money.

Then build the navigation page. Put the most used views at the top: "Tasks due this week", "Unpaid invoices", and "Pitches waiting for a reply". Add a short "Start here" block at the top of the page, with three steps: duplicate the template, delete the sample data, and add your first client. Keep the instructions to three steps, because buyers will not read a long manual.

Add example data, but keep it clean. Use realistic names, such as "Sample Client", and realistic numbers. Do not use your real clients, your real income, or your personal notes. A buyer who finds your private data in a template will not trust you again.

Step 2: Clean it and test duplication in a second account

Before you sell a template, clean it. Remove your personal notes, your test pages, and any data that belongs to you. Check every page for a link to a page you did not mean to share. Check that every database view shows the right items, and that no view is empty by mistake.

Then test the duplication in a second account. This is the most important test in this guide, and most new sellers skip it. Make a free Notion account with a different email address, open your template link in that account, and click "Duplicate". Then check the copy: do the databases work, do the relations link correctly, do the rollups show the right numbers, and does the navigation page look right? If a relation breaks, fix it in the master copy and test again.

Also test what happens when the buyer has a free Notion plan. Some features, such as large file uploads, work differently on a free plan. State in your listing what the buyer needs: a free Notion account is enough for most templates, but say so clearly.

Step 3: Publish it as a Notion Site with Duplicate as template on

To sell a template, you publish it as a Notion Site. A Notion Site is a public web page made from a Notion page. Open the page, click "Share", then "Publish", and turn on the publish setting. Then turn on "Duplicate as template", which lets anyone who opens the page copy it into their own workspace. Turn off editing and commenting for visitors, so that nobody can change your master copy.

Check the publish settings carefully: the link should never expire, editing should be off, commenting should be off, and "Duplicate as template" should be on. If any of these is wrong, the buyer may not be able to duplicate the template, or a stranger may be able to edit your master copy.

Set up a creator profile if you plan to sell on the Notion Marketplace. The profile is at app.notion.com/profile, and it is where your templates are listed. You will need to join the waitlist, and the review may take a few months, according to the official Notion help page. Do not promise buyers a Marketplace listing until you are approved.

Step 4: Choose where to sell

There are four main places to sell a Notion template: the Notion Marketplace, Etsy, Gumroad, and your own store. Each one has different fees, different buyers, and different rules.

Step 5: Price it

Notion templates usually sell for 9 to 49 dollars, according to competitor guides, with the price set by depth. A simple tracker with one database might sell for 9 to 15 dollars. A full system with linked databases, dashboards, and a setup guide might sell for 29 to 49 dollars. Price by the problem it solves, not by the number of pages.

Cheap templates lose margin fast. On a 5 dollar sale on the Notion Marketplace, the fee is 8 percent plus 0.40 dollars, which is 0.80 dollars, leaving 4.20 dollars. On a 15 dollar sale, the fee is 1.60 dollars, leaving 13.40 dollars. On a 20 dollar sale, the fee is 2.00 dollars, leaving 18.00 dollars. On a 30 dollar sale, the fee is 2.80 dollars, leaving 27.20 dollars. The maths is simple: the fixed 0.40 dollar fee hurts small sales most, so do not price a useful template at 5 dollars.

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine you sell a client tracker template for 20 dollars on the Notion Marketplace, and you sell 20 copies in a month. That is 400 dollars in sales. The Marketplace fee is 8 percent plus 0.40 dollars per sale, which is 2.00 dollars per sale, or 40 dollars in total. You keep 360 dollars before tax and before any currency conversion fee. If you are outside the United States, Notion charges an extra 1 percent foreign exchange fee, which is 4 dollars on 400 dollars of sales, leaving 356 dollars. That is an example, not a promise.

Step 6: Write the listing and the setup guide buyers receive

Your listing must answer three questions: what problem does this solve, who is it for, and what will the buyer get? Show screenshots of the navigation page, the main database, and one dashboard view. Use real screenshots from the template, not mockups of a different product. State what the buyer needs: a free Notion account, and about three minutes to duplicate and set up.

Write a one-page setup guide that the buyer receives with the template. It should cover: how to duplicate the template, how to delete the sample data, how to add their first item, and what to do if a relation looks broken. Keep it to one page. Buyers will not read a ten-page manual, but they will read one page.

If you sell on Etsy or Gumroad, package the duplicate link inside a PDF. The PDF should have the template name, the duplicate link, the setup guide, and your terms, which are the rules for use, such as "for personal use, do not share the link". Test the PDF link before you publish, and test it again after you publish, by buying your own listing.

Step 7: Protect the link

A Notion template is a link, and a link can be shared. You cannot stop sharing completely, but you can make it harder and less useful.

First, keep the live page private except for the published site link, and do not share the edit link with anyone. Second, use access locking, which is a Notion setting that stops a duplicated template from being shared to another workspace. The official help page describes two settings, Restricted and Open, and you should choose Restricted for a paid template. Third, version the link: if a link is shared widely, make a new copy of the template, publish the new copy, and send the new link to your buyers. Fourth, put your terms in the setup guide, and state that the template is for the buyer's own use.

Do not share your master copy. Buyers should duplicate the published site, not edit your master page. If a buyer asks for edit access to the master file, say no, and send them the duplicate link again with the setup guide.

Getting paid: approval, payout holds, and country rules

The Notion Marketplace has rules that surprise new sellers, so here they are, from the official Notion help page read in full on 2026-11-09. You must join the waitlist and be approved, and the review may take a few months. Once approved, you onboard with Stripe, which is the payment service Notion uses, to sell directly. All sales are in US dollars. Creators outside the United States pay an extra 1 percent foreign exchange fee. Payouts are made every two weeks, on Thursdays at 9am Pacific time, with a minimum balance of 20 dollars, and funds are held for 14 days before payout. Buyers can ask for a refund within 14 days, and a refunded copy is removed from their workspace.

There is also a country rule that matters for many readers. Creators in countries where direct Marketplace selling is not available can still list a paid template that links to a third-party site, such as Gumroad or Etsy, where the buyer pays. Check the eligible countries list on the Notion help page before you plan your launch. For the official rules, read Sell templates on Notion Marketplace.

Tax rules differ by country. Notion handles regional sales tax or VAT as the merchant of record, but your own income tax is your responsibility. Keep records of every sale, and check your local tax authority or a qualified accountant before your first tax deadline. This guide gives no tax advice, and the US tax detail in one competitor guide does not apply to readers in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, or elsewhere.

How to get first buyers

A free template is the best way to get your first buyers. Make a small free version, such as a simple habit tracker, and offer it in exchange for an email address, or list it free on the Marketplace. Buyers who use the free template and like it are more likely to buy the paid one.

A short demo video also helps. Record your screen for two minutes, show the template in use, and post it on Pinterest, YouTube Shorts, or in a niche community. Show the buyer doing one real task, such as adding a client and seeing the unpaid total update. That one clip does more than ten screenshots.

Write one tutorial for the problem your template solves, such as "How to track freelance clients in Notion", and link to the template at the end. For the general template method, read how to sell templates online.

What it costs and what to expect

Your costs are low. A free Notion account is enough to build and sell a template. Etsy charges 0.20 dollars per listing, and the Marketplace charges only when you sell. Your main cost is time: a good template takes 10 to 30 hours to build, clean, and test.

Income varies widely, and no honest guide can promise a figure. Some templates sell a few copies a month, and some sell many more. Your results depend on the problem you solve, the quality of the template, the price, and how many people see it. Expect a slow start, and expect to wait for Marketplace approval if you choose that route. Do not build ten templates before you have sold one. Build one, test it, list it, and learn from the first buyers.

Common mistakes

  • Building an all-in-one life system that tries to do everything, instead of one template that solves one problem.
  • Skipping the duplication test in a second account, then learning from a buyer that a database relation is broken.
  • Sharing the master link instead of the published duplicate link, so buyers can edit your original.
  • Pricing a useful template at 5 dollars, then losing a large share to the fixed fee.
  • Building before validating, so you spend 30 hours on a template nobody searched for.
  • Forgetting the setup guide, so buyers message you with the same three questions.
  • Using a PLR template, which is a template made by someone else that you resell, because it risks duplicate products and licence trouble.

Risks and scams to watch for

Watch for buyers who ask for edit access to your master file, or who ask you to send the template by email before they pay. Real buyers buy through the Marketplace, Etsy, or Gumroad, and they duplicate the published link after payment. Never share your master copy.

Watch for refund requests from buyers who duplicated the template and then changed their mind. On the Marketplace, buyers can ask for a refund within 14 days, and a refunded copy is removed from their workspace. On Etsy or Gumroad, state your refund terms clearly in the listing, and help the buyer first if the template does not work as described.

Also watch for the risk of outdated templates. Notion changes features from time to time, and a template built on an old feature may break. Check your templates every few months, update them when Notion changes something, and tell past buyers how to get the new version.

Frequently asked questions

How much money can I make selling Notion templates?

There is no reliable typical figure, because earnings depend on the problem you solve, the price, and how many buyers find the template. Some sellers earn a few dollars a month from one template, and some earn much more from several templates. Treat any income figure in a guide as a report, not a promise.

Do I need a business licence to sell Notion templates?

It depends on where you live. Some countries require a business registration for any selling, and some do not. Check your local rules before you start, and ask a qualified accountant if you are unsure.

What are Notion Marketplace fees in 2026?

Notion charges 8 percent plus 0.40 dollars per transaction, and Notion is the merchant of record. Creators outside the United States pay an extra 1 percent foreign exchange fee. These figures are from the official Notion help page, as of November 2026.

How long does Notion Marketplace creator approval take?

The official help page says the review may take a few months. There is no fixed time, so do not plan a launch date that depends on approval. List on Etsy or Gumroad while you wait.

Can I sell Notion templates on Etsy and Gumroad at the same time?

Yes. You can sell the same template on more than one channel, as long as each listing has its own duplicate link and its own setup guide. Keep the price consistent, or buyers will notice the difference.

How do I stop buyers from sharing my Notion template link?

You cannot stop sharing completely, but you can make it harder. Use access locking set to Restricted, keep the master page private, version the link if it is shared widely, and state in your terms that the template is for the buyer's own use.

Conclusion: build one template, test the duplication, list it

Learning how to sell Notion templates online comes down to a simple sequence. Build one template that solves one problem, clean it, test the duplication in a second account, publish it with Duplicate as template on, price it above the cheap trap, and protect the link. Then make a free version to bring in your first buyers, and learn from what they ask.

Your next step is to write the one-sentence problem your template solves, and check it against what buyers search for.
