How to Sell Digital Products with YouTube in 2026: Guide

To sell digital products with YouTube, you make free videos that solve a real problem, then link the matching product, such as a template, a preset, a printable or an ebook, in the description, the pinned comment and the end screen. This guide explains which videos sell which products, how to link your store, how Shorts can bring buyers, and how to track what works.

How to sell digital products with YouTube: the basics

YouTube works as a sales channel because it is a search engine as well as a video site. People search for how to do something, watch a video that helps them, and then buy the product that makes it easier. A video that teaches a skill keeps sending traffic for months, long after you publish it, because it keeps appearing in search results.

Give free value first, then offer the product, because a video that teaches something useful earns trust, and trust is what turns a viewer into a buyer. The product should be the natural next step after the free video, not a surprise at the end. This guide covers templates, presets, printables, planners and ebooks. It does not cover selling courses on YouTube, because that has its own guide, how to sell courses on YouTube.

Matching products to videos

Each product type suits a different video. A template sells best in a tutorial that shows it in use. A preset sells best in a before and after video. A printable sells best in a planning or organisation video. An ebook sells best in a video that teaches the same topic in short form. Match the video to the product before you film anything.

Product Video that sells it Why it works
Canva templates Tutorial using the template Viewers see the result and want it
Lightroom presets Before and after edit The change is visible in seconds
Printables Planning or organisation video Viewers want the tool you used
Planners Daily routine or reset video The planner fits a habit they want
Ebooks Short lesson on the same topic The book goes deeper than the video
Workbooks Workshop or worked example Viewers want the pages to follow along

Plan one product per video. A video that offers five different products confuses viewers, while a video with one clear product and one link gets more clicks. If you sell several products, make a separate video for each one.

Ten video formats that sell digital products

You do not need ten kinds of video to start, but knowing the formats helps you plan. Pick three that fit your product, and repeat them.

  1. A trailer that shows what the product is and who it is for.
  2. A tutorial that teaches a skill and uses the product throughout.
  3. A how-to video that solves one problem step by step.
  4. A free sample video that gives away part of the product, such as one template from a pack.
  5. A behind the scenes video that shows how you made the product.
  6. A comparison video that shows the product next to doing the job by hand.
  7. A frequently asked questions video that answers buyer questions on camera.
  8. A repurposed blog post, turned into a video with the product linked below.
  9. An interview with a buyer or a creator who uses the product.
  10. A Short, under 60 seconds, that shows the product working in one quick clip.

The tutorial and the free sample are the strongest formats for most sellers, because they prove the product works before the viewer pays. Record them first.

How to set up your store

Your store is where the sale happens, so set it up before you publish product videos. Gumroad suits simple digital downloads with a quick setup. Etsy suits templates and printables with built in search traffic. Stan Store suits creators who sell from a link in their bio. Shopify suits sellers with many products who want full control. Fourthwall suits creators who also sell physical products. Each store has its own fees, and fees change, so check the current fee page of any store before you rely on a figure.

Store Best for YouTube sellers Setup
Gumroad Simple downloads, fast start Quick, no monthly fee to start
Etsy Templates, printables, planners Shop setup with listing fees
Stan Store Link in bio selling Quick, monthly fee
Shopify Many products, full control More setup, monthly fee
Fourthwall Digital and physical products Quick, connects to YouTube Shopping

Pick one store to start with, and link every video to it. A buyer who must hunt for the link will give up. Etsy charges a $0.20 listing fee and a 6.5 percent transaction fee, plus payment processing fees, according to Etsy's Fees and Payments Policy. For the Etsy route, read how to sell digital products on Etsy, and for the Shopify route, read how to sell digital products on Shopify.

How to link your product in every video

Link the product in three places, because viewers look in different spots. Put the link in the first two lines of the description, so it shows without clicking to expand. Pin a comment with the same link, and say in the video that the link is in the pinned comment. Add an end screen that links to the product page or to a video about the product.

Add the store link to your channel banner and your channel About page, so new visitors can find it. Say the product name out loud in the video, because many viewers listen without reading the description.

YouTube Shopping in general terms

YouTube Shopping is a set of features that lets eligible creators connect products to their videos, Shorts and live streams. In general terms, you connect your store to your channel, then you can tag products in your videos so viewers can tap and buy. The exact steps and eligibility rules change, and they were not checked on Google's own help pages in this session, so read YouTube's official help before you plan around them.

YouTube's own help page on tagging products explains that only eligible creators can tag products, and that tags must follow its guidelines, such as only tagging products that are clearly shown in the video. You can read it in YouTube's help page on tagging products in your content. In general, selling your own products through YouTube Shopping needs membership of the YouTube Partner Programme and a connected store, and the subscriber threshold is lower than many older guides say, so check the current rules in YouTube Studio before you rely on a figure.

Do not build your whole sales plan on YouTube Shopping alone. Description links, pinned comments and end screens work for every channel, even a small one, while Shopping needs eligibility first.

Faceless channels: selling without appearing on camera

You can sell digital products on YouTube without showing your face. Faceless channels use screen recordings, voiceovers, slides, animations and stock footage. A template seller can record their screen while they edit a template. A preset seller can show before and after photos with a voiceover. A printable seller can film their hands filling in a planner.

Keep the voice natural, and use captions, because many viewers watch without sound. Use a clear script, and cut any part that does not help the viewer. A faceless channel needs the same plan as any channel: one product per video, one link, and a clear next step.

YouTube SEO for product videos

YouTube SEO means helping YouTube and viewers find your video. Put the main topic in the title, in plain words, such as "How to Plan Your Week with a Printable Planner". Write the first two lines of the description to say what the video teaches and what product it links to, because those lines show in search. Add a few tags that match the topic, and make a thumbnail that shows the product and the result, not just your face.

Group related videos into playlists, such as one playlist for planning videos and one for template tutorials. Playlists keep viewers watching your videos instead of leaving, and they help YouTube understand what your channel is about. Check which titles get clicks in YouTube Studio, and make more videos with titles in the same style.

Shorts and repurposing

Shorts are vertical videos under 60 seconds, and they can bring new viewers to your channel fast. A Short that shows a template changing colour in a few seconds, or a printable being filled in, can reach people who would never find your long videos. Link the full product in the Short's description, and mention it in the video, because Shorts viewers scroll fast.

Repurpose every long video into several Shorts. Cut one tip from a tutorial into a Short, turn a before and after into a Short, and turn a frequently asked question into a Short. Each Short points back to the long video or to the product. This gives you many chances to reach buyers from one day of filming. For a TikTok version of the same idea, read how to sell digital products with TikTok.

Driving traffic beyond YouTube

YouTube alone is not a full marketing plan. Share each video on Pinterest, where buyers search for planners, templates and printables, and on Instagram, where short clips do well. For a full Pinterest plan, read how to sell digital products with Pinterest.

Build an email list with a free sample, such as one template or one printable, in exchange for an email address. Email your list when you publish a new video, and tell them what product it links to. A viewer who joins your list is worth more than a viewer who watches once and leaves.

Measuring what works: clicks, conversion and tracking

You cannot improve what you do not measure. YouTube Studio shows how many people watched each video, how long they watched, and how many clicked on links. It also shows which videos send the most traffic to your store, when you use trackable links.

Use a separate link for each video where your store allows it, so you can see which video brings buyers. For example, add a simple tag to the link, such as the video name, so your store reports show which video the buyer came from. Check the numbers once a week, not once a day, because daily numbers jump around. Look for the pattern: which video format gets the most clicks, which product gets the most sales, and which titles get the most views. Make more of what works, and stop making what does not.

Conversion means the share of viewers who buy. It is usually low, often well under one in a hundred viewers, because most viewers watch to learn, not to buy. Do not expect every video to sell.

Example only: imagine one tutorial video brings 5,000 views in a month. Of those viewers, 100 click the link to your store, which is a click rate of 2 percent, because 100 divided by 5,000 is 0.02. Of those 100 visitors, 5 buy a printable planner priced at $15, which is a conversion rate of 5 percent from clicks, because 5 divided by 100 is 0.05. Gross sales from that video are 5 times $15, which is $75, before store fees and tax. These numbers are an example only, and they are not typical results. Track the trend over months, not days.

What it costs and what to expect

You can start with a phone, free editing software and a free YouTube account. A better microphone and a simple light improve the sound and the picture, but they are not needed for your first videos. Your store may charge listing fees or a monthly fee, and you may pay a payment processing fee on each sale, so check the current fee page of any store before you rely on a figure.

Expect a slow start. A new channel with a few videos may wait weeks or months for its first sale, because it has no audience and little search history. Plan to publish at least one video a week for three months before you judge the channel. Sales often come in waves, with quiet weeks followed by a few orders at once. Do not expect steady daily income in the first months, and do not spend money on ads until you know which videos already bring clicks.

Common mistakes

  • Selling in the video before giving any free value, which makes viewers leave.
  • Hiding the product link deep in the description, where viewers never see it.
  • Making one video that offers five different products, which confuses viewers.
  • Waiting until the channel is big before linking a product, when small channels can sell too.
  • Using only one video style, so viewers get bored and stop watching.
  • Ignoring YouTube Studio data, so you keep making videos that do not bring clicks.
  • Tagging products that are not shown in the video, which breaks YouTube's tagging guidelines.
  • Promising that a product will earn the buyer money, when results vary and depend on the buyer.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be careful of services that offer to buy views, subscribers or comments for your channel. Buying views breaks YouTube's rules, and YouTube removes fake views and can close channels that do it. Grow slowly with real viewers, even if it feels slow at first.

Watch for messages that claim your channel breaks a rule and ask you to click a link to fix it. These are often phishing messages. Only follow links from YouTube Studio or the official YouTube site, never from a message. Do not pay anyone who offers guaranteed sales for a fee, because no one can guarantee sales. Keep every sale in your store, and never take payment through a direct message.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell digital products on YouTube?

Make free videos that solve a problem your product helps with, then link the product in the first two lines of the description, in a pinned comment, and on an end screen. Set up a store first, such as Gumroad, Etsy or Stan Store, and link every video to it. Give value first, then offer the product as the natural next step.

Can I sell digital products without showing my face?

Yes, you can sell digital products with a faceless channel. Use screen recordings, voiceovers, slides and stock footage, and show the product in use. A template seller can record their screen, and a preset seller can show before and after photos with a voiceover. Viewers care about the help, not about seeing a face.

How do I tag products in YouTube videos?

Product tagging is part of YouTube Shopping, and the exact steps and eligibility rules change, so check YouTube's official help and YouTube Studio before you plan around them. In general terms, you connect a store to your channel, then tag products in your videos so viewers can tap and buy. Only tag products that are clearly shown in the video, and follow YouTube's tagging guidelines.

Do I need a big channel to sell digital products?

No, you do not need a big channel to sell digital products. A small channel with the right viewers can sell, because relevance matters more than size. A video with 1,000 views from people who want your product can outsell a video with 100,000 views from people who do not. Publish regularly, link every video to your store, and let the audience grow over months.

What videos sell digital products best?

Tutorials that show the product in use sell best, followed by free sample videos that give away part of the product, and before and after videos for presets and art. Shorts can bring new viewers fast, and frequently asked questions videos answer buyer doubts on camera. Test three formats, check which ones bring clicks in YouTube Studio, and make more of what works.

Conclusion

To sell digital products with YouTube, make free videos that teach a skill, match each video to one product, link that product in the description, the pinned comment and the end screen, and use Shorts to reach new viewers. Check your numbers in YouTube Studio each week, and make more of the videos that bring clicks and sales.
