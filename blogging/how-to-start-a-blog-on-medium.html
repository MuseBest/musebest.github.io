How to Start a Blog on Medium in 2026

To start a blog on Medium, you need an account, a complete profile, and one clear topic to write about first. Medium is a free publishing platform where you can write and publish stories without building a website. This guide shows you how to set up your account, write and publish your first story, submit to publications, import posts from your own blog, and earn through the Partner Program.

Medium versus your own blog

Medium and your own blog serve different purposes. Medium is faster to start and brings built in readers, but you do not own the platform, and your income depends on Medium's rules. Your own blog takes longer to build, but you own the site, the email list, and the monetisation.

Factor Medium Your own blog
Setup time Minutes, no hosting needed A weekend, plus hosting
Cost to start Free to write and read some stories 45 to 300 dollars in year one
Built in readers Yes, Medium shows stories to readers No, you must earn every reader
Design control Very little, one standard layout Full control of design
Earning options Partner Program only Ads, affiliates, products, sponsors
Ownership Medium owns the platform You own the site and the list
Best for Testing ideas and building a habit A long term business

Many writers use both. They test ideas on Medium, then build their best work into a blog they own. If you already have a blog, you can use Medium to reach new readers, and import your posts with a canonical link, as explained below.

Step 1: Create your Medium account

To start a blog on Medium, go to Medium and sign up with your email address or your Google account. Confirm your account through the link or code Medium sends you. Choose a username that is short and easy to remember, because it forms part of your profile address. Pick a display name that matches the name you want readers to know you by.

After signup, Medium asks you to pick three to five topics you are interested in. Pick topics you actually want to write about, because Medium uses them to show your stories to readers.

Step 2: Set up your profile

A complete profile matters more on Medium than on most platforms, because readers decide in seconds whether to trust a new writer. It is also one of the Partner Program requirements. Add a clear photo of yourself, and write a short bio of one or two sentences that says what you write about and who it helps.

Keep your bio honest and specific. "I write about saving money on a small income" tells readers far more than "writer and dreamer". Do not use a fake name or a stock photo, because readers trust real people.

Step 3: Pick your topic and plan your first story

Pick one topic for your first story, and one reader you are writing for. Good first stories answer a question, explain a process, or share a lesson learned. Avoid writing about ten topics at once. Medium rewards writers who publish regularly on a clear theme.

Plan the story before you write it. Write the main point in one sentence, then list three to five points that support it. Aim for a story that takes five to seven minutes to read, which is roughly 800 to 1,200 words.

Step 4: Write your first story in the Medium editor

Click your profile picture, then choose Write. The Medium editor is simple on purpose: you type, and the text appears cleanly on the page. Start with a clear title that says what the reader will learn, then add a subtitle of one sentence that adds a detail or a benefit. Write a short first paragraph that names the reader's problem, then use headings to break the story into sections.

Use short paragraphs of two to four sentences, because most Medium readers are on phones. Add examples from your own work or life, but do not invent experiences, earnings, or results. If you quote a fact, link to the source. Read your draft out loud before you publish, and cut any sentence that does not help the reader.

Step 5: Format, images, and alt text

Good formatting makes your story easy to read and helps Medium show it to more readers. Use headings for each section, bold only the few key ideas, and use bullet lists for groups of items.

Add at least one image, ideally near the top of the story. You can use your own photos, or free images from sites such as Unsplash or Pexels. Always credit the image source, and add alt text that describes the image in plain words. Never use an image you do not have the right to use.

Step 6: Tags, SEO title, and SEO description

Before you publish, Medium lets you add up to five topics, which act as tags. Choose all five, and pick tags that readers actually follow, such as Writing, Personal Finance, or Productivity. Tags help Medium show your story to the right readers, so choose them with care.

Medium also lets you set an SEO title and an SEO description in the story settings. Keep the SEO title to about 40 to 50 characters and put the main topic first. Keep the SEO description to about 140 to 156 characters and say what the reader will learn. These settings help your story rank on Google, so do not skip them.

Step 7: Publish your first story

When your draft is ready, click Publish, then check the title, subtitle, and tags one last time. Preview the story to see how it looks on a phone, then publish it.

After you publish, share the story once where your readers already are, such as on LinkedIn. Reply to every comment you receive, because comments help your story reach more readers. Do not use clickbait titles, because Medium may limit stories that mislead readers.

Publications: what they are and how to submit

A publication is a collection of stories on Medium, run by an editor, on one theme. Publishing in a publication can bring your story to far more readers than your own profile, because publications have followers who read everything they publish. Look for publications that publish writers like you, and read their submission rules before you submit.

To submit a story, follow the publication's own process, which is usually shown on its homepage. Common steps are: follow the publication, read its rules, write your story as a draft, then use the "Add to publication" option in the story menu and wait for the editor to review it. Editors may take up to a week to reply. If your story is not accepted, you can still publish it on your own profile. Never pay anyone to publish your story in a publication.

Importing a post from your own blog

If you already have a blog, you can bring your posts to Medium with the import tool. The import tool is only available on the web. Go to Stories, click "Import a story", paste the URL of your post, and publish. According to the official Medium Help Center page on importing, updated 11 November 2026, the import tool automatically backdates the post to the original publication date and adds a canonical link to the original source.

A canonical link is a tag that tells search engines which version of a page is the original. Only import content you have the right to publish. If the import tool cannot read a page, copy the text into a new draft by hand, then set the canonical link yourself in More settings, under Advanced Settings.

Growing readers slowly

Medium rewards consistency, not speed. Publish one story a week, every week, rather than ten stories in one week and none for a month. Reply to comments on your stories, and leave thoughtful comments on stories by writers in your topic.

Watch your story stats, which show views, reads, and read ratio. The read ratio tells you how many readers finished your story. If a story has many views but a low read ratio, the title promised more than the story gave, or the opening was weak. Use that lesson in your next story.

Making money: the Partner Program

Medium pays writers through the Partner Program, which shares revenue from paying members who read your stories. The rules change often, so always check the official Medium Help Center page on Partner Program eligibility, updated 11 November 2026, before you rely on them. As of November 2026, the page lists these requirements: an active membership, at least 1 published story, a complete profile, a bank in an eligible country, age 18 or older, and agreement to the Partner Program Terms and the paywall content policy.

Note that the list includes Pakistan among the eligible countries. Older guides still quote different rules, such as 6 published stories and 3 months of activity. Those guides are out of date, so follow the current official page, not the old summaries.

Membership pricing is reported by secondary sources as 5 dollars a month or 50 dollars a year for a standard membership, and 15 dollars a month or 150 dollars a year for Friend of Medium. These prices were not checked on the official membership page, so confirm them on Medium before you pay.

Earnings depend on how many paying members read your stories and how long they read, so income varies widely and is not guaranteed. Most new writers earn very little in the first months. Treat Medium as a way to build an audience and a writing habit first, and as income second.

Worked example: what one story could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.

Imagine you publish a story that gets 1,000 reads from paying members in a month. If Medium paid an average of 2 cents per member read, which is a made up figure for this example only, that would be 1,000 times 0.02 dollars, which is 20 dollars. If only 200 of those readers were paying members, the same rate would give 200 times 0.02 dollars, which is 4 dollars.

The lesson is that Medium income depends on paying members, not on total views, and on read time, not on clicks. Ten stories each earning 4 dollars is 40 dollars a month. One hundred stories earning the same is 400 dollars a month, but that level needs a year or more of steady writing and a loyal audience. That is why consistency matters more than one viral story.

Starting a blog on Medium is free, which is its main advantage. The table below shows typical costs as of November 2026. Prices vary, so confirm each one on Medium before you spend.

Friend of Medium 15 dollars a month or 150 dollars a year Higher tier, prices from secondary sources
Images Free Use your own photos or free stock sites

Expect the first month to bring almost no readers. Months 6 to 12 is when a steady writer starts to build a small following, and some writers earn a few dollars a month from the Partner Program by the end of year one. These are broad ranges, not promises.

Common mistakes

  • Writing about ten topics at once, which leaves readers unsure what to follow you for.
  • Publishing long unplanned stories, which readers abandon halfway through.
  • Using clickbait titles, which Medium may limit and readers will not trust.
  • Never editing a draft, which leaves typos and weak openings in place.
  • Giving up after three stories, before Medium has had time to show your work to readers.
  • Quoting old Partner Program rules from out of date guides, instead of checking the official Help Center.
  • Publishing the same story in several publications at once, which editors dislike.
  • Ignoring comments, even though replies help your stories reach more readers.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be wary of anyone who offers to grow your Medium following for a large upfront fee, or who guarantees a set income from the Partner Program. No one can guarantee Medium earnings or distribution. Avoid expensive courses that promise fast riches.

Watch out for services that sell claps, followers, or reads. Buying engagement breaks Medium's rules and can get your account limited or removed. Never copy another writer's story and publish it as your own, because Medium may remove it and limit your account. Also be careful with imported posts. Only import content you own or have the right to publish, and always keep the canonical link in place so your original blog gets the credit.

Frequently asked questions

Is Medium free to start a blog?

Yes. Creating an account, writing, and publishing stories is free. You only pay if you choose a membership, which costs a monthly or yearly fee, and which you need if you want to join the Partner Program. Check the current price on Medium, because it can change.

Can you make money on Medium?

Yes, through the Partner Program, which pays writers based on paying members reading their stories. Income is not guaranteed, and most new writers earn very little at first.

Do I need a website to write on Medium?

No. Medium hosts your stories, so you do not need a website, hosting, or design skills. If you already have a blog, you can import your posts to Medium, and the import tool adds a canonical link to your original post.

How do I get readers on Medium?

Publish one story a week on a clear topic, use all five tags, submit to publications, reply to comments, and share each story once where your readers already are. Growth is slow at first, and consistency matters more than one viral story.

What is the Medium Partner Program?

It is Medium's program that pays writers from member revenue, based on how much paying members read their stories. Eligibility rules change often, so check the official Medium Help Center page before you enrol, and do not rely on older guides that quote different rules.

Should I start on Medium or on my own blog?

Start a blog on Medium if you want to test ideas quickly, build a writing habit, and reach readers without building a website. Start your own blog if you want full control, an email list you own, and more ways to earn. Many writers use both, and Medium is a good place to start before you build a blog.

Conclusion

To start a blog on Medium, create a free account, complete your profile, pick one topic, plan and write your first story in the editor, add images with alt text, choose five tags and set your SEO title and description, publish the story, then submit later stories to publications and grow readers by publishing weekly and replying to comments. Check the current Partner Program rules on the official Help Center before you enrol, and treat Medium as a place to build an audience first and earn second.
