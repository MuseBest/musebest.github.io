23:Taff,<h2 id="pick-a-topic-narrow-enough-to-win">Pick a topic narrow enough to win</h2>
<p>"The best laptops" is a topic you will not rank for as a beginner. "Laptops for architecture students on a budget" is a topic you might. A narrow topic lets you write the best answer on the internet for a specific reader — and that is how small blogs grow.</p>
<h2 id="choose-a-platform">Choose a platform</h2>
<p>You need a platform you control, with your own domain name. Free hosted platforms are fine for practice, but if you want the blog to earn money later, own the domain and be able to move your content. Keep the setup simple: a fast theme, a clear structure, and analytics you actually look at.</p>
<h2 id="the-technical-setup-briefly">The technical setup, briefly</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Domain:</strong> short, memorable, and easy to spell out loud.</li>
<li><strong>Hosting:</strong> any reliable shared or cloud host with backups.</li>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> a lightweight theme, compressed images, and no unnecessary plugins.</li>
<li><strong>Structure:</strong> categories for your main topics, and a clear URL pattern such as /category/article-slug/.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="plan-your-first-ten-posts">Plan your first ten posts</h2>
<p>Before publishing, write down ten article ideas that answer real questions people search for. Each post should target one question and answer it better than the current results. A good first list mixes:</p>
<ul>
<li>Beginner guides ("How to…")</li>
<li>Comparisons ("X vs Y")</li>
<li>Lists with real criteria ("Best X for…")</li>
<li>Answers to specific questions</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="writing-for-readers-and-search">Writing for readers and search</h2>
<p>Write the answer first, then the background. Use headings that match the questions readers ask, short paragraphs, and examples. Add internal links to your own related posts — it helps readers and search engines understand your site.</p>
<p>Do not write for search engines at the expense of readers. The sites that last are the ones people return to.</p>
<h2 id="how-blogs-earn-money">How blogs earn money</h2>
<p>Common routes, in order of realism for a new blog:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Affiliate links</strong> in genuinely useful articles.</li>
<li><strong>Display ads</strong>, once traffic is meaningful.</li>
<li><strong>Your own products or services</strong>, once you have an audience that trusts you.</li>
<li><strong>Sponsorships</strong>, once brands can see your reach.</li>
</ol>
<p>None of these work without traffic, and traffic follows useful content published consistently.</p>
<h2 id="a-realistic-schedule">A realistic schedule</h2>
<p>One good post a week beats five rushed posts. Publish weekly for six months before judging the blog. Most blogs that fail do so because they stop in month two.</p>
