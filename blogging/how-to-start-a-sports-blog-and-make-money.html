How to Start a Sports Blog and Make Money in 2026

To start a sports blog and make money, you need a niche you can own, evergreen guides that keep ranking long after match day, and a monetisation plan that does not depend on betting tips. Many new sports blogs fail because they only cover breaking news, which big sports sites publish faster and rank higher for. This guide shows you how to pick a niche, set up your blog, plan content that lasts, handle match day traffic, and earn honestly, with real costs and a realistic timeline.

Is a sports blog still worth starting in 2026?

Yes, to start a sports blog and make money in 2026, focus on a narrow niche and write evergreen content first. Sports fans search every day for explainers, rules, tactics, gear advice, and history, and those searches last for years. A blog that only reports last night's score will always lose to big sports sites. A blog that explains how a tactic works, or which boots suit a beginner, can rank for years and earn steadily.

Sports blogging suits people who follow one sport closely and enjoy writing about it in depth. It also needs patience, because a new blog takes months to rank, and sports traffic is seasonal. If you can publish steadily for a year, it is a realistic side income.

Step 1: Pick your sports niche

A niche is the small corner of sport your blog focuses on. It tells readers what to expect and tells Google what your blog is about. Pick a sport and an angle you know well, because you will need dozens of post ideas. The table below shows niches that work well, with the audience and the income fit for each one.

Sports niche Who reads it How it can earn
One football team or league Fans of that team or league Affiliate links for tickets and gear, ads
Fantasy football advice Fantasy players, mostly weekly Affiliate links for apps, premium tips, ads
Running for beginners New runners of all ages Affiliate links for shoes and gear, courses
Cricket tactics and history Cricket fans, often in South Asia Affiliate links for gear, ads, memberships
Women's football Fans underserved by big sites Affiliate links, sponsorships, memberships
Esports guides Gamers and young fans Affiliate links for gear, streaming, ads
Sports equipment reviews Buyers comparing gear Affiliate links, sponsored reviews

Step 2: Choose a blog name and domain

Your blog name should be short, easy to spell, and clear about the sport or angle. Avoid names that only make sense to you, and avoid names that lock you into one team if your interests will grow. "Anfield Notes" works while you write about one club, but it feels wrong if you later cover the whole league.

Be careful with trademark risk. Do not use a team name, a league name, or a famous athlete's name in your domain, because clubs and leagues protect their names and can ask you to stop. Use your own name or a descriptive phrase instead. Check that the exact domain name is free, ideally with a .com ending, and that the same name is free on social media. Keep the name under three words if you can.

Step 3: Set up your blog

You can set up a sports blog in a weekend with a small budget. Use WordPress, which is the free publishing platform that most independent blogs run on, because it gives you full control of your site and your content. You can read more on the WordPress website before you start. Avoid a free hosted blog if you plan to earn money, because free platforms limit ads, affiliate links, and design.

Buy hosting first, then install WordPress through your host, which usually takes a few clicks. Pick a fast, light theme, and keep the design simple so match reports load quickly on phones. Add these pages before you publish: About, Contact, Privacy Policy, and Affiliate Disclosure. The disclosure matters, because you must tell readers when a link earns you a commission. Our guide to how to start a blog on WordPress walks through the setup step by step.

Install only a few plugins: one for SEO, one for backups, one for security, and one for caching, because sports blogs get traffic spikes on match days. Set up Google Search Console so you can see which searches bring readers to your blog.

Plan evergreen content first, then add timely coverage

Evergreen content is content that stays useful for months or years, such as a guide to the offside rule or a review of running shoes. Timely content is content about last night's match, which stops getting traffic in days. New sports blogs should write evergreen content first, because it builds search traffic that lasts, and it is the only way a small blog can compete with big sports sites.

Start with 20 evergreen posts before you add regular match coverage. Good evergreen topics include explainers, rules guides, best of lists, gear reviews, and history posts. Once the evergreen base is live, add one timely post per match or event, and link it back to your evergreen guides.

Write each post to solve one problem fully. Use short paragraphs, clear headings, and facts you have checked. Sports fans notice errors quickly, so check scores, dates, and player names against an official source before you publish. Never invent a quote from a player or a manager, and never present a rumour as a fact.

How to get readers: SEO, social, and email

Search is your slow but steady channel. Each post should target one question, use that question in the title and first paragraph, and answer it directly in the first few lines. Use schema markup for sports content where your SEO plugin supports it, because it can help search engines show your content clearly. Link to your own related posts where it helps the reader.

Social media suits sports blogs well, because fans talk about matches in real time. Share your posts on the platform where your fans already gather, such as X, Reddit, or a Facebook group for your team. Post during and just after matches, when fans are searching for reaction and analysis.

Email is your safety net. Offer a simple free download, such as a printable fixture list, in exchange for an email address. Send one short email a week with your new posts. A list of 500 engaged fans is worth more than 5,000 followers who never visit your blog.

How sports blogs make money

When you start a sports blog and make money from it, you will earn in six main ways, and most successful blogs use more than one. The mix changes as the blog grows. Early on, affiliate links are the most realistic. Ads and sponsorships usually come later, once traffic grows.

Display ads pay you when readers see or click adverts on your blog. They pay little at low traffic, so do not add them in the first months. Ad networks such as Mediavine have traffic requirements, so check their current rules before you apply. Sports blogs can earn well from ads once traffic grows, because match day spikes bring many readers at once.

Affiliate marketing means you recommend a product and earn a small commission if a reader buys through your link. Sports specific options include gear, shoes, tickets, streaming subscriptions, and apps. Amazon Associates is the easiest place to start, because readers already shop there. Always check the current commission rate on the program page, because rates change and vary by product type. Add a clear disclosure near any affiliate link, as the FTC endorsement guides require.

Sponsored content is paid content from sports brands, such as a kit maker or a streaming service. Brands usually approach blogs with steady traffic and a clear audience. Only accept products you would use yourself, and say clearly when a post is sponsored.

Digital products suit sports blogs well. A training plan, a fantasy football guide, or a tactics ebook can sell for a few dollars each. Memberships are a fifth option: a paid tier with extra analysis or an ad free version. Our guide to how to monetise a blog with memberships explains how to set one up.

Sports betting content: legality, disclosure, and responsible gambling

Betting content is a real income route for some sports blogs, but it carries the highest risk, so treat it with care. Betting laws differ by country and by state, and in some places betting adverts are banned or tightly limited. Never promote betting in a country where it is illegal, and never write betting content aimed at children or young people.

If you do write about betting, follow three rules. First, only link to licensed operators. Second, disclose clearly when a link earns you a commission. Third, include a responsible gambling message on every betting post, with a link to a support service such as the National Council on Problem Gambling, which lists the 1-800-MY-RESET helpline. Never promise that a tip will win, and never present betting as income. If you are unsure about the rules in your country, leave betting content out entirely.

Worked example: what one gear review could earn

Example only: these numbers are not typical results, and they are shown only to explain how the maths works.

Imagine you write a review of running shoes that cost 120 dollars. The affiliate program pays 5 percent commission on each sale. That is 6 dollars per sale. If 2,500 readers visit the post in a month, and 2 percent of them click your link, that is 50 clicks. If 6 percent of those clicks turn into a purchase, that is 3 sales. Three sales at 6 dollars each is 18 dollars for the month from that one post.

The lesson is that one post earns very little. A blog earns when many posts each bring a few dollars, and when older posts keep earning month after month. Twenty posts earning 18 dollars each is 360 dollars a month. One hundred posts earning the same is 1,800 dollars a month, but that level needs a year or more of steady work and strong search traffic. That is why evergreen content matters more than one viral match report.

Starting a sports blog is cheap, but it is not free. The table below shows typical first year costs as of November 2026. Prices vary by provider and by country, so confirm each one before you spend.

Expect the first three months to bring almost no income. Months 6 to 12 is when search traffic usually starts to grow, and some blogs earn 50 to 200 dollars a month by the end of year one. Year two is when ads, sponsorships, and memberships become realistic. These are broad ranges, not promises, and no trustworthy source gives a reliable average income for sports blogs, so be wary of any site that quotes one.

Handling match day traffic spikes

Sports traffic is spiky. A big match can send ten times your normal traffic in one evening, and a slow site will fail just when readers arrive. Use a caching plugin, compress your images, and keep your homepage light.

Publish your match preview before the game, not after it, because readers search for previews in the hours before kick off. Publish your reaction quickly after the game, and link it to your evergreen guides.

Common mistakes

  • Covering only breaking news, which big sports sites publish faster and rank higher for.
  • Using a team or league name in your domain, which can create trademark problems later.
  • Publishing match reports with unchecked scores, dates, or player names, which fans notice at once.
  • Adding display ads in the first month, which slows the site and earns almost nothing.
  • Promoting betting as income, or linking to unlicensed betting sites.
  • Writing about every sport at once, which leaves readers unsure who the blog is for.
  • Ignoring email, even though it is the only audience you truly own.
  • Giving up after three months, before search traffic has had time to grow.

Risks and scams to watch for

Be wary of anyone who offers to grow your blog for a large upfront fee, or who guarantees a set income. No one can guarantee search rankings or affiliate sales. Avoid expensive courses that promise fast riches.

Watch out for betting tip scams, where someone sells "guaranteed" tips or asks you to promote them. Never promote a tipster you have not checked, and never present a tip as a sure win. Be careful with affiliate programs that ask you to pay to join. Legitimate affiliate programs are free to join. Also be careful with images. Do not use photos from sports sites or agencies without permission, because they are copyrighted. Use your own photos, or free stock images with a clear licence.

Frequently asked questions

How do sports blogs make money?

Sports blogs earn through display ads, affiliate links for gear, tickets, and streaming, sponsored content, digital products, memberships, and services. Most use more than one route. Ads and sponsorships usually need steady traffic first, while affiliate links can start earning sooner.

How do I start a sports blog with no experience?

Pick one sport and one angle you follow closely, set up a simple WordPress blog, and write 20 evergreen posts that answer questions fans search for. You do not need journalism experience, but you do need to check facts carefully, because sports fans notice errors quickly.

What should I write about on a sports blog?

Write evergreen guides first, such as rules explainers, tactics guides, gear reviews, and history posts, then add timely match coverage. Evergreen posts keep ranking for months, while match reports stop getting traffic in days.

Can you make money from a sports blog?

Yes, some do, but most earn slowly. To start a sports blog and make money takes time: a blog with steady traffic after a year might earn 50 to 200 dollars a month, but those figures are broad ranges, not promises. Be wary of sites that quote a precise average income, because no trustworthy source gives one.

How long does it take a sports blog to grow?

Most blogs take 6 to 12 months to build meaningful search traffic. The first affiliate sales may come sooner, but ads and sponsorships usually need several months of traffic growth. Publishing one or two useful posts a week, with evergreen content first, speeds this up.

Can I use team names in my blog domain?

It is risky. Teams, leagues, and athletes protect their names, and using one in your domain can lead to a legal complaint. Use your own name or a descriptive phrase instead, and write about teams in your posts, not in your domain.

Conclusion

To start a sports blog and make money, pick one sport and one angle you can own, choose a domain that avoids trademark risk, set up a simple WordPress blog, publish 20 evergreen guides before you add regular match coverage, grow readers through search, social media, and email, and earn first with affiliate links before you add ads, sponsorships, digital products, or memberships. Treat betting content with care, follow the law in your readers' country, and always link to responsible gambling help.
