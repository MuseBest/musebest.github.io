<h2 id="wordpress-com-or-self-hosted-wordpress-what-each-is">WordPress.com or self hosted WordPress: what each is</h2>
<p>WordPress is free software for building websites and blogs. You can use it in two ways, and the difference is hosting, which is the service that stores your blog files and shows them to visitors.</p>
<p>WordPress.com is a hosted service. It includes managed hosting, and it handles updates, backups and security for you. You sign up, pick a plan, and start writing. The free plan shows WordPress ads and gives you a subdomain such as yourblog.wordpress.com. Plugins and custom themes need a paid WordPress.com plan.</p>
<p>Self hosted WordPress, often called WordPress.org, is the same free software that you install on hosting you choose and manage yourself. You buy a domain and hosting, install WordPress, and you control everything: themes, plugins, ads and your own data. The <a href="https://wordpress.com/support/com-vs-org/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">WordPress.com support page on the two versions</a> explains the difference in full, and it confirms that you own your content on either route and can move it.</p>

<h2 id="step-1-pick-a-topic-and-a-name">Step 1: Pick a topic and a name</h2>
<p>Before you buy anything, pick one topic for your blog, because the topic shapes every step of how to start a blog on WordPress. A blog about one subject grows faster than a blog about everything, because readers and search engines both know what it is for. If you need help, read our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-choose-a-blog-niche" rel="noopener" target="_blank">choosing a blog niche</a> and our general guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-start-a-blog" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to start a blog</a>.</p>
<p>Then pick a name. Keep it short, easy to spell, and easy to say out loud. Avoid hyphens, numbers and words that are hard to pronounce. A name that hints at your topic is better than a clever name nobody understands. Check that no other blog or business already uses the name before you buy the domain.</p>
<h2 id="step-2-register-a-domain">Step 2: Register a domain</h2>
<p>A domain is your blog's address on the web, such as yourblog.com. Buy it from a domain registrar or from your hosting company, so you have one bill and one account to manage. Check three things before you pay: the domain is free, the renewal price is clear, and domain privacy is included or cheap. Domain privacy hides your personal details from the public domain register, and many registrars include it free.</p>
<p>A domain usually costs about 10 to 15 US dollars a year, so check the renewal price carefully. A .com domain is the safest default because readers remember it.</p>
<h2 id="step-3-choose-hosting">Step 3: Choose hosting</h2>
<p>Hosting stores your blog and shows it to visitors. For a new blog, basic shared hosting is enough, and it usually costs about 3 to 6 US dollars a month. Check these six points before you buy:</p>
<ul>
<li>One click WordPress install, so you do not install it by hand.</li>
<li>Free SSL certificate, which gives your blog the padlock in the browser and the https address.</li>
<li>Daily backups, so you can restore your blog if something breaks.</li>
<li>PHP 8.3 or newer, and MariaDB 10.11 or newer or MySQL 8.4 or newer. These are the versions WordPress recommends as of November 2026, on the <a href="https://wordpress.org/about/requirements/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">WordPress.org requirements page</a>.</li>
<li>HTTPS support, which your host should provide with the free SSL certificate.</li>
<li>A clear renewal price, because introductory prices often rise sharply after the first year.</li>
</ul>
<p>Ask the host's support team if you are unsure about any of these. A good host will answer plainly.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-install-wordpress">Step 4: Install WordPress</h2>
<p>Most hosts offer a one click WordPress installer in their control panel. Open your hosting account, find the WordPress installer, and follow these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose the domain you registered for the blog.</li>
<li>Pick a site title, such as the name of your blog. You can change this later.</li>
<li>Create an admin username and a strong password. Do not use "admin" as the username.</li>
<li>Enter an email address you check often, because WordPress uses it for password resets and updates.</li>
<li>Click install and wait a minute or two.</li>
</ol>
<p>When it finishes, log in at yourdomain.com/wp-admin with the username and password you just created. That page is your WordPress dashboard, where you write posts, change the design and manage the blog. Bookmark it.</p>
<h2 id="step-5-pick-one-theme-and-set-up-the-basics">Step 5: Pick one theme and set up the basics</h2>
<p>A theme controls how your blog looks, and picking one is a quick step in how to start a blog on WordPress. Pick one clean, fast theme and stop there. A good theme is simple, works well on phones, and loads quickly. The free themes in the WordPress theme directory are a safe start. Avoid installing five themes to compare them, because switching themes later can break your layout.</p>
<p>Once the theme is active, set up the basics in your dashboard:</p>
<ul>
<li>Site title and tagline: your blog name and one short line that says what the blog is for.</li>
<li>Logo: a simple text logo is fine to start. You can add a designed logo later.</li>
<li>Menu: add Home, About, Blog and Contact, so readers can find their way around.</li>
<li>Permalinks: in Settings, then Permalinks, choose "Post name", so your post addresses look like yourdomain.com/your-post-title instead of yourdomain.com/?p=123.</li>
<li>Time zone: in Settings, then General, set your time zone so post times are correct.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="step-6-install-three-plugins-only">Step 6: Install three plugins only</h2>
<p>A plugin is a small add on that gives WordPress a new feature. New bloggers often install twenty plugins and then wonder why the blog is slow. Install three, and add more only when you have a clear reason.</p>
<ul>
<li>An SEO plugin, such as Yoast SEO or Rank Math, which helps you write titles and meta descriptions for each post.</li>
<li>Google Site Kit, which connects your blog to Google Search Console and Google Analytics so you can see how readers find you.</li>
<li>A backup plugin or an anti spam plugin, such as UpdraftPlus for backups or Akismet for comment spam. If your host already backs up your site daily, pick the anti spam plugin instead.</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep every plugin updated. An old plugin is the most common way a WordPress blog gets hacked.</p>
<h2 id="step-7-publish-your-legal-and-trust-pages">Step 7: Publish your legal and trust pages</h2>
<p>Before you promote your blog, add four pages that make it look trustworthy and keep you on the right side of the rules:</p>
<ul>
<li>Privacy Policy: what data you collect and how you use it. Your SEO plugin or your host may offer a starter template.</li>
<li>About: who you are, what the blog covers, and why readers should trust you.</li>
<li>Contact: a simple form or email address so readers can reach you.</li>
<li>Disclosure: a short note that you may earn a commission when readers buy through your links, if you use affiliate links. Add this page before your first affiliate link goes live.</li>
</ul>
<p>These pages take an afternoon to write and they matter for ads, affiliate programmes and reader trust.</p>
<h2 id="step-8-write-and-publish-your-first-three-posts">Step 8: Write and publish your first three posts</h2>
<p>Do not launch with an empty blog. Write three posts first, so a new visitor has something to read.</p>
<ol>
<li>A cornerstone guide: a long, useful guide to the main topic of your blog, such as "How to start running" for a running blog. This is the post you want search engines to find.</li>
<li>A specific how to: a short, practical post that answers one question, such as "What to eat before a morning run".</li>
<li>A personal post: your story, why you started the blog, and what readers can expect. This post builds trust and shows your voice.</li>
</ol>
<p>Keep each post focused on one question. Start with the answer, then explain the steps, then finish with what to do next. Short paragraphs and clear headings keep posts readable on a phone. Do <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/keyword-research-for-a-blog" rel="noopener" target="_blank">keyword research for a blog</a> before you write, so each post targets a question readers actually search for, and read our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-write-blog-posts-that-rank-on-google" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to write blog posts that rank on Google</a> for the structure that works.</p>
<h2 id="launch-checklist">Launch checklist</h2>
<p>Run through this checklist before you tell anyone about your blog:</p>
<ul>
<li>The blog loads on your phone and on a computer, and the menu works on both.</li>
<li>Every link on the homepage works, and there are no broken images.</li>
<li>The site title, tagline and About page are finished, with no placeholder text left.</li>
<li>The Privacy Policy, Contact and Disclosure pages are published.</li>
<li>In Settings, then Reading, the box "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is unchecked. This is the trap that catches many beginners: if it is checked, Google will not list your blog at all.</li>
<li>You have published your first three posts.</li>
<li>You have tested the contact form by sending yourself a message.</li>
</ul>
<p><img src="/covers/wordpress-blog-launch.jpg" alt="A phone and a laptop side by side on a desk showing the same simple blog page, suggesting testing a blog on mobile before launch" /></p>
<h2 id="connect-search-console-and-analytics">Connect Search Console and Analytics</h2>
<p>Google Search Console is a free tool that shows you how Google sees your blog: which pages are indexed, which search terms bring visitors, and whether anything is broken. Connect it through Google Site Kit, which you installed in Step 6, or add your site directly at search.google.com/search-console and verify it with the method Site Kit or your SEO plugin offers. Google Analytics, also through Site Kit, shows you how many visitors you get and which posts they read. Set both up in your first week, because they only start collecting data from the day you connect them.</p>

<ul>
<li>Week 1: finish the setup, publish your first three posts, and connect Search Console and Analytics.</li>
<li>Week 2: publish two more posts, share each one on one social platform, and fix anything you missed on the launch checklist.</li>
<li>Week 3: publish two more posts, reply to every comment, and start a simple email list.</li>
<li>Week 4: publish two more posts, check Search Console for errors, and update your first posts.</li>
</ul>
<p>That gives you about 10 posts in your first month, which is a realistic target. Keep the same rhythm in month two, and read our guide to <a href="https://earnquill.com/blogging/how-to-grow-blog-traffic" rel="noopener" target="_blank">how to grow blog traffic</a> when you are ready for the next step.</p>
<h2 id="common-mistakes-and-security-basics">Common mistakes and security basics</h2>
<ul>
<li>Starting on a free platform and then finding you cannot use ads, plugins or your own domain properly.</li>
<li>Picking a domain with hyphens or numbers that readers cannot remember or spell.</li>
<li>Installing twenty plugins, which slows the blog and creates security risks.</li>
<li>Leaving the "Discourage search engines" box checked, so Google never lists the blog.</li>
<li>Publishing one post and then stopping for months.</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep your blog safe with four habits. Use a strong, unique password and turn on two factor authentication for your WordPress login. Update WordPress, your theme and your plugins whenever an update appears. Keep a backup, either through your host or a backup plugin. And never install a theme or plugin from an unknown site, because free downloads from random sites often hide malware.</p>
<h2 id="frequently-asked-questions">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3 id="is-wordpress-com-or-wordpress-org-better-for-a-new-blog">Is WordPress.com or WordPress.org better for a new blog?</h3>
<p>It depends on your goal. WordPress.com is simpler, because hosting, updates and backups are handled for you. Self hosted WordPress gives you full control over themes, plugins, ads and your own data. For a blog you plan to grow and earn from, self hosted WordPress is usually the better choice.</p>
<h3 id="how-much-does-it-cost-to-start-a-wordpress-blog">How much does it cost to start a WordPress blog?</h3>
<p>A WordPress blog usually costs about 50 to 150 US dollars in the first year, for a domain, basic hosting and a free theme. Prices change, so check the live pages before you buy anything, and watch the renewal price, not just the first year price.</p>
<h3 id="do-i-need-to-know-coding-to-start-a-wordpress-blog">Do I need to know coding to start a WordPress blog?</h3>
<p>No, you do not need to know coding to start a WordPress blog. The block editor lets you write and format posts without code, and a one click installer handles the setup.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-start-a-wordpress-blog-for-free">Can I start a WordPress blog for free?</h3>
<p>Yes, you can start a free blog on WordPress.com, but the free plan shows WordPress ads, gives you a subdomain instead of your own domain, and limits themes and plugins. If you plan to earn from the blog, self hosted WordPress with your own domain is the better long term home.</p>
<h3 id="how-many-posts-should-i-publish-before-launching">How many posts should I publish before launching?</h3>
<p>Three good posts are enough to launch. A new visitor should find something useful to read, and three posts show what the blog is about.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-until-my-blog-appears-on-google">How long until my blog appears on Google?</h3>
<p>It usually takes a few days to a few weeks for Google to find and index a new blog. Submit your site to Google Search Console, publish regularly, and link your posts to each other.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>You now know how to start a blog on WordPress: pick self hosted WordPress if you plan to grow and earn, or WordPress.com if you want the simplest start. Register a domain, choose hosting that meets the WordPress requirements, install WordPress with one click, pick one theme, install three plugins, and publish your first three posts. Then run the launch checklist, connect Search Console, and publish every week.</p>
