How to Start a Tech Blog and Make Money in 2026

How to start a tech blog and make money is a question many tech lovers ask when they spend hours testing gadgets, apps, and software and wonder if that time can pay. You may enjoy explaining new tools to friends, but turning that habit into a blog that earns income takes a plan. This guide shows you how to pick a narrow tech niche, set up the blog, write content readers can trust, and add income in layers.

Can you make money with a tech blog?

Yes, a tech blog can earn money, but it is a slow build, not a quick win. Tech blogs earn through affiliate links, display ads, sponsored posts, digital products, and services such as setup help or consulting. Software and gadget affiliate programs often pay well, because one sale can be worth a lot, and readers often buy soon after reading a review.

A new blog usually earns little or nothing for the first few months. You need readers first, and readers need useful posts first. Tech moves fast, so your posts must stay current, and that takes steady work. Most blogs that earn steady income have been publishing for a year or more, with dozens of helpful posts and a growing email list.

You do not need to be a programmer to start a tech blog. You do need curiosity, honest testing, and the patience to explain things in plain words. Readers trust bloggers who admit what they have not tested and who update old posts when facts change.

Step 1: Pick a narrow tech niche

A narrow niche helps you stand out. A blog about "technology" competes with huge news sites. A blog about one clear topic for one clear reader has a real chance to rank and to build loyal readers.

Tech niches that work for new bloggers

Pick a niche that matches what you already use and enjoy. Good starting niches include budget smartphones, home office setups, productivity apps, WordPress tools, AI tools for small businesses, smart home devices, or laptop buying guides for students. Each of these has clear questions readers search for, and each one lets you write from real use.

Your niche should also fit products you can recommend honestly. A smartphone blog can link to phones and accessories, and a software blog can link to apps and tools. If the niche has no honest product attached to it, earning from it will be harder.

How to check demand before you commit

Search for your niche topic and read the top results. If the first page is full of big tech news sites, look for a smaller corner. For example, instead of "best laptop", try "best laptop for students on a budget" or "best laptop for video editing under a set price". Smaller corners are easier to win.

Check that people ask questions about the niche. Search for "how to" phrases and read forum threads. A niche with real questions is a niche with real readers. If you want a wider method for picking a topic, read our guide on how to choose a blog niche before you decide.

Step 2: Work out what the blog will cost

A tech blog is cheap to start, but the costs are real and they repeat each year. Work them out before you buy anything, so a renewal price never surprises you. The ranges below are typical first year ranges for a self hosted WordPress blog. Prices change, so check each provider's live pricing page before you buy.

Item Typical first year cost Notes
Domain name 10 to 15 US dollars per year Renewal is often higher than the first year price
Web hosting 30 to 100 US dollars per year Basic shared hosting is enough to start
WordPress theme Free to 60 US dollars A fast, simple theme is best for tech readers
Email tool Free to 120 US dollars per year Free plans cover small lists
Test products 0 to 200 US dollars per year Start with products you already own
Total About 40 to 495 US dollars Plus your time, which is the biggest cost

Your time is the real cost. Writing one good review or tutorial can take 5 to 10 hours, including testing and screenshots. Keep costs low at the start. You do not need to buy gadgets to review them. Start with the tech you already own.

Step 3: Set up the blog

Set up the blog in one focused weekend, then move on to writing. Do not spend weeks on design.

First, buy a short domain name that is easy to spell and say out loud. Avoid hyphens and numbers. A name that hints at your niche helps readers understand the blog at a glance.

Next, buy hosting and install WordPress. WordPress is free software, and most hosts offer a one click install. You can read more on the official WordPress.org site, which describes WordPress as the open source publishing platform. Choose a fast, clean theme, because tech readers leave slow sites quickly. Make sure the theme looks good on a phone, since many readers will visit on mobile.

Then install a small set of essential plugins: one for backups, one for security, and one for SEO basics. Keep the count low, because too many plugins slow the site down.

Then create four essential pages before you publish your first post: an About page, a Contact page, a Privacy Policy page, and an Affiliate Disclosure page that says you may earn a commission from some links. These pages build trust and they matter for affiliate programs later.

Step 4: Plan your content mix

New tech bloggers often publish only news, then wonder why traffic never builds. News dates fast and big sites cover it first. A mix of lasting content works better.

Your first 10 posts

Plan the first 10 posts as a mix of four content types. Reviews explain one product in depth, with what is good and what is not. Tutorials show how to do one task step by step. Comparisons help a reader choose between two products. Buying guides answer one question for one reader, such as which laptop suits a student budget.

A good first 10 might include three tutorials, three reviews of products you already own, two comparisons, and two buying guides. That mix shows readers what the blog covers, and it gives search engines clear topics to rank. Write the titles as questions readers actually ask, and use keyword research for a blog to check which phrases people search for. Long, specific phrases are easier for a new blog to rank for than short, general ones.

Keep a simple list of post ideas and add to it every week. When a friend asks you a tech question, write it down, because other people will search for the same answer.

Step 5: Write tech content readers can trust

Tech readers are quick to spot a review written by someone who never touched the product. Honest testing is what sets a good tech blog apart from a site that only rewrites press releases.

How to test a product before you review it

Use the product for real tasks before you write about it. Use a phone for a week of normal calls, photos, and apps. Use software for a real project, not just the demo. Note the battery life, the speed, the ease of setup, and the problems you hit. Take your own screenshots and photos. They prove you used the product, and they help readers more than stock images.

List both strengths and weaknesses in every review. No product is perfect, and a review with no downsides looks like an advert. Say who the product suits and who should skip it. That honest framing builds trust and helps readers decide.

What to do when you have not used a product

Sometimes you need to write about a product you have not used. Be honest about that in the post. Say clearly that the post is based on the maker's specs, on user reviews, and on comparisons with products you have used. Do not pretend you tested it. Do not copy the maker's claims as facts. Use words such as "the maker claims" and link to the official spec page so readers can check for themselves.

Check specs and prices on the day you publish, and add a note with the date. Tech facts change fast. A price can rise, a feature can change, and a model can be replaced. Set a reminder to update popular posts every few months, and change the date note when you do.

Step 6: Get readers

A new tech blog gets readers from search first, then from other channels. Search takes time, so start the other channels early.

Learn basic SEO. Use one clear keyword per post, write a useful title, and structure the post with clear headings. Link to your own related posts where it helps the reader. Our guide on how to write blog posts that rank on Google explains the basics in plain words.

YouTube works well beside a tech blog. A short video that shows a product in use can send readers to the full written review, and the blog can send readers to the video. A phone camera and clear audio are enough to start. If video interests you, read our guide on how to start a tech YouTube channel and earn money for the video side of the same niche.

Pinterest also works for tech content, especially setup guides and buying guides. Make a simple image for each post and pin it to a matching board.

Start an email list from the first month. Offer a simple free download, such as a checklist for buying a laptop, in exchange for an email address. Email readers come back, and they are the readers most likely to buy a product you recommend later.

You can also use AI as a drafting aid, but always test the facts yourself and rewrite in your own words. AI can draft a structure, but it cannot test a product for you.

Step 7: Add income in layers

Do not add every income method on day one. Add them in layers, as the blog grows, so each one has a real chance to work.

Monetisation comparison table

Method When to add it What it needs Income pattern
Affiliate marketing After 10 useful posts Product links in reviews and guides Grows with buyer traffic
Display ads Once traffic reaches a network minimum Steady page views Slow at first, steadier later
Sponsored posts Once brands can see your audience A media kit and traffic data Flat fee per post
Digital products Once you know what readers pay for A checklist, template, or short course Higher per sale, slower to build
Services Once you have proven skill A services page and a way to book Highest per client, most time

Affiliate marketing usually comes first for tech blogs, because reviews and buying guides naturally recommend products. Only link to products you have checked, and disclose the link clearly near it. Google's own guide to how to make money blogging explains the main models, including ads, affiliate marketing, products, and subscriptions.

Display ads come next, once traffic reaches the level an ad network accepts. Read our guide on how to monetise a blog with display ads when you are ready. Sponsored posts come later, when brands can see your traffic and your audience. Say no to sponsors whose products you would not use yourself, and disclose every sponsored post at the top.

What it costs and what to expect

Be honest with yourself about the timeline. A tech blog is a long project, and the stages below are typical, not promised. Your results will vary with your niche, your pace, and your traffic.

Stage Time What usually happens
Setup and first posts Months 1 to 3 10 to 20 posts published, almost no traffic, no income
Early growth Months 4 to 9 Search traffic starts, a few hundred visits a month, first small affiliate income possible
Steady growth Months 10 to 18 Traffic grows, email list grows, income becomes more regular but still modest
Established After 18 months A larger library, steadier traffic, and several income layers working together

Example only: these numbers are not typical results. Imagine a tech review post that gets 10,000 visitors in a month. If 3 percent of those visitors click an affiliate link, that is 10,000 times 0.03, which equals 300 clicks. If 5 percent of those clicks turn into a sale, that is 300 times 0.05, which equals 15 sales. At 40 US dollars commission per sale, that is 15 times 40 US dollars, which equals 600 US dollars for that post in that month. Check the maths: 10,000 times 0.03 is 300, 300 times 0.05 is 15, and 15 times 40 is 600. That figure is only an example. Most new blogs take a year or more to reach that traffic, and click and conversion rates vary widely.

Common mistakes

  • Picking a broad niche such as "technology" instead of one narrow topic you can win.
  • Publishing only news, which dates fast and competes with big sites.
  • Writing reviews of products you have never used, and pretending you tested them.
  • Publishing a review with no downsides, which reads like an advert.
  • Adding affiliate links before the blog has useful content, which erodes trust.
  • Ignoring mobile layout, even though many tech readers visit on a phone.
  • Letting specs and prices go out of date instead of updating popular posts.
  • Quitting after three months because the income has not arrived yet.

Risks and scams to watch for

Tech blogging has its own risks. Be wary of brands that offer a free product in exchange for a guaranteed positive review. A free product is fine if you can write an honest review, including the downsides. A guaranteed positive review is not honest, and it can cost you reader trust.

Watch out for fake review sites and paid placements presented as independent tests. If a "review" reads like a sales page, treat it with care, and do not copy its claims. Check any affiliate program before you join it, and never pay a fee to join. Real programs are free to join.

Also protect your readers. Never ask readers for passwords or account details. Never link to pirated software or cracked apps. If a sponsor asks you to make a claim you cannot verify, say no and walk away.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a programmer to start a tech blog?

No, you do not need to be a programmer. Many tech blogs cover gadgets, apps, and buying guides, and none of those need code. Some coding knowledge helps for developer topics, but it is not a requirement to start. Pick a niche that matches what you already know.

How much does a tech blog cost to start?

A basic self hosted blog costs about 40 to 100 US dollars in the first year for a domain and hosting, plus your time. A free theme and free tools are enough to start. Test products add cost only if you buy new gadgets to review, so start with products you already own.

How do tech blogs make money?

Most tech blogs earn through a mix of affiliate commissions, display ads, sponsored posts, digital products, and services. Affiliate links pay when a reader buys, ads pay per page view, sponsored posts pay a flat fee, and products pay per sale. The mix changes as the blog grows.

What should I write about on a tech blog?

Write about the tech you use and the questions people ask you. Tutorials, honest reviews, comparisons, and buying guides work best for a new blog. News is hard for a small blog to win, because big sites publish it first.

Is tech blogging saturated?

Tech is crowded, but it is not closed to new bloggers. Big sites cover broad topics, and a new blog can win smaller corners, such as one app, one device type, or one reader group. A narrow niche, honest testing, and regular updates are how small blogs stand out.

Can I start a tech blog without coding?

Yes, you can start without coding. WordPress with a theme lets you build a professional blog without writing code. Enough basic knowledge to fix small layout problems helps, and it is worth learning, but it is not a requirement to start.

Conclusion: how to start a tech blog and make money in 2026

That is how to start a tech blog and make money in 2026: pick one narrow niche, keep costs low, set up a simple WordPress site, publish a mix of tutorials, reviews, comparisons, and buying guides, test products honestly, keep facts current, grow readers through search and email, and add income in layers as traffic grows.
